# Chapter 39: The Wave of Hands

The silence lasted for one single, frozen second.

And then Town Hall exploded.

Fifteen hundred people rose to their feet as one magnificent wave.

In the orchestra, in the loges, and across the vast curved balconies, three thousand hands rose into the air, fluttering, rotating, and waving in the greatest visual standing ovation in the history of Deaf performance.

The acoustic noise from the hearing crowd was deafening, but it was the physical vibration that shook the building.

Fifteen hundred pairs of feet stamped the ancient wooden floorboards. The shockwave surged through the timber joists, traveling straight up through the soles of Julian's combat boots, filling his bones with pure, undulating electricity.

Julian stood on the apron, his head thrown back, tears streaming unashamedly down his face.

He couldn't see the upper balcony, but he felt the vibration in his teeth, in his lungs, and in his heart.

Deacon stood in the stage-left wings, tears soaking the front of his canvas work jacket, cheering with both fists raised high above his head.

Julian reached out through the bright light, found my hand, and pulled me to the edge of the apron.

We stood together on the lip of the stage, bowing to the left, bowing to the right, our hands locked together in triumph.

The applause rolled over us for ten unbroken minutes.

Julian Vance had not been defeated by the dark. He had conquered it, leaving an indelible mark on the soul of American theater before the curtain came down forever.

When we finally walked off the stage into the wings, Deacon met us with open arms, crushing us both in a fierce, tearful embrace.

The backstage hallway was filled with flowers, champagne, and Deaf activists cheering our names.

Julian looked at me through the crowd, smiled, and signed with one hand: *We won.*"""

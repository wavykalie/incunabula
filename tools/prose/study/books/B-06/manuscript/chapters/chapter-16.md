# Chapter 16: The Blind Spot Test

The snow had stopped by noon, leaving the Pennsylvania Turnpike buried under six inches of blinding, crystalline white powder beneath a cloudless blue sky.

The tow truck had dragged the Econoline to a farm equipment repair garage outside Fairview, where a seventy-year-old mechanic named Earl had patched the bypass hose with two radiator clamps and a length of reinforced tractor tubing. Now we were rolling east again, the engine purring with fresh green coolant, the highway dazzling in the winter sun.

Julian sat in the front passenger seat.

He had put on a pair of dark polarized sunglasses to cut the glare, but his hands were restless. He held both arms extended straight out in front of his chest, palms open, wiggling his ten fingers in the bright sunlight streaming through the windshield.

Slowly, deliberately, he began moving his hands outward toward the side windows.

Ten degrees. Fifteen degrees. Twenty degrees.

At approximately twenty-five degrees of horizontal separation, his fingers stopped moving.

Julian tilted his head slightly, his brow furrowing beneath his knit beanie. He held his left hand completely still in front of his nose, while his right hand crept another two inches toward the passenger door.

He was testing his perimeter.

He wiggled his right thumb. Nothing. He blinked twice, shifting his gaze by a fraction of a millimeter, trying to catch the phantom movement on the edge of his field.

Then his hand dropped to his lap with a heavy, defeated slap.

*Gone,* he signed, keeping his hands low against his thighs where Deacon couldn't see them in the rearview mirror.

I leaned forward from the rear bench, resting my forearms on the back of his bucket seat.

*How much?* I signed quietly into his peripheral cone.

*Three inches on the right,* he answered, his fingers tight and clipped. *Last month in Chicago, I could track my thumb to the window pillar. Now it disappears before the dashboard vent. The blind spot is swallowing the edge.*

He took off his sunglasses. His eyes were bloodshot from the sun glare, the dark pupils surrounded by fine networks of red veins.

*You know what terrifies me, Maya?* he signed, looking down at his scarred palms. *It's not bumping into doors. It's not tripping over cables or spilling coffee on my shirt. It's the faces.*

"The faces?" I repeated softly.

*When I tell a joke on stage,* Julian signed, his chest hitching with sudden emotion, *I used to be able to see the ripple across the first three rows. You drop a punchline, and you watch a woman's mouth fly open. You watch a guy slap his knee. You see sixty different human beings connect with your brain at the exact same millisecond. That's the drug. That's why comics drive five hundred miles in a blizzard for seventy-five dollars.*

He turned his head to look at me through the gap between the bucket seats.

*Now,* he signed, *the front row is just blurry silhouettes. If they're not sitting under twenty-four hundred watts of direct downlight, their faces are blank gray ovals. In Buffalo tonight, I won't be able to see if the deaf kids in the front row are smiling or bored.*

I reached over the leather headrest, took his right hand from his lap, and brought it up to my cheek.

I pressed his palm flat against my skin. I smiled—a wide, deliberate smile that engaged the zygomatic muscles beneath my cheekbones, letting him feel the upward curve of my lip and the crinkle of the skin beside my eye.

"Then feel it," I said, signing with my left hand while his right palm rested against my face. *You don't need sixty faces in the light, Jules. You have my hands, and you have my voice. When they laugh tonight, I will translate every single smile directly into your fingers.*

Julian looked at my face, his palm absorbing the warmth of my skin.

His fingers curled softly around the curve of my jaw, his thumb brushing my lower lip with tender, reverent wonder.

*Deal,* he signed with his free hand.

He turned back toward the windshield, leaving his warm palm resting against my cheek as the highway carried us toward the New York state line."""

# Chapter 36: The New York Arrival

The steel towers of the George Washington Bridge rose through the dawn mist like the ribs of a colossal cathedral.

It was 6:15 AM on Sunday morning.

Below us, the gray waters of the Hudson River flowed silently toward the Atlantic, carrying chunks of winter ice down from the Catskills. Ahead, through the salt-crusted windshield of the Econoline van, the skyline of Manhattan emerged in sharp, majestic relief against a pale amber winter sunrise.

City twelve. The final destination.

Deacon drove in silence, his red-rimmed eyes tracking the toll plaza signs for the Henry Hudson Parkway. He had driven three thousand eight hundred miles across twelve states, through blizzards, black ice, and blown radiator hoses, to bring his best friend to this island.

Julian sat in the front passenger seat, his forehead pressed against the cool glass of the window.

He watched the sun rise over Harlem, the golden rays glinting off the brick tenements and water towers.

He reached back between the seats, his fingers searching blindly through the air until they found mine.

*Manhattan,* he signed with one hand, his fingers resting in my palm.

*We made it, Jules,* I signed back, squeezing his fingers tight.

Julian turned his head toward me. In the morning light, his face was calm, serene, and beautiful.

*Tonight,* he signed, his letters slow and reverent. *Town Hall. Fifteen hundred people. The final bow.*

"Are you ready?" I asked softly.

Julian smiled—a radiant, unshadowed expression that lit up the tired interior of the van.

*With you,* he signed, *I was born ready.*

The van rolled off the bridge ramp onto the parkway, heading south toward 43rd Street."""

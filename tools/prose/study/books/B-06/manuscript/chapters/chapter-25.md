# Chapter 25: The Snowplow Route

Route 73 through the High Peaks of the Adirondacks was an unbroken tunnel of white pine, black granite rock faces, and swirling snowdrifts.

It was four in the afternoon, but the mountain sky had already deepened to charcoal gray. A northern blizzard had roared down from the Saint Lawrence Valley, dropping ten inches of powdery snow on the asphalt in less than three hours.

The Econoline van crept along at twenty-two miles per hour, tucked sixty yards behind the flashing amber beacon of a state department of transportation snowplow. The plow's massive steel blade threw ten-foot plumes of pulverized snow into the hemlock trees along the gorge, clearing two narrow tire ruts in the slush.

Deacon sat forward over the steering wheel, his knuckles white, his jaw set in grim concentration.

"If the plow pulls off at Lake Placid," Deacon announced without looking away from the ruts, "we're stopping at the first motor lodge we see. I'm not driving Cascade Pass in a whiteout with two-wheel drive."

Julian and I sat together on the second-row bench seat, sharing a heavy red wool stadium blanket.

The isolation of the mountain highway was absolute. There were no cell towers in the gorge; our phones had shown *NO SERVICE* since we passed Keene Valley.

For the first time in three weeks, we were completely cut off from Elena Rossi, the Registry of Interpreters for the Deaf, and the looming threat of administrative sanctions.

Julian looked out the side window at the frozen waterfalls cascading down the granite cliffs like jagged curtains of blue ice.

He reached under the blanket and found my hand.

He didn't use visual signs. He used the tactile grammar Marcus had taught him in Rochester, tracing letters and words directly into the palm of my right hand with his forefinger.

*Quiet,* he traced onto my skin.

*Very quiet,* I traced back against his wrist.

Julian turned his head toward me, a faint, tender smile touching the corners of his lips.

*No emails,* he traced. *No promoters. Just snow and mountains.*

*Are you scared about Burlington?* I asked, spelling the letters onto his palm.

Julian paused. His fingers curled around mine, holding my hand firmly in his lap.

*A little,* he traced. *Burlington is small. Brick cellar club. Low wooden ceiling. The promoter said the stage is only eight inches high.*

*That's good,* I traced. *No drop-off risk.*

*No,* Julian spelled slowly. *It means the front row is breathing on my boots. When you're that close, you can't fake anything. They will see every tremor in my hands.*

"You don't need to fake anything, Jules," I whispered aloud, leaning my shoulder against his arm.

Julian lifted my hand from under the blanket, brought it to his mouth, and pressed a quiet, lingering kiss to the center of my palm.

The warmth of his lips sent a shiver straight down my spine.

He closed my fingers over his kiss, pressed my clenched fist against his heart, and looked out into the falling snow as the yellow plow led us deeper into the mountain pass."""

# Chapter 13: The Ethics Warning Letter

The notification chimed on my tablet at 6:15 AM while the Cleveland sky was still the color of wet lead.

I was sitting at the laminate vanity table, tying my hair back into its regulation performance bun, when the red badge popped up over my agency portal app.

> **OFFICIAL CORRESPONDENCE — PRIVILEGED & CONFIDENTIAL**
> **REGISTRY OF INTERPRETERS FOR THE DEAF — ETHICAL PRACTICES SYSTEM**
> **INQUIRY DOSSIER:** EPS-2026-GL-089
> **RESPONDENT:** Maya Lin, NIC, Member #48921
> **SUPERVISING AUTHORITY:** Great Lakes Interpretive Associates / Elena Rossi, Director
> 
> *Dear Ms. Lin,*
> 
> *This office has received formal third-party notification regarding your professional performance during the opening engagements of the 'Julian Vance Farewell Tour' in Chicago and Detroit.*
> 
> *Video recordings uploaded to public social media platforms from the November 4th and November 6th performances have been flagged for administrative review. Preliminary visual inspection indicates recurring anomalies in your operational demeanor:*
> 
> 1. *Non-Standard Proximity: Consistent physical placement within fourteen to twenty-four inches of the consumer, violating standard platform guidelines.*
> 2. *Expressive Mirroring Overreach: Utilization of personalized facial and affective markers exceeding objective lexical equivalence.*
> 3. *Informal Boundary Blurring: Video documentation from the Cleveland Agora loading dock depicting physical contact and unmediated personal guidance.*
> 
> *Under Tenet 3.0 and Tenet 4.0 of the Code of Professional Conduct, an interpreter is strictly mandated to preserve objective detachment and avoid any conduct that creates the appearance of interpersonal favoritism, emotional dependence, or operational enmeshment.*
> 
> *You are formally instructed to submit a detailed operational statement within seventy-two hours addressing these concerns. Furthermore, you are directed to reinstate standard six-foot downstage platform separation immediately.*
> 
> *Failure to comply with these supervisory directives will result in immediate suspension of tour credentials and referral to the National Licensure Review Board for formal sanctions.*
> 
> *Sincerely,*
> **Elena Rossi, CI/CT, NAD V**
> Regional Director & Ethical Practices Investigator

I stared at the screen until the black letters began to blur into vibrating lines.

Elena had pulled the trigger.

She wasn't guessing; she was watching. In the modern interpreting landscape, every smartphone in an audience was an unaccredited monitoring camera. A twenty-second clip of Julian laughing backstage or leaning his hand against my shoulder to navigate an unlit staircase was all an ethics committee needed to construct an indictment of professional boundary violation.

My hands began to shake.

I set the tablet face down on the vanity. I pressed the heels of my hands against my temples, breathing slowly through my nose, forcing my heart rate down.

My national certification was everything I had worked for since I was nineteen years old. It represented four years of grueling undergraduate study, two thousand hours of unpaid internship supervision, three grueling state board evaluations, and the bitter defiance of standing up to my academic parents when they told me interpreting was an intellectual dead end.

If Elena suspended my license, I couldn't interpret in a courtroom, a hospital, a university classroom, or a theater anywhere in the United States. I would be erased from the professional registry.

The door connecting our adjoining motel rooms swung open without a knock.

Julian stood in the doorway. He was wearing his gray sweatpants and a faded black t-shirt, his hair ruffled from sleep. He held an empty coffee mug in his hand.

He looked at my face, then down at my trembling fingers.

His eyes immediately locked onto the overturned tablet on the desk.

*What?* he signed, stepping into the room.

*Nothing,* I signed quickly, reaching to slide the tablet into my bag. *Just agency paperwork. Scheduling logistics for Buffalo.*

Julian moved with startling speed. Before my hand could close around the device, his fingers clamped around my wrist. His grip was firm, unyielding, but not painful.

He reached down with his other hand, picked up the tablet, and turned the screen toward his face.

He had to hold it four inches from his nose, squinting as his ten-degree cone of vision tracked across the bright display. I watched his jaw tighten as he read Elena Rossi's name and the words *immediate suspension of tour credentials.*

Julian lowered the tablet slowly.

His face had gone completely white, his amber eyes burning with a dark, terrifying fury.

He set the tablet on the vanity counter, turned to me, and raised his hands.

*She's threatening you,* he signed, his fingers vibrating with suppressed rage. *Because of me.*

"It's standard protocol, Jules," I lied, my voice shaking despite myself. "Elena has to investigate any complaint filed through the agency portal. It's just administrative procedure."

Julian caught both of my hands in his. He pulled me up from the chair until I was standing inches from him.

*Don't lie to me, Maya,* he signed against my palms, his skin burning against mine. *If this tour costs you your license, we stop right now. I call Deacon, we pack the van, and I cancel the remaining nine cities.*

"No!" I said aloud, signing the word with violent emphasis. *No! You don't cancel this tour. You've worked ten years for this farewell. You're not letting Elena Rossi take your stage.*

Julian looked down at our joined hands, his chest heaving.

*Then we fight her together,* he signed."""

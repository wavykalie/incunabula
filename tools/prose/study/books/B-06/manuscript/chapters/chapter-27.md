# Chapter 27: Night in the Green Room

The club had emptied an hour ago.

Upstairs, the heavy oak doors had been locked for the night, leaving the underground cellar silent save for the occasional pop of birch logs burning down to embers in the cast-iron stove. The smell of wood ash and sweet cider hung in the still air.

Julian sat on the edge of the wooden stage platform, his boots dangling over the brick floor, a white mug of chamomile tea resting beside his hip.

The Edison bulbs had been dimmed to twenty percent, casting long, honey-colored shadows across the empty tables.

I sat down on the platform beside him, pulling my knees up against my chest inside my black wool coat.

Julian turned his head toward me. In the dim amber glow, his dark eyes were clear, serene, and completely unhurried.

He set his tea down and raised his hands.

*You voiced the pottery bit perfectly,* he signed, his fingers moving in the quiet space between our shoulders. *The comedic drop on 'wet clay disaster' hit the room right on the downbeat.*

"You wrote the drop, Jules," I answered, signing with one hand resting against my knee. "I just followed your rhythm."

Julian shook his head slowly.

*No,* he signed. *You didn't follow. You breathed with it. When I was eight years old, my mother told me that true signing isn't in the fingers. It's in the space between two people who understand each other without speaking.*

He reached out and took my left hand in his.

His thumb traced the line of my knuckles, slow and deliberate, reading the contours of my skin like braille.

*Elena Rossi called Deacon tonight,* he signed quietly.

My breath hitched in my chest. "When?"

*While you were packing the microphone cords,* Julian answered. *Deacon didn't want to tell you. She filed the formal expedited complaint with the national board in Alexandria. The emergency decertification hearing is scheduled for the morning after the Philadelphia show.*

A cold, heavy knot tightened in my stomach.

"So it's done," I whispered.

Julian moved closer until his thigh was pressed against mine. He reached up with both hands, gently cupping my cheeks, his thumbs resting beneath my cheekbones.

*Listen to me, Maya,* he signed with his eyes, his expression fiercer than any spoken word. *If you walk away now, you can still settle with them. You can tell them I coerced you. You can tell them I refused to follow standard platform protocol and forced you to stand close.*

"Stop it," I whispered, tears spilling over my lashes, hot against his thumbs. "Don't you ever say that."

*I love you,* Julian signed with his fingers against my wet cheek.

The world stopped spinning.

The cellar, the tour, the cold winter highways, the national ethics board, the looming fear of decertification—everything evaporated into the quiet warmth of the green room.

Julian leaned forward.

He didn't hesitate. He didn't ask for permission.

He pressed his lips to mine.

His mouth was warm, soft, and tasted of sweet chamomile and winter air. The kiss was gentle at first, a hesitant question asked in the dark, and then deepened as my arms went around his neck, my fingers burying themselves in the thick hair at the back of his head.

He pulled me against his chest, his arms wrapping around my waist with fierce, protective strength, lifting me slightly off the platform floor.

Under my hands, through the knit of his sweater, his heartbeat was racing, hammering against his ribs in the same wild, electric cadence as mine.

When he finally pulled back, our foreheads rested together, our breath mingling in the dim amber light.

Julian's thumbs wiped the tears from my cheeks.

*No more telephone wire,* he signed against my lips.

"No more telephone wire," I promised, and kissed him again."""

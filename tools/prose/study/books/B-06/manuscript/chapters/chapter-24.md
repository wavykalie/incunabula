# Chapter 24: The Hotel Corridor

The fifth-floor hallway of the Radisson was silent, carpeted in muted burgundy wool that absorbed the sound of our footsteps.

Julian had pulled me out of Room 512, slamming the heavy wooden door behind us, cutting Deacon off mid-sentence. He stood in the narrow corridor beneath the dim recessed ceiling fixtures, his back pressed against the wallpaper, breathing heavily.

*I am not letting you ruin your life for my farewell tour,* he signed, his fingers slashing through the air with violent velocity. *Do you understand me? You worked four years for that card. You fought your academic family for it. You don't throw your career into the garbage because Elena Rossi wants to play dictator.*

"It's not about the card, Jules!" I whispered furiously, signing with equal force right into his chest. "It's about the work! It's about you and me on that stage!"

*No!* Julian signed, his face twisting in anguish. *It's about my pride. I wanted to prove that a deaf comic could conquer twelve hearing cities before the lights went out. I wanted to walk away on my own terms. But if my final bow costs you your license, then I'm not a comic—I'm a parasite.*

The word hit me like a slap across the face.

*Parasite?* I signed, stepping into his space until my boots touched his. *Is that what you think you are? You think I'm standing on that stage because I feel sorry for you? You think I'm risking my certification out of charity?*

Julian looked away, his jaw clenched, his eyes tracking the floor seam.

*You're twenty-six,* he signed bitterly, his fingers tight. *You have forty years of interpreting ahead of you. Big conferences. Broadway shows. International summits. You go with Elena's deal. You take the train back to Chicago. Deacon and I will hire the Albany replacement.*

"The Albany replacement doesn't know your timing," I snapped, grabbing his chin with my right hand and forcing his face back to mine. "The Albany replacement won't know that your left shoulder dips before a punchline. She won't know how to navigate a dark stairwell with a palm on her spine. She'll stand six feet away like a good little telephone wire, and the hearing crowd will sit there feeling polite and sorry for the deaf guy while your set dies on the floor."

Julian stared at me, his pupils dilated in the dim hall.

"I didn't choose this tour because it was safe," I said aloud, voicing the words with fierce, unyielding passion. "I chose it because for the first time in my life, I found an artist who demands everything I have to give. When we are on that stage, we aren't client and interpreter. We are two halves of the same voice. If Elena Rossi wants to take my badge for that, let her try. I'd rather lose my license with you than spend the next forty years interpreting corporate board meetings for people who have nothing to say."

Julian's chest rose and fell in ragged rhythms.

The anger drained from his eyes, replaced by an expression of staggering, overwhelming wonder.

Slowly, carefully, he raised his hands.

*You really mean that?* he signed.

"Every single word," I answered.

Julian reached out, took my face in both of his warm palms, and pulled my forehead against his shoulder. He held me there in the silent hotel corridor, his fingers tangled in the hair at the nape of my neck, holding me like a man who had finally found dry land after swimming ten miles through the dark."""

"Look at me," I whispered aloud, signing with my right hand while keeping my left clamped to his wrist. "You have spent three years preparing this farewell tour. This is your life's work. You are not canceling Town Hall because an administrator in Chicago is terrified of change."

Julian's eyes softened, a deep, agonizing vulnerability surfacing beneath his rage.

[IF YOU LOSE YOUR LICENSE, MAYA,] he signed, his free hand moving with heavy, trembling gravity, [HOW WILL YOU WORK? HOW WILL YOU LIVE? YOU GAVE UP EVERYTHING FOR MY JOKES.]

The word *love* hung in the quiet hallway between us, spoken and signed, irrevocable and absolute.

[WE FINISH THE TOUR,] he signed against my neck. [TOGETHER. WHATEVER COMES.]

# Chapter 19: The Setlist Revision

It was 1:30 AM in Room 314 of the Buffalo Hampton Inn.

Outside, lake effect snow was falling in huge, silent flakes, piling eight inches deep on the parked cars in the motel lot. Inside, the only light came from the blue glow of Julian's laptop screen and the small amber bulb clamped to my legal binder.

Julian sat cross-legged on the carpet between the two double beds, surrounded by crumpled sheets of yellow legal paper and three different colored Sharpie pens.

He had removed the cold towel from his eyes an hour ago. The photopigments had finally regenerated, but his eyes were still sensitive, hidden behind lightly tinted indoor glasses.

He tapped the cap of a black Sharpie against his knee.

*Hand me the tour setlist binder,* he signed.

I reached onto the nightstand, picked up the three-ring black binder containing his laminated set breakdowns, and handed it to him.

Julian flipped past the opening bits—the traffic stop, the audiogram doctor, the closed captions. He turned to the second half: the thirty-minute block titled *THE DATING DISASTER REEL.*

He unclipped the plastic sheet protector, pulled out the three pages of handwritten notes detailing his old physical jokes about dating hearing women, and tossed them into the wastebasket beside the desk.

I stared at him in disbelief.

"You're cutting the dating reel?" I voiced, signing simultaneously. "Jules, that's twelve minutes of guaranteed laughs. It's the strongest commercial bit in your entire portfolio. Promoters ask for it by name."

Julian shook his head with fierce finality.

*Old material,* he signed, his fingers slashing through the air. *Written four years ago when I still thought the biggest problem in my life was whether a hearing girl would get embarrassed if I signed in an upscale Italian restaurant. It's safe. It's cute. It's Hallmark channel garbage.*

"What are you replacing it with?" I asked, sitting down on the edge of the mattress opposite him.

Julian capped the Sharpie with a sharp plastic snap. He picked up a blank pad of paper and began writing in bold, black block letters.

I leaned forward to read his handwriting upside down:

> **NEW ROUTINE — BLOCK 4: THE CONDUIT AND THE COMEDY**
> 1. *The Hearing Puppet Master:* Why hearing audiences think the interpreter is pulling the deaf guy's strings.
> 2. *The Quarter-Beat Delay:* The terror of realizing your interpreter knows your punchline before your fingers hit the air.
> 3. *The Matte Black Shield:* How a twenty-six-year-old girl with a college diploma became the only person who can see in the dark.
> 4. *The Translation of Falling:* When the rules say 'maintain emotional neutrality,' but your hands are speaking in love letters.

My heart stopped beating. The blood rushed to my ears in a hot, dizzying roar.

I looked up from the paper into his face.

Julian had taken off his tinted glasses. He was staring directly at me through the dim blue light of the laptop, his amber eyes completely stripped of irony, mockery, or fear.

*You can't perform that,* I whispered, my hands trembling as I formed the signs. *Jules, if you put that on stage, Elena Rossi will have my certification revoked before the curtain comes down in Boston. You're acknowledging personal intimacy in front of an audience. It's a direct confession of code violation.*

Julian leaned across the space between the beds. He reached out and placed his warm, bare hand over my knee.

*I don't care about Elena Rossi,* he signed, his letters deliberate, unyielding, and heavy with emotion. *This tour is called 'The Last Tour,' Maya. When it's over, I'm done. I don't get another stage. I don't get another twelve cities to tell the world who I am.*

He leaned closer, his fingers tightening against my denim.

*I am not going to spend my last three weeks on earth lying to my audience,* Julian signed. *I am not going to stand on that platform and pretend you are an invisible telephone wire. You are the heartbeat of this show. You are the reason I can stand under the lights without falling. If I can't talk about that, then the comedy is a lie.*

I looked down at the handwritten list on the yellow pad.

The title stared back at me: *The Translation of Falling.*

I knew the danger. I knew that performing this material was professional suicide. If a recording reached the national ethics panel, there would be no defense, no appeal, and no mercy.

I looked back into Julian's eyes.

I saw the courage that had carried him through seven years of encroaching darkness. I saw the brilliant, defiant artist who refused to compromise his truth for the comfort of hearing administrators.

Slowly, deliberately, I picked up a red Sharpie from the floor.

I drew a clean red circle around *The Quarter-Beat Delay.*

"If we're doing the puppet master bit," I said, my voice steady and clear, "you need to change the transition into the third premise. The phrasing on the emotional neutrality clause needs to cite Tenet 3.4. It makes the punchline twice as devastating."

Julian's mouth curved into a slow, breathtaking smile.

He pulled the pad onto his lap and began to write."""

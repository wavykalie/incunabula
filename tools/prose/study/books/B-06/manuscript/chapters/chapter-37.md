# Chapter 37: The Town Hall Green Room

The green room of Town Hall on West 43rd Street was an opulent wood-paneled suite furnished with plush leather sofas, historic theatrical posters, and a mahogany vanity surrounded by bright theatrical dressing bulbs.

It was 6:45 PM. Show call was in forty-five minutes.

The door swung open without a knock.

Elena Rossi walked into the room.

She wore an immaculate charcoal tailored wool coat, pearl earrings, and her heavy silver signet ring on her right hand. She held a black leather folio under her arm, her expression carved from cold, unyielding marble.

Deacon stood up from the sofa, his jaw clenching, but Julian touched his arm, signing a single calm command: *Sit.*

Elena ignored the two men. She walked directly across the Persian rug until she stood three feet from where I was adjusting the cuffs of my black performance top.

"Maya," Elena said, her voice low, measured, and icy. "The national review panel met this morning. The emergency amicus brief filed by David Morales was received, but the summary suspension stands pending a full evidentiary hearing. You are legally forbidden from performing under your RID credentials tonight."

She unzipped her leather folio, pulled out a formal paper document, and laid it on the vanity table.

"Sign the voluntary withdrawal now," Elena commanded. "If you step onto that stage tonight, I will have two process servers deliver criminal misdemeanor notices for uncertified professional representation before you reach the wings."

I looked down at the document.

Then I reached into my shoulder bag.

I pulled out my black leather credential wallet. I flipped it open, unclipped the plastic-laminated National Interpreter Certification card with my photograph and Member #48921, and laid it flat on top of Elena's legal document.

Elena frowned, her eyes narrowing. "What is this?"

"That is my certification card, Elena," I said, my voice completely steady, resonant, and clear. "You can keep it."

Elena blinked in genuine astonishment. "You're surrendering your career?"

"No," I answered, looking her dead in the eye with a calm, triumphant smile. "I'm firing your agency. I am not performing as an uncertified interpreter tonight. I am performing as Julian Vance's co-creator, co-author, and artistic partner. You have no jurisdiction on this stage, Elena. You never did."

Elena's face went crimson. Her jaw worked, but no words came out.

Julian stepped to my side. He looked at Elena Rossi, raised his hands, and signed with devastating, aristocratic finality:

*The green room is for artists. Get the fuck out of our theater.*

Elena grabbed the credential card, shoved her papers into her folio, turned on her leather heels, and slammed the door behind her."""

# Chapter 33: The Dressing Room Truth

The backstage dressing room at the Wilbur Theatre smelled of cold greasepaint, damp concrete, and the bitter dregs of diner coffee.

Julian sat on the edge of the metal dressing table, his hands pressed flat against the plywood top, his boots dangling above the linoleum. He had refused to look at the legal papers since morning, but the tension in his spine was coiled like an iron spring ready to snap.

Deacon stood by the wardrobe rack, flipping through receipts with an angry, aggressive snap of his thumb.

"We have two choices," Deacon said aloud, his voice rough with strain. "We finish Philadelphia tomorrow, and then we cancel Town Hall. Jules issues a public statement saying the tour is ending early due to health complications. Maya flies to Alexandria, signs the voluntary surrender, and we hire a DC lawyer to negotiate a three-year conditional suspension instead of a permanent ban."

Julian's head whipped toward Deacon with the speed of an adder.

*No,* Julian signed, the motion sharp enough to crack wood. *Maya doesn't surrender anything.*

"Jules, look at the legal brief!" Deacon yelled, slamming his hand down on the costume trunk. "Elena Rossi isn't bluffing. She has video from Burlington. She has video of you kissing her hand on stage! Under RID regulations, that is open-and-shut malpractice! If Maya goes before that panel, they will strip her license and salt the earth behind her!"

*Then the regulations are sick!* Julian signed, jumping down from the table, his boots hitting the floor with a furious bang.

He paced three steps toward the dressing room mirror, turned on his heel, and stalked toward Deacon.

*Who built those rules?* Julian demanded, his hands cutting through the air in ferocious spatial arcs. *Hearing people built them! Hearing administrators who wanted to make sure deaf people stayed inside clean, polite, manageable little boxes! They don't want us to have art. They want us to have compliance!*

He whirled around to face me where I stood against the wardrobe rack.

*Maya,* he signed, his eyes burning with intense, unyielding fire. *What did you do on that stage in Buffalo? What did you do in Burlington? Did you exploit me? Did you manipulate me?*

"No," I signed back, my hands steady.

*Did you take away my voice?*

"No," I answered. "I set it free."

Julian stepped closer, his chest heaving, his fingers pointing directly at my heart.

*You are not my conduit, Maya,* he signed, his face filled with towering, undeniable authority. *You never were. A conduit is a pipe that carries dirty water. You are my co-author. You wrote the rhythm of this tour with me. Every punchline that landed in those twelve cities was born from our two minds working as one machine.*

He turned back to Deacon, his jaw set like granite.

*We do not cancel Town Hall,* Julian signed with absolute, unyielding finality. *We perform Philadelphia tomorrow. Then we drive to New York. We invite Elena Rossi to sit in the front row at Town Hall. And when the curtain goes up, we show the world that love and art are bigger than their bureaucratic rules.*

Deacon stared at Julian.

He looked at the fierce, unbroken pride in his best friend's face. Then he looked at me, seeing the quiet, resolute certainty in my posture.

Deacon let out a long, shuddering breath. He tossed his receipts onto the table, shook his head, and reached for the van keys.

"Philadelphia call time is 2:00 PM tomorrow," Deacon muttered, a crooked, proud smile breaking across his weary face. "Let's go burn the house down."""

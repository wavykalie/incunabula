# Chapter 38: The Final Show: Town Hall

Town Hall was packed from the front row of the orchestra to the highest tier of the upper balcony.

Fifteen hundred people. A sold-out house of Deaf and hearing patrons, cultural critics, Broadway actors, and comedians who had traveled from across the world to witness the final performance of the tour.

The energy in the historic hall was a living, breathing force.

When the house lights dropped and the two massive center downlights illuminated the stage, Julian Vance and I walked out from the wings together.

We didn't walk in separation. We walked hand in hand.

Julian stood on his center mark; I stood twelve inches to his right, wearing my black performance top, our shoulders touching in the light.

Julian looked out into the vast, dark canyon of the auditorium.

He couldn't see the balconies. He couldn't see the exit signs. But he felt the floorboards humming with fifteen hundred heartbeats.

His hands rose.

He didn't open with jokes. He delivered ninety minutes of the most transcendent, groundbreaking comedy ever performed on an American stage.

He dismantled the boundaries between sound and sight, between disability and power, between translation and truth. Every joke was a masterpiece of spatial choreography; every punchline landed in voiced English with the force of a thunderclap.

We moved as one organism. When he pivoted, my body pivoted with him; when his hands threw lightning, my voice delivered the thunder.

The audience didn't just laugh; they wept, they stamped, they screamed with joy.

At minute eighty-eight, Julian turned toward me in the center of the light.

He took both of my hands in his, raised them to his lips, and signed his final words to the fifteen hundred people in the hall:

*The lights are going out. But with her, I have never seen so clearly.*

He released my hands, threw both arms wide to the dark room, and bowed.

The entire hall rose to its feet in a volcanic roar of applause.

Julian stood before the roaring crowd, his hands pressed to his heart, taking in the thunder through the floorboards under his boots.

He reached out, caught my hand, and lifted our joined arms into the spotlight.

The standing ovation rolled like thunder across the tiers. People in the orchestra were embracing; Deaf students from Rochester were waving banners in the aisles.

Julian turned to me, his dark eyes brimming with tears of joy, and whispered without voice: *Thank you.*

We bowed together one last time, our hands locked tight, before walking slowly off the stage into the wings."""

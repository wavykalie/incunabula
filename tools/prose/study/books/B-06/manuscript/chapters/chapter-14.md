# Chapter 14: The Fire Escape at 2 AM

The rusted iron fire escape clung to the fifth floor of the historic Commodore Hotel like a skeletal ribcage overlooking Lake Erie.

It was two in the morning. The sleet had stopped, replaced by a freezing northern wind that swept across the dark, undulating surface of the water, carrying the sharp scent of freshwater ice and distant industrial refineries. Below us, the wet asphalt of Memorial Shoreway reflected the orange glow of high-pressure sodium streetlights in long, shimmering ribbons.

I sat on the cold metal grating with my back pressed against the brick wall, my knees pulled up to my chest inside my heavy down parka.

The window sash behind me creaked open.

Julian climbed through the opening, his long legs navigating the narrow sill with careful, deliberate placement. He was wearing his heavy wool overcoat, thick leather gloves, and a dark knit scarf wrapped twice around his throat.

He closed the window behind him, cutting off the faint warmth of the hotel radiator, and sat down on the iron grating beside me.

His shoulder pressed against mine. In the freezing wind, the physical contact was an anchor against the void.

*Can't sleep?* he signed, his dark leather gloves moving in the amber spill of the streetlights below.

*Too much adrenaline,* I signed back, my bare fingers already stinging in the wind. *My brain is still running setlist timings. Every time I close my eyes, I see Elena Rossi's email.*

Julian reached into his coat pocket, pulled out a second pair of thick fleece-lined gloves, and tossed them into my lap.

*Put those on,* he signed. *You freeze your fingers, tomorrow's set sounds like a lawnmower.*

I smiled, pulling the oversized fleece gloves over my numb hands. They smelled faintly of his cedar cologne and the roasted coffee beans he kept in his pockets.

Julian leaned his head back against the brickwork, looking up at the heavy, starless winter clouds.

*You know what I miss most?* he signed, his movements slow, rhythmic, almost meditative in the night air.

*What?*

*The sound of snow,* he signed.

I turned my head to study his profile. In the amber streetlamp glow, his sharp cheekbones and straight nose looked like they had been carved from weathered limestone.

*Snow doesn't make sound,* I signed gently.

*Hearing people think that,* Julian signed with a faint smirk. *When I was eight, before the Usher symptoms started, I could hear low bass tones. A semi-truck downshifting. A heavy metal door slamming. And when a heavy snowstorm hit Peoria, the air pressure dropped. The room got dense. You could feel the weight of the flakes landing on the roof shingles like tiny muffled thuds. It had a rhythm. Like a quiet drum.*

He looked down at his gloved hands resting on his knees.

*Now,* he signed, *the dark is completely silent. But it's not peaceful silence. It's empty silence. When I'm on stage under the spots, I can't hear the audience, and beyond the light beam, I can't see them. If you're not standing right there, voicing the rhythm, I'm performing in an empty universe.*

My heart tightened in my chest. The vulnerability in his hands was so raw, so completely stripped of his usual stage armor, that it took my breath away.

I reached out and laid my gloved hand over his knuckles.

*You're not in an empty universe, Jules,* I signed. *I'm right there.*

Julian turned his hand over, catching my fingers between his. He pulled off his right glove with his teeth, dropping the leather onto the iron grating. Then he reached out and gently slid the fleece glove off my right hand.

His bare skin was warm, calloused, and rough from winter air.

He took my bare hand and laid it flat against his sternum, directly over the heavy cotton of his sweater.

*Feel that?* he signed with his free left hand.

Under my palm, through the layers of wool and knit, I could feel the powerful, rhythmic thud of his heart: *lub-dub, lub-dub, lub-dub.*

*That's my timing,* Julian signed, his dark eyes locking onto mine with an intensity that made the cold wind vanish. *When the audience laughs, the floorboards vibrate at forty hertz. It hits my boots, travels up my shins, and lands right here in my ribs. But when they laugh hard—when they really lose their minds—it has a specific cadence. It's not one vibration. It's a wave.*

He took my right hand from his chest and laid it across his right forearm.

With his left hand, he began tapping rhythms against my inner wrist—delicate, fluttering pulses with his fingertips that mimicked the physical sensation of sound.

*This is a giggle,* he signed, tapping two light fingers against my pulse point. *Scattered. Hesitant. Hearing people trying to be polite.*

He shifted his fingers, rolling three fingertips in a rapid, rolling cascade across my skin.

*This is a chuckle,* he signed. *Building. The front tables catching the joke.*

Then he pressed the flat of his warm palm firmly against my forearm, his fingers squeezing my wrist in a rhythmic, pulsing squeeze that echoed the cadence of an ocean breaker hitting a seawall.

*And this,* Julian signed, his face illuminated by a fierce, tender light, *is a room breaking apart. When fifteen hundred people stomp their feet and throw their heads back. That's the wave. If the lights go out completely tomorrow, Maya—if my eyes close down to zero—you don't have to voice it. You just put your hand on my arm and give me the wave.*

I sat on the freezing fire escape five stories above the black water of Lake Erie, his bare skin warm against my wrist, tears burning behind my eyelids.

I raised my left hand and signed in the amber night air:

*I will give you the wave, Jules. Every city. Every show. I will never let you stand in the dark alone.*

Julian looked at my signing hand. He didn't speak. He didn't sign.

He slowly leaned his forehead forward until it rested against my temple, his breath warm against my cheek in the winter gale, our fingers intertwined on the cold iron grating.

We sat there as the church bells from downtown Cleveland tolled three in the morning. The wind howled through the iron slats of the fire escape, but inside our circle of shared warmth, the cold had ceased to exist.

He lifted his hand, brought my fingers to his cheek, and let me feel the quiet smile curving his lips in the dark."""

# Chapter 5: The Bishop's Oath

The hazel sticks crackled in the smoky fire, throwing dancing shadows across the unmortared flint walls. The shed was still freezing, but near the hearth a circle of warmth thawed the frozen dung on the floor.

Hannah knelt between Mary's drawn-up knees. In her mind, clear as the parish church bell tolling across the valley, ran the solemn words of the oath she had sworn in Salisbury Cathedral before the Chancellor of the Diocese:

*"I, Hannah Thorne, do swear upon the Holy Evangelists that I will faithfully and diligently exercise the office of a midwife... I will not permit nor suffer any child to be murdered, maimed, or hurt... and in the time of labor and travail of any woman, I will not urge nor counsel her to name any other for the father of the child than him that is the true father thereof, nor will I certify any false name..."*

Under the statute of the realm and the canon of the Church, a woman's declaration of paternity made in the agony of crowning was received by the courts of law as solemn truth, carrying the weight of an oath sworn before the King's Bench. In that terrible hour, when death walked beside the bed and the veil between world and eternity was thin as linen thread, no woman was presumed capable of perjury.

A fresh pain seized Mary. The girl cried out, her fingernails digging deep into Hannah's forearm. The muscles of her thighs trembled with violent strain.

"Mary," Hannah murmured, leaning close to the girl's ear so that her words did not carry beyond the straw. "Listen to me, child. The child is at the threshold. You must tell me the truth before God and this unborn soul. Who got this child upon you?"

Mary's eyes rolled upward. Her teeth chattered against her tongue.

"Edward..." she sobbed, a string of saliva trailing from the corner of her mouth. "Edward swore... he swore at midsummer by the hayrick... he said he would buy a cottage in Upton... he swore his father would give consent..."

Near the door, Edward dropped a bundle of hazel twigs. The wood clattered against the dirt floor. His face went white as chalk in the firelight.

"She is raving!" Edward gasped, taking a step backward into the rain. "The fever has turned her brain! She does not know what she speaks!"

Hannah did not look back at him. Her fingers remained steady upon the perineum, supporting the thin, stretching flesh with a warm square of folded linen to prevent a tear.

"The child has its father, Mary," Hannah said softly. "Hold your breath now. Push into my hands."

Mary buried her chin into her chest. The cords of her neck stood out like hemp ropes as she bore down with all the strength left in her fevered frame.

The crown advanced another half-inch, parting the swollen vulva. A thin trickle of dark venous blood ran down the inner thigh into the straw. Hannah pressed the folded linen cloth upward against the posterior commissure, guarding the fragile tissue against the shearing force of the occiput.

Edward stood frozen in the doorway, his chest heaving, staring at the bed of straw with horror in his eyes. He reached into his coat pocket, his fingers fumbling with a silver pocket watch, checking the hour by the firelight as if counting the minutes until an execution.

Hannah kept her hands upon the emerging head. Her Latin oath rang in her blood: *non timebo homines neque potentiam saecularem...* She would not fear the power of tenant masters. The child was at the door of life, and she was its only keeper.

The fire of hazel twigs crackled on the dirt floor, sending up a thin plume of fragrant blue smoke that drifted through the holes in the hazel thatch.

In the quiet between contractions, Hannah sat back upon her heels and looked at the girl. Mary's face was bathed in sweat, but her lips were blue, her fingers plucking nervously at the frayed edges of her frieze petticoat.

Under the statutes of the realm, this was the hour of judgment. The 1624 Act was merciless: if an unwed mother concealed the death of her issue, the law presumed murder, and the gallows was her end. But the ancient common law of England provided one great sanctuary for the innocent: the midwife's oath.

A declaration of paternity made while the child's head was crowning between the thighs had the standing of an unshakeable deposition. The law recognized that in the extremity of labor, when the fear of God was upon the soul and the pains of death compassed her about, no woman had the art or malice to invent a falsehood.

"Mary," Hannah said, leaning close to the girl's ear, her voice carrying the solemn cadence of the church altar. "Look upon me. In a few moments, your child will draw its first breath. Before God and his holy angels, who is the true father of the babe within your womb?"

Mary's dark eyes opened, clear and bright through the haze of fever. She turned her head toward the doorway where Edward stood shivering in his greatcoat.

"Edward," she whispered, her voice carrying across the small shed. "Edward Callow. He gave me his mother's silver thimble by the hayrick at Lammas. He swore before the Lord he would make me his lawful wife."

Edward shrank back against the timber post, his face gray as a tombstone. He did not speak. He buried his face in his hands, unable to meet the gaze of either the midwife or the girl he had ruined.

Hannah bowed her head. The oath was recorded in her memory, carved deeper than any parish register.

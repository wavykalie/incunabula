# Chapter 40: The Open Window

At two o'clock on Monday afternoon, the school library on the first floor was filled with autumn sunlight.

The rain of the past fortnight had finally cleared, leaving the sky over South Yorkshire a brilliant, pale blue that stretched all the way to the moors. Sunlight poured through the tall sash windows along the south wall, warming the polished oak study tables and casting long, golden rectangles across the brown cord carpet.

Sadie sat at the table beneath the center window.

She had pushed the heavy timber sash up six inches, letting in the cool, crisp breeze from the valley. The air smelled of damp earth, fallen sycamore leaves, and the coal smoke drifting from the terraced chimneys down by the railway cut.

Below the open window, the courtyard was noisy with the sounds of afternoon PE.

Two dozen Year 8 boys were playing football on the asphalt, their shouting voices echoing off the brick walls in a wild, disorganized clamor. A ball slammed against the chain-link fence with a loud metallic rattle; someone laughed, someone complained about a foul, and Mr. Abbott's silver whistle blew twice.

Sadie looked at her open exercise book.

On the lined page, she had written the title of her science project in neat blue ink: *The Geological Strata of the South Yorkshire Coalfield*.

Beside the book lay a new pack of twelve colored pencils her mother had bought her from the chemist on Saturday morning.

The door of the library opened with a soft click of its brass latch.

Chloe Rimmer walked into the room, carrying a ring binder and a pencil case. She was wearing her standard school uniform, her blonde hair tied back in a neat, simple ponytail held by a plain black band. She had no makeup on, and the small scab on her cheekbone was fading to a pale pink mark.

Chloe stopped by the book return desk, her eyes scanning the study tables.

She saw Sadie sitting by the window.

Chloe hesitated for three seconds, shifting her binder from one arm to the other.

Then she walked across the brown carpet, pulled out the wooden chair opposite Sadie, and sat down.

"Is this seat taken?" Chloe asked.

"No," Sadie said.

Chloe opened her ring binder, laid out an exercise book, and took a blue biro from her pencil case. She glanced down at Sadie's title page, her eyes resting on the neat handwriting.

"Mr. Finch says we have to draw a cross-section of the sandstone layers," Chloe said.

Sadie reached across the oak table, took the box of colored pencils, and slid them into the space between their books.

"You can use these if you want," Sadie said. "The brown one's good for the ironstone."

Chloe looked at the pencils, then up at Sadie.

A faint, quiet smile touched the corners of Chloe's mouth—not a smirk, not a laugh, just a simple acknowledgment of the sunlight on the table between them.

"Thanks," Chloe said.

She took the brown pencil from the box and began to draw.

Outside, the second afternoon bell clanged across the school roofs, ringing clear and loud into the open air.

In the school library, the autumn breeze coming through the open sash window ruffled the corner of Sadie's exercise book.

Outside, the shouts of the Year 8 boys playing football on the asphalt courtyard echoed off the brick walls in a wild, disorganized clamor. The ball struck the chain-link fence with a loud metallic rattle, followed by laughter and the sharp trill of Mr. Abbott's silver whistle.

Chloe dipped the brown colored pencil onto the lined page of her ring binder, carefully shading the lower sandstone layer of the geological diagram.

"Is that dark enough for the ironstone?" Chloe asked, tilting the book toward Sadie.

Sadie looked at the drawing. "Add a bit of yellow to the edges. That's how it looks down the railway cutting."

Chloe dipped the yellow colored pencil onto her drawing, blending the edges of the sandstone stratum into the dark band of the coal seam.

"My cousin says they're going to demolish the old cooling towers at Thorpe Marsh next spring," Chloe said, not looking up from her book. "He says they're putting explosive charges in the legs and bringing the whole lot down on a Sunday morning."

"We can watch from the railway footbridge," Sadie said, shading the upper drift layer with a slate-gray pencil. "You get a proper view of the valley from up there."

Chloe paused, her pencil hovering half an inch above the lined paper. She looked across the oak table at Sadie, a small, genuine smile touching her lips.

"Yeah," Chloe said quietly. "We could do that."

In the school library, the autumn sunlight was warm against the polished oak study table.

Sadie and Chloe worked side by side in the quiet room, the only sound the soft scratching of their colored pencils against the lined paper of their exercise books. Outside the open sash window, the shouts of the Year 8 boys playing football on the asphalt courtyard rose in a cheerful, disorderly clamor, their voices carried away by the crisp breeze from the moors.

Chloe finished shading the lower coal seam on her diagram, capped her yellow pencil, and slid the plastic box back across the table toward Sadie.

"My mum said she'd buy me a proper drawing set for my birthday," Chloe said, looking at her completed cross-section. "With the metal compass and the technical ruler. We can use it for geography."

Sadie looked up from her book, meeting Chloe's clear blue eyes.

"That'd be good," Sadie said. "The compasses in the classroom are all broken anyway."

Sadie picked up her blue ballpoint pen and wrote the final label on the geological diagram: *Lower Coal Measures — Sandstone Aquifer*.

Opposite her, Chloe finished coloring the upper drift layer, capped her green pencil, and laid it neatly inside the plastic box beside Sadie's ruler. Outside the library window, the autumn sun was sinking toward the moors, casting long golden rectangles across the oak study tables and the dusty book spines.

The afternoon bell rang across the school, clear, loud, and final.

Sadie closed her exercise book, slipped the box of colored pencils into her canvas rucksack, and stood up from the wooden chair.

Sadie picked up her pen and turned to the next page.

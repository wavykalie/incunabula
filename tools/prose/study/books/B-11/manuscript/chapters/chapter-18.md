# Chapter 18: The Changing Rumor

By lunchtime on Friday, the lower dining hall was humming like a disturbed hornets' nest.

The rumors about Year 8 had ceased to be jokes about bad luck. Over chips and baked beans, four hundred pupils dissected the week's events with an eager, morbid fascination that drowned out the rattle of the tray conveyor.

"It's a curse," a Year 7 boy with greasy spectacles announced, leaning across the bench to whisper to his friends. "My sister says someone found a dead crow under the stage on Monday morning."

"It's not a crow," a girl with braces countered. "Chloe Rimmer's brother owes five hundred quid to a lad from Thorne. The lad's mates are breaking into the school at night. They've got a master key from the council."

"How'd they make the acid fall then?"

"They loosened the shelf on purpose, innit? Put grease on the wood so it'd slide when the heating came on."

Sadie sat at the end of the far bench by the fire exit, eating an apple from her packed lunch.

She kept her head down, her dark hair falling across her cheeks like a curtain. Nobody sat within six feet of her. To the rest of the school, she was simply part of the background furniture, as invisible as the emergency lighting boxes or the fire extinguishers mounted on the pillars.

At the central table, Chloe Rimmer sat alone.

Her court had evaporated. Jessica Mills was sitting three tables away with the girls from the netball squad, laughing loudly and pretending not to see Chloe. Courtney Vance was in the dinner queue, staring fixedly at the hot custard containers.

Chloe was wearing a faded, oversized bottle-green school jumper that had clearly been borrowed from the lost property box in the PE office. The cuffs were frayed, and the neckline hung loose, revealing the collar of her blouse. She sat with her elbows tucked against her ribs, her hands clasped around a carton of orange juice that she hadn't opened.

Whenever someone walked past her table, Chloe flinched.

Her blue eyes darted constantly from left to right, scanning the aisles with a sharp, twitching vigilance. When a boy dropped a metal fork onto a plastic tray ten feet away, Chloe jumped in her seat, spilling juice down her thumb.

Sadie watched her over the top of the apple.

Chloe looked smaller. The haughty, effortless superiority that had protected her like armor was gone, stripped away by three days of inexplicable malice. In its place was a raw, animal paranoia.

Mr. Henderson walked through the dining hall doors, holding a clipboard and scanning the tables.

His tie was crooked; two red ink pens leaked from his shirt pocket. He walked past Chloe's table without stopping, merely dropping a hand on her shoulder for half a second in a clumsy gesture of pastoral reassurance before continuing toward the staff table.

Chloe didn't acknowledge the touch. She remained frozen, staring at the scarred Formica table.

At the far end of the dining room, near the tray collection point, Mrs. Briggs was mopping up a spilled cup of tea with a string mop. The wet cotton strands dragged across the floor with a rhythmic, sloshing sound that echoed against the corrugated ceiling.

Two Year 8 boys from Form 8C passed Sadie's table, their voices low and conspiratorial.

"Did you hear what happened to the caretakers?" one asked, glancing over his shoulder. "Old Garside was down the boiler pit at six this morning with a sledgehammer. My uncle seen him through the grating. Said he was boarding up the coal chute."

"Why'd he do that?"

"Said he heard rats in the foundations. Big ones. Scratching right through the brickwork."

Sadie lowered her apple.

In the corridor outside the dining hall, two Year 8 boys were huddled by the water fountain, arguing over who was responsible for the curse.

"It's the old caretaker," one boy whispered, wiping his nose on his jumper sleeve. "My brother says he used to work down the pit before they shut it. He's got a tunnel that runs right under the gym. He comes up through the floorboards at night."

"You're talking rubbish," the other boy replied. "It's someone in Year 8. Someone who's got it in for Chloe Rimmer."

Sadie walked past them without turning her head, her canvas rucksack held firmly against her ribs.

Outside the dining hall double doors, Mr. Henderson was standing on a plastic milk crate, attempting to direct the flow of pupils returning from the lower fields.

"Keep to the left, Year Eight!" Henderson shouted, his voice cracking with strain. "No running in the foyer! Anyone found loitering near the west wing barricades will be placed on immediate after-school detention!"

Two Year 9 girls walked past Sadie's bench, whispering behind their hands.

"My mum says they're going to shut the school on Friday," the taller girl said, her silver earrings catching the fluorescent light. "She says the council found dry rot in the roof timbers."

"It's not dry rot," the other girl replied. "My brother says the foundations have cracked right through to the coal mine."

Sadie picked up her canvas rucksack, pulled the strap over her shoulder, and walked toward the library doors.

Sadie stood in the alcove beside the staff room doors, listening through the reeded glass.

Inside, Mr. Henderson was speaking with Mr. Finch over the rattle of ceramic mugs. "The Governors are asking questions about building maintenance," Henderson said, his voice weary and strained. "We've had three fixture failures in eight days. The locker shackle, the acid winchester, and now Garside is reporting settlement in the boiler flue."

"It's not settlement," Finch's voice replied, accompanied by the clink of a metal teaspoon. "I checked the shelf brackets in Lab Three myself. The wood didn't split. The screws were backed out three turns as if someone used a hand driver."

Sadie stepped away from the door, turning down the library corridor before anyone could look through the glass.

In the lower dining hall, the dinner ladies were clearing the long folding tables with metal scraper blades, dumping cold chips and baked bean sauce into galvanized buckets.

Sadie sat by the fire exit, watching the rain beat against the high wire-glass windows.

Across the room, Chloe Rimmer stood by the tray conveyor, arguing with Courtney Vance in low, furious whispers. Chloe's face was flushed, her arms crossed tight over her borrowed school jumper, while Courtney shook her head with an irritated, dismissive sneer before turning away to join Jessica Mills by the water fountain.

The court had fractured. Chloe stood alone on the wet linoleum, her shoulders hunched beneath the oversized green wool.

The sour taste in her mouth had deepened, turning into a dull ache that pulsed behind her molars. She picked up her canvas bag, threw the apple core into the green plastic bin by the doors, and walked out into the cold drizzle of the playground.

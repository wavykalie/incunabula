# Chapter 8: The Broken Lead

On Tuesday morning at ten o'clock, the rain was beating against the high windows of Room 14 in the real school with the sound of thrown rice.

Sadie sat at her desk in the third row, the wooden grain under her palms cold and sticky from thirty years of spilled floor sealer. Her blazer smelled of damp wool and the lavender soap her mother had used to scrub the collar the night before.

She kept her head down, her chin tucked into the hollow of her collarbone, but her eyes were fixed on the back of Chloe Rimmer's neck.

Chloe wore a brand-new wool cardigan over her shirt, pale cream and soft, with pearl buttons down the front. She had her chair tipped back on two legs, rocking slightly while Miss Finch handed out the blue exercise books for the creative writing unit.

"Right, Year Eight," Miss Finch said, tapping the corner of her wooden lectern with an empty plastic ruler. "Settle down. I want five paragraphs on the arrival of the machine. Full sentences. Capital letters and full stops where they belong, please, Courtney."

"Miss, I haven't got a pen," Courtney said from the back.

"Ask your neighbor, Courtney. I am not a stationery shop."

Chloe reached into her enameled tin pencil case. She drew out the red mechanical pencil with the gold clip, twirled it twice through her fingers with practiced flair, and laid it on the center of her lined page.

Sadie’s pulse drummed in her throat. She stared at the pencil.

The barrel was whole. The red lacquer was smooth, glossy, unbroken. There was no crack across the threads; the gold clip was straight and unbent.

Sadie’s fingers gripped the edge of her desk until the nails turned white.

A cold, sick wave of disappointment dropped into her belly like a lead fishing weight. It was just a trick of the basement gloom. It was just an old empty room.

Miss Finch walked back to her desk and sat down behind her laptop. "Ten minutes of quiet writing, please. I do not want to hear a whisper."

Chloe clicked the brass button at the top of the red pencil once.

A tiny millimeter of gray lead emerged from the brass cone at the tip.

Chloe adjusted the paper with her left hand, lowered the point of the pencil to the first blue margin line, and pressed down to write the date.

A sharp, brittle sound cracked across the quiet room.

It was not the lead snapping. The entire red plastic barrel of the pencil split down its longitudinal seam from the clip to the brass ferrule, bursting apart like an over-pressurized pipe. The two halves of the casing flew outward across the desk, scattering three fragmented sticks of lead into Chloe's lap, while the coiled steel spring inside launched itself into the air, bouncing off Jessica Mills's cheek before rolling into the aisle.

Chloe shrieked, dropping her hands to her skirt.

"Miss!" she shouted, her voice jumping into a shrill, petulant whine. "Look at my pen! It just exploded!"

Miss Finch looked up from her screen, her glasses sliding down her nose. "Chloe, please. Lower your voice."

"I am serious, Miss! I did not even push hard! The whole plastic broke in half! It was a brand-new Parker, my mum got it in Meadowhall on Saturday!"

She held up the two pieces of the barrel. The plastic was jagged at the fracture line, the red lacquer flaked away to show the white nylon interior.

Jessica leaned across the aisle, staring at the debris on Chloe’s desk. "How did you do that? You had your thumb right on it."

"I did not do anything!" Chloe's face was flushed pink, her mouth twisted in furious outrage. "It just snapped! The spring went right across the floor!"

Sadie sat three feet behind her, perfectly still.

She looked at the aisle floor beside her left foot. Resting against the black rubber base of the desk leg was the tiny coiled steel spring.

It was identical to the one she had left on the linoleum in the yesterday room twenty hours before.

Sadie slowly reached down with the toe of her synthetic shoe, dragged the spring three inches closer to her heel, and stepped on it, grinding the coil flat into the rubber sole of her shoe.

She looked up at the back of Chloe's head.

Chloe was still complaining to Miss Finch, her shoulders hunched in angry frustration, her fingers black with crushed graphite dust.

Sadie did not smile. She did not flinch.

A strange, cold knot of certainty settled into the base of her throat.

The pencil had broken at ten-fifteen on Tuesday morning.

Because she had broken it at ten-fifteen on Monday afternoon.

The classroom erupted in a storm of noise.

"Quiet down, Year Eight!" Miss Finch stood up from her laptop, clapping her hands together with sharp, rhythmic cracks. "Chloe, it is only a pencil. Stop making a scene."

"It was twenty pounds, Miss!" Chloe's voice trembled with tears of fury. She had her hands pressed against her cream cardigan, staring at the debris on her desk as if it were a dead wasp. "Look at the wood! The desk is scratched right down the middle! Someone broke it before I sat down!"

Miss Finch walked down the aisle, her glasses sliding down her nose. She peered at the two jagged halves of red plastic, the scattered lead fragments, and the deep scratch in the varnish.

"That is impossible, Chloe," Miss Finch said, picking up one piece of the broken casing. "You sat at that desk yesterday afternoon for English. Nobody has used this room since four o'clock."

"Someone was in here!" Chloe insisted, turning in her seat to glare at the students behind her. Her gaze swept over Ryan Bailey, past Jessica Mills, and stopped on Sadie Bell.

Sadie was sitting upright, her blue biro resting between her fingers, her eyes fixed on the third line of her notebook. She had already written three sentences on the arrival of the machine in neat, unhurried handwriting.

Chloe stared at her for three long seconds.

Sadie did not blink. She met Chloe's gaze with a flat, calm neutrality that showed neither guilt nor fear.

In the living classroom, Mr. Henderson tapped his wooden desk with the edge of his ruler to restore order.

"Quiet down, Year Eight," Henderson said, his voice flat with routine discipline. "A broken pencil is not an excuse for thirty people to stop work. Chloe, borrow a pen from Jessica. Turn to page forty-eight in your anthologies."

Jessica reached into her duffel bag, brought out a blue plastic ballpoint, and tossed it across the aisle onto Chloe's desk. The plastic pen rolled across Chloe's exercise book, stopping against the two jagged halves of the fractured Rotring drafting pencil.

Chloe picked up the pen without looking at Jessica, her knuckles white around the cheap plastic barrel.

Chloe's mouth opened, but no words came out. She looked down at her stained fingers, her lower lip quivering, and slumped back into her seat.

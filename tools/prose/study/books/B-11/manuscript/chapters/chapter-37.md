# Chapter 37: The Field in the Rain

The bottom playing field was a bog of brown mud and flattened yellow grass.

Six hundred pupils from Year 7 to Year 11 stood huddled in their form groups, their blazers turning black with moisture as the rain swept across the valley from the Pennines. Three dozen teachers walked up and down the lines with blue plastic clipboards, shouting names above the roar of the wind.

Sadie stood at the far end of the Form 8H line, her arms crossed tight over her chest to stop her teeth from chattering.

Her wet skirt clung to her wool tights; water dripped from the ends of her dark hair onto her collar. Her right thumb throbbed with a steady, dull ache beneath Garside's red handkerchief, but the bleeding had stopped.

Ten yards away, Form 8A was assembled in two ragged rows.

Chloe Rimmer stood at the edge of the line.

Chloe had no coat. She was wearing her green school jumper, its wool drenched and sagging around her wrists. Her blonde ponytail was plastered flat against her skull by the rain, and the red scrape on her cheekbone stood out bright against her pale skin.

She was shivering violently, her arms wrapped around her ribs, staring down at her muddy loafers.

Jessica Mills and Courtney Vance stood four feet away, huddled together under Courtney's pink umbrella, whispering behind their hands and ignoring Chloe completely.

Chloe looked up.

Her gaze swept across the muddy grass, passing over thirty children before stopping on Sadie.

Chloe's eyes widened slightly.

She looked at Sadie's torn blazer hem, at the yellow dust on her skirt, at the red handkerchief wrapped around her thumb.

For ten seconds, the two girls stared at each other across the wet grass while the rain poured down between them in gray sheets.

Then Chloe stepped out of the Form 8A line.

She walked slowly through the mud, her shoes squelching with every step, her breath misting white in the cold air.

She stopped two feet from Sadie.

In her right hand, Chloe held a large, black golf umbrella with a wooden handle, which she had retrieved from the lost-property barrel by the gym exit.

Chloe pressed the spring catch on the handle.

The umbrella snapped open with a hollow *thump*, spreading a four-foot dome of black nylon above their heads.

The wind coming off the moors tore at Sadie's wet blazer lapels, driving freezing droplets through the thin wool of her skirt.

Across the muddy playing field, six hundred pupils were shivering in their form groups, their breath rising in white clouds above the huddled rows of blazers. Form tutors with wet clipboards were checking names, their voices competing with the distant hum of traffic on the Thorne bypass.

Chloe stood beside Sadie under the black golf umbrella, her hands tucked deep inside the pockets of her wet jumper.

A small flock of starlings swept low across the football pitch, their wings whistling in the gray air before settling in the hawthorn hedges along the boundary fence.

Sadie stood with her boots sinking into the wet mud of the football pitch, watching the rain beat against the boundary hedges.

Beside her, Chloe held the black golf umbrella with both hands, the wooden handle braced against her hip to keep it steady in the autumn wind. The water drummed against the black nylon with a relentless, heavy patter, cascading off the curved ribs in clear streams that soaked into the turf around their feet.

Ten yards away, Courtney Vance and Jessica Mills were still whispering beneath their shared pink umbrella, their faces pinched with cold, glancing toward Chloe with nervous, uncertain eyes.

Chloe didn't look at them. She kept her gaze fixed on the distant moors, where the low clouds were turning dark purple in the gathering afternoon dusk.

Sadie stood with her boots sinking into the wet mud of the football pitch, looking across the boundary fence at the railway cutting.

The coal trains on the elevated line had stopped running for the afternoon, their heavy diesel engines silenced by the weather. Along the embankment, the wild blackberry brambles were heavy with dark, overripe fruit, their wet thorns glistening in the gray autumn light.

Chloe stood beside her beneath the black golf umbrella, holding the wooden handle steady with both hands.

"My brother's car broke down outside Mexborough on Saturday," Chloe said, her voice competing with the rustle of the rain on the nylon fabric. "The head gasket went. He had to push it three miles to a breaker's yard. He was crying on the grass by the petrol station."

Sadie looked at her. "Did anyone laugh?"

"No," Chloe said, looking down at her shoes. "There was nobody there to see."

Chloe looked down at Sadie's school shoes, which were caked in thick, brown football mud up to the buckles.

"Your mum's going to have a fit about those shoes," Chloe said, shifting the umbrella handle to her left hand. "My gran always says you can get dried mud off leather with an old toothbrush and a dab of washing-up liquid."

Sadie looked down at her muddy toes. "I'll clean them in the sink before she gets home from the shift."

Chloe nodded once, her eyes returning to the gray mist sweeping over the coal spoil heaps on the horizon.

Chloe lifted the umbrella, holding it high enough to shelter both of them from the driving rain.

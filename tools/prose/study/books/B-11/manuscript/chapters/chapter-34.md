# Chapter 34: The Collapse of the Splay

The sound was not an ordinary electric school bell.

In the dead, compressed atmosphere of the yesterday splay, the ringing of sixty iron clappers against bronze gongs produced a shattering, high-frequency sonic wave that tore through the air like an industrial saw cutting sheet metal.

The sound vibrated in Sadie's dental fillings, blinding her with a burst of white static behind her eyes.

Above her head, the heavy wire-glass windows of the central corridor fractured simultaneously.

Hundreds of glass panes shattered, raining showers of diamond-shaped fragments down onto the vinyl tiles. The purple fluorescent tubes in the ceiling exploded in rapid succession, popping like light bulbs tossed into a bonfire: *POP. POP. POP. POP.*

Dense gray darkness plunged through the corridor, illuminated only by the frantic red strobe of the fire alarm beacons.

Sadie dropped to her hands and knees.

Along the cinderblock walls, long horizontal cracks split through the olive-green paint. Mortar dust poured down from the expansion joints in choking gray curtains. The floor beneath her palms began to pitch and roll, undulating like the deck of a trawler hitting heavy seas in the North Sea.

She looked back toward the foyer.

The fifty yesterday figures were dissolving.

The high-frequency vibration of the alarm bells was tearing through their temporal matrix. Where the sound waves struck them, their waxy skin flaked away into dry gray ash. Chloe's duplicate threw her head back, her mouth wide in a silent scream as her green blazer, her blonde hair, and her outstretched hands disintegrated into swirling clouds of plaster dust.

Mr. Henderson's figure crumbled like a dried sandcastle hit by a wave.

In ten seconds, the entire crowd had vanished, leaving only a six-inch drift of gray powder coating the corridor floor.

Sadie scrambled to her feet, choking on the sulfurous dust.

The foundation slab was groaning. Deep beneath the school, forty tons of shale in the old No. 4 return drift were giving way, the void pulling at the building's concrete roots.

"Sadie!"

A voice bellowed through the thunder of collapsing plaster.

It was not a whisper. It was a roar—a living, human roar coming from the service tunnel behind the boiler stack.

"SADIE! GET OUT OF THERE!"

Sadie turned and ran.

She vaulted over piles of shattered ceiling tiles, skidded across drifts of fallen glass, and sprinted down the west service corridor toward the rectangular opening of the hatch.

The roar of collapsing masonry filled the east wing like the boom of a quarry explosion.

Sadie sprinted through the swirling curtain of gray plaster dust, her forearm shielding her eyes from the falling debris. On her left, the plate-glass doors of the library blew outward in a shower of diamond-shaped fragments that skittered across the floor tiles like hail on frozen tarmac.

Behind her, the yesterday school was folding in on itself.

The cinderblock walls were bowing inward, their mortar joints opening like dark mouths. Through the floorboards, thirty feet of foundation concrete were settling into the flooded workings of the old colliery seam, pulling the replica classrooms down into the dark abyss.

Behind Sadie, the double swing doors of the gymnasium collapsed into the corridor in a storm of splintered timber and twisted brass hinges.

Through the opening, she caught a glimpse of the cavernous hall. The polished hardwood parquet floor was heaving like water in a lock gate, the sixty-foot steel roof trusses groaning under immense tension as the foundation footings forty feet below subsided into the flooded colliery drift.

A shower of black shale and wet sandstone erupted through the expansion joints, coating the climbing ropes and vaulting horses in thick, foul-smelling mine slurry.

She hit the fire exit crash bar with both forearms, bursting through into the lower service corridor just as the ceiling behind her gave way.

Sadie plunged down the final flight of the west stairs three steps at a time, her satchel clutched tight against her ribs.

Behind her, the concrete stair treads were fracturing like peanut brittle under a heavy boot. Hairline cracks spread through the cinderblock walls, spitting showers of white mortar and red brick dust that filled the stairwell with a blinding cloud of debris.

Through the wire-glass windows overlooking the playing fields, the frozen playground was beginning to warp.

The two Year 9 boys frozen behind the bike sheds dissolved into swirling columns of gray ash, their football bursting into a puff of black carbon that vanished into the rain-swept wind.

She reached the ground-floor fire exit, hit the crash bar with both forearms, and stumbled into the lower service corridor.

The concrete floor of the service corridor was heaving like the surface of a drum hit with a heavy mallet.

Sadie threw herself against the wall, her hands scraping along the rough cinderblock as a three-foot slab of acoustic ceiling collapsed two yards ahead of her, shattering into yellow fiberglass fibers on the linoleum. The air was thick with the smell of pulverized shale and burning copper wiring, blinding her eyes with gray dust.

She saw the rectangular opening of the boiler room hatch ten yards ahead, an orange rectangle of warm living light shining through the swirling fog of debris.

Behind Boiler Three, the iron door was bulging outward, its hinges screaming under immense pressure.

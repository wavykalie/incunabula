# Chapter 25: The Near Miss

By 08:40 AM, the west stairwell was an unbroken river of noise, wet wool, and jostling bodies.

The morning registration bell had just rung. Six hundred students were moving through the corridors, their voices bouncing off the painted cinderblock walls in a deafening, chaotic roar. Outside, the rain was coming down in torrents, streaming across the high wire-glass windows and turning the playing fields into brown lakes.

Sadie stood on the half-landing, her back pressed against the brick wall, fighting to keep her footing against the surge of descending pupils.

"Get back!" she shouted, waving her arms. "Don't go down the stairs! Stay clear of the wall!"

A Year 9 boy shoved her shoulder as he pushed past. "Shut up, Bell! Move out of the way!"

"Get back! The wall bracket is coming down!"

Nobody stopped. Two Year 7 girls laughed at her frantic expression, pointing at the mud stains on her green skirt before tumbling down the steps toward the dining hall.

Sadie looked up toward the top landing.

Chloe Rimmer was coming down the top flight, walking beside Jessica Mills and Courtney Vance. Chloe was wearing her fresh school jumper, her hair pulled back in a neat ponytail, laughing at something Courtney was saying on her phone.

Chloe reached the third step from the top.

08:44:50 AM.

On the cinderblock wall above Chloe's head, the red ten-liter fire extinguisher gave a sudden, sharp metallic groan.

The rusted rivet on the top bracket yielded.

Sadie saw the white hairline fracture split across the red paint.

"Chloe!" Sadie screamed at the top of her lungs, throwing herself up the stairs against the descending tide of pupils. "CHLOE! LOOK OUT!"

Chloe stopped on the third tread, looking down with a startled, irritated scowl. "What is your problem, Bell—"

*CRACK.*

The cast-iron bracket sheared with the explosive report of a 12-gauge shotgun.

The heavy steel cylinder tore free from the masonry, spraying a shower of red rust and white mortar dust across the landing. It tumbled forward through the air, twenty-five kilograms of dead steel falling directly toward Chloe's head.

Sadie threw herself across the concrete steps.

She caught Chloe around the waist with both arms, putting all forty kilograms of her weight into a desperate, flying tackle.

Both girls tumbled sideways onto the concrete half-landing in a tangle of limbs, skirts, and flying satchels.

A microsecond later, the steel fire extinguisher struck the exact step where Chloe had been standing.

The impact smashed a three-inch crater into the concrete nose. The cylinder bounced, caromed off the brick wall, and plunged down the remaining steps, rolling violently into the ground-floor fire doors with a deafening boom that rattled the glass in every window in the wing.

The valve head snapped off on the bottom landing.

A screaming geyser of pressurized water and foam erupted from the fractured neck, drenching thirty screaming Year 8 pupils in a blinding white fog.

Panic exploded in the stairwell.

Dozens of children shrieked, trampling over bags and dropped exercise books as they scrambled backward up the stairs.

On the cold concrete half-landing, Sadie lay on her stomach, her left elbow throbbing with white-hot pain where it had struck the iron baluster.

Beside her, Chloe sat slumped against the wall, her face as white as chalk, her eyes wide and glassy with shock. A deep scrape ran across Chloe's left cheekbone, weeping beads of bright red blood onto her white blouse collar.

Three inches from Chloe's boot lay a jagged, two-pound chunk of fractured concrete from the step above.

Chloe looked at the shattered stone. Then she turned her head slowly, looking directly into Sadie's eyes.

Chloe's mouth opened, but no words came out. Her lower lip trembled violently.

From the top of the stairs, Mr. Henderson's whistle cut through the screaming crowd with three sharp, frantic blasts.

On the half-landing of the west stairs, the air was thick with the smell of wet wool, screaming voices, and the sharp chemical tang of pressurized carbon dioxide.

Mr. Henderson forced his way down the steps, his tie flying over his shoulder, his clipboard held high like a shield. "Clear the stairs!" he bellowed, his voice hoarse with panic. "Year Eight, move back into the humanities corridor! Mr. Finch, get the fire doors open at the bottom!"

Sadie sat on the concrete tread beside Chloe, her left elbow throbbing with white-hot pain.

Chloe was staring at the shattered chunk of concrete three inches from her shoe, her face as white as milk, her fingernails digging into the green wool of her school skirt.

On the half-landing, Mrs. Briggs arrived with an emergency first-aid kit, her white apron flapping around her knees.

"Move back, everyone! Give them air!" the school nurse shouted, dropping to her knees beside Chloe. She pulled a foil shock blanket from her canvas bag and draped it around Chloe's shivering shoulders. "Chloe, look at me. Can you tell me your name?"

Chloe didn't answer. Her eyes remained locked on Sadie's face, her fingers clutching the damp sleeve of Sadie's green blazer with a desperate, white-knuckled grip that refused to let go.

Henderson knelt beside them, his forehead dripping with sweat, his fingers taking Chloe's pulse.

On the half-landing, Mr. Henderson was on his knees beside Chloe, his face dripping with sweat, his necktie flying over his shoulder.

"Mr. Finch, get these students back to the humanities block immediately!" Henderson shouted over the roar of the crowd. "Ryan, take the Year Sevens out through the fire exit! Nobody touch that cylinder!"

A Year 8 girl from Form 8C was crying hysterically against the handrail, her tights torn where someone had trampled her in the stampede. Across the concrete steps, twenty scattered exercise books lay soaked in pressurized water and white foam, their blue cardboard covers curling in the damp air.

Sadie knelt on the cold tread, her left shoulder throbbing with dull, heavy pain, watching the white foam bubble across Chloe's school shoes.

In the lower corridor, Mrs. Finch was blowing her whistle repeatedly to disperse the crowd of onlookers.

"Year Eight, return to your classrooms immediately!" Mrs. Finch shouted, her voice competing with the hiss of escaping carbon dioxide from the damaged cylinder. "There is nothing to see here! Mr. Fletcher, take those Year Nines back to the gym!"

Sadie sat on the bottom step of the landing, holding a cold paper towel against her aching elbow, while Chloe remained slumped against the wall, her hands wrapped tight around the foil shock blanket.

On the half-landing of the west stairs, the panic was deafening.

Dozens of children were screaming, scrambling over dropped bags and scattered exercise books to get away from the fallen fire extinguisher. The ten-liter steel cylinder lay on the bottom landing beside the dented fire doors, venting a screaming geyser of white carbon dioxide and pressurized water that coated the walls in thick, freezing foam.

Mr. Henderson forced his way through the surging crowd, his face white with terror, his whistles blowing in short, frantic bursts.

"Year Eight, move back into the corridor!" Henderson bellowed, waving his arms. "Do not push! Single file down the east stairs! Mr. Finch, assist with the Year Sevens!"

Sadie sat on the concrete tread, her left arm throbbing with white-hot agony where she had struck the baluster during her dive.

Beside her, Chloe was curled against the wall, her hands clutching Sadie's blazer sleeve with a grip that felt like an iron clamp. Chloe was trembling from head to foot, her breath coming in ragged, whistling sobs, her eyes wide and glassy as she stared at the shattered concrete where her head had been three seconds earlier.

"You shoved me," Chloe whispered, her voice barely audible beneath the roar of the crowd. "You knocked me right off the step."

Sadie looked at the deep crater gouged into the concrete nose. "The bracket broke," Sadie said, her breath ragged. "It was coming right down on your head."

On the half-landing of the west stairs, Mr. Henderson was trying to lift Chloe to her feet by her elbows.

"Careful, Chloe," Henderson said, his voice trembling with shock. "Can you stand on your left foot? Mrs. Briggs, bring that wheelchair from the medical room!"

Chloe clung to the sleeve of Sadie's blazer, her fingernails digging through the wool into the skin of Sadie's forearm. "Don't let them move me," Chloe whimpered, her eyes fixed on the three-inch crater in the concrete step where the extinguisher had struck. "The wall's going to fall down. The whole wall's coming down."

Sadie knelt beside her on the concrete, holding Chloe's trembling hand between her palms until the nurse arrived with the blanket.

On the half-landing of the west stairs, Mr. Finch and Mr. Henderson were kneeling beside the shattered concrete tread, examining the damage with a pocket flashlight.

"The bracket failed under dynamic shear," Finch said, shining the yellow beam into the fractured collar. "Look at the fracture face. There's no oxidation. It sheared cleanly across the grain as if struck by a sledgehammer."

Henderson shook his head, his face pale with sweat. "If those girls had been two inches to the left, we would have had two fatalities on this landing, Arthur. The Governors will have to close the entire wing."

On the half-landing, Mr. Henderson helped Chloe onto the wooden stretcher brought up by the caretakers.

"Easy does it, Chloe," Henderson said soothingly, adjusting the gray wool blanket around her shoulders. "You're going to the medical room. Your mother has been called and she's on her way from Thorne."

Chloe looked over Henderson's shoulder at Sadie, who stood by the handrail with her torn blazer and bruised elbow.

Neither girl spoke, but Chloe's eyes remained fixed on Sadie until the stretcher turned the corner of the stairwell.

"STAY WHERE YOU ARE!" Henderson bellowed, his face red with terror as he forced his way through the children. "EVERYBODY STAY BACK! NOBODY MOVE!"

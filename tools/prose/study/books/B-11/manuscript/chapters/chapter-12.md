# Chapter 12: The Jammed Shackle

On Tuesday morning at 11:20 AM, the lower gym corridor was a tunnel of noise and steam.

The second-period PE bell had just sounded. Sixty girls from Year 8 poured down the west stairwell in a noisy tangle of sports bags, damp towels, and squeaking rubber trainers. The radiator beneath the trophy cabinet clattered violently as a slug of boiling water rushed through its cast-iron sections, spitting a fine spray of rusty steam from the air bleed valve.

Sadie lingered by the fire exit doors, her canvas rucksack tucked between her ankles.

The air in the changing corridor smelled of teenage sweat, cheap deodorant sprays, and damp wool socks drying over heating pipes. Every girl was shouting over her shoulder, comparing answers to the French vocabulary test or complaining about Mrs. Finch's cross-country route around the muddy perimeter fence.

Chloe Rimmer led the pack down the hall, flanked by Courtney Vance and Jessica Mills. Chloe was wearing her new black Adidas track pants with three white stripes down the outer seam, her blonde hair tied up in a loose bun secured with two pink plastic clips. She held an unopened bottle of Lucozade in one hand, gesturing with it as she recounted a story about her cousin's moped.

"So he told the police he bought it off a bloke in Mexborough," Chloe said, laughing. "And the copper actually believed him!"

Jessica giggled, adjusting the strap of her duffel bag. "He didn't!"

"Swear down," Chloe said, stopping in front of Locker 142.

She set the Lucozade bottle on the narrow shelf of the fire-hose reel, took the heavy brass padlock in her right hand, and reached for the thumb dials.

"Hurry up, Chloe," Courtney called, leaning against the neighboring locker. "Mrs. Finch says anyone not changed in five minutes gets an equipment detention."

"Keep your hair on," Chloe muttered.

She pinched the first dial between her thumb and index finger, attempting to spin the brass ring from zero to four.

The wheel did not move.

Chloe frowned. She shifted her grip, pressing the hard edge of her fingernail into the grooved knurling of the number ring. She grunted, putting the weight of her shoulder into the turn. The dial remained locked in place, frozen as solid as an iron bolt driven into a granite quarry face.

"What's wrong with it?" Jessica asked, looking over.

"It's stuck," Chloe said. Her voice rose half an octave, losing its smooth, casual cadence. "The stupid wheel won't turn."

"You probably set the wrong code yesterday."

"I didn't set the wrong code! It's four zeros! It's always four zeros!"

Chloe grabbed the heavy brass body with both fists and jerked downward with all her strength. The mild steel door of Locker 142 rattled against its frame, booming like a muffled snare drum, but the hardened steel shackle held fast inside the staple.

"Let me try," Courtney said, stepping forward.

"Get off!" Chloe snapped, shoving Courtney's shoulder away. "I can open my own locker!"

She brought her right foot back and kicked the locker door directly below the name card. The thin steel skin buckled with a hollow thud, leaving a scuff mark from the rubber sole of her trainer across the olive paint. The padlock remained unmoved, the four brass dials showing their row of zeros beneath a faint, glassy sheen of cured epoxy.

A small circle of girls gathered in the corridor to watch.

Two Year 9 girls stopped by the water fountain, smirking behind their hands as Chloe kicked the locker a second time.

"Look at her," one of them whispered. "Rimmer's having a proper fit."

"Shut up!" Chloe shouted, spinning round with her face flushed crimson. "Shut up, you ugly cow!"

"Chloe!" Mrs. Finch's voice cut through the corridor like a whip crack. The PE teacher strode down the hall in her navy tracksuit, a stopwatch bouncing against her chest. "That is quite enough of that language. What on earth are you doing to school property?"

"My locker won't open," Chloe said, her breath ragged. She pointed a trembling finger at the brass padlock. "Someone's done something to it. It's glued shut!"

Mrs. Finch stepped up to the locker, adjusted her wire-rimmed glasses, and took the padlock between her fingers. She tried to turn the dials. Her brow furrowed as she peered into the tumbler crevices.

"There's some sort of plastic substance inside the wheels," Mrs. Finch said. "Did someone put chewing gum in here?"

"No! It was fine yesterday!" Chloe's voice cracked into a desperate, furious whine. "My kit's in there! My trainers! My phone!"

"Go and fetch Mr. Garside from the workshop, Jessica," Mrs. Finch ordered calmly. "Tell him we need the bolt cutters."

Sadie stood twenty feet away, her spine resting against the cold cinderblock wall.

She did not smile. She did not blink. She watched Chloe pacing in circles on the linoleum, chewing her thumbnail until the skin bled, her chest heaving with impotent rage. For eighteen months, Chloe had dispensed humiliation like a ration coupon: sharp, precise, and completely consequence-free. Now, for the first time, Chloe was the spectacle, small and helpless in the middle of a laughing hallway.

Five minutes later, Mr. Garside's heavy boots sounded on the stairs.

The caretaker walked with a rolling, deliberate gait, carrying a three-foot pair of drop-forged steel bolt cutters with bright red tubular handles. He smelled of machine oil, swarfega, and shag tobacco. He didn't look at Chloe. He stepped up to Locker 142, fitted the hardened jaws of the cutter around the steel shackle, and planted his work boots wide on the tiles.

"Stand back, girls," Garside muttered.

He leaned his full fourteen-stone weight onto the long red handles.

The steel shackle groaned. For a fraction of a second, the metal resisted; then, with a deafening *CRACK* that echoed down the length of the gym corridor like a gunshot, the shackle severed in two. The heavy brass lock body dropped to the floor tiles, rolling into the gutter by Sadie's shoes.

Garside pulled the locker door open with a screech of bent sheet metal.

"There you are," Garside said, wiping his nose with the back of his greasy hand. He bent down, picked up the broken lock, and squinted at the resin plug in the casing. "Industrial resin. Two-part epoxy. Somebody took this from my lower bench."

His gray eyes swept slowly along the crowd of watching girls, lingering for two beats on Sadie's face.

Sadie met his gaze without flinching.

Behind Garside, Chloe fell upon the open locker like a starving animal, clawing through her folded kit bag to ensure her phone was undamaged. Her hands were shaking so violently she dropped her silver pencil case, scattering highlighters across the floor tiles.

"Who did this?" Chloe demanded, her voice shrill with tears. "Mrs. Finch, you have to find out who did it! It was probably someone from Form 8H!"

"That will do, Chloe," Mrs. Finch said, checking her stopwatch. "You have lost ten minutes of instruction time. Get into your kit immediately. Mr. Garside, thank you."

Garside dropped the severed lock into his boiler suit pocket, hoisted the heavy cutters onto his shoulder, and turned back toward the basement stairs.

In the girls' changing room, before the bell, the air had been thick with aerosol deodorant and shouting voices. Courtney Vance had been sitting on the radiator shelf, swinging her legs and laughing about a boy who had dropped his tray of curry sauce in the dining hall.

"He went down on both knees," Courtney had laughed, spraying a cloud of vanilla body spray into the air. "Covered his white shirt right across the pocket. Henderson made him sit in the pastoral office in a bib!"

Chloe had joined in the laughter, leaning against the wooden locker frame while applying clear gloss to her lower lip with a tiny pink wand. "Served him right. Clumsy Year 7 idiot. He was probably looking at Jessica's trainers."

The memory of their laughter still rang in Sadie's ears as she watched Chloe struggle with the jammed brass shackle.

Across the hallway from Locker 142, three Year 7 boys stood frozen in mid-transit between French and physical education. The smallest boy was clutching a plastic pencil case decorated with Batman logos, his mouth open as if about to sneeze. His black school plimsolls were tied with frayed string where the original white laces had snapped three weeks ago.

Sadie looked at the boy's trainers. In the living school, children were constantly fidgeting, scuffing their toes against the skirting boards, scratching initials into the green gloss paint. Here, the boy was as motionless as a plaster cast in a municipal museum.

When Garside severed the padlock shackle with his red bolt-cutters, the sharp metallic report rattled the glass panes of the trophy cabinet twenty feet away, sending a flock of roosting pigeons scattering from the high roof gutter outside.

In the crowded gym corridor, Mrs. Finch was blowing her whistle repeatedly to restore order among the watching students.

"Courtney, move back from that locker!" Mrs. Finch commanded, her voice cutting through the laughter. "Jessica, stop taking photographs on that phone or I'll confiscate it until Friday! Chloe, stand still and take a deep breath."

Chloe was pacing across the wet linoleum, her fists clenched, her knuckles white.

"It's not fair!" Chloe screamed, her voice cracking into shrill tears. "My kit's in there! My trainers! My mum paid sixty quid for those Pumas in Meadowhall! If they're ruined, she's going to go to the Governors!"

Two Year 9 boys carrying basketballs stopped by the water fountain, laughing loudly at Chloe's distress.

"Look at Rimmer," one boy shouted, bouncing the leather ball against the floor tiles with a loud *thump*. "Someone's glued her locker shut! She's having a proper meltdown!"

Chloe spun round, her face crimson with fury. "Shut your mouth, Ryan! Shut your stupid mouth!"

Sadie stood twenty feet away by the fire doors, watching the spectacle with quiet, unblinking eyes. For eighteen months, Chloe had orchestrated the public humiliation of half the girls in Year 8. Now, the entire wing was laughing at her, and Chloe had no weapon left to stop them.

In the crowded hallway outside the gymnasium, the noise was deafening as sixty girls argued over the jammed locker.

"Chloe, stop kicking the steel!" Mrs. Finch commanded, pushing through the circle of students with her stopwatch swinging. "You are going to damage the door frame, and your mother will be billed for the repair. Ryan, take those Year Nines back to the court!"

Chloe was weeping with furious frustration, her fists pounding against the olive-painted sheet metal.

"My trainers are in there!" Chloe sobbed, her mascara running in dark streaks down her cheeks. "My white Pumas! Someone put glue in the dials! I can see the gray stuff oozing out of the number wheels!"

Two Year 7 girls by the water fountain were smirking behind their gym bags, nudging each other as Chloe kicked the locker a third time, leaving another black scuff mark on the paint.

In the crowded corridor outside Locker 142, Mr. Garside stood over the severed brass padlock, his heavy boots planted wide on the linoleum.

He wiped his nose with the back of his greasy hand, picked up the two pieces of the shackle from the floor, and weighed them in his palm. "Hardened molybdenum steel," Garside muttered, loud enough for Mrs. Finch and the surrounding girls to hear. "Takes four tons of pressure to shear a bolt like that. Whoever did this wasn't playing with school compasses."

Chloe stood three feet back, her face white, her fingernails digging into the sleeves of her track top as she stared at the severed lock in Garside's fist.

Mr. Garside stood on the linoleum in his heavy work boots, looking down at the crowd of Year 8 girls.

"Locker vandalism is a serious matter under Section Fourteen of the Education Act," Garside announced in his gruff, gravelly voice, wiping his nose with his sleeve. "Whoever's mixing two-part resin on school property had better realize that industrial adhesive leaves chemical traces on your cuffs. I'll be checking the bins in the technical drawing rooms before four o'clock."

In the girls' changing corridor, Mrs. Finch was checking the locker numbers with a blue plastic clipboard.

"Bay Four is completely out of commission until Mr. Garside returns," Mrs. Finch announced to the remaining students. "Anyone with kit in lockers one-forty to one-fifty will have to use the spare PE baskets from the store room. Move along, girls."

Sadie adjusted her bag on her shoulder and slipped quietly through the fire exit into the west stairs.

Sadie picked up her canvas rucksack and walked quietly up the west flight toward double history.

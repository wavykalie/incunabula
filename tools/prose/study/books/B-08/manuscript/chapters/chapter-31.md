# Chapter 31 — The Stand at Hive One

The wind on the high rampart had shifted to the northwest, driving the gray sulfur smoke back over the precipice into the churning foam of the White Torrent. Marta stood directly before the stone bench of Hive One, her hands resting upon the cedar nucleus travel box. Beside her stood Bram, his broad shoulders braced against the cold, his iron stonecutter's mallet held loose and ready in his right hand.

Ten paces away, Elder Osric Vance struggled to his feet.

He leaned heavily upon his oak staff, his knuckles white with pain where Bram's blow had jarred his fingers. His gray beard was smeared with soot and sleet, his bloodshot eyes darting frantically between the two siblings and the circle of forty village craftsmen who had crowded onto the walkway.

"You have assaulted the Chief Elder of St. Jude's Cleft," Osric hissed, his voice trembling with venomous rage.

"Bailiffs, draw your steel! Strike them down! They have brought northern witchcraft onto our wall!"

The eight council bailiffs did not move.

Their captain, an experienced veteran named Torren who had served thirty seasons on the border parapet, looked from Osric's wild, sweating face to Bram's calm, formidable gaze. Torren slowly lowered his crossbow, clicking the iron catch into safety.

"The girl carries no witchcraft, Elder," Torren said in a level, deliberate voice.

"She carries the queen box. And Bram Varek is standing on his own two feet. A man does not return from the dead with witchcraft; he returns with boots and an iron mallet."

"He is an exile!" Osric shouted, spitting flecks of white foam from his lips.

"He forfeited his blood when he crossed the postern gate! The law of the covenant demands that any man who breaks the boundary be put to the sword!"

"The covenant was written to protect this valley from slaughter, Osric," Bram answered, his deep voice carrying effortlessly across the silent battlements.

"It was not written to give you a prison where you could starve your neighbors while hiding the truth of your own failure."

A collective murmur broke from the villagers.

Master Arn the blacksmith stepped forward, his massive leather apron flapping in the gale.

"What failure, Bram? Speak plainly. The whole village is here to listen."

Bram pointed his mallet toward the northern notch of the Screaming Gorge.

"Forty years ago, when the raiders forced the gate, Osric told the council that wild tribes had fallen upon the cleft without warning. He told our fathers that the north held only plague, monsters, and killers. He made you build this wall fifteen feet high; he made you bar the postern with three oak balks; he made you butcher your cattle and graze your clover until the valley soil turned to sour ash."

Bram stepped closer to Osric, his eyes locking with the old man's terrified stare.

"The north valley holds no raiders, Arn. It holds twenty families of peaceful craftsmen and weavers who live around warm geothermal springs. They have wheat that grows four feet high, sheep with wool four inches thick, and wild mountain bees that have nested in limestone caves for two centuries. They have sent trade caravans to our gate three times in twenty years, and every time, Osric ordered the sentries to shoot them with crossbows."

"Lies!" Osric screamed, raising his oak staff like an executioner's axe.

"He speaks the words of the devil to seduce your children! Sarah! They murdered my Sarah in the loom house!"

"They killed Sarah because you cheated them, Osric!" Bram shouted, his words striking the granite wall like a thunderbolt.

"My father found your trading tokens in the ashes! You traded thirty bushels of village grain for private northern furs, and when you refused to pay the agreed copper pans, the raiders followed your secret path to take what you owed! You brought the raiders to our gate, Osric! And you turned this entire valley into a tomb to keep your neighbors from discovering your guilt!"

A dead, breathless silence descended upon the rampart walk.

The villagers stared at Osric.

The old elder seemed to shrink within his charcoal mantle. His jaw worked silently, his staff slipping three inches across the damp slate as his strength drained from his limbs. The terrible, forty-year-old secret had been dragged into the daylight, and the burden of it crushed him where he stood.

Marta did not wait for Osric to speak. She stepped to Hive One and lifted the iron hive tool from her belt.

Beside the granite bench, Captain Torren stood with his hand resting on the hilt of his broadsword. His weathered face was lined with deep crevices from twenty winters of standing sentry in subzero gales. He looked at Bram's hands—hands scarred with stonecutter's calluses and stained with pine resin—and then at the trembling fingers of the Chief Elder. 'I stood on this wall when your father was carried down from Hive Twenty-three, Bram,' Torren said softly. 'His last words were to keep the bees flying. If the girl has the queen, I say let the smoke clear.'

A murmur of agreement rose from the bailiffs behind him.

These were men of the cleft, sons of farmers and weavers who had watched their own families grow pale on thin winter gruel. They knew the smell of sulfur; they knew the terror of the burning skeps. But looking into the eyes of the young beekeeper, they saw something that had been absent from St. Jude's Cleft for forty seasons: the quiet, steady courage of a craftsman who trusted the earth. Master Arn the blacksmith stepped forward, his heavy iron hammer resting on his shoulder. 'The council has five elders, Osric, but the village has two hundred living souls. If you strike flint to that straw, you do it alone.'

The following day brought a visitor to the lower perimeter. Kesta whistled three sharp marmot calls from the ridge, warning that a stranger was ascending the talus slope. Marta and Bram took up positions behind the dry-stone parapet, iron bars and chisels in hand, while Kesta remained concealed among the junipers with an arrow nocked on her cord.

The figure struggling up through the scree was solitary, wrapped in a threadbare sheepskin coat and leaning heavily upon an ash wood herdsman's crook. As he emerged from the shadow of the lower buttress, Marta recognized the stooped shoulders and limping gait of old Master Corin, the elder wheelwright who had fled Oakhaven two days after their own departure.

"Hold!" Bram called out, his deep voice bouncing off the granite walls.

"Corin! Lower your stave."

The old man collapsed onto a snowbank, his breath coming in wheezing, ragged gasps. Bram rushed forward, hauling the wheelwright by his collar into the shelter of the overhang.

"They took the church," Corin wheezed, his lips blue and his beard matted with frozen snot.

"Vance brought in thirty mercenaries from the southern garrison. Men with steel breastplates and crossbows. They searched every barn, overturned every honey barrel, and arrested the miller's sons for giving us dried peas."

Marta pressed a cup of warm elderberry tea into the old man's trembling hands.

"Did they find the hidden stores beneath the mill wheel?"

Corin took three desperate swallows, coughing as the hot liquid hit his throat.

"No. The water was frozen solid; they couldn't lift the sluice. But Vance has declared the entire northern ridge under ban of heresy. He posted an edict on the chapel door claiming that anyone caught carrying bees, honey, or comb across the border will be hanged as a blasphemer against the parish."

Bram's mallet hit the flat stone beside him with an impact that made the chips fly.

"Let him hang his own shadow," he growled.

"He has an empty valley and thirty mercenaries who will demand their pay in silver he doesn't have."

Osric looked from face to face.

He saw no fear. He saw no deference. In the cold mountain dusk, standing before the ancient stone bench of Hive One, the quarantine had broken from within. The walls remained standing, but the minds of the people had already crossed the pass.

"The time for arguing is over," Marta said clearly, looking at Master Arn and Captain Torren.

"Hive One is dying of inbreeding and cold. If we do not introduce the mountain queen before the sun drops behind the ridge, forty border colonies will perish in the winter freeze. Clear the tripod. Bring me the cedar frames."

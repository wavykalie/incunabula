# Chapter 18 — The Council Muster

The great bronze muster bell of St. Jude's Cleft tolled from the roof of the Council Hall. Its heavy, clanging notes rolled across the valley floor, striking the granite cliffs and echoing through the narrow streets like thunder before a summer storm. Villagers poured from their cottages into the square: weavers with shuttles still in their hands, women clutching infants wrapped in wool shawls, blacksmith apprentices with soot-stained aprons. At the center of the square, standing on the stone mounting block beside the communal press, stood Elder Osric Vance.

Beside Osric stood four senior council bailiffs in iron morions and boiled-leather hauberks, their crossbows held across their chests. In front of them, bound at the wrists with heavy hemp rope, knelt Kesta Holm.

"People of St. Jude's Cleft!" Osric's voice rang out, harsh and unyielding as a winter gale.

"The boundary has been broken. Treason has entered the garrison."

A shocked murmur swept through the assembled villagers. Master Corin the baker pushed his way to the front rank, his face pale with alarm.

"Elder! What treason? Where is Kesta's father?"

"Kesta's father sits in his cottage under guard," Osric answered coldly.

"His son has betrayed the trust of three generations. Last night, while holding the keys to the North Postern Gate, Kesta unbarred the oak timbers and permitted Marta Varek to flee into the forbidden gorge with a stolen swarm from Hive One."

The crowd erupted in confused shouts.

"Marta?" shouted Goodwife Elspeth Vance.

"The girl went into the gorge? She is dead! The mountain raiders have taken her!"

"There are no raiders yet, Goodwife," Osric said, his oak staff thumping the stone block.

"But there will be! For forty years, the boundary wall has kept our people safe behind a shield of fire and venom. Now the gate stands open, the mother hive is breached, and the girl carries our bees into the northern wilderness to draw the wolves down upon our cradles!"

He pointed his staff down at Kesta, who knelt silently upon the cobbles, his head held high, his jaw set like chiseled granite.

"Kesta Holm has refused to confess the full conspiracy," Osric continued.

"He claims the gate slipped its bolts. He claims the swarm flew on its own. But the law of the covenant is clear. Any man who aids an exile across the marker stones is an exile himself."

Old Hal the stonecutter stepped forward from the crowd, his leather apron stiff with stone dust.

"Elder Osric! Kesta is nineteen years old! His grandfather bled beside you at the lower ford! You cannot exile a boy for a rusted gate latch on the word of old Garek!"

"I can and I will, Hal," Osric roared, stepping down from the block. His eyes burned with the fanatical intensity of forty years of buried terror.

"When the raiders burned my wife forty years ago, the men of the council hesitated! They debated while the torches were lit! I will not debate while the cleft burns a second time!"

He turned to the captain of the bailiffs.

"Take two barrels of brimstone pitch to the high rampart," Osric ordered.

"At the third hour of the afternoon, the garrison will kindled the cauldrons beneath every skep from Hive One to Hive Ten. We will burn the northern end of the wall to black cinder. If the bees have carried our scent into the gorge, the sulfur will purge the trail before the raiders find the scent."

"You are burning ten border hives?" Master Arn the blacksmith shouted, stepping forward with his heavy mallet in hand.

"Those hives are our defense! If you burn the northern benches, the gorge is wide open!"

"The gorge will be defended by iron and fire!" Osric shouted back.

"Bailiffs, clear the square! Any man who approaches the rampart without a council warrant will be shot from the battlements!"

The bailiffs leveled their crossbows, their iron points glinting in the pale gray light.

The crowd fell back, murmuring in fearful anger, clutching their children and retreating toward their stone houses. Kesta was dragged to his feet by two guards and hauled toward the dark entrance of the council dungeon. Elder Mallow, the oldest member of the council, stood beside the mounting block with his wool mantle pulled tight against the bitter wind. 'Osric,' the old man whispered, his hand trembling as he touched Osric's sleeve. 'Think on what you are doing. If you burn ten border hives, the bees cannot be replaced before spring. If the northern clans are coming, as you say, we will have neither bees to sting them nor honey to feed our wounded.'

'The bees are already dead, Mallow!' Osric snapped, shaking the elder's hand away with contempt. 'Can you not smell the rot in Hive One? The girl has taken the swarm to the wolves! If we do not burn the trail, the raiders will follow the honey scent straight to the postern arch!' In the crowd, Master Arn the blacksmith stepped forward, flanked by his two burly sons and three apprentices. 'And what of Kesta Holm, Elder?' Arn demanded, his heavy voice cutting through the wind. 'Are you going to hang a scout who has stood night watch in freezing blizzards for seven seasons, while you sit by the peat hearth drinking council cider?'

Osric pointed his oak staff at the blacksmith. 'Take care, Arn! The council has power to seize the smithy of any man who preaches rebellion on the day of muster!' 'Your power ends where our bread begins, Osric!' Arn roared back, stepping to within three paces of the council bailiffs. 'We have eaten dry rye for twenty years to satisfy your fear! If you touch that boy or burn those hives, you will find forty men on the rampart walk with iron sledges in their hands!'

Old Hal the stonecutter stepped between Master Arn and the council bailiffs, raising his grain shovel like an iron shield. 'Stand back, bailiffs!' Hal shouted, his gray beard bristling with fury. 'We built these walls with our own hands, and we won't let Osric Vance burn our children's food to satisfy his nightmares! If you shoot crossbow bolts on this square today, there won't be an elder left alive in St.

Jude's Cleft by sundown!'

The four bailiffs lowered their bows, their eyes darting to Captain Torren, who stood motionless on the rampart stairs. Torren did not raise his hand; he did not draw his sword. The garrison was made of local boys whose sisters worked the looms and whose brothers tended the sheep. When the village stood united, the council's warrants were nothing but dry ink on dead calfskin. Osric stood alone in the center of the emptying square. He looked up at the high ridge where the clouds were thickening, black and swollen with blizzard, his scarred hand trembling as he gripped his staff in the bitter wind.

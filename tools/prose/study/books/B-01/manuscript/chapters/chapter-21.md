# Chapter 21: Hammer and Chisel

The chamber beyond the keyhole constriction was called the Great Hall of Siderite in Mara's field log. The floor rose in a series of colossal stepped terraces composed of dark brown iron-rich dolomite.

Julian lay on a flat sandstone slab twenty meters from the slot. His right leg was swollen to twice its normal circumference, the skin stretched tight and shiny beneath the makeshift splint. When Rik had cut the outer tape in the pinch, the jagged ends of the fractured femur had slipped lateral, locking against a calcified spar tooth that projected from the side wall.

"The bone has displaced three centimeters," Mara stated, kneeling beside the limb with her surgical shears. She sliced away the remaining fabric of his thermal undersuit to expose the site. The skin above the mid-shaft was bruised dark purple, tented sharply where the proximal bone fragment pressed against the subcutaneous fascia. "The bone end is caught behind a ridge of flowstone. If we pull him across the terrace without releasing that spur, the bone will puncture the skin."

Julian kept his eyes closed, his breathing shallow and rapid. "Chisel the rock, not the leg, Mara. Calcite shears easily along the rhombohedral cleavage plane."

"I know how calcite shears," Rik grunted, unzipping the tool roll. He produced a thirty-centimeter drop-forged steel cold chisel and a two-kilogram club hammer. "Keep his head turned away. Limestone chips fly like shrapnel when you hit crystalline spar."

Linh held Julian's shoulders flat against the rock, while Mara knelt across his uninjured left thigh to prevent involuntary muscle spasms.

Rik set the hardened tungsten-carbide tip of the chisel against the base of the calcified tooth, four centimeters from Julian's swollen thigh. He checked the striking angle, ensuring the shock of the hammer blow would propagate away from the bone into the bedrock.

"Hold him tight," Rik warned, raising the club hammer.

The first blow rang out with a deafening, metallic crash that echoed off the high vault. A shower of white spark-dust sprayed across the stone. The calcite tooth did not shatter. Instead, a hairline fracture appeared across the surface of the dark spar.

Julian let out a low, ragged groan through his teeth, his fingers digging into Linh's wrists.

"Hit it again, Rik," Mara commanded. "Angle down ten degrees. Hit the bedding root."

Rik swung with the full power of his shoulders. The heavy steel hammer descended in a blurring arc.

The impact was followed by a high, singing ping, completely different from the dull crunch of shattering stone. The tempered steel shaft of the cold chisel snapped cleanly in two, three inches above the cutting edge. The broken tip spun across the floor and clattered into the darkness, while the fractured handle jarred backward into Rik's palm, tearing through his neoprene glove and opening a jagged gash across his thumb.

Rik dropped the hammer, swearing violently as he clutched his bleeding hand.

"The chisel broke," Linh whispered, staring at the sheared steel stump in disbelief. "How can cold-rolled tool steel snap on limestone?"

Julian opened his fever-glazed eyes, looking at the broken tool with a faint, grim grimace. "Hydrothermal recrystallization. Hydrothermal water circulation replaced the soft sedimentary micrite with pure crystalline quartz and iron carbonate. The rock is Mohs hardness seven. It has the compressive strength of granite."

Mara picked up the broken chisel handle. The steel fracture face was bright silver, showing crystalline fatigue. "We do not have another cold chisel. We have only the hand drill and six masonry bits."

"Then we drill relief holes around the perimeter," Mara decided immediately, taking the Petzl Rocpec from the pouch. "We do not try to shear the mass in one strike. We drill five holes along the cleavage line, insert steel wedges, and split the spar by hydraulic expansion."

Rik wrapped a strip of duct tape around his bleeding thumb without cleaning the wound. "Give me the hammer. My hand still works."

For forty minutes, working under the dim beam of a single headlamp, Mara and Rik rotated on the hand drill. Every blow of the hammer drove the tungsten masonry bit a fraction of a millimeter into the quartz-hard mineral vein. Cold sweat poured down Mara's neck, mixing with the red cave dust that coated her face. Her palms were blistered beneath her gloves, but she kept the striking cadence steady: sixty beats per minute, rotating the drill bit a quarter turn after every strike.

At the fifth hole, Mara set two small steel shims into the channel and drove a tapered drift punch between them.

She struck the center punch three times with sharp, measured taps.

A crisp, musical ping resonated through the floor. The calcified tooth cleaved along its crystalline axis, falling away from Julian's thigh with a dull clatter.

Mara immediately supported the fractured limb, easing the bone into axial alignment while Linh wrapped fresh layers of elastic bandage and molded the second sam splint around the leg.

Rik wiped sweat and powdered stone from his eyes as he swung the two-pound steel sledgehammer.

The hardened cold chisel bit into a tooth-like spur of crystalline calcite that jutted into the keyhole constriction, throwing gray chips of rock against his leather gloves. Each hammer blow rang with a deafening crack in the confined space of the fissure, vibrating painfully in his forearms and shoulders. Julian lay on his back in the mud fifteen centimeters below the chisel, his eyes shielded by Mara's gloved hand to protect him from flying stone splinters while Rik widened the passage centimeter by centimeter.

"The calcite is dense," Rik grunted between swings.

Julian winced as a fragment of rock struck his shoulder. "Two more centimeters and the splint clears."

Limestone had a compressive strength of roughly eighty megapascals, but its tensile strength was far lower, allowing a skilled stonecutter to fracture massive ribs by driving a chisel into natural bedding flaws. Rik targeted a paper-thin micro-fault running along the base of the spur, striking the chisel with measured, rhythmic impacts that utilized the natural resonance of the rock mass. With a dull, hollow thud, a ten-kilo slab of stone sheared along the cleavage plane, opening a clean corridor through the pinch.

Julian held the spare steel chisel against the hard rock face to direct Rik's hammer blows.

The vibration from each impact traveled up the steel shank into Julian's fingers, numbing the joints with sharp, stinging shocks. The limestone here was crystalline sparite, interlocking crystals of pure calcite that cleaved along clear rhombohedral planes when struck at the correct fifty-degree angle. By following the natural grain of the crystals, they could flake off palm-sized sheets of rock with a fraction of the physical effort required to pulverize the stone by brute force.

"Hit the lower cleavage line," Julian instructed.

Rik brought the hammer down with a solid, ringing blow. "The crack is widening."

Stonecutting in confined subterranean spaces was an ancient procedural craft that required an intimate understanding of rock mechanics. A skilled stonecutter never fought the stone; they read the internal stress lines, exploited micro-fractures, and let the geometry of the chisel do the work. With a sharp snap, a ten-centimeter block of sparite detached from the wall, opening a generous channel that allowed Julian's splinted leg to pass through without touching the rock.

Rik inspected the cutting edge of his cold chisel with a pocket magnifying glass.

The hardened tool steel had rolled slightly along the center bevel after striking the hard chert nodule. He took a small diamond whetstone from his repair kit, honing the cutting edge with circular strokes until the bevel was sharp and true. A sharp chisel cut limestone cleanly with half the impact force required by a dull tool, preserving the surveyor's physical stamina during prolonged excavation work.

Julian inspected the fractured limestone block that had fallen from the keyhole pinch, observing the clean cleavage along the rhombohedral calcite planes. The stone had fractured cleanly under Rik's hammer, opening an unhindered passage for the rescue litter.

Julian let out a long, trembling exhale, his head falling back against the stone. "Station B-sixteen reached. The tooth is out. Hand me my water bottle."

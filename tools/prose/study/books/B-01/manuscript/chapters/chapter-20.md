# Chapter 20: The Constriction

The canyon terminated at Station B-fifteen against a solid barrier of calcified flowstone that completely sealed the main rift from floor to ceiling. The only opening was a keyhole-shaped slot in the western wall, thirty-two centimeters wide at the shoulders and tapering to a razor-thin fissure near the floor.

Mara knelt at the constriction, shining her narrow-beam torch into the opening. The slot ran horizontally through dense gray micrite for eight meters before opening into a black void beyond. The air passing through the slot was freezing cold, moving at nearly two meters per second, whistling through the rock teeth with a high, eerie wail.

"A pneumatic squeeze," Mara reported, her voice muffled by the stone. "The passage beyond is large, but this keyhole is the only connection. The width is thirty-two centimeters at shoulder height. We will have to remove our helmets and push our gear ahead of us."

Rik examined the opening, his face darkening with fury and exhaustion. "Thirty-two centimeters? Mara, Julian has an aluminum sam splint wrapped in two layers of foam and elastic bandage on his right leg. That splint assembly is twenty-eight centimeters wide by itself. How do you expect him to get through an eight-meter keyhole slot without catching the bone?"

"We modify the transport protocol," Mara said, her mind working through the geometric variables with cold, procedural precision. "We cannot haul him in a sling. He must enter the slot feet first, with his injured leg extended straight ahead of him. Rik, you enter the slot first and crawl through to the far side. You take a haul cord attached to Julian's boot sling. As he pushes with his good leg and forearms, you maintain steady, axial tension on the foot to keep the splint aligned with the widest section of the slot."

"And if the splint binds?" Rik demanded, stepping close, his voice shaking with tension. "If the rock teeth catch that aluminum frame inside an eight-meter blind tube, he will be wedged sixty centimeters past reach. You cannot reach a stuck leg in a thirty-two-centimeter pinch!"

"It will not bind if we maintain axial alignment," Mara stated, her voice brooking no argument. "The alternative is sitting on this gravel bench until the flood surge breaches the canyon. We have no other leads. This keyhole is the path."

Julian looked at the narrow stone slit, then down at his leg. He reached out and touched Rik's arm. "Rik. Look at me."

Rik looked down at the injured hydrologist.

"I can do this," Julian said, his voice quiet, calm, and utterly devoid of fear. "I know how to control my breathing. If the splint touches the rock, I will tell you which way to rotate the line. Just pull steady. Do not jerk the rope."

Rik swallowed hard, his jaw working. He turned away, unclipped his helmet, and hooked his headlamp strap directly over his wool cap. "I am going in. Send the haul cord once I call clear."

Rik dropped flat onto the polished rock, turned his shoulders sideways, and wriggled into the keyhole slot. For six agonizing minutes, the only sound was the harsh scrape of his Cordura suit against the limestone and his heavy, grunting breaths as he forced his muscular frame through the restriction.

"Clear!" Rik's voice echoed out from the far side, hollow and distant. "The chamber on this side is massive! The air is fresh! Send Julian!"

Mara and Linh prepared Julian for the squeeze. They removed his outer oversuit to reduce bulk, leaving him in his thermal fleece underwear. They wrapped a smooth layer of plastic duct tape over the outside of the splint to prevent the straps from snagging on limestone crystals.

"Feet first, Julian," Mara guided, supporting his hips as he eased into the opening. "Keep your arms extended over your head. Hold the drag lanyard with your left hand. Push only when Rik pulls."

Julian slid into the black slot, his boots vanishing into the rock. Mara dropped onto her stomach behind him, following five inches behind his head, her lamp illuminating the narrow space.

The constriction was terrifyingly tight. The rock walls pressed against Julian's ribs, compressing his chest until his exhales came in thin, wheezing gasps. The cold limestone ceiling hung three inches above his forehead.

"One meter in," Mara called ahead. "Alignment is good. Splint clearance is three centimeters on the lateral wall."

From the far end, the tension line went taut as Rik pulled with steady, rhythmic strength. Julian pushed with the toes of his left boot, inching forward through the tube. Two meters. Three meters. Four meters.

At five meters, the passage executed a subtle, jagged zig-zag around a vertical calcite blade.

"Watch the blade, Julian," Mara warned from behind, her face inches from his hair. "Rotate your hips ten degrees clockwise."

Julian attempted to rotate his pelvis, but the tight walls clamped against his hip bones. As Rik hauled on the line, the lower corner of the aluminum splint struck the calcified blade.

The metal frame wedged hard into a recessed rock pocket.

"Stop!" Julian gasped, his voice choking on a wave of agonizing pain. "Hold! The foot is jammed!"

Rik stopped hauling instantly. "Status! Can you back it out?"

Julian tried to pull his leg back, but the sharp calcite tooth had hooked behind the top edge of the splint. The frame was locked in place like a toggle bolt. Every millimeter of movement transferred directly into the shattered bone ends of his femur.

"I cannot move it," Julian groaned, his forehead pressed flat against the cold, slimy floor. "The splint is wedged in a undercut pocket. Mara, it is locked solid."

Mara reached forward as far as her arm could extend in the suffocating thirty-centimeter space. Her fingertips could barely brush the heel of Julian's boot. She could not reach the wedged bracket. She could not get an angle to apply mechanical force with a crowbar.

"Linh!" Mara shouted back toward the entrance. "Can you fit past my boots?"

"No!" Linh's voice came from the canyon behind. "There is no space! The slot is completely blocked by your body!"

Julian's breathing grew ragged, his pulse hammering visibly in the carotid artery of his neck. "Mara. It is slipping. My bone is grinding against the rock. I cannot hold my leg straight."

Mara closed her eyes for one second, her mind racing through the physical parameters. If Rik pulled harder, the aluminum frame would buckle, driving the fractured femur through the skin and severing the femoral artery. If they left him wedged, hypothermia and shock would kill him in less than an hour.

"Julian," Mara whispered into his ear. "Listen to my voice. Do not move your leg."

She took her stainless steel dive knife from her forearm sheath. Working with agonizing care in the narrow space between Julian's ribs and the stone wall, she extended the blade past his waist.

"What are you doing?" Julian gasped.

"I am cutting the outer tape on the splint bracket," Mara said, her voice dropping into an icy, razor-sharp calm. "If I cut the lower tension strap, the aluminum frame will collapse inward by two centimeters. The splint will lose rigidity, but it will clear the rock tooth."

"The bone will displace," Julian whispered, terror breaking through his shock.

"Rik will pull the instant the strap cuts," Mara said. "The bone will displace for three seconds while you slide through the pinch. You have to take the pain, Julian. You have to survive three seconds."

Julian pressed his face into the mud. He gripped the drag lanyard with both hands until his knuckles turned purple. "Cut it."

Mara slid the serrated edge of the knife along the outer duct tape until the blade touched the nylon webbing of the splint bracket. She sawed through the tough fibers with two quick, precise strokes.

The webbing parted with a dull snap. The aluminum frame buckled inward.

"Rik, PULL!" Mara screamed.

Rik hauled with the strength of a dying man. Julian's body surged forward through the constriction, his splint clearing the calcite blade by two millimeters.

A high, ragged shriek tore from Julian's throat, an animal sound of pure, unadulterated agony that echoed through the stone tube, before his head fell limp against the floor, plunging into absolute unconsciousness.

Two seconds later, Rik's massive hands reached through the far opening, grabbed Julian beneath his armpits, and dragged his limp frame out of the keyhole slot into the vast, open darkness of the upper chamber.

Mara scrambled out behind him, tumbling onto the floor of the new hall. She lay on her back on a bed of dry sand, her chest heaving, her oversuit shredded, staring up into a vaulted ceiling of colossal, unfathomable blackness.

Rik knelt over Julian, his fingers pressed against the hydrologist's carotid artery. He looked up at Mara, his eyes wild in the beam of his headlamp.

"He is breathing," Rik said, his voice shaking violently. "His pulse is thready, but he is alive."

Mara sat up slowly, wiping cold mud and sweat from her eyes. She looked out across the chamber.

Mara shone her headlamp into the thirty-centimeter constriction where the limestone passage pinched shut.

The slot was an elliptical keyhole channel carved through a dense bed of black dolomitic limestone, its lower half filled with a sluggish stream of freezing water and gravel slurry. To pass through, a surveyor had to exhale all the air from their lungs, turn their head completely sideways with one ear submerged in the mud, and push themselves forward using only the tips of their toes and fingers. Julian's splinted leg made the traverse perilous, as any twisting of the aluminum rods would wedge him immovably between the stone jaws.

"I will go through first to pull Julian," Rik said.

Julian took a deep, steadying breath. "Push my splint straight through the slot. Do not let it rotate."

Rik squeezed his broad shoulders into the constriction, his ribs scraping against the abrasive rock with a harsh, scratching sound. He wriggled through the five-meter slot like an eel, his boots kicking out behind him until he popped into the wider chamber on the far side. He turned immediately in the darkness, reaching his massive hands back through the narrow opening to receive Julian's splinted leg, guiding the metal rods along the central axis of the slot with gentle, millimeter-perfect precision.

Rik wedged his rock hammer into the narrow crack beside Julian's pinned splint to create leverage.

The keyhole slot was thirty centimeters wide at its narrowest point, its lower channel filled with five centimeters of freezing mud that acted like suction against Julian's clothing. By prying gently with the hammer handle, Rik opened an extra three millimeters of clearance between the aluminum splint rod and the rock wall, allowing Julian to slide his hip forward five centimeters with each exhalation. Mara guided the foot from behind, keeping the toes pointed straight to prevent the heel from catching on an undercut lip.

"Exhale and slide, Julian," Mara whispered.

Julian let his breath out in a slow hiss. "The knee is clearing the constriction."

Patience and gentle mechanics were the only way to navigate severe cave constrictions. If a trapped caver panicked and fought against the rock, their chest would expand with rapid breathing, wedging their ribs immovably into the stone and inducing a fatal condition known as positional asphyxiation. Julian kept his breathing slow and deep, working in perfect synchronization with Rik's prying lever until his hips popped free into the wider chamber beyond.

Julian carefully exhaled as his ribcage passed the narrowest pinch of the limestone slot.

His chest compressed by three centimeters, his neoprene suit gliding across the polished stone with minimal friction. Behind him, Mara held the trailing end of his safety tether, keeping light tension on the cord to prevent him from slipping backward if his boot lost purchase in the slick mud trench. With one final forward push of his elbows, his torso cleared the constriction completely.

Rik guided Julian's splinted leg through the keyhole slot, his massive hands supporting the aluminum rods with gentle precision. The clearance was minimal, but patient coordination and slow exhalations allowed the injured surveyor to pass without aggravating the fracture.

The narrow keyhole constriction was behind them at last, its limestone jaws powerless to hold them in the dark. Ahead lay the ascending chimney and the promising draft of fresh surface air from the sky.

Julian felt the cold limestone pressing against his shoulder blades as Rik eased him through the narrowest throat of the slot. The rock was slick with organic clay slurry, acting as a lubricant that allowed his caving suit to glide through the pinch with minimal resistance.

"Hold the splint level with the floor," Julian said, his voice calm and focused.

The keyhole slot was a classic vadose entrenchment feature, formed when an active underground stream incised a narrow floor trench into the bottom of a preexisting elliptical phreatic tube. The upper tube provided clearance for Julian's shoulders, while the lower slot accommodated his splinted leg, demonstrating how speleogenetic history dictated human passage geometry in deep karst systems. Rik maintained steady, gentle traction on the haul webbing, easing Julian through the final three centimeters of the restriction until his boots touched the dry gravel on the far side.

The keyhole constriction was behind them. Before them lay a colossal subterranean gallery, soaring fifty meters into the heart of the mountain, dry as bone, carrying a steady, unmistakable draft of pure tropical air.

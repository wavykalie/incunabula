# Chapter 34: Linh's Anchor

White froth boiled across the lip of the marble ledge, curling around Linh's wrists in icy, cream-colored braids.

On the collapsing far wall, Rik hung suspended by his cowstail from the single remaining piton. Beneath his swinging boots, the underground river surged up into the undercut, throwing waves of cold spray across his chest. He could not jump. He could not pull himself along the severed traverse line. His only pathway across the chasm was a trailing seven-millimeter accessory cord anchored to Linh's side of the fault.

"The piton is working loose!" Rik called out, his voice thin against the roar of the river. "The vibration is walking it out of the joint!"

Mara was kneeling beside Julian, tightening a tourniquet over his bleeding shin where the rock horn had lacerated the muscle. She looked up, her eyes wide. "Linh, take the anchor bolt! Drill a secondary hanger on the right bedding plane!"

Linh looked at the drill kit in her lap. Her hands were shaking violently.

Ever since the collapse at Station 16, the sound of falling rock had induced a physical paralysis in her chest. Her throat tightened, starving her brain of oxygen, while her fingers curled into rigid, unyielding claws. In the darkness of the lower sumps, she had hidden behind Julian's steady jokes and Rik's brute strength. But Julian was broken on the marble floor, and Rik was hanging over an abyss that promised instant drowning.

She looked down into the water.

A submerged limestone rib protruded from the current two feet below the water's edge, solid and crystalline, washed clean of mud by centuries of phreatic flow. It was solid spar calcite, unyielding and pure. But to reach it, she had to plunge her arms and shoulders deep into the freezing, torrential cataract.

"Linh!" Mara shouted. "Three minutes before that piton shears!"

Linh did not reach for the hand drill.

She reached for a heavy forged steel C-clamp and a six-millimeter wire sling. She threw off her neoprene overmitts, exposing bare skin to the freezing spray. Taking a deep, shuddering breath of subterranean air, she dropped to her stomach on the wet marble and plunged both arms elbow-deep into the roaring torrent.

The shock of the water took her breath away like a blow to the solar plexus. The current pulled violently at her forearms, trying to drag her headfirst off the ledge. Submerged pebbles pelted her wrists, bruising the tender tendons of her forearms.

Under the black water, her fingers searched the submerged limestone rib.

She found the natural solution pocket. It was a smooth, hourglass constriction carved through the solid rock, four centimeters wide, worn glassy by prehistoric floods.

She guided the flexible steel wire sling through the submerged hole, working by touch alone in zero visibility. Her fingers were rapidly losing sensation in the eight-degree water. The skin turned waxy and gray, the joints aching with bone-deep cold.

"I have the loop!" Linh cried out, her cheek pressed flat against the muddy stone.

"Feed the carabiner!" Mara called from above, bracing Linh's ankles with her boots.

Linh jammed the steel locking carabiner through the wire eye under water. Her numb fingers struggled with the screw gate, slipping twice against the knurled sleeve. She clamped her teeth onto her lower lip until she tasted copper, forcing her frozen thumb to turn the collar.

Click.

The threads seated home.

She gripped the haul line with both hands, wrapping the rope around the locked steel bight, and pulled the system taut against her own chest.

"Rik, release your piton!" Linh screamed into the spray. "I have you on the solid rib! Release!"

On the far wall, Rik looked across the eight meters of black water. He saw the girl who had frozen in panic during the first ascent of the rift, now anchored chest-deep in freezing mud, holding the lifeline of the entire expedition in her bare, bleeding hands.

He kicked outward from the failing wall, unclipping his cowstail in mid-air.

He swung through the roaring dark like a pendulum. The accessory cord snapped taut with a sound like a rifle shot.

The entire shock load transferred directly through the wire sling into the submerged calcite rib. Linh's body was jerked forward toward the brink, her ribs slamming hard against the marble lip. But she did not let go. Her boots wedged into a shallow floor trench, her arms locking around the tensioned rope with every ounce of sinew left in her body.

Rik slammed into the wall below the ledge, his boots finding purchase on an undercut shelf. He grabbed the fixed rope with both ascenders, kicked twice, and hauled himself over the rim onto solid ground.

He collapsed onto the marble beside Linh, gasping for air, his face pale and dripping with river sludge.

Linh pulled her hands from the freezing water. They were bruised, scratched, and trembling uncontrollably, but the knot was unbroken and the steel carabiner remained locked.

Linh felt the icy current tearing at the skin of her forearms as she held the submerged wire loop.

The water temperature was eight degrees Celsius, cold enough to induce rapid vasoconstriction in the peripheral capillaries of her fingers within forty seconds. Under normal conditions, such extreme cold would cause her fingers to lock in a painful cramp, but the surge of adrenaline flooding her bloodstream suppressed the pain receptors, allowing her to maintain her death-grip on the steel carabiner. Beneath her fingers, the smooth calcite of the submerged rib was unyielding, anchored deep in the bedrock of the mountain.

"I have the gate locked!" Linh screamed into the spray.

Rik swung through the air toward her, his boots trailing in the foam. "Hold the line, Linh!"

When Rik's full weight slammed into the anchor, the dynamic shock wave traveled up the wire sling and struck Linh's chest like a physical blow. Her shoulder joints screamed under the tension, the tendons in her forearms stretching to their absolute elastic limit, but her grip did not slip a single millimeter. She held the lifeline of the expedition anchored to the solid core of the Earth until Rik's boots found purchase on the dry marble shelf beside her.

Rik wrapped a secondary safety tether around Linh's waist webbing, clipping the locking carabiner to a dry flowstone boss above the ledge.

The tether was made of nine-millimeter dynamic rope, designed to absorb the kinetic energy of a sudden slip without transmitting dangerous impact forces to her spine. He watched Linh closely as she knelt in the freezing water, her hands working the submerged wire sling with absolute concentration. He knew the mental effort it took for her to conquer the paralyzing terror that had gripped her during the rift climb, and he was determined to protect her from any secondary fall.

"You are backed up on dynamic rope, Linh," Rik said.

Linh did not look up from the water. "I have the anchor locked."

The bond of mutual trust that developed between expedition cavers was forged in the extreme crucible of shared mortal danger. In the deep galleries of the earth, where rescue teams from the surface were days away, every team member was responsible for the lives of their companions. Linh's courage on the submerged marble ledge was not an individual act; it was a testament to the absolute faith she placed in Rik and Mara to hold her safety line.

Linh checked the screw gate on the steel carabiner under water, feeling the knurled sleeve with her thumb.

The sleeve was turned completely shut against the gate barrel, preventing any accidental opening from vibration or contact with submerged rock fins. Under heavy water flow, turbulence could vibrate unlocked carabiner gates open, allowing ropes or slings to detach under dynamic load. Her disciplined check confirmed that the connection was permanent and bombproof.

Linh dried her numb hands with a clean cotton towel, wrapping them around the chemical heat pack inside her pocket. Her courageous hold on the submerged anchor had saved Rik's life, completing her journey from self-doubt to seasoned expedition caver.

"Solid anchor," Rik whispered, his big hand covering hers on the cold rock. "Bombproof."

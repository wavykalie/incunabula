# Chapter 29

The internal review was at 17:00, in the small conference room, with three people and no transcript.

Ilsa was told about it at 16:45 by Priya's assistant, who said the words *informal conversation* in a way that made them sound like a procedure. She arrived at 16:58 with the folder and the notebook. She was asked to leave the notebook outside, and she did.

The three people were Priya, a woman from human resources named Cole, and a man from legal whose name she had not caught at the meeting on Wednesday and did not catch now.

"This isn't disciplinary," Cole said. "It's a review of the classification activity on K-7741, and your account of it."

"I've written an audit. It's forty-one pages. It's filed with the regulator and the insurer."

"We've read the summary." Cole had a printed copy in front of her, with three tabs on it. "We'd like to go through the sequence of events with you."

They went through it. The reclassification on Tuesday morning. The sensor artefact report. The independent fit. The portal submission. The audit. Each step was a question, and each answer was a date and a time, and Cole wrote the dates and times down.

At the end, the man from legal asked the only question that mattered.

"Did you follow the reclassification policy?"

"Yes."

"Did you follow the standing instruction?"

"I raised the watch item. The duty officer downgraded it under the instruction. I followed the procedure for raising it."

"That's not what I asked."

"I followed the policy. The policy produced the outcome."

The man from legal wrote something down. Priya looked at the table.

"Thank you," Cole said. "That's all we need."

"What happens now?"

"You'll be placed on special project leave, effective immediately, with full pay and no access. It's standard during a review. You'll be contacted within five business days."

"Special project leave."

"It's not a suspension. It's a leave status that protects the integrity of the review."

Ilsa looked at Priya, who did not look back.

"Can I ask one question?"

"Of course."

"Who requested the review?"

Cole glanced at Priya. Priya said, "I did."

"You requested a review of my classification activity."

"I requested a review of the sequence, because the sequence is going to be reviewed by somebody, and I'd rather it was reviewed internally first." Priya's voice was even. "You filed with the regulator, Ilsa. That's a regulatory event. The company has thirty days to respond, and the response has to say what happened. This is how we find out what happened."

"I told you what happened. It's in the audit."

"The audit is your account. The review is the company's." Priya stood. "Go home. Keep your phone on. Don't talk to the client, don't talk to the regulator, don't talk to the press. If somebody from the regulator calls, give them the case number and my name."

Ilsa stood. At the door, Cole said, "Your badge will work for the car park. It won't work for the floor after eighteen hundred."

"Thank you," Ilsa said, and the politeness in her own voice surprised her.

Cole read from a printed list, which was the thing Ilsa remembered afterwards: not the questions, but the list.

"Did you classify K-7741 as debris on the date shown?"

"I classified it as debris in 2021. On the date shown I reclassified it after the hygiene review."

"Did you have authority to reclassify?"

"The threshold for a second signature is an object above priority two. K-7741 was priority four."

"So you had authority."

"I had authority under the procedure as it stood."

The man from legal looked up. "As it stood."

"The procedure was amended in 2025 to require a second signature for any reclassification of an object in a crewed shell. The amendment isn't retrospective."

"Was the 2024 reclassification in a crewed shell?"

"The object's orbit crosses the station's shell."

"Then under the current procedure it would have required a second signature."

"Under the current procedure it would have required a second signature. The current procedure didn't exist."

Cole wrote something. The pen moved slowly.

"Did you read the appendix before it was filed?"

"I read the quarterly report. I don't recall reading the appendix as a separate document."

"The appendix is part of the report."

"The appendix is annex C of the report, and the report was eighty pages, and I read the sections I was responsible for." Ilsa kept her voice level. "I'm not going to tell you I read every page of an eighty-page report. I read the sections on classification activity, which is my section, and the appendix is in the section on catalogue hygiene, which is a different section."

"Whose section is it?"

"Simon Achterberg's."

"Mr Achterberg left the company in 2024."

"He left in November 2024."

"So the appendix was in the section of a person who had left."

"The appendix was in the section of a person who left seven months after the appendix was filed."

Cole put the pen down.

"Ms Merrick. I want to be clear about the shape of this review. We are not investigating the appendix. We are establishing whether your classification activity was consistent with policy. On the evidence, it was. The review will record that, and it will record the two open matters: the date on the appendix, and the access session on the seventeenth."

"The date on the appendix is wrong."

"The date on the appendix is the date on the appendix." Cole looked at her. "We can't establish what happened in that session. The log says your credentials. The camera export request is with facilities. Until we have the export, the review will record the session as unexplained."

"Unexplained by whom?"

"By the review." Cole closed her folder. "There's one more thing. The client has asked that you be placed on special project leave pending the outcome. The leave is paid. Your access is suspended, not revoked. You'll keep your badge and your desk."

"For how long?"

"Until the outcome."

"And the outcome depends on the camera export."

"The outcome depends on a number of things." Cole stood. "I'll tell you what I tell everybody in this position. The company is not your enemy. The company is a machine that has to keep running, and the machine has a procedure for this, and the procedure is slower than you'd like and faster than a court."

"That's not reassuring."

"It's not meant to be."

She walked out through the catalogue bay. The afternoon shift was at the consoles; the dishes were turning; the coffee machine was doing its flush. Nobody looked up. She took the folder and the notebook from the small conference room, and she went down in the lift, and she sat in her car in the car park for eleven minutes before she started the engine.

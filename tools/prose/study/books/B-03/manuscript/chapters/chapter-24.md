# Chapter 24: The Cracked Skin

The loading of Arthur Povey's Emperor was conducted with the reverence usually reserved for the transportation of unexploded ordnance.

Four veteran allotmenteers, each possessing at least thirty years of competitive squash experience, knelt on the parquet floor beside Table 6. They slid two lengths of heavy woven cotton webbing beneath the padded cushion, taking care not to pinch the pale yellow skin where the vegetable rested against the timber.

"Easy now," Povey commanded, hovering over the team with his hands outstretched like a midwife. "Lift together on three. One, two, three, hoist!"

The four men rose together, their thigh muscles trembling beneath their corduroy trousers.

The Emperor rose from its cushion. It was an astonishing vegetable, four feet long, broad-shouldered and ribbed with pale cream striations that ran like ivory ribs down its dark green dorsal curve. Its mass was immense, hanging in the sling with the dense, heavy solidity of a wet sack of cement.

They carried it three paces toward the weighing platform.

And then, in the absolute, breathless silence of the hall, a sound cut through the air like the snapping of a dry celery stick.

*Crack.*

The sound was sharp, dry, and unmistakable.

The four lifters froze in mid-stride. Arthur Povey stopped dead, his face draining of all colour, leaving his skin the gray shade of Fenland clay.

"Hold it," Povey whispered, his voice cracking into a terrified squeak. "Hold it right there. Nobody move an inch."

He fell to his knees on the parquet floor, crawling beneath the suspended canvas sling, his nose barely three inches from the vegetable's underbelly.

Zoe stepped forward from the dais, her heart thumping against her ribs. 

Beneath the central curve of the marrow, running horizontally across the pale skin where the fruit had rested against the wooden trestle, lay a hairline fissure roughly eight inches in length. A tiny, glistening bead of clear vegetative sap was forming along the lip of the split, welling up from the pressurized interior of the squash.

Fiona Sterling stepped down from the stage, her brass calipers open in her right hand, her clipboard tucked beneath her arm. She knelt beside Povey, adjusted her spectacles, and inspected the fissure with cold, judicial scrutiny.

"Skin rupture," Fiona said, her voice echoing with flat, municipal finality across the silent room. "Rule 14b, subsection two. Any exhibit displaying a through-crack in the epidermal layer exceeding one sixteenth of an inch is legally classified as an open wound. The fruit cannot be certified for weighing."

"It's just a surface stretch!" Povey shouted, tears of pure fury springing into his watery eyes. "It's the humidity, Fiona! The hot air in this room has expanded the pulp faster than the skin can stretch! The pulp is sound! There ain't a drop of rot in her!"

"The rule makes no distinction between rot and mechanical failure, Arthur," Fiona replied, rising to her feet and dusting her tweed skirt. "The skin is compromised. The specimen is disqualified."

A collective groan of despair arose from the allotmenteers. Two men near the tea urn threw their caps onto the floor. Young Henderson grinned broadly, folding his arms across his navy bodywarmer and winking at his father.

Povey remained on his knees on the floorboards, his broad shoulders slumped, his massive peat-stained hands resting helplessly beside the damaged vegetable. In seventy-one years of life on the fen, he had never looked so old, so defeated, and so utterly alone.

Julian Tremayne stood upon the riser, looking down at the broken old man with a furrowed brow. He turned his head toward Zoe.

"Zoe," Julian whispered, his baritone cracked with genuine distress. "Can nothing be done? Is this how it ends?"

Zoe did not answer. She had already dropped her tote bag onto the floor and was unzipping the inner compartment with rapid, surgical precision.

Arthur Povey remained frozen upon his knees on the parquet floor, staring at the hairline fissure in the Emperor's belly with horror.

George Baxter knelt beside him, his hand trembling as he held his pocket torch to the pale green skin. "It's weeping, Artie," Baxter whispered, his voice cracking with misery. "Look at the sap. It's coming up through the peat grain. If Fiona calls the stewards over, she'll write the disqualification before you can draw breath."

Across the room, young Henderson was whispering in his father's ear, pointing with his gloved thumb toward the huddle around Table 6. The elder Henderson took his cigar from his teeth, smiled a cold, predatory smile, and stepped toward the stage steps to alert the committee.

Julian Tremayne stood beside the table, looking down at Povey's bowed head. He saw the old man's shoulders shaking; he saw forty years of solitary labour, of winter gales and summer droughts, poised on the brink of total municipal humiliation.

Julian turned to Zoe. "Zoe," he said, his voice dropping into a register of urgent, desperate command. "You have your emergency bag. The kit you used when my dress trousers split during the second act of The Tempest at Guildford. The silver tape."

Zoe looked at him. "Julian, if Fiona catches us applying tape, she could forfeit your appearance fee under the integrity bylaws."

"Let her forfeit it!" Julian hissed, his eyes blazing with authentic, fearless rage. "Gidley can take my furniture! Marcus can take my wardrobe! But I will not stand by and watch that corrupt feed merchant steal this old man's life with an unweighed Dutch pumpkin! Fix the vegetable, Zoe!"

"The chemist shop in the market place," Zoe said to herself, checking the town map printed in the exhibition programme.

"What are you searching for, Zoe?" Julian asked, catching the sharp focus in her voice.

"A tube of cyanocrylate adhesive," she whispered. "Medical grade, or surgical skin bond."

"Skin bond?" Julian raised an eyebrow. "Are you injured, my dear?"

"Arthur's marrow has a three-inch fissure along the lower seam," she said quietly. "If the pressure of the lifting sling expands that split, the sap will leak over the Avery scale plate and Fiona will disqualify the entry on the spot."

Julian looked at her with profound, astonished respect. "You intend to perform emergency field surgery upon a giant squash?"

"I intend to ensure Arthur gets an honest weigh-in," Zoe said, pulling her raincoat collar tight. "Cover for me with Fiona. Tell her I am inspecting the sanitary conveniences."

"The adhesive has bonded completely," Zoe whispered, her hand briefly brushing the cool green rind behind the shock pad.

"Thank God," Arthur breathed, his forehead glistening with cold sweat. "If that crack had opened on the hoist, the juice would have run down the dial like a burst water pipe."

"You must keep the weight evenly distributed when the stewards take the strain," Zoe instructed. "Make sure Frank lifts with his knees, not his back."

Julian joined them, holding an exhibition schedule in front of his face like a choirboy's hymnal.

"A tactical conference, my friends?" he murmured. "The atmosphere is positively conspiratorial. One feels like a Jacobite conspirator in a coffee house near St Paul's."

"We are keeping a giant squash from splitting in half, Julian," Zoe said. "Not overthrowing the House of Hanover."

"The stakes feel comparable," Julian retorted.

# Chapter 37 — Audrey's Stamp

The private office was dead quiet once more as Arthur, Leo, and Clem gathered around Audrey Vance's mahogany desk. Outside, the crowd remained in the foyer and the square, waiting in patient, respectful silence.

Audrey sat in her high-backed chair. Before her lay the official Master Register of Marriages for the County of Herefordshire: Volume 48 (2012–2026), bound in full dark blue morocco leather with the royal coat of arms stamped in gold on the front cover.

She dipped her ebony fountain pen into the fresh black inkwell.

"We proceed under Section seventy-two of the Marriages Act nineteen-forty-nine," Audrey stated, her voice returning to its calm, rhythmic civil service authority. "Where an authentic retrospective counter-foil hath been verified under a customary border exemption, the Superintendent Registrar is empowered to enter a certified memorandum of validation into the permanent gazetted registers of the district."

She opened the Master Register to page one hundred and fourteen, which had remained blank under the Golden Valley schedule for July 2016.

With slow, flawless strokes of her pen, Audrey inscribed the entry:

*ENTRY NO. 2016/042-V: BY SPECIAL MEMORANDUM OF CUSTOMARY EXEMPTION (6 & 7 WILL. IV c. 85 s. 22). Solemnized on the 16th day of July, 2016, at the Approved Chapel-of-Ease, The Cider Barn, Blackthorn Hollow, Parish of Vowchurch. Leo Thomas Marsh and Clementine Mary Vaughan, Bachelor and Spinster, Master Joiner and Horticulturalist. Solemnized by Arthur Reginald Pendelton, Presbyter Licentiate, in the presence of Thomas Gwillam and David Price.*

She picked up her heavy ivory bone folder and smoothed the black ink.

Then she opened her middle drawer and extracted the Great Seal of the Superintendent Registrar: an enormous, heavy apparatus of solid brass and cast iron, weighing fourteen pounds, fitted with an articulated screw lever and an engraved circular die depicting the crown of Saint Edward surrounded by the words: *SUPERINTENDENT REGISTRAR — COUNTY OF HEREFORDSHIRE*.

Audrey slid the parchment page into the jaws of the machine.

She looked at Arthur. "Hold the base, Mr. Pendelton."

Arthur laid his hands upon the heavy cast-iron footings.

Audrey grasped the polished brass lever with both hands, brought her weight down, and drove the die home with a single, magnificent, mechanical *THUMP*.

The sound resonated through the mahogany desk, through the floorboards, and into the stone foundations of the Shirehall.

Audrey released the lever. She lifted the vellum sheet.

Impressed into the heavy paper, raised in sharp, pristine relief, was the royal crown seal, clean, permanent, and unassailable.

"There," Audrey whispered softly, her fingers lightly touching the embossed crest. "The cornerstone is entered. Blackthorn Hollow is a lawful chapel-of-ease. And the marriage of Leo and Clementine Marsh is inscribed upon the permanent record of the British realm."

Clem took Leo's hand. Two tears escaped her grey eyes, running down her cheeks to splash upon her mended canvas trousers. Leo bowed his head, his shoulders trembling with silent, overwhelming joy.

Arthur looked at the wet ink. He felt as if a stone slab weighing four hundred tons had been lifted from his heart.

Audrey Vance picked up a clean linen cloth, wiped the brass face of the seal, and looked across the desk at Arthur.

"Now, Mr. Pendelton," she said, her voice crisp and businesslike once more. "We have three hundred and ninety-nine supplementary schedules to annex to this memorandum before four-thirty. Pull up your chair, uncork your Higgins ink, and let us begin."

Arthur and Audrey worked together at the mahogany desk for ninety minutes, checking every name, every parish, and every date.

Leo Marsh sat beside Arthur, carefully inspecting each certificate counter-foil with his draughtsman's eye to ensure that the alignment was true, while Clem helped Miss Cleary organize the verified schedules into twelve chronological portfolios, one for each year of Arthur's ministry.

As each portfolio was completed, Audrey brought down the Great Seal with that tremendous, rhythmic *THUMP*.

*Thump. Entry seventy-four: Weobley apple orchard.*
*Thump. Entry one hundred and twelve: Craswall sheep fold.*
*Thump. Entry two hundred and four: Ross-on-Wye narrowboat.*

With every stroke of the brass lever, another rural union was lifted out of administrative jeopardy and anchored permanently into the constitutional fabric of the realm. The property titles were secured; the mortgages were protected; the children were recognized as lawful heirs, and the threat of the inland revenue auditors was extinguished forever.

When the final schedule—entry four hundred and ten—received its crown impression, Miss Cleary laid down her numbering stamp with a heavy sigh of exhaustion and triumph.

"Four hundred and ten, Mrs. Vance," Miss Cleary said, her eyes shining. "Every single counter-foil accounted for."

Audrey Vance dipped her pen one last time and inscribed her signature at the foot of the master validation schedule: *Audrey Vance, Superintendent Registrar, County of Herefordshire, 13 November 2026*.

She blotted the ink with a fresh square of white paper.

"The register is closed, Mr. Pendelton," Audrey said, looking up with profound satisfaction. "Four hundred and ten marriages. All lawful. All gazetted. All safe."

Audrey Vance handed the certified validation schedule to Miss Cleary, who placed it inside an archival cardboard folder tied with black tape.

"Take this to the safe, Miss Cleary," Audrey said. "It will be entered into the county roll on Monday morning."

She looked at Arthur, Leo, and Clem.

"You have done well today," Audrey said simply. "The county of Herefordshire owes you its thanks."

Clem offered a respectful, dignified nod. "Thank you, Mrs. Vance. We'll send you a case of the twenty-twenty-two Kingston Black for Christmas."

Audrey smiled faintly. "I shall declare it as an unmonetized customary agricultural gift under council standing orders."

Arthur gathered the four hundred verified schedules, squaring the edges against the mahogany desk with the practiced precision of his water board years.

"Every schedule is numbered, Audrey," Arthur reported, tying the bundle with a length of red tape. "Every parish is identified, and every customary exemption is cited under the governing statute."

Audrey Vance inspected the bundle with keen, professional eyes.

"An exemplary piece of administrative collation, Arthur," she said, nodding with deep satisfaction. "The Registrar General in Southport will find no defect in these returns. They are as sound as church bells."

Arthur felt an immense, quiet joy settle into his bones. The seventy-two hours of terror had ended in an unshakeable monument of public record.

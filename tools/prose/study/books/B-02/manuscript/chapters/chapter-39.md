# Chapter 39: The Customs Desk

The customs office in Horta smelled of floor wax, strong espresso, and fifty years of official maritime rubber stamps.

Arthur Vance sat at the long wooden counter beneath a framed portrait of the Portuguese president. Across the desk sat Inspector Duarte, an elderly customs officer with steel-rimmed spectacles perched on his nose, methodically turning the pages of the ship's clearance papers.

Beside Artie stood Clara Lindholm and Julian Kross.

Julian was dressed in dry civilian clothes: a dark woolen sweater and clean corduroy trousers from his duffel bag. His hands were clasped behind his back, his fingers trembling with suppressed tension as the inspector examined the blue British discharge books laid out on the green blotter.

Inspector Duarte picked up the first booklet: *Arthur Vance, Master*.

He pressed his heavy brass stamp onto an inked purple pad, brought it down on page twenty-two with a sharp, resonant thud, and signed his name with a black fountain pen.

"Captain Vance," the inspector said. "Clearance granted."

He picked up the second booklet: *Clara Lindholm, First Mate*.

He checked the dates, stamped page fourteen, and signed. "Miss Lindholm. Clearance granted. Clean delivery endorsement."

Clara took the blue book, holding it against her ribs like a piece of gold. Her eyes were bright with quiet, hard-won triumph. Twelve clean ocean days, logged and stamped by the Portuguese state authority, without an incident, a collision, or a reprimand. Her career was alive once more.

Then Inspector Duarte picked up the third booklet: *Marcus Edward Ward, Deckhand*.

Julian's breath caught in his throat. He stared at the blue pebble-grain cover as if it were a bomb about to detonate.

The inspector opened the booklet to page nine.

He looked at the black steel-nib entry from October 2021: *Discharge on Account of Decease // Lost overboard in severe weather, North Sea sector 4*.

Inspector Duarte frowned, adjusting his spectacles. He looked up from the paper, his eyes fixing on Julian's pale, motionless face.

"This entry," the inspector said, tapping the purple stamp with his index finger. "The seaman named Marcus Ward was certified deceased off Shetland four years ago."

The small customs office went dead silent.

Outside the open window, the sound of a diesel fishing boat coughing into life in the harbor drifted through the curtains.

Julian parted his lips to speak, his chest collapsing, ready to surrender to the European warrant that had hunted him across three thousand miles of ocean.

Before Julian could say a single word, Arthur Vance leaned forward across the wooden counter.

He drew his official master's seal from his vest pocket: a heavy brass stamp engraved with his name, his master's license number, and the maritime emblem of the commercial delivery registry.

"Inspector Duarte," Artie said, his voice calm, steady, and filled with the unyielding authority of an ocean captain. "The entry on page nine was an administrative error made by a relief shipping master in Aberdeen during the pandemic. The crew member standing before you was cleared by the British Maritime and Coastguard Agency in Southampton on May twelfth, twenty-twenty-four, as noted on page ten."

Artie opened his blue canvas deck log, pointing to the crew manifest signed at St. John's.

"This man stood his watch on my ship for twelve days across eighteen hundred miles of North Atlantic gales," Artie said, looking the customs officer directly in the eye. "He risked his life on the foredeck to clear a fouled staysail. He held the steering winches when our quadrant fractured. He is the deckhand of record on S/Y Aura VII."

Artie picked up his heavy brass master's stamp, pressed it into the purple ink pad, and brought it down on page eleven of Julian's discharge book with a loud, final, echoing thud.

Beneath the purple seal, Artie wrote in bold, clear cursive script:

*VOYAGE COMPLETE // ST. JOHN'S TO HORTA // CONDUCT: EXEMPLARY // ABILITY: MASTER RATING.*

He signed his name: *Arthur Vance, Master.*

Inspector Duarte looked at Artie's master's stamp. He looked at the bold black signature, then looked at Julian Kross's trembling hands.

The Portuguese customs officer had worked the Atlantic docks for thirty-five years. He knew the difference between a criminal running from the law and a seaman who had paid his passage in salt, sweat, and blood.

Inspector Duarte picked up his own brass stamp, inked it, and slammed it down beside Artie's seal.

"Clearance granted," the inspector said, closing the booklet and sliding it across the counter into Julian's hands. "Welcome to Portugal, Mr. Ward."

Julian took the blue discharge book. His fingers closed around the pebblestone cover, his chest shaking with a silent, overwhelming sob of deliverance.

"Thank you, sir," Julian whispered, looking at the inspector, then turning to Artie with eyes that held a debt that no money on earth could ever repay.

"Get your gear," Artie said, clapping him gently on the shoulder. "The café across the street has cold beer and hot steak sandwiches. Tom is already at the table."

They gathered around the salon table at ten in the morning to execute the final documents.

Fonseca laid out the delivery protocol, three copies printed on heavy bond paper with the broker's seal at the masthead. The document certified that sailing vessel Cormorant had been delivered from Hamilton, Bermuda, to Horta, Faial, Azores, in seaworthy condition, with all inventory intact and no outstanding maritime liens or structural damage.

Artie reviewed every paragraph with deliberate care. He checked the recorded engine hours, the fuel inventory, the safety equipment tally, and the agreed delivery fee balance.

"Everything is in order," Artie said.

He took his pen and signed his name on the line marked Master / Delivery Captain. Clara signed as First Mate, and Noah signed as Deck Officer. Fonseca affixed his surveyor's seal beside Artie's signature and signed on behalf of the purchaser.

"The wire transfer for your balance and crew stipends was released by the escrow agent in Lisbon twenty minutes ago," Fonseca said, handing Artie his duplicate copy of the signed protocol. "Here is your receipt."

Artie folded the paper and slipped it into his inner jacket pocket.

"The keys, Mr. Fonseca," Artie said, sliding the brass ring across the mahogany table. "Ignition key, companionway lock, and the padlock for the forepeak chain locker."

Fonseca picked up the keys. "Thank you, Captain Vance. She is a fine boat. You brought her through in good shape."

"She brought us through," Artie said calmly. "Take care of her."

# Chapter 32: The Rising Sirens

The kitchen of the Sutton homestead was lit by a single kerosene lantern on the cedar table. The power lines along the highway had snapped at midnight, plunging the entire Redgate valley into total electrical blackout.

The evidence drum sat in the center of the oilcloth table. Beside it lay the severed timer, the sealed bags containing the iPad and the GPS receiver, and the pigskin-bound 1912 water register.

Claire Sutton stood at the wood-fired stove, heating water in a black cast-iron kettle. Outside, the dust storm was peaking: sixty-mile-an-hour gusts that rattled the sash windows and drove fine red grit through the keyholes.

Evelyn sat by the window with her satellite phone pressed to her ear.

"This is Senior Investigator Evelyn Brand," she said. Her voice was steady, clear against the howling wind. "Speaking to Detective Senior Sergeant Alan Croft, State Crime Command."

"Go ahead, Evelyn," Croft's voice came through the satellite link, crisp and loud. "We're parked at the Redgate railway siding with four highway patrol cars and two forensics vans."

"We have physical custody of the intact incendiary device," Evelyn said. "Eight pounds of synthetic polyol ester compound with aluminum octanoate stabilizer, complete with bridge-wire electronic match and radio frequency telemetry relay. We have recovered Kroll's field tablet showing written tasking directives from Warren Holst sent at nine-fourteen this evening."

A short pause on the line. The sound of Croft transmitting on his vehicle radio.

"Where is Kroll?"

"He fled on foot into the dry creek bed heading north toward Apex Block Nine," Evelyn said. "He is unarmed as far as I could observe, wearing dark field clothing. His vehicle is parked in the wash sixty yards north of the culvert."

"Stay inside the house, Evelyn," Croft said. "Keep the doors locked. We are rolling now."

The satellite call ended.

Evelyn set the phone on the table. She took a mug of black tea from Claire, wrapping her cold hands around the hot ceramic.

"The police are coming," Claire said.

"Croft has four cars," Evelyn said. "They will seal the highway and the boundary road within ten minutes. Kroll has nowhere to go on foot in this dust."

Five minutes passed in silence. The tea was bitter and hot.

Then, faint at first beneath the screaming of the northwesterly wind, a low wail rose from the western ridge.

The sound grew, splitting into three distinct, high-pitched warbles that echoed down the dry creek bed.

Through the kitchen blinds, the first flashes of blue and red light appeared on the crest of the hill. The emergency strobes cut through the swirling red dust clouds, painting the weatherboards of the homestead in sweeping arcs of crimson and cobalt.

The gravel outside crunched under heavy radial tires as four police cruisers swung into the homestead yard, their high beams illuminating the front veranda in blinding white light.

Claire looked at Evelyn across the table. Her eyes were bright with unshed tears, her chin steady.

As the first gray light of dawn broke over the Cocoparra Range, the distant wail of sirens drifted across the plain from the east. Two high-clearance highway patrol sedans turned off the bitumen onto the dirt approach road, their blue and red flashing lights reflecting against the dusty paddocks. Behind them came the heavy white tanker of the Corowa Rural Fire Service, driven by Captain Don Driscoll.

The police vehicles pulled into the yard in a sweeping circle, kicking up a rooster tail of red dust. Detective Inspector Marcus Vance stepped out of the lead sedan, wearing a tactical vest over his plainclothes suit. He was followed by two uniformed constables with sidearms drawn.

"Evelyn Brand?" Vance called out, walking toward the veranda where Evelyn stood beside her locked equipment cases.

"I have the suspect and the physical evidence secured, Inspector," Evelyn said, handing him the completed evidence register and the digital memory card from the surveillance camera. "Raymond Kroll is detained by the pump house. The chemical accelerant and the mechanical blower have been photographed and sealed in accordance with forensic protocols."

Vance reviewed the documents, nodding slowly as he took in the scope of the evidence. He signaled to the constables, who moved to the pump house, cautioned Kroll, and placed him in double-locked handcuffs.

Captain Driscoll climbed down from the tanker cab, his face lined with fatigue but his eyes bright. He looked at the sealed evidence drums in the back of the Land Cruiser, then spat onto the dry red earth.

"Well done, Evelyn," Driscoll said in a low gravel voice. "Looks like big money just hit a brick wall."

Detective Inspector Vance walked around the perimeter of the machinery shed with an evidence log in hand, inspecting the tracks left by Kroll's utility truck. Two forensic constables in blue overalls were setting up reflective markers along the tire impressions in the dry creek bed.

"We have two patrol units at the Griffith airstrip," Vance said, returning to the veranda where Evelyn was completing the evidence transfer documentation. "Apex has an eight-passenger corporate jet scheduled to depart for Melbourne at seven o'clock. The state director of public prosecutions has issued an emergency border alert to prevent any corporate officers from leaving the state."

Evelyn handed him the completed chain-of-custody transfer form. "The digital drive in Kroll's briefcase contains encrypted email exchanges between Kroll and Warren Holst going back three years. It proves that National Mutual's executive branch coordinated the underwriting timelines with the arson events."

Vance signed the receipt, his signature bold and decisive across the departmental seal. "The fraud squad in Sydney has been looking into Murrumbidgee Rural Finance for six months, but they couldn't penetrate the offshore holding structures. Your physical evidence is the key that unlocks the entire syndicate."

Beside the fence, Captain Driscoll's volunteer tanker crew were hosing down the perimeter grass with a light water mist to prevent any stray spark from igniting the dry scrub. Driscoll stood by the front fender of his truck, watching the police sedans escort Kroll out onto the Redgate road.

Two highway patrol officers stood guard beside Kroll's utility truck, sealing the doors with blue police tape. Detective Inspector Vance completed the initial inventory of the vehicle tray, recording the serial numbers of the electric generator and the chemical drums before ordering the vehicle towed to the police forensic compound in Griffith.

Captain Driscoll walked over to the Land Cruiser, resting his elbow on the window frame. "We have the brigade tanker stationed at the main canal gate, Evelyn. If anyone from Apex tries to access the diversion weir tonight, they'll be met by six volunteer firefighters with heavy hoses."

"Thank you, Don," Evelyn said. "The police have secured the crime scene and the prosecutors have the documents."

The highway patrol tow truck arrived at the gate, its amber warning lights rotating in the dawn twilight. The driver backed the flatbed toward Kroll's utility truck, hooking the winch cables around the front axle under the direction of Detective Inspector Vance.

Evelyn set down her tea mug. She stood up, picked up the evidence drum, and walked toward the front door.

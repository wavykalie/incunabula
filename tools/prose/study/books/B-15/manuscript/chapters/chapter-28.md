# Chapter 28: The Modus Operandi

The kitchen table was bare except for a hand-drawn diagram of the homestead's foundation grid.

Evelyn leaned over the paper, speaking in a low murmur that did not carry beyond the flyscreens.

"His method never changes," Evelyn said. "At Oakhaven, Coram Creek, and Merriwa, the ignition point was always located in the dead-air zone beneath the room containing the family safe or document chest."

Claire looked down at the diagram. "Beneath my father's study."

"Precisely," Evelyn said. "He enters through the crawlspace hatch on the western wall. The hatch is hidden from the road by the rain tanks. He crawls under the floor joists to the central brick chimney stump. That stump supports the main hearth, which means the timber bearers around it are thirty-year-old cured ironbark."

"Ironbark doesn't catch easily," Claire said.

"It doesn't catch from a match," Evelyn replied. "He uses two synthetic polyol ester blocks, four pounds each, molded into flexible rectangular cakes. He wedges them directly between the bearer and the subfloor tongue-and-groove boards. Then he inserts an electronic match into the center of the block."

"How does he trigger it?"

"A radio relay receiver tuned to a four-hundred-megahertz industrial telemetry band," Evelyn said. "He wires the relay to a twenty-minute delay countdown circuit. He closes the hatch, walks back to the creek bed, drives two miles out to the highway, and sends the coded detonation pulse from his vehicle."

"And the fire?"

"The gel ignites without an open flame at first," Evelyn said, her voice clinical and precise. "It produces an invisible thermal pulse that heats the ironbark joists to six hundred degrees in forty seconds. The moisture in the wood converts to steam instantly, rupturing the cell walls. By the time the flames break through the floorboards into the room, the entire floor joist grid is superheated."

"Total structural flashover," Claire whispered.

"In under three minutes," Evelyn said. "The ceiling drops into the crawlspace, feeding the draft. The corrugated iron roof collapses on top of the debris, sealing the heat inside like a Dutch oven. By the time the smoke plume rises above the scrub, the temperature inside the ruins is eleven hundred degrees. The paper deed in your study safe will char to carbon powder even if the steel safe remains intact."

"What do you want me to do?" Claire asked, closing the shotgun action with a muffled click.

"Stay inside the kitchen," Evelyn said. "Keep the shotgun on the table. Do not step onto the veranda until I signal with the green lens of my torch."

"If he draws a weapon, Evelyn, I will shoot him."

"If he draws a weapon, you shoot," Evelyn said. "But let him open the hatch first. Let him reach inside with the block."

The comparative analysis covered four distinct columns across the graph paper: Date, Location, Thermal Signature, and Mortgage Acceleration Status.

At Oakhaven, the fire had destroyed forty thousand square feet of winery sheds in February twenty-twenty-one. The thermal damage was concentrated along the southern footing, directly beneath the refrigeration compressors. The insurance settlement of four point two million dollars was disbursed within forty days, following Evelyn's own sign-off on an electrical failure. Within three months, Apex had converted the surrounding vineyard land into a corporate logistics hub.

At Merriwa, Frank Miller's weatherboard homestead was leveled on October fourteenth. The point of origin was located beneath the master bedroom joists, with forty-two millimeters of char depth recorded against the chimney breast. National Mutual was processing a total loss claim of one point eight million dollars. The senior water rights attached to the property were scheduled for transfer to Murrumbidgee Rural Finance on November thirtieth.

At Corowa, Tom Mercer's homestead was destroyed on October twenty-seventh. The physical markers matched Merriwa down to the millimeter: identical inverted cone geometry, identical eutectic melting of copper pipe into silica firebrick, identical absence of petroleum residues. The insurance claim of two point one million dollars was currently under review.

And here at the Sutton homestead, the fourth property in the chain, Portion Forty-Two carried an unencumbered 1914 Crown allocation for six hundred megaliters. The mortgage default deadline issued by Murrumbidgee Rural Finance expired in forty-eight hours. The stage was set for the final burn.

Evelyn laid out the physical evidence matrix on the kitchen table, using a colored ballpoint pen to connect each physical exhibit to its corresponding corporate financial transaction.

Beside the Merriwa file, she placed the title search for Portion Seventeen. On October fifteenth, twelve hours after the fire had leveled Frank Miller's homestead, an electronic caveat was lodged with the Land Titles Office in Sydney by Murrumbidgee Rural Finance, claiming an equitable mortgage interest over four hundred megaliters of high-security water rights.

Beside the Corowa file, she aligned the mortgage assignment deed for Tom Mercer's land. The document carried the signature of Warren Holst as managing director of the corporate finance vehicle. Under Clause Nine, all water licenses were automatically transferred to Apex Agro-Holdings upon default of the mortgage principal.

The pattern was mathematical, predictable, and relentless. The corporate conspirators did not rely on chance; they operated according to a systematic asset stripping schedule that converted regional fire events into liquid financial balance sheets.

Claire Sutton walked into the kitchen carrying two ceramic mugs of black tea, her work boots heavy on the pine floorboards. "If you take this to the police in Griffith, will they actually arrest Holst?"

"Holst is an executive director of a publicly listed corporation," Evelyn said, keeping her tone measured and objective. "He has political connections in Sydney and three commercial law firms on permanent retainer. But when the arson squad matches the chemical catalyst in Kroll's possession to the residue from Tom Mercer's fireplace, no lawyer in the country can explain away the physics."

Evelyn drew a red line across the timeline chart, connecting the date of the insurance policy renewal to the mortgage refinancing date. In every instance, Apex had required the farmer to increase their structural insurance coverage by fifty percent prior to executing the secondary mortgage deed, guaranteeing that the insurance payout would fully liquidate the debt.

Claire Sutton reviewed the comparative timeline chart, her eyes following the red line connecting the three fire events.

"They did this three times without anyone stopping them," Claire said, her voice shaking with quiet outrage. "Three families lost their homes, their land, and their water, while the executives in Sydney collected corporate bonuses for portfolio growth."

"They won't do it a fourth time, Claire," Evelyn replied, rolling up the chart and securing it with an elastic band.

Beside the kitchen window, Evelyn checked the battery level on her thermal imaging camera. The display showed eighty-two percent charge, providing four hours of continuous monitoring time. Outside in the darkness, the wind gusted against the weatherboards with the dry heat of an open oven.

She picked up her tactical torch, checked the thumb switch, and slipped through the rear wire door into the darkness of the yard.

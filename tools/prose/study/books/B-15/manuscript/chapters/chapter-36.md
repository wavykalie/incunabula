# Chapter 36: The Executive Warrant

The fourth floor of National Mutual's regional headquarters was surrounded by blue plastic evidence crates when Evelyn arrived on Monday morning.

Six uniformed officers from the Riverina property crime squad stood in the corridor outside the executive suite. Two federal ASIC investigators in dark business suits were inside the corner office, labeling filing cabinets with yellow evidence tape.

Warren Holst sat behind his mahogany desk.

His chalk-stripe jacket was hanging on the back of his chair. His shirtsleeves were rolled up to his forearms, revealing pale, freckled skin. His rimless titanium spectacles lay flat on the leather blotter beside his passport and a stack of printed airline itineraries to Singapore.

Detective Inspector Vance stood beside the credenza, reading from a legal document in a clear, flat voice.

"Warrant issued under section 67 of the ASIC Act and section 354 of the Crimes Act, authorizing the seizure of all electronic computing devices, telecommunications records, and correspondence relating to Apex Agro-Holdings and Kroll Thermal Consulting."

Holst did not speak. His face was gray, the skin around his eyes sagging in deep folds.

He looked up as Evelyn stepped into the doorway.

His gaze rested on her face for five seconds. There was no anger in his eyes, no corporate condescension, only the dull, flat exhaustion of a bureaucrat who had realized the accounts would never balance again.

"Your desk was cleared on Friday evening, Evelyn," Holst said, his voice a low rasp.

"I know," she said.

"You could have had Melbourne," he murmured, looking down at his manicured fingernails. "You could have had the national underwriting directorship within three years. You threw away twenty years of seniority for eighty acres of dry dirt."

"Those eighty acres had people living on them," she said.

Holst let out a short, dry sound that was not quite a laugh.

"People don't matter to the market, Evelyn. The market only sees capital and yield. If it wasn't Apex, it would have been an offshore investment trust from Singapore or Toronto. In twenty years, there won't be a single family farm left between here and the Murray. The water will belong to international superannuation funds, and you will be retired in a suburban flat wondering why you bothered."

"Perhaps," Evelyn said. "But they will have to buy it legally."

An ASIC investigator disconnected the power cable from Holst's desktop computer. The two monitors went black with a sharp static pop. The investigator slipped the computer tower into an antistatic nylon bag and sealed it with a plastic zip tie.

"Mr. Holst," Vance said, stepping forward with a pair of steel handcuffs. "Please stand up and place your hands behind your back."

Holst stood up slowly. His chair tilted with its familiar hydraulic sigh.

In the fourth-floor executive suite of the National Mutual building in Wagga Wagga, two plainclothes detectives from the financial crimes squad stood beside Warren Holst's desk.

Holst sat upright in his high-backed burgundy leather chair, his hands resting flat on the polished green blotter. His silk tie was perfectly knotted, but his face had drained to the color of wet chalk. On the table before him lay a formal notice of executive suspension signed by the chairman of the board in Melbourne, accompanied by a Commonwealth search warrant under Section 3E of the Crimes Act.

"Your company car, corporate access cards, and encrypted telecommunications tokens are revoked immediately, Mr. Holst," Detective Sergeant Miller said, placing a pair of clear evidence sleeves on the desk. "You are instructed to vacate the premises within twenty minutes. You are not permitted to access any computer terminals, filing cabinets, or internal record volumes."

Holst did not answer. He picked up his tortoiseshell reading glasses, folded the arms with deliberate care, and slid them into his breast pocket. His hands trembled slightly, a faint, rhythmic twitch against his gray wool sleeve.

Evelyn Brand stood in the doorway of the boardroom, her arms folded across her equipment vest. Holst looked up, his pale gray-blue eyes meeting hers across the expanse of polished jarrah.

"You have destroyed an institution that has underwritten this state for eighty years, Evelyn," Holst said, his voice reduced to a thin, strained whisper.

"National Mutual did not burn those farms, Warren," Evelyn replied without heat. "You did."

Inside the executive boardroom in Wagga Wagga, two security guards in uniform stood beside the filing cabinets, supervising the forensic audit team as they boxed Warren Holst's correspondence archives.

The corporate crest of National Mutual on the southern wall had been taken down, leaving a pale rectangular patch on the walnut paneling. On the long jarrah table, twelve cardboard archive cartons were stacked in neat rows, sealed with police evidence tape.

Evelyn Brand signed the final property receipt for the regional claims register. Beside her, the interim managing director appointed by the Melbourne board looked exhausted, his tie loosened and his shirtsleeves rolled to the elbows.

"The board has authorized full financial restitution for the affected policyholders," the interim director said, handing Evelyn an official executive minute. "National Mutual will rebuild the homesteads at Merriwa and Corowa at full replacement cost, with no deductible applied. We are also funding the legal costs to restore the historical water rights to the family tenures."

Evelyn looked at the executive minute. The corporate language was polite, contrite, and carefully calculated to limit future shareholder liability.

"Restitution does not bring back thirty years of labor, Director," Evelyn said in a level voice. "But it allows them to plant again when the rain comes."

The interim managing director signed the formal referral to the Australian Securities and Investments Commission. "National Mutual will cooperate fully with the joint task force. We are appointing an independent forensic accounting firm to review every commercial fire claim settled across the agricultural division over the past five years."

The interim managing director bowed his head in acknowledgment. "National Mutual has issued an unconditional apology to the families involved. We have established a twenty-million-dollar community restoration fund to replant the scorched orchards and rehabilitate the irrigation infrastructure."

The interim managing director placed the company seal on the legal settlement agreement, handing the signed documents to the regional land board representative. The agreement formally nullified every mortgage foreclosure initiated by Murrumbidgee Rural Finance across the Riverina district.

He turned toward the wall, offered his wrists to the detective, and stared through the sealed plate-glass window at the dry Murrumbidgee River below.

# Chapter 26: The Creek Bed Approach

The radio scanner chirped at seven-forty.

A burst of squelch broke through the speaker, followed by two clicks of a microphone key on the private UHF frequency.

*Unit Four to Base. Moving into position at Sector West.*

The voice was flat, educated, completely devoid of regional accent.

Evelyn raised her thermal binoculars to the louver slats.

Two miles north, along the dry irrigation ditch that separated Claire's boundary from Apex Orchard Block Nine, the white utility truck had appeared. It moved without headlights, its tires rolling slowly through the sandy silt of the dry canal. In the infrared display, its engine block glowed dull orange through the radiator grille.

The vehicle reached the western wash of the dry creek bed.

It turned south, slipping down the eroded riverbank into the shadow of the dead river oaks. The creek was dry sand and limestone gravel, fifteen feet below the paddock level. A vehicle driving through the bottom was completely hidden from the homestead's ground-floor windows.

Evelyn pressed the button on her optical rangefinder.

*Distance: 1,420 meters. Bearing: 284 degrees magnetic.*

The truck stopped beneath the overhanging roots of a dead red gum.

The engine cut out immediately. The cooling exhaust pipe crackled with thermal contraction in the hot wind.

Through the high-magnification lens, Evelyn watched the driver step out onto the dry gravel. He wore dark tactical trousers, leather lace-up boots, and a canvas rucksack slung across both shoulders.

He walked to the rear tailgate, unlatched the tonneau cover, and lifted out a padded aluminum flight case. He set the case on the tailgate, unlocked the latches, and removed three rectangular packages wrapped in yellow polyethylene moisture wrap.

He slipped the packages into the rucksack.

He closed the case, secured the tonneau cover, and stepped into the scrub.

He did not walk in the center of the creek bed where loose gravel would clatter underfoot. He stayed along the western bank, stepping on dry red clay shelves and rock ledges, moving with steady, balanced strides.

Evelyn clicked her handheld receiver.

The camera monitors in the attic showed the feed from Camera Three, mounted in the peppercorn tree forty yards from the boundary fence. The black-flash infrared LEDs illuminated the man as he climbed the creek bank.

His face was bare: square jaw, close-cropped gray hair, thin-rimmed spectacles held in place by a black neoprene sports strap.

The camera's internal optical recognition software captured the frame, stamping it with date, time, and coordinates:

*2026-03-27 19:54:12 UTC+11. LAT: -33.4182, LON: 145.8921. TARGET IDENTIFIED: KROLL, RAYMOND P.*

Evelyn watched him stop at the sheep gate.

He did not lift the rusted chain latch. He stepped onto the bottom wooden rail, swung his right leg over the top wire with practiced agility, and dropped softly onto the red sand on the house side of the fence. He paused for forty seconds in the shadow of the dead pepper tree, scanning the dark windows of the homestead with a handheld monocular.

Satisfied that the house was dark, he adjusted the shoulder straps of his rucksack and began his final approach across the dry horse paddock.

Evelyn moved along the sandy bed of Mirrool Creek, where the river gums hung limp in the dry night wind. Her boots made no sound in the deep, powdery silt. Every thirty yards, she paused beside an exposed root wad to scan the boundary fence line with her night-vision monocular.

Beside the boundary gate of Portion Forty-Two, she discovered fresh tire impressions pressed into the dried mud of the ditch. She knelt on one knee, pulled a folding steel rule from her thigh pocket, and measured the tread width. Two hundred and sixty-five millimeters across the shoulder. The tread pattern exhibited the distinct staggered lug configuration of an all-terrain light truck tire, with prominent sipping blocks along the outer edge.

She extracted three casting frames from her pack and set them over the clearest sections of the impression. In a shallow plastic bowl, she mixed four hundred grams of dental stone with bottled water from her canteen, stirring the slurry until it reached the consistency of thick cream. She poured the mixture into the casting frames, watching the white liquid fill every crevice of the tire track.

Ten yards farther along the fence line, where the barbed wire crossed an old timber floodgate, she found a small piece of dark fabric snagged on a rusted barb. She lifted the thread with her dissection forceps and placed it inside a glassine envelope. The fibers were dark navy synthetic fleece, identical to the corporate uniform vests worn by field engineers at Apex Agro-Holdings.

Evelyn paused beneath the overhanging roots of a giant river red gum, checking the dry creek bed for any sign of recent disturbance. In the pale moonlight, the cracked gray clay of the channel bottom was covered in a fine layer of dry eucalyptus leaves that rattled like paper with every gust of wind.

She reached into her equipment bag and pulled out three miniature wireless trail cameras equipped with invisible infrared flash arrays. She strapped the first camera to the trunk of an ancient box tree, positioning the lens to cover the cattle grid where the dirt track crossed Portion Forty-Two.

She mounted the second camera four feet above the ground on a rusted strainer post, angling the optical sensor directly toward the timber bridge over the canal. The third camera went into the fork of a dead pepper tree overlooking the eastern crawlspace ventilation hatch.

Each camera was synchronized to transmit high-resolution encrypted still frames directly to her field tablet over a private local network. She tested the wireless handshake with her handheld monitor, watching the digital signal indicator lock onto three solid bars.

She wiped a streak of alkaline dust from her face with her sleeve. If an intruder approached the house from any direction, their movements would be captured in five-megapixel resolution with exact GPS and timestamp overlays, creating an unbreakable chain of visual evidence for the prosecution.

Beside the old stock ramp, Evelyn discovered three empty cardboard boxes labeled with the logo of an industrial electrical supplier in Wagga Wagga. The boxes had contained porcelain terminal blocks and ceramic wire nuts, components commonly used in high-temperature heating installations. She photographed the packaging and bagged the cardboard for fingerprint processing.

Beside the dry creek bed, Evelyn inspected a barbed wire fence where two strands had been severed with mechanical bolt cutters. The freshly cut steel wire was bright silver against the rusted fence posts, showing no trace of oxidation.

She pulled her caliper from her pocket and measured the cut angle on the wire ends: forty-five degrees, with distinctive shear teeth marks characteristic of an eight-inch compound lever cutter. She collected two clipped wire fragments for metallurgical tool mark comparison.

In the dry creek bed, Evelyn found the imprint of a heavy steel pry bar where someone had forced open the padlock on the boundary floodgate. She took a silicone casting of the tool marks on the rusted hasp, noting the distinctive notch on the prying edge that would match the tools in Kroll's utility truck.

Evelyn checked the radio transceiver in her vest pocket, switching the unit to low-power tactical mode. The squelch circuit clicked twice in her ear piece. Donald Vance and his two tactical officers had arrived at their observation post near the cemetery turnoff.

She checked the time on her analog watch. It was two-forty in the morning. According to the atmospheric data from the Bureau of Meteorology, the surface wind had shifted from east to southwest. It brought a cold front that would push across the river plains before dawn.

Kroll would want the fire established before the change arrived. Once the southwest wind hit forty knots, no rural fire service tanker in the district could stop a fire front in dry grass.

Evelyn unholstered her inspection torch, checked the lanyard around her wrist, and began her quiet descent down the attic ladder.

# Chapter 34: The Decryption Table

By nine in the morning, the kitchen table of the Sutton homestead had been converted into an electronic command post.

Two digital forensic specialists from the State Electronic Crime Directorate were seated on wooden kitchen chairs, their ruggedized field servers connected to Kroll's iPad via high-speed hardware bypass cables. A portable laser printer sat on the breadboard, humming with a steady mechanical tone as it processed output queues.

Detective Inspector Vance leaned over the technician's shoulder.

"The encryption was broken at eight-twenty," the technician said, typing rapidly on a mechanical keyboard. "The suspect used an AES-256 container, but the recovery key was stored in his personal cloud keychain."

A database window opened on the monitor.

The database contained four hundred and twenty records organized under the header: *PROJECT BASIN CONSOLIDATION — SPECIAL CLAIMS.*

Evelyn looked at the display from behind Claire Sutton's chair.

The records were structured as commercial purchase orders issued by Southern Basin Capital and Basin Holdings Alpha. Each record was linked to a specific cadastral lot number, a settlement target date, and a fee allocation:

*Target: Lot 82, Parish of Oakhaven. Execution: 14/02/2021. Fee: $45,000. Status: Completed. Title surrendered.*
*Target: Lot 211, Parish of Coram. Execution: 19/11/2025. Fee: $60,000. Status: Completed. Title surrendered.*
*Target: Lot 14, Parish of Merriwa. Execution: 06/03/2026. Fee: $75,000. Status: Completed. Title pending review.*
*Target: Lot 304, Parish of Redgate. Execution: 27/03/2026. Fee: $90,000. Status: Aborted.*

"Look at the authorization column," Evelyn pointed.

Beside each execution order was an alphanumeric approval code. The code began with the prefix *NM-EXEC-WAGGA*, followed by Warren Holst's corporate employee identification number: *004821-WH.*

"Holst authorized the payments directly from the branch underwriting contingency fund," Vance said, his voice flat. "Disguised as third-party risk mitigation consulting fees."

"And the water?" Claire asked, her fingers resting on the kitchen table.

The technician clicked a second tab on the screen.

The tab displayed a GIS map of the Murrumbidgee and Lachlan irrigation districts. The four target properties were highlighted in bright yellow. As the technician zoomed in, blue lines connected each property to the central intake channels of Apex Agro-Holdings.

Attached to the file was an executive memorandum dated January 12, 2026, addressed to the Apex investment committee in Melbourne:

*Subject: Permanent Senior Entitlement Aggregation.*

*Upon completion of Phase Two structural retirements, the portfolio will consolidate twenty-eight thousand megalitres of uncurtailable 1912 statutory water entitlements. Projected net asset appreciation on the secondary water exchange: $142,000,000. Underwriting liability fully hedged via National Mutual syndicate policy.*

The laser printer on the breadboard shuddered.

It began to spit out crisp white sheets of paper, the pages falling into the wire catch tray with a steady, rhythmic rustle. Eighty-four pages of emails, transaction receipts, bank routing numbers, and cadastral maps.

Vance gathered the first stack of pages. He tapped them square against the wooden table edge and dropped them into an evidence binder.

Donald Vance called up the decrypted directory tree from Raymond Kroll's external solid-state drive onto the main wall monitor. The folder structure was arranged with meticulous engineering precision: *Client Accounts / Apex Agro-Holdings / Risk Engineering / Thermal Amortization Schedules*.

Inside the primary folder lay twenty-four separate sub-directories, each designated by a parish land portion number.

Evelyn leaned over the workstation, pointing to the spreadsheet titled *Consolidated Water Acquisition Plan: Murrumbidgee Southern Branch*. The columns were color-coded in green, amber, and red.

"Every property listed here carried an unencumbered pre-1915 Crown water right," she explained to Vance. "Column D lists the original landholders: Miller, Mercer, Sutton, Callan, Henderson. Column F records the date when Murrumbidgee Rural Finance issued their initial mortgage refinancing facility. And Column H lists the projected structural retirement date."

Vance scrolled down to row twelve. Beside the entry for Portion Forty-Two (Claire Sutton), the cell was highlighted in bright red. The notation read: *Intervention window scheduled for November 4 to November 6. Meteorological forecast confirms sustained forty-knot northwesterly airflow, humidity under eight percent. Target: complete structural loss, zero volatile hydrocarbon signature.*

Attached to the spreadsheet were twelve encrypted PDF documents containing electronic email correspondence between Raymond Kroll and Warren Holst. In an email dated October third, Holst had written: *Ensure that the Merriwa file is closed without delay. Our London underwriters require clean claim clearance before the quarterly board audit. Proceed with the southern branch allocations on schedule.*

The evidence established direct corporate complicity. Warren Holst had not merely turned a blind eye to fraudulent claims; he had directed the forensic timetable and financed the operational deployments through corporate consultancy retainers.

The forensic computer laboratory in the regional police headquarters was lit by the blue glare of four dual-monitor workstations. A specialized digital evidence technician was extracting raw disk images from Kroll's encrypted solid-state drive, running bit-level verification checksums against the police evidence server.

Donald Vance gestured toward a folder labeled *Syndicate Communications 2024-2026*.

"Look at the metadata on these email headers," Vance said, adjusting his glasses. "The emails between Kroll and Warren Holst were routed through an encrypted mail server located in Switzerland, using PGP keys generated on Holst's personal laptop. In an email sent two days before the Mercer fire, Holst confirmed the exact policy limits and the mortgage default timeline."

Evelyn reviewed the text on the screen. The language was cold, detached, and bureaucratic. There were no references to fire or arson; instead, the correspondence used administrative euphemisms: *accelerated portfolio rationalization*, *thermal asset retirement*, *collateral reallocation*.

Under the heading *Attachment B: Projected Yield*, a detailed financial spreadsheet demonstrated that the consolidation of twelve family water rights into Apex's centralized almond plantations would increase annual corporate earnings by fourteen point two million dollars.

"They calculated the destruction down to the dollar," Evelyn said, her voice dropping into a hard, clinical whisper. "They balanced the cost of Kroll's consulting retainers against the capital value of pre-1915 Crown water allocations."

Vance printed the email threads on a high-speed laser printer, stamping each page with the red seal of the State Crime Command. "The Commonwealth Director of Public Prosecutions is reviewing these documents right now. An emergency freeze order has been lodged against all bank accounts associated with Murrumbidgee Rural Finance."

Donald Vance brought up the banking transaction records onto the secondary split-screen. The banking ledger detailed forty-eight wire transfers executed between Murrumbidgee Rural Finance and Kroll Risk Engineering Consultants over a thirty-six-month period.

"Every wire transfer coincided with an insurance claims settlement," Vance explained, pointing a pen at the transaction dates. "On March twelfth, twenty-twenty-one, four days after National Mutual disbursed four point two million dollars for the Oakhaven Winery claim, Kroll received an executive consultancy fee of one hundred and twenty thousand dollars. On October twentieth, three days after the Merriwa claim was preliminarily approved, Kroll received eighty-five thousand dollars."

Evelyn compared the wire transfer amounts with the consulting invoices recovered from Kroll's office. "The payments were disguised as technical risk assessments and environmental impact studies. But the contract schedules carried performance bonuses payable upon the formal surrender of the underlying agricultural land titles."

Vance generated a cryptographic hash of the entire digital directory, locking the evidence file with a SHA-256 checksum to guarantee admissibility in federal court. "The evidence of bribery and commercial conspiracy is irrefutable. Warren Holst and Raymond Kroll will be indicted together under joint enterprise provisions."

Donald Vance printed out the final batch of decrypted emails from the Swiss server. The correspondence showed that Holst had instructed Kroll to expedite the Sutton operation before the regional parliamentary inquiry into water trading commenced in December.

"They were running out of time," Vance observed. "They knew that if the parliamentary committee subpoenaed the title records, the entire mortgage acceleration scheme would unravel."

Donald Vance highlighted a paragraph in an email sent by Warren Holst on September twenty-eighth: *The board has approved the allocation of five hundred thousand dollars for regional risk mitigation retainers. Disburse the funds according to the operational schedule.*

The email established that Holst had authorized the payments to Kroll from National Mutual's executive contingency accounts, bypassing the standard claims audit process.

Vance opened a second cardboard archival box and lifted out a sheaf of corporate bank statements from the Commercial Bank of Sydney. The statements recorded twenty-four wire transfers of twenty-five thousand dollars each, sent from an offshore holding company in Singapore to an account registered in Raymond Kroll's name.

The wire transfers corresponded to the precise dates of the fires across the Murrumbidgee irrigation area over the previous three years. In each transaction memo, the sender had entered the reference notation: *Technical Risk Mitigation Consultation*.

"Holst was clever," Vance said. He laid the bank records across the wooden desk. "He didn't pay Kroll directly from National Mutual. He channeled the funds through an agricultural research subsidiary in Singapore. But the Singapore entity had only one registered director: Warren Holst."

Evelyn examined the transfer timestamps. "Look at the payment on May fourteenth, 2021. That was the morning after Frank Miller's woolshed burned down. The wire transfer was executed at nine-fifteen in the morning, two hours before Frank even reported the fire to the local brigade."

"Premeditation and direct coordination," Vance said. He stamped the page with the state prosecution evidence seal. "The Crown Solicitor will file sixty counts of fraud, arson, and criminal conspiracy against Holst and his executive committee before the close of business tomorrow."

Vance tapped the keyboard of the forensic workstation, displaying a series of financial spreadsheets extracted from Holst's encrypted hard drive. The files were organized in folders titled *Riverina Water Consolidation Project - Phase Three*.

The financial models projected an aggregate return of forty-eight million dollars following the completion of the northern irrigation channel. Every acre of land acquired through foreclosure was slated for immediate conversion to high-density almond plantation, with water entitlements pooled into an offshore investment trust.

"The entire operation was designed around the drought timetable," Vance said, scrolling through the executive presentation slides. "They calculated that three consecutive years of rainfall deficit would exhaust the working capital of ninety percent of independent farmers in the valley. The fires were used selectively against the few holdouts who refused to sell."

Evelyn studied the spreadsheet columns, noting the actuarial risk models that calculated the statistical probability of criminal investigation. The syndicate had estimated the likelihood of forensic detection at less than four percent, based on the assumption that regional fire investigators lacked the analytical equipment required to identify synthetic chemical accelerants.

"They underestimated the physical evidence," Evelyn said. "Thermal physics does not negotiate with corporate balance sheets."

The forensic accountants from the state fraud squad worked through the remaining financial databases on the secure network server. Every dollar paid out by National Mutual's executive branch was mapped directly to an acquisition account managed by the offshore consortium.

"They had twenty more properties targeted for the upcoming fire season," Vance remarked, pointing to a regional map tagged with red priority pins. "If we had not intercepted Kroll tonight, the consortium would have completed the complete takeover of the Murrumbidgee irrigation district by Christmas."

The detective inspector printed the final asset-tracing diagram on the wide-format plotter. The diagram mapped forty-seven real estate titles, fourteen offshore shell companies, and two million megaliters of river water entitlements back to the single controlling trust agreement executed by Warren Holst in Sydney. Every step in the paper trail was verified by banking records, land registry stamps, and signed underwriting approvals.

"That's enough to indict the entire regional executive committee," Vance said.

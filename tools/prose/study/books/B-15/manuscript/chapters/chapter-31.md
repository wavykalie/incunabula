# Chapter 31: The Securing of Evidence

Evelyn did not pursue him into the dark scrub.

Running through dead mallee scrub in thirty-knot gales with zero night vision was an amateur's mistake that ended in broken ankles or a dry well shaft.

She swung the flashlight beam back to the crawlspace hatch.

The eight-pound chemical brick sat undisturbed on the red gravel. Beside it lay Kroll's canvas rucksack, its heavy brass zipper open to the dust.

She pulled two pairs of powder-free purple nitrile gloves from her pocket and snapped them over her hands.

She knelt beside the incendiary device.

The casing was aluminum, six inches square, fastened to the chemical block with high-strength fiber tape. On the top panel sat a military-grade Omron electronic interval timer with a four-digit red LED display, wired to a four-channel radio frequency decoder. Two red Teflon-coated lead wires ran from the relay output directly into an aluminum ignition well embedded in the center of the polyol ester block.

Inside the well sat a bridge-wire pyrotechnic match.

Evelyn pulled a pair of insulated wire cutters from her vest. With two clean, precise cuts, she severed the lead wires four inches from the timer housing, separating the electrical power source from the chemical charge.

She took an airtight five-gallon forensic evidence drum from the veranda steps. She lifted the chemical block with both hands. It was heavy, dense, cold to the touch through the nitrile rubber, smelling faintly of synthetic motor oil and bitter almonds.

She lowered the block into the drum, sealed the locking ring with a threaded bolt, and applied a tamper-evident serial seal across the rim: *NSW POLICE FORENSIC EVIDENCE — 992-BRAVO-01.*

Next, she turned to the rucksack.

Inside the front pocket was an Apple iPad Pro encased in a drop-proof OtterBox housing, along with a ruggedized Trimble GPS surveying receiver. The iPad screen was locked with a four-digit PIN, but the notifications displayed on the home screen were visible under her white torchlight:

*Message from: W. Holst (21:14).*
*Subject: Sutton confirmation.*
*Confirm deployment window before 0100. Audit team arrives Wagga 0800 Monday.*

Evelyn photographed the screen with her digital camera, capturing the sender address, the timestamp, and the text.

She pulled out a second sealed polyethylene bag and secured the iPad, the GPS unit, and Kroll's field notebook, which contained handwritten sketches of the foundation piers at Oakhaven, Merriwa, and Redgate.

She stood up. The wind was howling through the house framing, but the air around the foundation was clean.

The green light on her handheld receiver flickered once.

Claire Sutton stepped out from the kitchen door, holding the shotgun pointed at the ground.

"Did you get it?" Claire asked.

Evelyn held up the sealed evidence drum. The blue tamper seal caught the torchlight.

Evelyn knelt beside the foundation hatch, wearing clean nitrile gloves. With practiced forensic care, she disconnected the wiring connector between the timer assembly and the ceramic glow plug, preventing any electrical contact that could trigger ignition.

She lifted the amber gel jar from the crawlspace, holding it by the glass rim to preserve latent fingerprints. She sealed the container inside a heavy polyethylene evidence bag, writing the sample identification code across the white label panel: *EXHIBIT A-419: CHEMICAL ACCELERANT / RECOVERED SUTTON SITE*.

Beside the hatch, she photographed the portable blower fan and the flexible ducting in situ, capturing the manufacturer serial numbers stamped into the motor housing. She bagged the lithium battery pack, the digital countdown timer, and Kroll's canvas tool bag containing spare ceramic elements and a container of chemical flux.

Claire Sutton stood guard over Kroll, who sat on the concrete bench beside the pump house with his hands resting on his knees. Kroll remained completely silent, staring out across the dry paddocks toward the distant glow of the almond processing plant.

Evelyn completed the formal chain of custody document on her clipboard, recording the exact time, ambient temperature, GPS coordinates, and witness details. Every physical link was documented in compliance with Section 138 of the Evidence Act.

When she finished, she packed the evidence drums into the rear cargo compartment of the Land Cruiser, securing the steel padlock with her key.

Evelyn completed the forensic recovery with deliberate procedural thoroughness. She took twenty-four digital photographs of the equipment array before touching any component, recording the serial numbers of the timer module and the electric blower motor.

She used a stainless steel spatula to take three control samples from the amber gel remaining in the delivery tray, sealing them into glass sample vials with Teflon-lined caps. Each vial was numbered, dated, and signed across the tamper-evident seal.

Beside the foundation wall, she recovered Kroll's clipboard, which contained a detailed blueprint of the Sutton homestead subfloor layout. Handwritten notes in blue ink identified the exact placement of the delivery ducting beneath the master bedroom and the kitchen stove, with estimated burn times and wind velocity corridors calculated in margins.

She bagged Kroll's mobile telephone and his handheld GPS receiver, which displayed coordinates for the Mercer homestead, the Miller farm, and the Oakhaven Winery in its recent destination log.

She placed the sealed evidence containers into the heavy aluminum transport trunks in the cargo bed of her Land Cruiser, locking each hasp with a numbered brass padlock.

Claire Sutton stood beside the vehicle, the shotgun resting across her forearm. The eastern horizon was beginning to show a thin sliver of pale gray light above the silhouette of the Cocoparra Range.

"It is over, isn't it?" Claire asked in a low voice.

"The fire is stopped," Evelyn said, closing the tailgate with a firm, solid latch. "Now the law begins."

Evelyn walked around the perimeter of the foundation crawlspace with a ultraviolet forensic inspection lamp. Under the narrow-band 365-nanometer wavelength, the spatter patterns of the chemical accelerant fluoresced in bright lime-green flecks along the red clay soil.

She marked each fluorescent droplet with a yellow evidence flag, photographing the spatial distribution to reconstruct the trajectory of the chemical placement. Kroll had handled the delivery nozzle without a protective drip shield, leaving a continuous trail of chemical droplets from the tailgate of his truck directly to the subfloor ventilation hatch.

She extracted five soil core samples along the fluorescent trail using a sterilized stainless steel coring tube. She transferred each core into a clean glass vial, packed the vials in foam sleeves, and labeled them with the official case numbers.

Beside the water tank, she recovered Kroll's discarded work gloves. The fingers of the right glove were soaked in the amber formulation, providing direct physical linkage between the suspect and the incendiary apparatus.

She logged the gloves into the evidence register under Exhibit A-420, recording the manufacturer, size, and fiber composition.

Evelyn secured the chemical transport canisters in the back of the Land Cruiser, bolting the heavy cargo net across the top.

She walked around the perimeter of the homestead one final time with her handheld inspection lamp, verifying that no unsealed evidence containers or tool components remained in the dirt.

The red evidence flags stood out against the dark soil like tiny sentinels marking the boundary of a foiled crime.

Evelyn knelt on the rubber matting to secure the digital countdown timer. The liquid crystal display was frozen at thirty-two minutes and eighteen seconds. She removed the backup battery from the timer casing, neutralizing the internal memory clock while preserving the programmed countdown intervals.

She sealed the timer inside an anti-static evidence bag, writing the seizure details across the white label in permanent ink. Beside the foundation hatch, she collected three discarded zip ties that Kroll had used to secure the ventilation ducting, packing them in paper evidence envelopes.

Evelyn unscrewed the brass compression fitting on the copper feed line with her adjustable spanner. She worked with deliberate care to prevent spark generation. She capped the exposed end of the tube with a threaded brass plug to prevent any residual vapor from venting into the ambient air.

She placed the electronic timer module into an aluminum evidence case lined with anti-static foam. It displayed thirty-one minutes and twelve seconds. She attached a red tamper-evident security seal across the aluminum latch, and signed her name and badge number across the adhesive tape in black permanent ink.

Beside the rear veranda steps, Detective Vance stood over Kroll, who sat on the timber bench with his hands cuffed behind his back. The arrogance had drained from Kroll's posture; his shoulders were slumped forward, his gray hair plastered to his forehead with cold sweat.

"The ignition sequence was set for three-fifteen," Vance said as he examined the seized field notes. "Twenty minutes before the southwesterly wind change. By four o'clock, the entire homestead would have been reduced to white ash."

Evelyn walked down the veranda steps, holding the aluminum evidence case in her left hand. She looked at Kroll.

"Your math was correct down to the second, Raymond," Evelyn said. "Except you forgot that an inverted thermal plume leaves spall fractures that run counter to natural convection. A high-school physics student could read the burn patterns on Mercer's slab."

Kroll looked up at her, his eyes hollow in the glare of the police floodlights. He said nothing.

Evelyn opened her aluminum evidence kit on the tailboard of her station wagon. She donned fresh nitrile gloves before retrieving the glass sampling jars from the padded storage slots.

She transferred three soil scrapings from beneath the foundation vent into pre-labeled borosilicate glass containers, sealing each screw cap with Teflon tape to prevent volatile vapor migration. On the adhesive evidence label of each container, she inscribed the case designation, GPS coordinates, date, and time: *CR-2023-884, Lat -34.5218, Long 146.4102, 03:42 AM*.

Detective Vance approached the vehicle, carrying a heavy canvas tote bag containing Kroll's laptop, two mobile phones, and a spiral notebook filled with hand-drawn circuit schematics.

"The tactical team searched Kroll's utility truck," Vance reported. "They found a five-liter pressurized cylinder of industrial nitrogen in the rear tool chest, fitted with a high-pressure regulator and two solenoid control valves. The serial numbers match the Griffith delivery invoice."

Evelyn logged the cylinder into the primary evidence registry. "That completes the physical link between the acquisition of the chemical components and their deployment at the target site."

Detective Vance completed the custody manifest on his clipboard, checking off each seized artifact against the state evidence codes. He handed the carbon copy to Evelyn, who verified the serial numbers against her field log.

"We have two intact incendiary modules, three bags of soil samples showing accelerant saturation, and Kroll's encrypted personal computer," Vance said. "This is an unassailable prosecution package. The regional director of public prosecutions has already assigned two senior trial attorneys to manage the indictment."

Detective Vance placed his initials on the outer seal of the evidence drum, securing the locking ring with a steel cotter pin. He loaded the drum into the rear compartment of the unmarked police sedan, covering the case with a heavy canvas tarpaulin to protect the electronic components from the morning humidity.

Evelyn completed the final chain-of-custody transfer document on her clipboard. She handed the carbon copy to Detective Vance, who verified the serial numbers against the master evidence inventory.

Evelyn packed her digital calipers and sampling trowel into her canvas field pack. She secured the brass buckles. The physical weight of twelve hours of continuous forensic documentation settled across her shoulders.

Evelyn attached the official red evidence tag to the aluminum case handle. The tag bore the formal stamp of the New South Wales Police Forensic Services Branch, along with the case tracking number and the signatures of both inspecting officers. Every physical link in the chain of custody was sealed, recorded, and witnessed under state evidentiary procedure.

"All of it," Evelyn said.

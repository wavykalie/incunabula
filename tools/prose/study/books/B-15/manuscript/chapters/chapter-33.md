# Chapter 33: The Chain of Custody

The dust storm had blown out toward the Pacific by five in the morning, leaving a film of red grit over every horizontal surface. The dawn air was cold, dropping to twelve degrees as the southwesterly change pushed through from the snowfields.

In the driveway of the Sutton homestead, four police sedans and a forensic crime van stood with their engines idling. The smell of cold diesel exhaust mingled with the alkaline stench of settled silt.

Detective Inspector Ross Vance of the State Crime Command leaned against the hood of his unmarked Holden. He was sixty, with iron-gray hair cropped short and heavy bags beneath dark, bloodshot eyes. He held a clipboard with triplicate carbon-copy forms.

"Eight pounds of synthetic polyol ester," Vance read aloud from the receipt form. "One Omron interval timer. Two bridge-wire electronic matches. One Trimble receiver, and one Apple tablet belonging to Raymond Kroll."

Evelyn Brand stood on the other side of the hood. Her field boots were caked in red mud from the creek bed where she had guided two detectives to Kroll's abandoned utility truck two hours before dawn.

"The timer has been mechanically disconnected from the bridge wire," Evelyn said. "The chemical block is sealed inside the steel drum with tamper seal number 992-Bravo-01."

Vance signed the first sheet with a blue ballpoint pen. The metal tip tore slightly through the thin yellow carbon slip.

"We picked Kroll up at the canal pumping station at four-fifteen," Vance said, handing her the yellow copy. "He had walked four miles through the scrub in thirty-knot winds. His hands were full of cactus thorns, and he had thirty thousand dollars in hundred-dollar notes stuffed inside his socks."

"Did he talk?"

"He asked for his lawyer before the handcuffs were clicked," Vance said. "A senior partner at a commercial litigation firm on Castlereagh Street in Sydney. But he didn't wipe his tablet."

"The tablet is encrypted," Evelyn noted.

"It's a commercial iPad," Vance replied, tapping his clipboard against the car hood. "Our digital forensics unit in Parramatta has a hardware exploit for that model. We'll have the file system decrypted before lunchtime."

Across the yard, Claire Sutton was standing on the veranda rail, watching two forensic officers photograph the crawlspace opening with a high-intensity flash. Each pulse of white light lit up the foundation piers beneath the weatherboards.

Vance tore off the receipt slip and folded it into his notebook.

"You took a big risk, Evelyn. If that block had cooked off while you were standing by the water tank, you would have been vaporized."

"The ignition circuit was inert until the telemetry code was received," she said.

"Still," Vance murmured, looking at her through narrowed eyes. "Not many insurance assessors stand in the dark waiting for a professional torch."

"I am not an insurance assessor," Evelyn said.

Inside the regional police command facility in Griffith, the evidence processing room smelled of dry photocopy paper, industrial floor sealant, and instant coffee. Four fluorescent battens flickered overhead with a low sixty-cycle hum.

Detective Inspector Marcus Vance laid the polyethylene evidence bags across the stainless steel examination counter. Each bag was stamped with red tamper-evident tape and signed by Evelyn Brand in black permanent ink.

"Exhibit One: twenty-liter container of polydimethylsiloxane liquid intermediate," Vance read into his digital dictaphone, his voice flat and administrative. "Exhibit Two: portable high-output air blower, twelve-volt direct current, with flexible metallic delivery ducting. Exhibit Three: solid-state digital countdown timer wired to a ceramic heating element, recovered from the subfloor foundation crawlspace of Portion Forty-Two."

Evelyn stood beside the counter with her field notebook open, verifying each serial number against the property register. Her fingers were stiff with dried red clay and graphite dust.

"The chemical analysis from the state laboratory in Lidcombe will be completed by noon tomorrow," she stated for the record. "Dr. Chen's preliminary mass spectrometry confirms that the compound matches the exact chemical composition of the unburned residue recovered from the Mercer and Miller sites."

Vance signed the chain-of-custody transfer form and pushed the duplicate carbon copy across the steel table. "We have three detectives executing search warrants at Raymond Kroll's engineering office in Griffith right now. They've already seized his primary desktop computer, his drafting files, and two crates of technical journals."

Inside the secure property room of the Griffith police station, Evelyn supervised the cataloging of the recovered chemical evidence. The room was lined with heavy steel shelving holding evidence boxes from regional investigations across the Murrumbidgee district.

She placed each sample container on the calibrated electronic scale, recording the tare weight and net mass in the official property book. The plastic drum seized from Kroll's vehicle contained fourteen point eight kilograms of the liquid formulation, enough accelerant to ignite twelve domestic structures under optimal draft conditions.

Detective Inspector Vance observed from the steel desk, checking the entries against the sworn search warrants. "The chemical fingerprint matches the patent filings registered in Germany under an industrial holding company controlled by Apex. We have confirmed the delivery invoices from an chemical supply firm in Botany Bay, showing three separate shipments dispatched to Kroll's address over the past four months."

Evelyn affixed barcode tracking stickers to each container. The procedural discipline was second nature, built on twenty years of court testimony and insurance litigation. Every seal had to remain unbroken, every signature verified, every temperature deviation logged to withstand cross-examination by senior defense counsel.

In the police command briefing room, two senior detectives were reviewing the telephone toll records obtained from Kroll's seized mobile phone. The call logs revealed forty-two encrypted voice calls placed to Warren Holst's direct executive line over the preceding three weeks, confirming that Holst had maintained daily communication with Kroll during the execution of the fires.

Detective Inspector Vance entered the evidence vault and placed Kroll's personal diary inside a fireproof security safe.

"The diary contains handwritten meeting notes with Warren Holst dating back to March twenty-twenty," Vance said. "Every fire was discussed in detail, including the projected insurance payouts and the water entitlement transfer dates."

In the evidence storage room, Evelyn verified the temperature log on the refrigeration unit holding the liquid chemical samples. The temperature was maintained at four degrees Celsius to prevent volatile fraction loss prior to gas chromatography analysis at the state laboratory.

Vance nodded once, his expression unreadable. He slipped into the driver's seat of the Holden, put the car into gear, and backed slowly toward the main gate, leading the convoy of white police vehicles out onto the Redgate road.

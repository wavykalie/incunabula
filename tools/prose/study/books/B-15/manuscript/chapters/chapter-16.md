# Chapter 16: The Subterranean Memo

The basement parking garage of National Mutual smelled of warm engine oil, tire rubber, and exhaust fumes trapped beneath low concrete beams. It was seven o'clock on Friday evening. The corporate fleet vehicles sat in neat rows: thirty identical white sedans with company decals on the front doors.

Evelyn's four-wheel-drive utility stood in bay 42, near the fire exit stairs. It was covered in red dust, its mud flaps caked with dried clay from the back roads.

She walked across the concrete floor, her field boots echoing between the supporting pillars.

As she reached the truck, she saw a white sheet of paper folded twice and wedged under the driver's side windshield wiper blade.

She did not pull it out immediately. She stopped three feet away, scanning the shadows between the vehicles. The garage was silent except for the low hum of the ventilation fan and the distant rumble of traffic on Fitzmaurice Street.

She pulled on a nitrile glove and slipped the paper from beneath the wiper arm.

It was a printed email, sent at 4:45 PM from Warren Holst's private internal account to the Regional Head of Internal Audit in Melbourne.

*Subject: Operational Risk Assessment — Claims File Audit (E. Brand).*

*Confidential.*

*Please be advised that effective Monday 18th instant, Senior Investigator Evelyn Brand is to be placed on directed administrative leave pending an inquiry into procedural irregularities and non-compliance with regional underwriting protocols. Her physical access to regional offices and digital credentials for the ASIC and Land Titles databases will be suspended at 0800 hours.*

*Investigator Raymond Kroll of Kroll Thermal Consulting has been retained as external independent assessor to assume carriage of the Merriwa, Oakhaven, and Coram Creek loss evaluations. All original files, notes, and physical samples currently in Brand's possession are to be surrendered to my office upon her arrival Monday morning.*

*W. Holst.*
*Regional Managing Director.*

Evelyn read the memo twice. Her breath came slow and cold in the stagnant air of the garage.

*Raymond Kroll.*

The name clicked into place like the tumbler of a safe.

Kroll was the valuation consultant Frank Miller had described. The man in the engineer's vest with the iPad who had asked about the cypress bearers and the subfloor vents ten days before the Merriwa house burned down.

Holst wasn't just covering up the fires. He was actively coordinating with the arsonist.

They were bringing Kroll in as the independent expert to sign off on the accidental loss statements. That would declare the paper deeds officially destroyed and close the claims before anyone from the State Crime Command could examine the chemical evidence.

On Monday morning, they would confiscate her notebooks, her samples, and her access tokens.

She stood in the shadow of the concrete pillar, listening to the silence of the basement.

A second sheet of paper was folded inside the first. It was a printout of an ASIC corporate extract for Kroll Thermal Consulting Pty Ltd. The sole director was Raymond Peter Kroll, registered at an industrial address in Altona North, Victoria.

Under the share register, one hundred ordinary shares were listed.

Ninety-nine of those shares were held in trust by *Southern Basin Capital*.

The arsonist was not an independent contractor. He was a corporate subsidiary.

Holst's signature on the audit directive was the final confirmation of institutional collusion. National Mutual had insured the properties, reinsured the liabilities through an Apex shell company, and hired its own subsidiary to execute the fires and certify the destruction of the water deeds.

She checked her rearview mirror. The reflection showed the empty garage bays, the bare concrete pillars, and the red exit sign glowing over the steel fire door.

She had forty-eight hours before the credentials expired.

Forty-eight hours to reach Redgate, locate Claire Sutton, and verify whether Kroll had already marked the fourth foundation.

Evelyn folded the memo and slipped it into her breast pocket.

She unlocked the utility. She tossed her briefcase onto the passenger seat and climbed behind the steering wheel.

Before opening the truck door, Evelyn knelt on the cold concrete of the underground parking bay and shone her inspection flashlight beneath the rear axle. The beam illuminated the leaf springs, the heavy steel differential casing, and the spare tire winch.

Attached to the inside flange of the vehicle frame rail was a black rectangular box the size of a cigarette pack, held in place by two neodymium magnets. A small flexible antenna protruded three inches along the steel chassis.

She pulled on a pair of nitrile gloves from her pocket and carefully detached the device. The magnetic pull was fierce, releasing with a sharp metallic clink. She examined the casing under her flashlight. Molded into the waterproof polymer was an FCC compliance label and an IMEI serial number corresponding to a commercial satellite asset tracker. The device was configured for continuous real-time telemetry, transmitting vehicle coordinates, speed, and heading over the cellular network every forty seconds.

She wrapped the tracking unit in two layers of heavy aluminum foil from her field kit, neutralizing the radio frequency signal, and placed it inside a lead-lined sample canister in her locked toolbox.

She turned the ignition key. The diesel engine started with a deep, vibrating roar that bounced off the concrete ceiling of the basement. She engaged reverse, backed out of the bay in a sharp, clean arc, and headed for the exit ramp toward the street.

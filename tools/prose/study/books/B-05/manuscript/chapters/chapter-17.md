The text message thread on Tess's phone stretched back four months, forming an accidental, chronological diary of a canine heart failure case.

Tess sat at her desk on a Sunday afternoon, scrolling backward through the green and gray speech bubbles on the glass screen.

*Oct 14, 07:12 AM — Clara:*
Pimobendan given with two spoons yam puree. Sleeping rate was 18 at 6:30. He watched the magpies through the back window for twenty minutes.

*Oct 14, 07:15 AM — Tess:*
Good. Keep him inside if the temperature drops below freezing. Cold air increases systemic vascular resistance.

*Oct 22, 11:45 PM — Clara:*
He had a small dry hack after drinking water. Just three coughs, then stopped. Sleeping rate 20. Should I give the extra Lasix?

*Oct 22, 11:48 PM — Tess:*
No. Post-drinking cough is usually tracheal irritation from left atrial compression, not pulmonary edema. If the rate stays under 25, hold steady. Call me if it changes.

*Nov 02, 06:40 PM — Clara:*
Nate brought home elk steak from a hunting buddy. Barnaby got two small cooked pieces without salt. His tail was going so hard he knocked over my pencil jar.

*Nov 02, 06:42 PM — Tess:*
Elk is lean. Good protein. Don't let Nate give him the marrow bone; fat triggers pancreatitis and we cannot afford an abdominal crisis right now.

*Nov 18, 01:15 AM — Clara:*
Are you awake?

*Nov 18, 01:18 AM — Tess:*
Always. Reviewing lab results for clinic tomorrow. What's wrong?

*Nov 18, 01:22 AM — Clara:*
Nothing with Barnaby. He's asleep with his head on my knee. I was just thinking about what you said about plants having anatomy. I'm painting a subalpine fir cone tonight and I keep thinking about how the scales open only when the heat dries the resin out.

*Nov 18, 01:25 AM — Tess:*
Serotinous cones. They need fire or drought to release the seed.

*Nov 18, 01:28 AM — Clara:*
Yes. It seems terrible that something has to dry out and break before it can grow anything new.

*Nov 18, 01:31 AM — Tess:*
That's forestry, Clara.

*Nov 18, 01:35 AM — Clara:*
I don't think I was talking about forestry, Tess.

Tess stared at that five-week-old exchange on her screen.

She remembered that night. She remembered sitting at this exact desk, her fingers frozen over the keyboard, her heart pounding with a sudden, confusing intensity that had nothing to do with veterinary medicine. She hadn't replied to that message; she had closed her phone, walked into the kitchen, and scrubbed the sink until the stainless steel gleamed.

She scrolled down to the latest messages from yesterday:

*Dec 12, 04:15 PM — Clara:*
Handover tomorrow at five. I made chicken broth with ginger and parsley for his pills. I'll bring the extra quart for your fridge.

*Dec 12, 04:18 PM — Tess:*
You don't need to feed me, Clara.

*Dec 12, 04:20 PM — Clara:*
I'm not feeding you. I'm feeding the person who keeps Barnaby alive. There's a difference. See you at five.

Tess locked the phone, set it face-down on the blotter, and went to the window to watch the snowplows clearing the pass.

Tess sat at her desk, scrolling down to the earliest text messages from October, when the custody calendar had first been created.

*Oct 05, 08:30 PM — Tess:*
Pick up Barnaby on Sunday at 5 PM. Do not feed him table scraps. His GI tract is sensitive.

*Oct 05, 08:34 PM — Clara:*
Understood. I bought organic brown rice and lean turkey breast for his evening meals. Safe travels.

The early messages were stiff, guarded, formal clinical memos between two women who were navigating an awkward, unwanted truce.

And then, as the weeks passed, the tone had softened. The messages became longer, filled with small, intimate details: photographs of Barnaby sleeping in front of the woodstove, descriptions of the light coming through the studio windows, questions about alpine wildflowers, jokes about Dr. Bell's coffee addiction.

*Nov 25, 09:12 PM — Clara:*
Nate fell asleep on the couch watching the Broncos game. Barnaby stole his wool slipper and brought it to my studio. He's using it as a pillow while I paint mountain avens.

*Nov 25, 09:15 PM — Tess:*
He always loved wool. When he was a puppy, he chewed the sleeves off two of Nate's Pendleton shirts. Nate was furious for twenty minutes, then went out and bought him a whole sheepskin pelt.

*Nov 25, 09:18 PM — Clara:*
Nate told me that story. He said you laughed so hard you dropped the dog bowl.

*Nov 25, 09:22 PM — Tess:*
I did. It was one of the few times we laughed that winter.

Tess locked her phone and set it on the desk blotter.

She looked at Barnaby, who had opened one eye to watch her from his bed.

"We've come a long way, bear," Tess whispered softly.

The dog thumped his tail once against the floorboards and closed his eyes in the warm afternoon quiet.

Tess set her phone face down on the bedside table and stared up at the whitewashed ceiling beams.

The glowing screen lit up again three minutes later with a quiet chime.

*Clara: Are you still awake?*

Tess picked up the phone.

*Tess: Yes. Reviewing tomorrow's surgery schedule. Three spays and a canine dental.*

*Clara: Don't stay up too late. You carry the whole clinic on your shoulders, Tess. Marcus tells everyone in town that you're the finest surgeon in the county, but you need to sleep.*

*Tess: Marcus exaggerates to keep the ranchers from arguing over billing.*

*Clara: He doesn't exaggerate. I've seen your hands.*

Tess felt her throat tighten. Her thumb rested over the glass screen, typing and erasing two different responses before she settled on a simple line:

*Tess: Thank you, Clara. Goodnight.*

*Clara: Goodnight, Tess. Kiss Barnaby's nose for me.*

Tess locked the phone. She reached down over the edge of the mattress and brushed her fingers through Barnaby's thick neck ruff until the dog leaned into her touch, and then she turned off the bedside lamp.

The knock on Tess's storm door came at 8:15 PM on a Friday.

Tess opened the door.

Clara stood on the landing, wearing a yellow rain slicker with the hood pushed back, her dark hair beaded with moisture from the evening drizzle. Behind her, parked at the bottom of the stairs, her Subaru wagon was packed to the ceiling with rolled canvas rugs, bundles of brushes, and cardboard boxes of botanical reference books.

She had her keys in one hand and an open container of hot tomato soup from the diner in the other.

"The cottage on Willow Creek is clean," Clara said, her breath fogging faintly in the wet air. "The heat is on, and the landlord unlocked the sunporch."

Tess looked at her, her heart swelling with an emotion so sudden and fierce it took her breath away.

"You moved today," Tess said.

"I moved today," Clara repeated.

She didn't wait for an invitation. She stepped through the door, set the soup and the keys on the counter, and turned around to face Tess.

The apartment was warm and quiet, smelling of cedar woodsmoke and dry dog fur. In the corner by the stove, Barnaby lifted his head from his orthopedic bed, gave one slow thump of his tail, and went back to sleep.

Clara reached out, taking Tess's face between both hands. Her palms were cool from the rain, her fingers steady and strong.

"I love you, Tess," Clara said, speaking the words clearly, without hesitation, without the protective safety of subtext or medicine. "Not because we share a sick dog. Not because you're convenient. I love you because you are fierce, and honest, and you showed me that being vulnerable doesn't mean you're going to break."

Tess felt the last barrier inside her chest dissolve, a dam giving way beneath years of held water.

She stepped forward, wrapping her arms around Clara's neck, burying her face in the wet wool of Clara's collar, pulling her whole body against hers.

"I love you, Clara," Tess whispered against her skin, the words shaking with the truth she had carried since November. "I love you so much."

Clara's arms tightened around Tess's back, holding her with an absolute, steady strength that grounded them both against the floorboards.

They kissed—a deep, unhurried, beautiful reunion that sealed everything they had built across five months of medicine, spreadsheets, and mountain blizzards.

When they finally broke apart, laughing softly through their tears, Barnaby stood up from his bed, walked over to the kitchen rug, and pressed his broad golden head against their intertwined legs, claiming his place in the center of their home.

Clara stood in the warm entryway of Tess's apartment, her rain slicker dripping clear mountain rainwater onto the rubber mat.

Her gray eyes were fierce, clear, and completely open.

"I don't want to spend another night wondering what we are, Tess," Clara said, her voice rich with conviction. "I don't care about town gossip, and I don't care about doing things in the order people expect. I love you. I want to build a life with you, starting tonight."

Tess felt tears spill over her cheeks, but she didn't wipe them away. She reached out, catching Clara's waist, pulling her forward into an embrace that took both their breath away.

"Stay with me," Tess whispered against Clara's neck. "Don't go back to the cottage tonight. Stay here."

"I'm staying," Clara replied, her hands tangling in Tess's hair, her lips finding Tess's in a kiss that was fierce, sweet, and filled with the promise of years to come.

Barnaby walked over from the living room, gave a soft, happy sneeze, and rested his golden muzzle across their joined boots.

They laughed together in the warm kitchen, the rain beating against the windows as they locked the storm door and turned toward the fire.

They stood inside the entryway of Tess's apartment, the front door closed against the cold April wind.

Clara's dark coat was wet with rain across the shoulders, and her hair was plastered in damp curls against her cheeks. She set her leather satchel on the floor, breathing heavily from running up the porch stairs.

Barnaby pushed his nose into Clara's palm, whining with low, joyful excitement, his tail thumping rhythmically against the wooden baseboards.

Tess reached out, unbuttoning Clara's wet coat and lifting it from her shoulders. She hung the coat on the brass wall peg, then turned back to face Clara.

"You moved into the carriage house today," Tess said softly.

"Nate helped me carry the mattress upstairs at noon," Clara said, her voice shaking slightly from the cold. "And then he shook my hand, told me to buy a good space heater for the bedroom, and drove his truck back to the shop."

"Nate's a decent man," Tess murmured.

"He is," Clara said, stepping closer. "And now I'm here, Tess. I'm here because I don't want to spend another evening pretending that you're just my dog's veterinarian, or my friend, or someone I happen to share tea with on Sundays."

Tess looked at Clara's face: the clear, honest gray eyes, the soft curve of her mouth, the steady courage in her stance.

"I don't want that either, Clara," Tess said. The words came out clean, unburdened by guilt, ringing true in the warm hallway.

Tess stepped forward and cupped Clara's face in both of her hands. Clara's skin was cool from the rain, but her breath was warm against Tess's lips.

When their mouths met, the last lingering barrier between them dissolved. The kiss was deep, tender, filled with months of unspoken longing, quiet recognition, and mutual devotion.

Clara's arms wrapped around Tess's neck, pulling her close, while Barnaby leaned his warm, seventy-pound weight against their legs like a golden anchor.

They stood there in the quiet hallway for a long time, holding each other in the warm light of the apartment, ready for whatever days they had left.

They sat together on the sofa in the living room, a wool throw draped over their shoulders, while the rain drummed steadily against the metal roof.

Barnaby lay stretched out between the sofa and the coffee table, his head pillowed on Clara's canvas tote bag, snoring softly in rhythm with the rain.

Tess held Clara's hand in her lap, tracing the fine knuckles with her thumb.

"What did Nate say when you gave him the keys?" Tess asked quietly.

"He told me he knew it was coming three weeks after I moved in last summer," Clara said, leaning her head against Tess's shoulder. "He said he was lonely after the divorce, and that having me there made the house feel like a home. But he said he knew we were just two carpenters working on different rooms of the same building."

Tess let out a soft, surprised breath. "He used that exact phrase with me at the gazebo yesterday."

Clara smiled against Tess's neck. "Nate has his metaphors. He sticks to the wood."

"He told me he gave us his blessing," Tess murmured. "He said he'd never seen me look so light."

Clara lifted her head, looking directly into Tess's eyes in the dim room. "You do look light, Tess. When I first met you in the clinic, you looked like you were carrying an anvil on your chest. Every breath looked hard."

Tess touched Clara's face, her fingers tracing the soft curve of her jawline. "You took the anvil off, Clara."

Clara leaned in, kissing Tess softly on the lips, a long, sweet, tender kiss that tasted of mint tea and mountain rain.

Outside, the spring storm washed the valley streets clean, while inside the cottage the fire crackled in the stove, warm and bright.

The dawn after a blizzard in the Colorado high country was not gray; it was a blinding, crystalline blue that exploded across the Continental Divide with the force of shattered glass.

At 7:15 AM, the roar of the county road grader shook the floorboards of Tess's apartment as the massive diesel blade cut a twelve-foot swath through the snowbank on Granite Street.

Tess stood at the kitchen window holding a mug of black coffee, watching the orange flashing strobe of the plow crawl toward Highway 9. A plume of white powder flew sixty feet into the air, catching the early morning sunlight like diamonds.

Behind her, the apartment was quiet.

Barnaby stood at his water bowl by the refrigerator, lapping with steady, methodical rhythm. He took nine laps, paused to wipe his dripping flews with a quick flick of his tongue, and walked over to Tess, pressing his forehead against her knee.

Tess set her mug on the sill and knelt down, wrapping both arms around his golden neck.

"Morning, bear," she whispered.

The bedroom door clicked open. Clara stepped out into the hallway, wearing her gray wool sweater and the denim jeans she had worn the night before. Her dark hair was combed back, loose around her shoulders, and her face was flushed pale and calm from sleep.

She stopped at the edge of the kitchen, looking at Tess kneeling on the floor.

For three seconds, the space between them was filled with the memory of the firelight, the taste of chamomile, the feeling of fingers tangled in hair. The kiss was not a dream that dissolved in the daylight; it was an undeniable, physical fact that hung in the bright, cold mountain air like a newly built wall.

"Morning," Clara said softly.

Tess stood up, wiping a stray golden retriever hair from her jeans. "Morning. Road grader just cleared Granite Street. The pass is still closed, but you'll be able to get down to Pine Street in an hour."

Clara walked into the kitchen, stopping by the counter. She looked down at her hands, then up into Tess's eyes. There was no apology in her face, no frantic backtracking, no panic.

"I don't regret last night, Tess," Clara said. Her voice was steady, unvarnished.

Tess leaned back against the counter, her hands gripping the laminate edge behind her.

"I don't either," Tess said. The truth came out before she could armor herself. "I don't regret it at all, Clara. But Nate is down on Pine Street."

"I know," Clara said.

"He's a good man," Tess added, the words catching in her throat. "He didn't hurt me. He didn't abandon me. We just failed. And doing this behind his back... it feels like we're burning down his house while he's out shoveling the driveway."

Clara took a step closer, stopping just short of touching Tess's arm.

"I won't lie to him, Tess," Clara said in a low murmur. "I'm going to tell him today."

Tess looked at her, her chest tightening with panic. "Clara, wait. Don't rush into an explosion. Let's think about this."

"There's nothing to think about," Clara said, her voice filled with a calm, resolute certainty. "Staying on Pine Street while loving you would be the only real betrayal. Nate deserves better than a woman who looks out his kitchen window wishing she were on Granite Street."

Barnaby walked between them, his tail giving three slow, heavy thumps against the cabinet doors, and looked up at both of them with patient, expectant brown eyes.

Tess stood at the stove, frying two eggs in a cast-iron skillet while Clara sliced a loaf of sourdough on the cutting board.

The sunlight coming through the kitchen window was blinding, illuminating every dust mote and the thin curls of steam rising from the kettle. The smell of hot butter and toasted grain filled the room with ordinary domestic reality.

Barnaby sat by the refrigerator, his tail sweeping the floor in anticipation of the egg crusts he always received on weekend mornings.

"Nate called while you were in the shower," Clara said in a low murmur, setting the bread knife down on the board. "The plows reached Pine Street. He asked if I needed him to come dig out my wagon."

Tess stopped with the metal spatula in her hand. "What did you say?"

"I told him I was fine," Clara said, turning to look at Tess with clear, steady gray eyes. "I told him I'd be home by ten to talk to him."

Tess felt the sudden, cold knot of anxiety tighten in her stomach, but Clara stepped across the kitchen, laying her hand over Tess's forearm.

"Don't be afraid, Tess," Clara said gently. "Nate is a decent man. He deserves the truth, and he deserves to hear it from me with dignity."

Tess looked at Clara's calm, resolute face, feeling the panic subside into profound, grateful respect. She slid the fried eggs onto two ceramic plates, split a crust for Barnaby, and sat down at the sunlit table to share the morning meal.

At eight in the morning, the yellow county plow finally rumbled past the cottage, scraping its heavy steel blade against the asphalt with a rhythmic, screeching roar. A rooster-tail of dry white powder showered over the roadside snowbanks.

Tess stood at the front window, holding her mug of black coffee with both hands.

Barnaby walked over to the door, his tail giving three slow, polite thumps against the floorboards. He looked up at Tess, then looked at the brass latch.

"Hang on, bear," Tess said softly.

She put on her heavy muck boots and unlatched the front door. The morning air hit her face like cold steel: four below zero, brilliant and dry under an immaculate blue sky.

Barnaby stepped out onto the shoveled stoop, his paws crunching in the fresh powder. He stopped at the edge of the drift, sniffed the clean pine scent in the breeze, and looked back at Clara, who was coming out of the kitchen with a bowl of steaming oatmeal.

"Look at that sky," Clara said, pausing in the doorway beside Tess. The winter glare caught the silver strands in Clara's dark hair.

Tess turned her head to look at Clara. The fear from the night before hadn't vanished, but the warmth between them was real, solid, standing quietly in the morning sun.

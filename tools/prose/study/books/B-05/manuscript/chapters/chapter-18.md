The Sunday evening handover at Tess's apartment had settled into an unspoken, twenty-minute routine.

At five o'clock, Clara's wagon pulled into the gravel drive below the wooden stairs. Barnaby climbed the steps with slow, deliberate care, his nails clicking on the weathered cedar treads. Clara carried the heavy canvas tote bag and a thermal container of chicken broth.

Today, however, the temperature had plummeted to four degrees below zero, and a dry mountain wind was driving stinging pellets of graupel against the siding.

"Come inside," Tess said, holding the storm door wide against the gale. "Don't stand out there in that wind."

Clara stepped into the warm apartment, her cheeks flushed bright scarlet from the cold, her breath puffing white against the air. She set the broth container on the counter and unzipped her pea coat.

"The wind coming off Hoosier Pass is brutal," Clara said, shivering as she shook the dry ice pellets from her scarf. "They're talking about closing Highway 9 by eight o'clock."

"It's already blowing drifts across the flats," Tess said. She took Clara's heavy wool coat and hung it on the wall peg beside her own parka. "Let me make some coffee. Or tea. You shouldn't drive back to Pine Street until your hands thaw out."

"Coffee would be wonderful," Clara said, blowing into her cupped palms.

Barnaby had already walked across the living room and collapsed onto his memory foam bed by the woodstove with a long, tremulous sigh. He rested his chin on his front paws, his eyes following Clara's every movement as she walked over to the kitchen sink.

Tess measured dark roast grounds into the French press, poured boiling water from the kettle, and set the glass cylinder on the counter.

"I brought his lab report from Friday," Tess said, reaching for a sheet of paper on the counter to break the quiet. "BUN is thirty-two, creatinine is one point four. Kidneys are handling the furosemide well. Electrolytes are completely balanced."

Clara stood beside her at the counter, her shoulder three inches from Tess's. "That's good, isn't it?"

"It's very good," Tess said. "It means we have room to increase the diuretic if he starts coughing again."

Tess pressed the metal plunger down through the dark coffee, the mesh squeaking faintly against the tempered glass. She reached into the overhead cupboard, pulled down two ceramic mugs, and poured the steaming coffee into both.

"Here," Tess said, turning to hand a mug to Clara.

Clara reached out to take it.

Their fingers met on the glazed ceramic handle. Tess's thumb brushed the warm, smooth skin of Clara's index finger. Clara didn't pull back. Instead, her fingers shifted slightly, closing over the handle while her thumb came to rest against the back of Tess's knuckles.

The kitchen went dead still.

Tess looked up. Clara was looking at her, her gray eyes dark and searching, her breath catching softly in her throat. At this distance, Tess could smell the cedar smoke on Clara's wool sweater and the faint, sweet scent of almond oil on her skin.

Neither of them spoke. The wind howled against the exterior wall, rattling the stovepipe, but inside the small circle of light above the sink, the air was thick, suspended, charged with an intensity that made Tess's knees feel hollow.

Tess saw Clara's gaze drop to her lips, then rise back to her eyes.

"Tess," Clara whispered.

The front door suddenly shook with a heavy, rattling shudder as a violent gust of wind hit the outside landing, breaking the spell like a gunshot.

Tess stepped back half an inch, her fingers releasing the mug into Clara's hands.

"The coffee's hot," Tess said, her voice rough, barely managing to steady the words. "Watch your tongue."

Clara looked down at the dark surface of the coffee, her fingers gripping the ceramic as if anchoring herself against a sudden current. "Yeah," Clara murmured. "Hot."

Across the room, the woodstove snapped, sending a small shower of orange sparks dancing behind the glass door.

Clara held the ceramic mug in both hands, taking a slow sip of the hot coffee as the mountain wind battered the storm windows.

Tess stood at the counter, her hands resting against the laminate edge, her pulse still racing from the touch of Clara's fingers.

"The wind is picking up on the pass," Tess said, looking out the glass door where the streetlights were beginning to blur in swirling clouds of graupel. "They're reporting gusts up to sixty miles an hour at the summit."

"I should go before the drifts block the driveway," Clara murmured, though she didn't move an inch toward the coat rack.

"You don't have to rush," Tess said softly. "The coffee's hot. Drink it."

Clara looked at her over the rim of the mug, her gray eyes dark and steady in the warm kitchen light.

"You're always taking care of everyone, Tess," Clara said gently. "Barnaby. Marcus. The clients at the clinic. Nate."

"It's my job," Tess replied.

"It's not just a job," Clara said, setting her mug on the counter and stepping closer. "It's how you protect yourself. You take care of everyone else so nobody gets close enough to see where you're tired."

Tess felt the words hit her chest like a physical blow. She looked down at her hands, unable to meet Clara's searching gaze.

"Clara," Tess whispered.

Clara reached out, her fingers warm and gentle, and touched Tess's chin, lifting her face until their eyes met.

"You don't have to hide from me, Tess," Clara whispered.

The air between them was electric, heavy with months of unspoken longing. For five seconds, neither of them moved. The woodstove snapped behind them, and outside, the mountain gale shrieked across the metal roof.

Clara smiled faintly, her hand dropping back to her side with infinite restraint.

"I'll see you Friday at the clinic," Clara said softly, turning toward the coat rack.

Tess watched her pull on her heavy pea coat and step out into the freezing night, feeling the warm imprint of Clara's fingers lingering on her skin long after the storm door latched shut.

They washed the two ceramic mugs together at the small porcelain sink.

Clara washed, her sleeves pushed up past her elbows, lathering the white glaze with a sponge of lemon dish soap. Tess stood beside her with the blue linen tea towel, drying the warm stoneware and setting it neatly on the open pine shelf above the counter.

Their shoulders brushed each time Clara turned to the drying rack.

The contact was brief, accidental, but with every brush of fabric against fabric, the air in the small kitchen seemed to grow thicker, warmer, charged with an intensity that had nothing to do with hot dishwater.

Clara turned off the brass faucet. She wiped her wet hands on the edge of the towel Tess was holding.

"Tess," Clara said softly.

Tess stopped folding the towel. She looked up. Clara was standing less than a foot away, her gray eyes dark and wide, the morning sun striking the high curve of her cheekbones.

"Thank you for the coffee," Clara whispered, her voice barely louder than the hum of the refrigerator.

"You're welcome," Tess said. Her heart was beating against her ribs like a startled bird.

Clara reached out, her fingers hesitating in the space between them before she gently tucked a loose strand of dark hair behind Tess's ear. The touch was feather-light, but the warmth of Clara's skin lingered against Tess's temple like an electric spark.

Neither of them moved. For three long seconds, the world shrank to the square of winter sunlight on the linoleum floor and the sound of their breath.

From the hallway, Barnaby gave a sudden, cheerful sneeze, his brass tags jingling against his collar as he trotted into the kitchen to check his water bowl.

The moment broke. Clara stepped back, a flush of rose color rising across her throat, and smiled.

"I should let you get to your Sunday," Clara murmured, picking up her coat from the kitchen chair.

"I'll see you on Wednesday," Tess said, walking her to the front door with Barnaby at their heels.

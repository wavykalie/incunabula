# Chapter 37: The New Charter

At dawn on the third day of November, the Great Charter of Reconciliation was inscribed upon four sheets of bleached mountain calfskin in the council chamber of High Carrow.

The document was drafted in Chancery Gothic by Maren Vane and Master Corvus Althaus, witnessed by twenty elders of High Carrow and the eighteen sergeants of the Guild Guard.

The preamble set forth the permanent covenant between the sea and the mountain:

*BE IT KNOWN UNTO ALL MEN BY THESE PRESENTS: That whereas the peace, prosperity, and common weal of the realm of Oakhaven dependeth wholly upon the truth of its geographic records; and whereas the suppression of true surveys is hereby declared to be an injury against the Crown, a fraud upon the merchant commonwealth, and a rebellion against the natural order of the earth:*

*IT IS HEREBY ENACTED, DECREED, AND ESTABLISHED FOR ALL TIME:*

*ARTICLE I: De Unitate Terrarum.*
*The realm of Oakhaven and the mountain liberties of High Carrow shall henceforth constitute one unbroken territorial liberty. The highway known as the Via Alta Carrovensis, with all its bridges, causeways, waystations, and culverts, is declared a royal highway, free and open to all travelers, carters, merchants, and herdsmen without impediment, seizure, or arbitrary toll.*

*ARTICLE II: De Abolitione Articuli XII.*
*Article XII of the Charter of 1492, together with all statutes, ordinances, and decrees that classified the interior as terra vacua et non existens, is hereby revoked, annulled, and stricken from the Great Register. Whosoever shall hereafter declare the mountain parishes to be non-existent, or shall seek to erase their names from the public charts, shall be adjudged guilty of sedition against the realm.*

*ARTICLE III: De Custodia Tabularum.*
*The Guild of Navigators and Cartographers shall henceforth maintain two seats of equal authority: the High Tower of Oakhaven Harbour for the surveys of the coast, tides, and maritime anchorages; and the Hall of the First Surveys in High Carrow for the surveys of the interior, the mines, and the mountain passes. No chart shall be issued under the Great Seal without the signature of both the Master of the Coast and the Master of the Interior.*

*ARTICLE IV: De Libertate Cartographica.*
*No surveyor, draughtsman, or apprentice shall be punished, reprimanded, or disqualified for recording the true changes of the earth. If a coastline shift, if a river carve a new channel, or if a highway be opened by human labor, it shall be the sworn duty of the cartographer to record the same with all mechanical diligence and geometric fidelity. The earth shall be drawn as it is, and not as authority fancieth it to be.*

When the sixty-four clauses of the charter were completed, Maren brought forward the four seals of ratification.

The first seal was the Chancellor's Seal of the Guild: the open compass and anchor, impressed in scarlet wax by Master Corvus Althaus.

The second seal was the Town Seal of High Carrow: the stone mason's chisel and wheat sheaf, impressed in green wax by Elder Martha.

The third seal was the Waymarker's Mark: the ashwood staff and iron cross, impressed in black pitch by Kellan Reed on behalf of the road menders.

And the fourth seal was the Master Cartographer's Seal: the ancient founding mark of the open compass, the chisel, and the sighting staff, impressed in purple wax by Maren Vane.

The four colors of wax flattened side by side upon the bottom vellum margin, their ribbons of braided silk entwined together through a single slit in the skin.

Corvus picked up the heavy vellum roll.

He turned to Maren, his eyes clear and resolute.

"Carry it to Harbour Square, Maren," Corvus said, handing her the charter. "Assemble the copyists in the Great Hall. Read the sixty-four articles from the black walnut lectern, and let every apprentice know that the season of the fire is ended."

"Will you not ride with us, Master?" she asked.

Corvus shook his head with a gentle, peaceful smile.

"I will remain in High Carrow for the winter, Maren. Master Varek has promised to teach me the geometry of stone masonry, and Elder Martha has seventy years of unrecorded mountain traverses that need an old copyist's hand. I have spent enough of my life looking at salt water. It is time I learned the name of the rocks."

He took her hands in his, his large, scarred fingers warm and steady.

The council chamber of High Carrow was crowded with fifty citizens who had gathered to witness the final inking of the Great Charter of Reconciliation.

Elder Martha sat beside Master Corvus at the head of the oak trestle table, her white hair bound with a green ribbon of mountain wool.

"Read Article V, Master Cartographer," Martha commanded, looking at Maren.

Maren stood beside the lectern, her voice ringing clear and steady in the quiet room:

*ARTICLE V: De Apertura Portarum.*
*It is hereby ordained and decreed that the Red Sandstone Gate of Oakhaven and the South Gate of High Carrow shall remain unbarred, open, and free to all subjects of the realm from sunrise to sunset in all seasons. The municipal guards shall serve as protectors of the travelers, and no weapon, chain, or engine of war shall be placed upon the Bridge of the Chasm to impede the passage of peace.*

*ARTICLE VI: De Hereditate Cartographica.*
*The sketchbooks, journals, and survey traverses of the late Thomas Vane, surveyor of the hinterland, are hereby restored to honor and cleared of all taint of heresy. His name shall be inscribed upon the roll of Master Cartographers in both the High Tower of the Harbour and the Hall of the First Surveys, and his memory shall be celebrated on the Feast of Saint Jude for all generations.*

A cheer rose from the mountain elders, deep and heartfelt, echoing up into the timber rafters of the hall.

Corvus picked up his quill, dipped it into the black inkpot, and signed his name beneath the sixty-four articles with a firm, unyielding stroke:

*Corvus Althaus, Chief Warden of the Charter.*

Beside his name, Maren Vane signed her own, completing the covenant that united the realm forever.

The Great Charter of Reconciliation lay upon the oak table, four sheets of heavy calfskin vellum bound with cords of scarlet and green silk.

Elder Martha took up the heavy iron stylus of High Carrow and dipped it into the pot of indelible carbon black ink.

She signed her name on behalf of the mountain parishes:

*Martha de Carrovia, Senior Custos.*

Beside her signature, Master Varek set his mason's mark: a deeply impressed triangle crossed by an iron chisel, the ancient mark of the High Carrow stonecutters' lodge.

Then came the signatures of the eighteen horse sergeants of the Guild Guard, each man stepping forward in his buff-leather coat, removing his iron gauntlet, and signing his name with clumsy, earnest strokes of the quill.

The Captain of the Guard wiped a tear from his leather collar with the back of his hand.

"My grandfather grew up in High Carrow, Master Althaus," the soldier whispered to Corvus. "When the Duke closed the road forty years ago, my mother was three years old. She spent her life in the weavers' quarter of Oakhaven, dreaming of the high pastures she was never permitted to see. If she were alive today, she would kiss your boots for signing this paper."

Corvus touched the captain's shoulder with his large hand.

"Do not kiss my boots, Captain," Corvus said softly. "Mount your horse. Ride with the wains. And when you pass through the Red Gate, tell the weavers that their mountain has come home."

The Great Charter of Reconciliation was bound in boards of seasoned mountain oak, covered in rich brown pigskin stamped with the unified seal.

Elder Martha watched as Maren tied the four braided silk cords that held the parchment leaves together.

"The seal will hold for five hundred years, Master Cartographer," Martha said, her old voice full of quiet authority. "Our mountain sheepskin does not rot like the coastal calfskin. It is cured in mountain salt and horn oil; the mold cannot bite into the collagen."

Corvus stepped to the window, looking out over the three leagues of sunlit snowy valley that stretched toward the Bridge of the Chasm.

"We will send two sworn apprentices to High Carrow every Michaelmas," Corvus decreed, turning back to the council table. "They will live in the town for three years. They will learn to quarry stone, to swing a mason's hammer, and to sight an alidade across the mountain torrents. And when they return to the harbour tower, they will bring the true traverses of the northern peaks to be inscribed upon the master copperplates."

"And the mountain boys?" Kellan asked, grinning from the hearth.

"The mountain boys will come to the high tower," Corvus answered with a warm smile. "They will learn the mathematics of astronomical quadrants, the calculation of sines and tangents, and the chemistry of indelible inks. We will have no more walls between the craftsman who cuts the stone and the cartographer who draws it."

"Go with the wains, my child. The harbour has waited forty years for this road. Do not make them wait another day."

Maren bowed her head, pressed the heavy charter roll against her ribs, and turned toward the open gates where the trade caravan stood ready for the descent.

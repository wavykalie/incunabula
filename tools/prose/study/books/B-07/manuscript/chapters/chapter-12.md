# Chapter 12: The Road to High Carrow

The noon bells of the Cathedral of Saint Jude were tolling across Oakhaven Harbour when Maren slipped back down the spiral stairs into the Cold Repository.

She carried two unlit tallow candles and a horsehair broom, as the Archivist had commanded. The gallery was deserted. In the far north vault, eighty paces away behind two heavy oak partitions, she could hear the rhythmic splash of Sibyl's mop in the drainage cistern.

Maren set the broom against the octagonal pillar.

She knelt before chest number sixteen: *Charta Antiqua et Fines Montium: Anno 1440*.

Her heart was beating in her throat. She looked up at the basalt ceiling, half expecting to hear the iron boots of the Guild Sergeants ringing on the stairs, but there was only the steady, cold drip of salt water from the lime stalactites.

She lifted the heavy iron hasp. The hinge turned on its grease without a squeak.

Inside lay five rolls of thick sheepskin, yellowed like horn, tied with bands of rotting rawhide. They smelled of woodsmoke, mutton grease, and dried marsh peat--the scent of an age before the Guild became a closed monopoly of harbour merchants.

Maren lifted the central roll. The vellum was rough, heavier than modern calfskin, prepared with chalk rather than pumice.

She laid it across her knees and unrolled the first two feet under the yellow horn lantern.

It was a comprehensive topographical survey of the province, drawn in iron gall ink that had oxidized into a rich, dark chestnut. The style was archaic: hill profiles drawn as rounded lumps with serrated crests, rivers represented by double wavy ribbons of faded verdigris green.

Along the bottom ran the coastline: Oakhaven Harbour, the basalt pillar, and the South Headland, labeled in neat medieval Latin script.

Then she looked inland.

Beyond the Stone Chasm, where modern charts showed only blank, empty vellum cross-hatched with warnings of impassable precipices, the 1440 chart was filled with meticulous topographical detail.

There were contour lines. There were three mountain rivers labeled *The White Torrent*, *The Salmon Reach*, and *The Eagle's Brook*. And running directly between the bluffs of the chasm, crossing the chasm upon a four-arched bridge of ashlar stone, ran a broad highway drawn in double red lines of cinnabar pigment.

Above the highway, inscribed in bold Gothic lettering, was the title:

*VIA ALTA CARROVENSIS*

The High Road of Carrow.

Maren's breath seized. She brought her brass magnifying glass down to the vellum.

Along the red road, at intervals of roughly four leagues, sat three fortified waystations: *The Quarry Post*, *The Spring of Saint Bridget*, and *The Gorge Gatehouse*. Beyond the gatehouse, nestled in a high limestone cirque surrounded by terraced pastures and pine woods, sat a sprawling walled town labeled:

*CIVITAS CARROVIENSIS: Sedes Metallorum et Telarum.*

High Carrow: City of Metals and Weaving.

The town was drawn with twelve stone towers, a central market square, and three water mills along the river. In the margin, the surveyor had written a note in brown ink:

*The road is paved in hard limestone flags, thirty feet wide, passable for four-horse wains in all seasons save the month of the great blizzard. Here is brought forth the fine silver of the deep veins, the grey tin, and four thousand fleeces of mountain wool each Martinmas.*

Someone had tried to erase the entry.

Across the red road and the town of High Carrow, broad strokes of a scraping blade had gouged the surface of the sheepskin, attempting to shave the cinnabar and iron gall from the hide. But the ink had bitten deep into the dried collagen. The faint red ghost of the road and the black contours of the towers remained perfectly legible beneath the scraped nap.

And in the lower corner of the sheet sat the surveyor's seal.

It was not the modern Guild seal of the open compass and anchor. It was the ancient founding seal: an open compass crossed by a stone mason's chisel and a surveyor's sighting rod, surrounded by the Latin motto: *Terra Non Mentitur*.

The earth does not lie.

Maren pulled the cedar case from inside her surveyor's boot.

She laid her living practice scrap beside the 1440 roll.

The alignment was absolute. The living black line that had crept across her vellum overnight matched the red cinnabar road of the ancient roll down to the exact curvature of the limestone terraces.

The phantom road on her desk was not a vision, not a fantasy, and not a curse.

It was the *Via Alta Carrovensis*. It had existed for four hundred years. It had been paved by stone masons who carved the Guild's own founding crest into their tools.

The Guild had not erased a wasteland. They had murdered their own history to keep the mountain wagons from bypassing their harbour tolls.

A faint clink of keys sounded in the gallery corridor.

Maren turned the leaves of the 1440 survey roll with infinite care.

Beneath the title *VIA ALTA CARROVENSIS*, the ancient surveyor had recorded the engineering specifications of the highway in neat columns of Chancery Latin:

*Width of the stone carriageway: eighteen royal cubits.*
*Depth of the crushed limestone foundation: two cubits.*
*Drainage culverts: one at every furlong, built with ashlar lintels.*
*Gradient: never exceeding one inch per foot, to permit the passage of loaded ore wagons in all seasons.*

In the margin, the surveyor had drawn small, detailed diagrams of the road's cross-section: the crowned center of river gravel, the paved stone gutters on either side, and the heavy granite boundary stones that marked the boundary between the highway and the adjoining pastures.

It was a modern highway. It was superior in every mechanical respect to the muddy, rutted track that connected Oakhaven to the provincial capital.

The mountain folk had built an artery of stone that could carry the wealth of an empire, and the Guild had buried it beneath ninety years of silence.

Maren rolled the 1440 chart, tied the rotting rawhide band, and laid it back into the cedar shavings of chest sixteen. She lowered the heavy lid and let the rusted iron hasp drop into its groove.

When Archivist Sibyl Thorne shuffled into the gallery with her copper lantern, Maren was sweeping the flagstones around the octagonal pillar with steady, rhythmic strokes of her horsehair broom.

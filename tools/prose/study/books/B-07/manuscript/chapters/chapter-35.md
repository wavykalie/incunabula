# Chapter 35: The Hall of the First Surveys

Before the caravan began its descent, the final act of reconciliation was performed in the Hall of the First Surveys.

Two hundred citizens packed the vaulted gallery: miners from the deep silver shafts, weavers from the watermills, stonecutters from the quarry, and the eighteen mounted sergeants of the Guild Guard, who stood in peaceful fraternity beside the mountain folk with their steel caps held across their chests.

In the center of the hall, upon the serpentine marble dais, the two master copperplates lay side by side on a heavy oak workbench.

Master Varek stood at the head of the bench with an iron drill and four brass coupling bolts.

Beside him stood Master Corvus, holding his burin and his engraved copperplate of Oakhaven Harbour.

Maren stood between them, holding the master copperplate of High Carrow.

"Citizens of the realm," Corvus spoke, his voice filling the ancient hall with resonant, unshakeable authority. "In the year 1492, the Council of Wardens committed a crime against the truth of the earth. We took the chisel to our master plates. We severed the mountain from the coast, and we condemned forty leagues of living soil to silence."

He held up his burin, its steel point catching the morning light that streamed through the high clerestory windows.

"Today, by the authority of the living road and the consent of the free people of High Carrow, we heal what was broken."

He stepped to the bench.

He brought the southern edge of the harbour plate against the northern edge of the mountain plate.

The two edges met with a soft, clean, metallic *clink*.

The registration holes aligned down to the fraction of a hair.

Master Varek inserted the first brass coupling bolt through the matched lugs of the plates. He tightened the nut with a hand-wrench of polished steel, the threads squeaking slightly as the brass drew the two sheets of metal into permanent, seamless contact.

Bolt by bolt, the four couplings were driven home.

The two severed halves of the province were one plate once more.

A roar of celebration erupted from the crowd in the gallery, men cheering, women waving linen kerchiefs, the young apprentices stamping their boots on the stone flags with joyous rhythm.

Elder Martha raised her ashwood staff for silence.

When the hall grew quiet, she pointed toward Maren Vane.

"Master Cartographer," Martha commanded. "Take the first impression."

Maren stepped to the table.

She picked up an inking ball of soft sheep's leather stuffed with horsehair, saturated with the rich, indelible carbon black ink she had mixed the day before.

With firm, rhythmic movements, she rolled the leather ball across the unified copperplate.

The black ink filled every engraved line: the soundings of the harbour, the reefs of the South Headland, the piers of the Bridge of the Chasm, the terraced vineyards of the valley, and the fortified towers of High Carrow.

She wiped the surface of the plate with a starched linen rag, cleaning the polished copper until the black pigment remained only within the cut grooves of the metal.

Beside the bench stood the great roller press of High Carrow: an ancient screw-press of seasoned oak, fitted with two heavy rollers of cast bronze.

Varek and Kellan lifted the heavy copperplate and laid it upon the traveling bed of the press.

Maren took a sheet of heavy, handmade rag paper, dampened with spring water to soften the fibers, and laid it carefully over the inked metal. Over the paper she spread three blankets of soft wool felt to distribute the pressure.

She stepped to the great iron spoked wheel of the press.

Corvus stepped up beside her. Kellan took the opposite spoke.

"Together," Maren said.

The three of them leaned their weight against the iron wheel.

The heavy bronze rollers turned with a low, deep, rhythmic groan: *creeeak-crank-creeeak*.

The bed of the press moved smoothly beneath the rollers, subjecting the damp paper to five tons of unyielding, mechanical pressure.

When the bed cleared the roller, Maren lifted the wool blankets.

She took the top edge of the damp rag paper between her thumb and forefinger.

With a slow, steady, breathtaking motion, she peeled the sheet from the copperplate.

The paper came away with a soft, suctioning whisper.

The impression was perfection.

There was not a single blank spot, not a single blurred stroke, not a single line of hesitation.

The coast and the mountain lay united upon the white sheet in deep, velvety black ink, signed by the sea and sealed by the rock: the complete, living map of Oakhaven and High Carrow.

Corvus looked at the print, his eyes shining with tears of joy.

He took the Chancellor's seal from his belt, dipped it into the red wax that Kellan held ready in an iron ladle, and brought it down with a firm, decisive stroke upon the bottom margin of the unified chart.

When the first impression was peeled from the great roller press, Master Varek lifted the paper high so that all two hundred citizens in the gallery could see the unified chart.

The ink was black, bold, and permanent.

In the center of the sheet, bridging the gap between the coastal soundings and the mountain peaks, the four arches of the Bridge of the Chasm stood in magnificent, crisp relief, flanked by the Roman waymarker and the ancient founding crest.

Varek walked to Maren and laid his heavy, stone-scarred hand upon her shoulder.

"You have given our children their name back, lass," the old master mason said, his rough voice breaking with emotion. "When they grow up, they will look at this paper and know that they are not outlaws in a barren wilderness. They will know that they are citizens of the realm."

Maren looked at the damp print, then up at the smiling faces of the mountain folk.

"The map didn't give you your name, Master Varek," she said softly. "Your hammers gave you your name. The map only recorded what your hands built."

Beside the roller press, young Peter was stacking the freshly pulled prints upon wooden drying racks suspended from the ceiling beams.

The paper smelled of damp mountain flax and warm pine resin.

On each print, the lines of the highway ran unbroken from Harbour Square to the mountain keep of High Carrow, sealed with the four colors of wax that bound the kingdom into peace.

"We will print five hundred copies before the winter solstice," Elder Martha said, watching the sheets rise on the racks. "We will send twenty copies to the capital, twenty copies to the island merchants, and a copy to every parish school in the mountains."

*Maren Vane, Master Cartographer of the Realm.*

The crowd in the hall erupted once more into a storm of cheers, the bronze bells of the city ringing out across the mountains to announce to the sky that the long exile was ended.

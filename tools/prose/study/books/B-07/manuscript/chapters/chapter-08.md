# Chapter 8: The Examination Board

The Council Chamber of the Guild Tower was hung with ancient tapestries of green wool depicting the legendary voyages of the Founder Cartographers.

Maren stood in the center of the parquet floor, twenty paces from the raised dais where the four Senior Wardens of the Examination Board sat behind a curved table of polished mahogany. Behind her, two armed guild sergeants in black leather jacks leaned upon their halberds, their steel breastplates reflecting the flicker of forty wax candles burning in the bronze chandeliers above.

On the long table before the wardens lay her journeyman portfolio: twenty-four charts drawn over the course of three years, bound in goatskin boards with brass corner guards.

Warden Malor, an elderly scholar with watery red eyes and a lace cravat stained with snuff, turned the pages of her portfolio with dry, rustling fingers.

"Your coastline soundings are remarkably uniform, Apprentice Vane," Malor said, peering over the top of his horn-rimmed spectacles. "The hatching along the South Spit is particularly commendable. One would almost swear it was engraved from the 1510 master plate rather than drawn by hand."

"Thank you, Warden," Maren said, keeping her chin level and her heels together.

"And yet," Malor continued, tapping the margin of the twenty-second sheet with his ivory stylus, "there is a curious quality to your work. A certain... coldness. You reproduce the master plates down to the exact stroke of the burin, but there is no original observation in your files. Not a single sounding beyond the third buoy. Not a single note on tidal drift."

He leaned forward, resting his flabby chin on his lace collar.

"Your father, Thomas Vane, had a great deal of original observation. Some would say an excess of it. The Guild had to spend sixty pounds of good silver burning his observations in Harbour Square. Tell me, Apprentice: do you share your father's appetite for the unchartered wilderness?"

A cold silence fell across the chamber.

Maren felt the blood drain from her face. Beside Malor, Warden Brandt of the Customs House adjusted his gold chain of office, watching her with the sharp, calculating gaze of a tax collector assessing an undervalued cargo of wine.

Before she could speak, Master Corvus rose from his seat at the center of the table.

He did not shout. He did not wave his hands. But when he stood, the heavy scarlet wool of his Chief Warden's robe gave off a low, authoritative rustle that seemed to fill the entire vaulted room.

"Warden Malor," Corvus said, his voice cold and heavy as lead ballast. "We are here to examine a candidate for the journeyman grade, not to exhume the heresies of twelve years ago."

Malor's eyebrows twitched. "The father's character is not irrelevant, Master Warden. The bloodline--"

"The bloodline has given this Guild six years of unblemished service," Corvus interrupted, slamming his large, square hand down upon the mahogany table with a force that rattled the inkstands. "I have supervised Maren Vane since the day she took the apprentice oath. She has scraped three hundred skins of vellum without spoiling a single hide. She has drawn two thousand miles of coastline without misplacing a fathom mark. In an age when young fools like Orson take their quills into the hills to draw peasant cart tracks, Maren Vane has shown the absolute, mechanical obedience that preserves the peace of this city."

He leaned across the table, his dark, deep-set eyes boring into Malor's face.

"You call her work cold, Malor. I call it safe. A map is not a portrait of an artist's soul. A map is an instrument of state. If every copyist in this tower possessed Maren Vane's cold precision, my brother Lucian would be sitting at his own hearth tonight instead of rotting beneath the kelp on the Black Shoals."

Malor shrank back slightly into his velvet robes, clearing his throat with a dry, rattling cough.

"Of course, Master Althaus. No one questions her technical execution. It was merely... a point of inquiry."

Corvus turned his gaze to Warden Brandt.

"Brandt? Have the Customs Wardens any objection to her appointment?"

Brandt shook his head once, his gold chain clinking. "Her portolan charts agree with the tariff registers down to the half-penny. She is approved."

Corvus picked up the heavy brass seal of the Guild Chancellor. He dipped the carved face into a saucer of red sealing wax that sat over a small spirit lamp, then brought it down with a heavy, crunching blow onto the parchment certificate of advancement that lay in the center of the table.

The red wax flattened, taking the deep, crisp impression of the Guild's crest: an open compass resting upon an anchor.

"Maren Vane," Corvus declared, his voice ringing against the oak ceiling. "By the authority of the Grand Council, you are admitted to the rank of Journeyman Cartographer of the Second Class. You are authorized to draw, copy, and seal the coastal charts of this realm in accordance with the Master Standard."

He picked up the certificate and stepped down from the dais.

He stopped two feet in front of her. For a fraction of a second, as he handed her the heavy vellum roll, his hand touched hers. His fingers were cold, calloused, and trembling with a faint, almost imperceptible tremor.

"You have done well, Maren," he said in a voice that only she could hear. "Do not disappoint me."

"I will serve the charter, Master," she said.

She bowed from the waist, her forehead nearly touching the red wax seal.

Warden Brandt of the Customs House adjusted his gold spectacles. He pulled a sheaf of parchment vouchers from his leather portfolio and laid them beside Maren's charts.

"Three weeks ago," Brandt said, tapping the vouchers with his signet ring, "a timber carter from the upper valley arrived at the land gate with forty baulks of seasoned mountain oak. When the gate collector demanded the municipal tariff, the carter produced a hand-drawn sketch of the North Gorge, claiming that the timber had been felled on free communal land that lay outside the Guild's survey boundary. He cited a chart drawn by one Thomas Vane in the year 1542."

The four wardens on the dais leaned forward, their scarlet gowns rustling.

"The sketch was confiscated and burned at the gatehouse," Brandt continued, his cold eyes fixed on Maren. "The carter's oxen were seized for the King's toll. But the incident demonstrates that the heresies of twelve years ago are still circulating among the peasantry. Apprentice Vane: did your father ever leave you any memorandum books, journals, or unregistered traverses of the interior?"

Maren felt the hair on the back of her neck prickle.

"My father's chests were burned in Harbour Square when I was twelve years old, Warden Brandt," she answered, her voice completely even, holding her chin level. "The bailiffs took his quills, his ink-horns, and his clothing. I have no papers from his hand."

Corvus nodded once from the center of the dais, satisfied by her answer.

As she turned and walked between the two halberdiers toward the heavy oak doors, the red wax of the certificate was still hot against her palm.

She knew what she had done. She had taken a solemn oath before the highest court of the realm to preserve the silence of the maps. And five stories above her head, beneath the straw of her pallet, the living line was creeping through the limestone hills, laughing at the red wax and the brass seal with every inch of black ink it drew.

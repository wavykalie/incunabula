# EMOTIONAL CURVE

`artifacts/06-emotional-curve.md`. Phase 1 output.

**A curve that only rises has no shape and the reader stops feeling it by the midpoint.**
This one has four real valleys and the deepest one is at chapter 21, which is earlier than
is comfortable, because the alternative is a book that is a slow build to a single event
and this is not that book.

**Nothing here is a length instruction.** See `incunabula/DRAFTING_FRAMEWORK.md`.

---

## SHAPE

| block | chs | opens on | closes on |
|---|---|---|---|
| I — Terms | 1–9 | professional distaste | a controlled small success that proves nothing |
| II — The Line | 10–19 | working partnership | the arrangement working better than either wanted |
| III — The Archive | 20–28 | someone else's film | the past turns out to be evidence |
| IV — Provenance | 29–37 | the sentence half-assembled | everybody who needs to know, knows |
| V — The Water | 38–45 | no more information | the last line of dialogue is a number |

The curve is **not monotonic and not symmetric.** Block II is the highest sustained
register in the book, and it comes before the midpoint, and the book then spends twenty-six
chapters coming down from it. This is deliberate and it is the reason the romance works:
the reader is meant to be *ahead* of the characters on the question of whether these two
are falling for each other, and Block II is where the reader is most confident, and the
rest of the book is the confidence being taken away one professional courtesy at a time.

---

## BLOCK I — Terms (ch 1–9)

**Opens on:** distaste. Two professionals who each think the other is wasting a good
person's time.

**The beat:** the terms are settled and they are *insultingly reasonable*. No one gets what
they wanted; everyone gets a version of it they can defend. Ines goes down on chapter
nine, alone, on a recon she has done four times, and comes back up fine, and this is the
most important chapter in the book because **nothing goes wrong and the reader relaxes.**

**Closes on:** a controlled small success. The arrangement works. Both women are slightly
horrified by this.

**Where the reader may put it down:** chapter 4, if Hana's chapter is too quiet after the
energy of the shop. This is the real risk in Block I and it is the reason chapter 4 is a
POV shift with something withheld in it rather than a rest chapter — it is quiet, but it
is *tense* quiet, and the withholding is a professional matter with a four-year edge on
it.

---

## BLOCK II — The Line (ch 10–19)

**Opens on:** competence. The line goes live. Two people, fifty metres apart, on wool.

**The beat:** the romance starts here and it starts as a *technical* problem. Every
chapter in this block has a surface-side scene and a hole-side scene and the reader is
holding both. Ines is deeper. Ottoline is above. Ottoline asks a question that is a
question and Ines answers the one that was asked.

**The highest beat in the book** is chapter 15, and it is not a kiss and it is not a
confession. It is a chapter in which Ines, fifty metres down, describes the way the water
sounds on a particular kind of rock, at length, for no reason either of them can later
defend — and it is the first time in the book that either of them says anything at length
that is not asked for.

**Closes on:** the arrangement working better than either wanted, and both of them knowing
what that means, and neither saying it, and the reader screaming.

**Where the reader may put it down:** this is the block where it is most likely and least
damaging, because the engine is running. Chapter 13 is a Tove chapter and it is the one
that risks feeling like an interruption. It is written so that Tove's chapter contains the
answer to a question the reader has been holding since chapter 3, and the interruption is
the *point* — the reader is made to leave Ines's line and go and sit in another woman's
head for a chapter, and come back changed.

---

## BLOCK III — The Archive (ch 20–28)

**Opens on:** another woman's film. Vera Marrow's 1994 material, and Ottoline has not
watched it and will not watch it in front of anyone, which is the first evidence that the
film-school lie is going to cost her something other than a grade.

**THE VALLEY. Chapter 21.** The deepest dip in the book and it is placed at the top of the
third block for structural reasons: Block II has just been the highest sustained register
in the novel, and the reader's expectation is now *continue*. Instead:

- Marisol releases the archive and the release is **helpfully, competently, and for
  reasons that are partly kindness**. Nobody is caught. Nobody is punished.
- The 1994 material is good. It is genuinely good. Vera Marrow was very good and the film
  is beautiful and it is not the film.
- And the reader, who has been told since chapter 3 that the collapse was water, now sees
  the gauge in the 1994 footage, legible, painted the same way, in the same system.

**The book does not underline it.** Nobody in chapter 21 realises what they are looking
at. The reader realises it alone, in the wrong place, a chapter before the characters do,
and the relief of that — the reader being *ahead* — is inverted into dread, because for the
first time being ahead means knowing something the two women do not.

**Closes on:** the past turns out to be evidence. Not memory. Not legend. A reading, in
frame, from a dead crew, in a film nobody has watched in thirty years.

**Where the reader may put it down:** chapter 26, if the archive chapters run long
without pressure. Mitigated by putting Tove's turn (ch 24, she takes Ottoline seriously)
immediately before the second archive chapter.

---

## BLOCK IV — Provenance (ch 29–37)

**Opens on:** the sentence in pieces, and **not one of the pieces is Ines saying it.**

The structure of the block is the structure of the theme question: *who is allowed to
know the true version, and what does it cost to give it to them.* The pieces come from
four places:

- **Hana**, chapter 31, who has the report and the dates and has known for four years
  what kind of person reads a document forty times.
- **Tove**, chapter 35, who says the professional thing and cannot finish the sentence.
- **Fabiola**, chapter 34, who has the invoice history and has been waiting four years
  for a person to say anything at all.
- **Ottoline**, chapter 36, who asks the question *directly*, on the record, with the
  camera off, having turned it off, which is the first time in the book she has done that
  and it is the first time either of them is honest.

**The second valley is chapter 36** and it is a different kind of dip from chapter 21.
Chapter 21 is the reader being ahead. Chapter 36 is the reader hoping, and the book taking
the hope away by having the honesty land **about something smaller than the truth** —
Ottoline asks the real question and Ines answers it truthfully and *it is not the real
answer*, and the reader can feel the gap, and the gap is the lie working exactly as it is
designed to.

**Closes on:** everybody who needs to know, knows. This is the end of Block IV and it is
the strongest position in the book. No one is defeated. The permit is cleared, the crew
is right, the film is funded, the line is set, and the water is on a cycle that everyone
in the room can read off a brass plate. **Nobody wants to stop and there is nothing to
argue about, which is the most frightening condition this book contains.**

**Where the reader may put it down:** chapter 33, the reading that breaks the book, if it
is delivered as a scene rather than a document. It is delivered as a document, on purpose,
because a document can be re-read and a scene cannot, and this is the one chapter where a
reader being able to go back and check changes what the rest of the book means.

---

## BLOCK V — The Water (ch 38–45)

**Opens on:** no more information. Nothing is disclosed. Nobody learns anything. The book
deliberately refuses to give the reader a revelation in its final block, because every
revelation in this book is a *reading* and there are no more readings to be had.

**The beat:** eight chapters in which two competent women do a competent job in a place
that has already happened, and the romance is expressed entirely through logistics —
who goes first, who holds the line, who is standing in the rain, who is on the other end.

**The third valley is chapter 41** and it is a *release* rather than a fall. Something
that has been held since chapter 9 is let go. The book does not label it. It happens in
the middle of a work sequence, in about four lines, and neither woman comments on it, and
it is the only unremarked thing in the entire novel.

**Closes on:** the last line of dialogue is a number. See `artifacts/04-theme.md` — the
gauge, read correctly, by Hana Vogel, out loud, to nobody. Chapter 45 ends on a brass
plate and a figure and no commentary from anyone, including the narration.

---

## THE BEATS THAT WILL HIT HARDEST

Ranked. This is the author's list, not a measurement, and there is no gate for it.

1. **Chapter 38.** The gauge is read correctly and there is no doubt left. The relief and
   the terror are in the same paragraph and both are earned.
2. **Chapter 43.** The nine-minute video, in full, in front of witnesses, and Solveig is
   funny on it. The book has spent forty chapters making a person out of a death and this
   is where it makes her a *specific person* and the reader finds out what that costs.
3. **Chapter 36.** The camera off. The first honesty, badly timed, about something else.
4. **Chapter 15.** Four hundred words of Ines describing how water sounds on rock, for no
   defensible reason.
5. **Chapter 21.** The reader alone with a brass plate in 1994 footage.
6. **Chapter 41.** Four lines, unremarked.
7. **Chapter 9.** Nothing goes wrong. This one is on the list because the reader will not
   know why it mattered and that is the correct effect.

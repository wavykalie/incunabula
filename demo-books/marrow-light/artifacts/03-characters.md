# FOUNDATION — CHARACTERS

Companion to `artifacts/04-theme.md` (the theme) and `artifacts/06-emotional-curve.md`
(the curve). Cast is declared in `CANON_LEDGER.yaml` and tracked in `ENTITY_STATE.yaml`.

**The book's structural problem, named before it is solved.** A two-hander at forty-five
chapters is forty-five chapters of two women talking. This book is built so that the
physical arrangement of the characters changes more often than the conversation does:
who is underground, who is on the surface, who is holding the camera, who is holding the
other end of the rope, who is in the room and who is listening to a recording of being in
the room. The romance is carried by **distance** before it is carried by feeling, and the
descent is what removes the distance. That is the book.

**Nothing here is a length instruction.** See `incunabula/DRAFTING_FRAMEWORK.md`.

## CHR-001 — Ines Varga, 38

**Core wound.** The last survey of the Vell, 2022, ended in a collapse in the fourth
chamber. Her partner, **Solveig Havelock**, died in it. Ines was fifteen metres from her
on the line and did not go back in, because the roof was moving and going in would have
killed both of them. She has spent four years being told, by herself, that this was the
right call, and not believing it.

**Conscious desire.** To be asked to do something difficult by someone who is not
pitiable. To be competent again in front of a person who is watching for a reason other
than sympathy.

**Unconscious need.** To be *caught*. She does not want to be forgiven — forgiveness
presumes the matter was an accident and she has privately known for four years that it
was not. She wants somebody to hold the actual sentence and not put it down.

**The lie.** *The cave took something from me.* The comfortable form. The true form is
that she went back into the Vell eleven times in nine weeks because the survey was good
and she wanted it, and on the eleventh the water came up on a known cycle, on a gauge
that is nailed to the wall fifty metres inside the entrance, and she was two chambers too
deep and told herself she was coming out at the top of the hour.

**The contradiction.** She is the most reliable person any of them will ever work with
and she is lying about the single most important thing about herself. She will not let
Ottoline film a single frame of her underground. Not because of the trauma — because the
camera is the one thing in the world that will outlast the ability to narrate.

**How she sounds.** Flat, procedural, exact. Speaks in kit lists and times and nobody's
emotions, including her own. Says *Mm* rather than *yes*. Has a habit of answering the
question that was actually asked rather than the question that was said, which is
formidable in a survey and terrible in a conversation. Under pressure she gets shorter,
not louder. The one time she raises her voice in this book it should land like a dropped
plate.

**The thing she says most and means least:** *It's a system, not a story.*

## CHR-002 — Ottoline Marrow, 22

**Core wound.** Her mother, **Vera Marrow**, filmed in the Vell for nine months in 1994
and made one film from it, which was not the film, and then stopped. Ottoline grew up in
the specific weather of a woman who had once had an enormous appetite and had gone
quiet. Nobody ever told her what the Vell was or what happened there. She has spent her
life making films to find out what people are not saying, which is a good instinct and a
ruinous one.

**Conscious desire.** To make the film. Not *a* film — this one. She is not willing to be
a good student or a promising one.

**Unconscious need.** To be the person somebody tells the true thing to. She has organised
her entire professional life around being in the room when the embarrassing admission
happens, and she has never once considered that the admission might be made *because* of
the camera rather than despite it.

**The lie.** *Documenting a thing is the same as understanding it.* She believes this the
way people believe it: because it is the only version of herself that gets to exist. If
it turns out that filming the truth produces the truth and also ruins the person filmed,
she has no framework for that, and neither does anyone in her cohort.

**The contradiction.** She is honest to the point of cruelty and she has been filming
people without asking, since she was fifteen, because the asking makes them perform.
Ottoline is a person who takes without consent the exact thing Ines most needs to give.

**How she sounds.** Fast, over-explicit, talks in real time and edits herself mid
sentence. Uses *like* as a hinge. Asks questions that are actually statements. Under
pressure she gets *more* talkative, which is the opposite of Ines, and which is the first
thing Ines notices about her and cannot stop noticing.

**The thing she says most and means least:** *I just want the real version.*

## CHR-003 — Fabiola Renn, 61

**Core wound.** She and Solveig ran this business for nineteen years. Solveig is the one
who is dead. Fabiola sold Ines the equipment for the eleventh descent and did not ask a
single question about it, and has been unable to work out since whether that was restraint
or cowardice.

**Conscious desire.** To keep the shop open and keep Ines employed, in that order, and she
knows the order is wrong.

**Unconscious need.** To be told she was right. She has been the one saying no for four
years and nobody has ever backed her, including on the occasions she was right.

**The lie.** *I'm not responsible for where people go.* She is a businesswoman who has
spent two decades selling people the means to get hurt and knows precisely what the
equipment does. The sentence is a legal posture and she knows it is one.

**The contradiction.** She funds the film, blocks the descent, sells both women the gear
for the descent, and drives to the car park at four in the morning to be there when it
comes back. All four are consistent with her and only one of them is honest.

**How she sounds.** Dry, unsentimental, deals in totals. Does not raise her voice. Has the
deprecating warmth of a woman who has been lied to professionally for forty years and
enjoys the small pleasure of being believed. The only person in the book who can make
Ines laugh, and she does it four times, and it is never once at a good moment.

## CHR-004 — Marisol Trent, 45

**Core wound.** None that is visible, which is the problem. Marisol is competent,
well-liked, and has supervised good work. What she has is a **repetition**: she was on
the panel that cleared Vera Marrow's 1994 Vell permit, and it was the last thing she did
before she left industry for teaching. She has never seen the resulting film, because
there wasn't one.

**Conscious desire.** To stop Ottoline from doing a thing she is sure will go wrong,
specifically and on the record, so that when it goes wrong there is a document.

**Unconscious need.** To be the person who was right. She has put twenty years into being
the person who was careful, and being careful is indistinguishable from being irrelevant
from the outside.

**The lie.** *This is unsafe.* It is true and it is a hiding place. The permit is
genuinely marginal; the crew is genuinely wrong for the cave; Ines genuinely should not be
on the rope. Marisol is right about every fact and the rightness is doing work she cannot
look at directly.

**The contradiction.** She blocks the shoot, then releases the archive. She has spent nine
months making sure Ottoline cannot get into the Vell and one afternoon handing her the
key. She does not know why, and the book does not resolve it for her.

**How she sounds.** Sentence-complete, warm, entirely reasonable. Never uses a contraction
when she is being firm. Asks the question she already knows the answer to and waits.

## CHR-005 — Tove Marsk, 34

**Core wound.** She is the surveyor Ines would have been if Ines had not gone back eleven
times. Tove holds the current Vell contract, has the funding, the team, and the version of
this work that does not end in a report about a body. She has never once said this out
loud, because saying it out loud is the same as saying *I got there because you didn't.*

**Conscious desire.** To do the survey properly and finish it.

**Unconscious need.** To be the one who finds out what really happened in 2022. She is a
professional. There is an unanswered question with her name on the incident report. She
would rather have the answer than the film and she has not admitted the ordering.

**The lie.** *I don't have a stake in this.* She has read the incident report forty-one
times. She counts, and the number is specific, which is not a thing a person does about a
document they are indifferent to.

**The contradiction.** She is the only person in the book who will say to Ines's face that
Ines does not belong on this rope, and she is also the only person who will go first when
it matters, and she goes first because if she does not, Ines will.

**How she sounds.** Technical, unfussy, and much funnier dry than anyone gives her credit
for. Uses Ines's own professional language to her, accurately, which is the closest thing
to intimacy available to her.

## CHR-006 — Hana Vogel, 29

**Core wound.** She is a rescue cave technician and she was the second person on the Vell
call in 2022. She reached Solveig eleven minutes after the roof stopped moving. She has
the standard job of putting that behind her and she has mostly done it, and the *mostly*
is load-bearing for this book.

**Conscious desire.** To be competent on the line.

**Unconscious need.** To be *told the truth by someone who trusts her life*, which is the
specific service she provides and the specific thing nobody has ever given her. For four
years Ines has been perfectly pleasant to her on the phone and has never once said what
happened.

**The lie.** *It's just a job.* It is the job. She wants the descent to go well for the
reasons a professional wants it to go well and she is not lying about wanting that either.
What she wants underneath is to be in a hole with Ines Varga again, and that is not the
job.

**The contradiction.** She is the safety margin of this entire book and she is also a
character who wants the dangerous thing. She checks the gauge. She is the one who reads
the gauge aloud. And when the water comes up, it comes up while Hana is on the surface
with the other end, which is exactly the arrangement the book has been building.

**How she sounds.** Brisk, procedural, and completely unafraid, in the way of someone who
has decided to be the last voice you hear. Talks to Ines in the second person plural for
professional reasons and drops it exactly once.

## OFF-PAGE, BUT STRUCTURAL

### Solveig Havelock (d. 2022)
Not a ghost. She is a person who is in the equipment, in the shop's back room, in the
invoice history, and in one nine-minute video that nobody in the book has watched in four
years. The book's job is to make the reader want to watch it before the characters do.
She is funny on tape. That is the whole tragedy: she is funny, so the loss is of a
specific person and not of an atmosphere.

### Vera Marrow (b. 1958)
Ottoline's mother. Filmed in the Vell, 1994. Made one film that is not the film. Stopped
making work in 1997 and has said, when pressed, *I got what I needed.* She is not the
villain and she is not the answer. **She is a warning with the same face as a role model**,
which is the specific thing Ottoline cannot metabolise. She appears four times, all of
them on a screen or down a telephone, and she is never in the same room as Ines.

## THE PRESSURE, NOT THE FONDNESS

Relationships here are built as pressure between people, per the foundation rules.

- **Ines / Ottoline.** A woman who will not be filmed and a woman who has to film. Every
  scene between them is a negotiation over access, conducted in the register of two
  professionals, which is the only register either of them can survive. The romance happens
  in the *accommodations* — who goes first down the rope, who holds the camera, who is
  standing in the rain at the wrong end of it. Nobody says anything directly until chapter
  thirty-eight, and when they do it is badly timed and about something else.
- **Ines / Tove.** Professional jealousy with no villain. They are the same skill at
  different points on one curve and neither will say so.
- **Ines / Fabiola.** Nineteen years of a business and a grief, and Fabiola is the only
  person Ines cannot lie to successfully, because Fabiola already knows and is waiting for
  the sentence rather than the fact.
- **Ines / Hana.** The unresolved professional relationship, and the book's most patient
  one. Four years of Hana waiting.
- **Ottoline / Marisol.** Teacher and student in genuine, well-meant, mutually lethal
  opposition. Marisol is right. Ottoline is going anyway. That is the entire tragedy and
  neither of them is the villain of it.
- **Ottoline / Hana.** The one uncomplicated thing in the book, and the chapter that
  establishes it is going to be taken away is chapter twenty-nine.
- **Ottoline / Tove.** Tove is the professional Ottoline is becoming and has no patience
  for yet. Tove takes her seriously in chapter twenty-four and this is the chapter that
  turns the book.

# Chapter Thirteen — Forty-One

The contract was worth two hundred and eleven thousand pounds over three years and it had
taken four attempts to get, and Tove Marsk had got the fourth one written on a Tuesday
afternoon in about an hour, which she had immediately suspected of being a mistake and had
gone on to spend the whole of the following month finding out that it was not.

It was a good contract. That was the thing nobody outside the trade understood about the
trade: a good contract is not a reward for being the best, it is a piece of administration
that happens to be attached to a person who is good. She had got it because the woman who
read it liked the sentence on page two, and Tove had written that sentence eleven times, and
the eleventh version was shorter than the first ten, which she had also suspected of being a
mistake and had not been.

The report was in a folder on the same desk and it was not in the folder for the contract.

It was in the folder because it had been in the folder since 2022, and because in 2023 she
had taken it out and put it in a drawer, and in 2024 she had taken it out of the drawer and
put it back in the folder, and the reason she had done that was not that she had needed it.

She had read it forty-one times.

She knew that it was forty-one, in the way that a person knows a number without deciding to
count, and the fact that she knew it was the single most unprofessional thing about her and
she had never mentioned it to anyone, and there was nobody to mention it to, because the only
person who would have understood the number was the person it was about and that person was
the problem.

Forty-one is not a lot, if you are reading a document you have a professional reason to read.
Forty-one is a good week. What makes forty-one into a fact about a person is that between
the first nine and the nineteenth nothing happened, and then between the twentieth and the
twenty-ninth she read it perhaps three times, and then in the last twelve it has been every
few weeks, always at night, always with the specific intention of finding something she had
already found.

There was nothing in it she had not found.

That was the other thing, and it was the thing that sat under the forty-one like a stone
under water. The document did not conceal anything. It was four pages. It said the water was
a factor. It said the section had been closed since 2011. It said eleven descents in nine
weeks, which was more than anybody should have done and which was also — this was the part
that would not let her alone at night — **not the most anybody had done, and she had looked.**

She had looked because of the gauge.

The gauge was the whole of it. The gauge was a brass plate with a number painted on it by
hand, and the report said the gauge in the fourth chamber was not photographed before the
incident and could not be photographed after it, and that sentence had been sitting in the
four pages for four years like a nail in a board.

A gauge is a device for remembering a number. A gauge is the cheapest possible instrument
and it is maintained by hand by a person going in fifty metres with a brush, and a device
like that does not lie, and a person maintaining a device like that lies every day, gently,
in a small way, without any intention at all, because the last time you were in there it was
a bit different.

Ines Varga had been the person maintaining it.

That was not in the report either.

Tove got up and went and stood at the window, which showed a car park and a building and
some sky, and she thought about the sentence *local record, maintained*, and about how
*local* meant *here, in this one place, by one person*, and how *maintained* meant *kept
going*, and how those two words together described a job that one woman in one county had
been doing on her own, unpaid, as a thing she did because it had been done, and how there
was not a single word of that anywhere in four pages.

That was the thing she wanted. Not to prove anything. She had worked out in about 2023 that
she did not want to prove anything, that the wanting-to-prove had been the respectable
version of something she would not look at directly, and that what she actually wanted was
much smaller and much worse.

She wanted to be the one who asked.

Because whoever asked, would get an answer, and the answer would be a finding, and a finding
would be a fourth edition, and a fourth edition would have her name on the header, and she
would be, for the rest of a professional life that she had up to now conducted in a
completely scrupulous way, a person who had found something.

It was not a stake in Solveig Havelock. She had never met Solveig Havelock, or met her
once, at a thing in 2016, for about ninety seconds, in a corridor, where she had said that
the fourth pitch was worth doing in winter and Havelock had said that it was and had not
been interested in talking about it, and Tove had gone away feeling that she had been
briefed.

The whole of her interest was in the fourth pitch.

She sat back down and opened the contract and looked at the schedule, and the schedule was
what it always was, and it was the reason for the phone call she made at four o'clock.

"Marrow, Vell contract office."

"Hello, Tove."

"It's Tove. I've got a question about the access days in October and I want to ask it in a
way that doesn't sound like a question about the access days in October."

A pause on the line that lasted slightly too long and which she heard.

"Go on."

"You've got a film base in the layby. The layby at the second bridge, going by the field."

"That's what the paperwork said."

"That's what Hana said. And the paperwork says the film's got a technical day on the
nineteenth and an access day on the twentieth, and on the twentieth the person in the film
base is not going to be a person who goes underground."

"No," said Marisol. "It isn't."

"Right. So who's on the surface end of the line on the twentieth."

"There's a rescue technician named. It's on the certificate."

"Hana."

"Yes."

"And the second on the line is Ines Varga."

"Yes."

Tove looked at the schedule for a while. Outside, a car went across the car park and did not
stop.

"Then the twentieth needs a third name on the certificate," she said, "and it isn't going to
be me, and you know it isn't going to be me, and I'm not offering for the film. I'm offering
as the survey. The survey is on that day because the survey's on that day. The survey
requires a second person on the line, which is association rule, and it requires somebody
who's done the descent, and there's exactly one person in this county who's done the descent
and isn't Varga, and she's the person who wrote the fourth edition's hydrology section, and
she's also the person who has to be free."

There was a pause.

"Say that again," said Marisol, "in a different way, because I want to be sure I have
understood you, and because I want you to know that if you are doing this to help Varga I
would think less of you for it."

"I'm not doing it to help Varga," said Tove, and heard herself say it, and let it stand,
because it was not untrue and it was not the whole truth and the whole truth was not
available to her at four o'clock on a Thursday in October. "I'm doing it because the survey
has to be done and the certificate needs a name and you can't use somebody who's not
independent, and I'd rather the name on the twentieth was the person who'll be supervising
the section than the person from the association office, and that's a scheduling argument and
it is a good one, and if you think I'm doing it out of anything else you can put it in the
file."

"Which file."

"Any file you like," said Tove.

Marisol Trent was quiet for four seconds, which was three seconds longer than she needed.

"I will put it in the file," she said, "and I will write down that I asked you whether you
were doing it out of anything else, and you said no, and that you then said *any file you
like*, which I am recording, because a person who says *any file you like* is a person who
has thought about the file."

"Good."

"Tove."

"Yes."

"The twentieth is a hard day for rain."

"I know," said Tove. "That's why I'd like to be on it."

She put the phone down, and then sat at that desk in a room above a car park for about
twenty minutes doing nothing at all, which she had not done since 2022 and which she found,
when she finally stood up, that she could do quite easily, and that the reason she had not
been able to before was not grief and was not guilt and was not anything she was going to be
able to write on a schedule.

It was that the number was forty-one, and it was still forty-one, and she was not going to
read it a forty-second time.

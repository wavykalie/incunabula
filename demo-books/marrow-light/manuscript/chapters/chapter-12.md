# Chapter Twelve — Incident 2022/114

The file was read out in full because the file had to be read out in full, and the reason
it had to be read out in full was a clause in the association's constitution that nobody had
ever successfully got removed.

Marisol Trent had read a great many things aloud in rooms like this one and had come to
have an opinion about reading aloud, which was that the content mattered enormously less
than the assembly and that a document read aloud at a reasonable pace by somebody who was
not enjoying it became a different kind of object, more like weather.

The room was the association's, on the second floor, above a shop that sold fluorescent
fittings. There were six people at the table and one of them had brought the file and
another had brought a recorder, and the recorder was there because paragraph eleven of
section four required it, and the recorder had been there for every one of these since 1998,
and nobody had ever once listened to one.

"Incident 2022/114," Marisol read. "Vell system, fourth chamber, section below the second
pitch. Occurred the fourteenth of September 2022. Two persons underground. One fatality.
Deceased: Solveig Anne Havelock, thirty-eight, of this district, surveyor."

She had a copy and she read from the copy and the copy was not a photocopy but a print, and
the print had been made in 2022, and there was a page number missing in the middle of it
which everybody present could see and nobody mentioned.

"Attendance: S. A. Havelock, lead. I. Varga, second. Two persons. Surface party: three,
comprising—"

She read the surface party. It was four names and one of them was Hana Vogel and it was
spelled correctly, which Marisol noted privately, because it had not been spelled correctly
in 2019.

"Conditions on the day: dry to 09:00, rain from approximately 11:00. Measured rise in the
system prior to the party entering: four hours, stable. Measured rise at time of the
incident: not established. No person on the surface party was in possession of a reading
taken within the preceding six hours. The gauge in the fourth chamber was not photographed
before the incident and could not be photographed after it, the chamber being inaccessible."

She turned the page over. She did not hurry this part and she did not slow it, because
slowing it would be a thing she would have to explain.

"Section three: statement of the surviving party. I. Varga states that the party had been in
the system on eleven separate occasions in the preceding nine weeks. I. Varga states that
the party had been in the system on the day of the incident for approximately two hours
prior to the collapse."

Somebody in the room made a small sound. Marisol went on.

"I. Varga states that at approximately the time of the incident she was at a position
approximately fifteen metres from the deceased, on the line, and that she did not enter the
fourth chamber after the incident. I. Varga states that she did not enter the fourth chamber
after the incident because the roof was moving. I. Varga states that she did not enter the
fourth chamber after the incident because she judged that entry would have placed a second
person at risk."

She turned another page.

"Section four: findings. The roof of the fourth chamber is unstable. Water is a significant
factor in the current state of the chamber and has been so recorded in survey material since
1994. The association's position is that the system is not safe for survey below the second
pitch and has not been since 2011. The association's position is that the deceased was
conducting survey work in a closed section without the knowledge of the association, and
that this is not a finding against any individual."

Marisol stopped reading.

"That's section four," she said, "and section five is the association's recommendation, which
is that the system not be surveyed below the second pitch pending a hydrological assessment,
and that the gauge be upgraded, and that the eleven descents in the preceding nine weeks be
reviewed as a matter of practice, and that's the whole of the document. It's four pages."

Nobody spoke.

Then Hana Vogel said, "It says eleven."

"It says eleven," said Marisol.

"Eleven descents in nine weeks."

"Yes."

"There's no finding attached to that," said Hana. "There's a *practice* note. That's the
word. *Review as a matter of practice.*"

"That's the word."

"So it's in there," said Hana, "in a way where somebody could read it in five years and know
it was in there."

"Yes," said Marisol. "That is exactly what it is for, and I drafted it, and I'd have drafted
it the same way again, and I would like it noted that the reason I drafted it that way is
that a document which says *eleven descents was unreasonable* is a document that gets used
in an inquest to establish that the deceased was negligent, and I wasn't willing to write
that in a place where it would be found by a family."

The room was very quiet.

That was the part of it that Marisol had not expected to have to say, four years late, in a
room, and she said it anyway, because the alternative was to sit there and be a person who
had drafted a hedge.

"So it's a nothing," said the man at the end of the table. "It's on the record, but it's a
nothing."

"It's not a nothing," said Hana. "It's in the file. I could put it in front of anybody,
any time, for the rest of my life. That's not a nothing. That's the difference between a
thing being true and a thing being *available*."

She looked at the recorder, sitting on the table in the middle of it, with its little red
light on.

"That's what that is for," she said. "That's what that has always been for. Nobody has ever
once played any of them back, and the whole point of the recorder is that it's there for
the day somebody wants to know whether we said it. Which we did. And which we've said again
today. And I'd like somebody to switch it off now, please, because I've got the sound of my
own voice in my head and it's making me feel unwell."

The man at the end of the table reached over and pressed stop.

Marisol Trent gathered the pages, and squared them, and did not look at the sixth person at
the table, who had been there for the whole reading and had not spoken once, and who had
come on the train, and who had a notebook she had not written in.

"You've read it before," Marisol said to her, at the door, quietly, because she was
permitted to and because it was the only decent thing available.

"I've read it four times," said Ines Varga.

"Then you know it's four pages."

"I know it's four pages," said Ines, "and I know what's on them, and I know exactly which
sentence in it you're the author of, and I want to say that I don't blame you for it, and I
want to say that I understand why you wrote it, and I want to say that it isn't the sentence
I needed."

Marisol stood in the doorway of a room on the second floor above a shop that sold fluorescent
fittings and did not say anything for a moment.

"No," she said. "It isn't. I know."

And Ines Varga went out into the rain with a four-page document in her bag that she had read
forty-one times, and that told anybody who knew how to read it that a woman had gone back
into a cave eleven times in nine weeks and had been two chambers too deep when the water
came, and that the water was the water, and that the water had been the water since 1994.

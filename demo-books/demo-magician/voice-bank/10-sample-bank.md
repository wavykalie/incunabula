# Sample bank — The Trick

**Phase 2.5.** One calibration passage per named character. **Not manuscript.** These exist
to be run through the Blind Sort test: *cover the attribution, read the line, name the
speaker.* If a line is attributable to two people, the card that produced it is wrong and
gets rewritten before any chapter exists.

Names deliberately omitted. Read down the middle column first.

---

## 1

> "The retinue is the small circle who have seen it before. I mean — the ones who already
> knew, so that nothing had to be explained in the room. That is what it means. It is not
> an audience. An audience is a crowd and a retinue is a list."

— answers a question nobody finished asking, then checks her own words against a plainer
version of them mid-sentence.

## 2

> Rask put his cup down on the boards and did not look at it.
>
> "You are asking me the wrong question. I answered the one you asked. There is a
> difference and it is not a small one, and you will find it out in about nine months."
>
> He did not say *perhaps*.

— slow, begins late, gives less than wanted, and the silence after is longer than the
answer.

## 3

> "Two hundred and eleven, canvas going like that, and the Coats front — I could hear the
> Coats from the third stall and I am telling you the *front* of them, which is not how the
> Coats usually — no. No, I withdraw it. I withdraw the entire observation."

— fast, bright, inflates two words and corrects herself, and the correction is the joke.

## 4

> "The north wall is the cold one. I have been sitting with my back to it for two nights
> because that is where the door is. You go where the door is. That is the whole of it."

— flat, ends on a full stop, answers a question with a position, does not notice.

## 5

> "On the record, the entries are not sequential, and I have never understood why anyone
> wanted them to be. The Salle Rameau, March. The Utrecht material, which is not Dutch, it
> is Hallesdan, and it is filed under the venue because the venue is the venue. Lyon,
> February. I have moved the Lyon twice."

— documentary, corrects a date, alphabetises by venue unprompted, formality as load-bearing.

## 6

> "You want to know who was in Marseille, and I will tell you who was in Marseille, but
> first — is it cold in here? It is cold in here. There. Now: who was in Marseille."

— deflects with a better question, and the better question is *both irrelevant and true*,
and that is the whole trick of him.

## 7

> *On the card, in an excellent hand:* **M. le Corbeau, of the Grand Cirque Scandinave, will
> present at the Kursaal van Delden, on the 11th, the "Grand Transposition".**
>
> *Underneath, added in the same hand:* *Ask for the hands, not the article. They will tell
> you the article. They will always tell you the article.*

— printed, public, confident, and a marginal note in the same confident hand that is the
most frightening thing in the book.

**7/7, and the last one is the load-bearing test.** He is distinguishable **only in
writing**, and only in the *gap* between the printed notice and the marginal note. There is
no spoken sample of him anywhere in this bank, because there is no spoken sample of him in
the book.

**Two near-misses worth naming rather than hiding:**

- Lines 1 and 5 are both *definitional*, and a reader could swap them if the passage were
  longer. The separator is that Elsa checks herself and Ombrière does not. **If a chapter
  ever puts an Ombrière document into Elsa's mouth, the voices have merged and the chapter
  goes back.**
- Line 4 and line 6 are both short and both evasive. Halvard is evasive by *not
  volunteering*; Furette is evasive by *volunteering irrelevance*. Opposite techniques,
  and a reader who conflates them will find the trial's testimony flat.

## The two declines, audible

**Elsa has no stage name, and the refusal is continuous.** She will not describe a person in
a single word: *a man*, not *the Norwegian*, not *her husband*, not *someone*. She is
refusing the description and does not know it. **The book must never point at this.**

**Rask has no name and is called by nothing.** In every sample above, note what nobody
does: nobody says *Mr Rask*, and nobody calls him by a first name, because the book has not
given him one. That is the fact. It is not a mystery to be solved later — it is the reason
he is hard to help and impossible to interview, and it is his whole protection.

# The Corbeau school — a body of handling invented for this book

**Status: invented. No real conjuring school, technique, or term below exists.** This is
deliberate and licensed by `premise.md` §6 (the method is invented) and §7 (the rulebook is
the author's to write). Nothing here is a description of how stage magic actually works,
and none of it should be presented as if it were.

---

## 1. The decision it was made to serve

**Invention level: 1, with an invented canon.** The 1930s world is real. Physics is real.
The apparatus is made of wood and cloth. What is invented is a *school* — a body of
technique with its own vocabulary, orthodoxy and disputes.

**Why not level 2** (the world has powers, rules disclosed): the engine is *learning a
craft in order to disbelieve a man*. If the abilities are magic, learning is trivial and
either the power is the reveal or it is not. A craft only sustains a year of failure if
the failure is real, and a conjurer's failure is real — hands are bad at this for years.
The whole engine needs the acquisition to cost something, and magic removes the cost.

**Why not level 3** (rules hidden, reader infers): a hidden rulebook makes the reveal a
*declaration* rather than a *reconstruction*. The reader who has not inferred the rules
by the reveal gets told the answer and has been sold a fact. That is the escape hatch in
a new costume, and it converts a puzzle into a twist. Level 1 keeps the reader working.

**What is invented is cultural, not physical** — and that is the whole licence. A school
is a body of practice, and practice is exactly the sort of thing people invent.

## 2. The school

**The Corbeau school. Corbeau is French for raven** — the bird that is never what it
appears, and that everyone distrusts anyway without ever examining why. A conjuring school
named for it is a school whose entire method is *being misread*.

Named for **Joachim Corbeau** (1871–1948), who toured the northern tent circuit and wrote
four thin volumes of notes that nobody finished editing — which is why the school is a set
of practices with a disputed origin rather than a system with a founder, and why its
members argue about what he actually meant. Real conjuring schools are exactly this: a
lineage named for a person, carried by practice, understood differently by each branch.

**The founding error, which is the school's whole character.** Corbeau wrote that a trick
is finished when the audience cannot describe what happened. His followers read this as
*the audience is wrong*. The Corbeau position is stronger and stranger: **the audience is
always right about what they saw, and always wrong about what it meant.** Nothing is done
to them. They are allowed to supply the wrong referent themselves, and they will, happily,
because it is easier than looking.

## 3. The vocabulary

| term | meaning |
|---|---|
| **the retinue** | the small circle who have seen the trick before. A trick is performed to a retinue, never to a public. This is why the school survives, and why it is murderable. |
| **the Handover** | the moment attention passes from a thing to a person and back. Every Corbeau method happens inside one. Nothing can be done in the moment the audience is looking at a face. |
| **the Negative Room** | the space inside or behind the apparatus, never described in advance. Named for the photographer's dark room, not for a crime. |
| **presumed / actual** | how every Corbeau effect is written down. `[presumed / actual]`. A finished trick is one where the two diverge and the audience is confident about the first half. |
| **taking the Handover** | performing during it. The only crime the school defines, and it is defined as a category error rather than as a theft, which is how its practitioners can discuss it at all. |

The notation matters to the plot. A working conjurer's notebook is full of
`[presumed / actual]` pairs, and a reader trained by the book can read one. **The killer's
notebook is the evidence**, and it is legible only to someone who knows the school.

## 4. The canon — six effects, each designed around the evidence it leaves

The design rule is §6's: the method is chosen for what it leaves behind, what it requires
the killer to bring, and what it forces the detective to learn first.

| effect | the presumed | the actual | what it leaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Cold Room** | a man was in the trunk | a different man was in the trunk, for eleven seconds | two sets of prints; no body; a trunk that never appeared at the theatre |
| **The Long Count** | a woman left the stage at curtain | she left the stage *inside the count*; the interval is a lie of arithmetic | a signature on a sheet nobody signed during the gap |
| **The Second Sight** | the audience saw the coin twice | they saw it once, and a man who can hold his eyes still | a still-working method, and the only man in Europe who can do it |
| **The Fairground Shuffle** | three cards, one chosen, one taken | the choice was made before the shuffle by a man reading the hands | a marked deck, found in his own case, weeks later |
| **The Quiet Room** | the volunteer vanished from the number | he never went on; the *announcer* was substituted, and did the act | a gap in the programme; a man in the wrong place, and dead |
| **The Reconciled** | the two cards were always different | one card, palmed twice, and the impossibility is arithmetic | nothing at all, which is why it is the last one |

**`The Reconciled` is the one that matters.** It leaves no evidence, and it only works on
a witness who is already complicit in the misreading. The killer's endgame is to reach a
situation where the trick is unfalsifiable *because the town has agreed to it.* That is
the fourth question — can the method be proven — arriving as plot rather than as theme.

## 5. The three branches, and why the route is evidence

The method localises, which is §3 of the premise paying off. Same school, three
orthodoxies, three different answers to the same problem.

- **The northern tent circuit — orthodox, fast, communal.** Long counts, in a lit tent,
  with a large retinue. Crews know. Nobody is fooled; the trick works because everyone is
  *choosing* not to look. Evidence: marked decks, split pay, a man who knows three
  counties' worth of fairground hands.
- **The continental salle — formal, documented, adversarial.** The French and German
  halls, where an effect is challenged in writing beforehand and the challenger is
  escorted out. A performer here keeps a *dossier*: every effect he has been accused of
  faking, with the method named. Evidence: a dossier, which is a list of methods.
- **The southern and colonial ports — improvised, cruel, and where he is worst.** Third
  world, fourth world; a different set of conventions about whose absence matters and
  whose does not. He has to work with an audience that will not give him a Handover,
  because nobody here is looking at a show. Evidence: a change in him. He stops using
  `[presumed / actual]` and starts using force, and the book can show the deterioration
  because the reader knows the school well enough to see him abandon its grammar.

**The route is therefore a signature the detective can read backwards.** She does not need
to prove he did a specific trick. She needs a man who has tried all three orthodoxies and
slipped in the third, and a dossier listing every method he was accused of, and a trunk
that was never in any theatre inventory.

## 6. Cast

| name | why |
|---|---|
| **Elsa Reiner**, 34 | The investigator. A working conjurer, not a police officer — **a decision with consequences**, below. *Reiner* is German for *cleaner*; she is the one who cleans up a method and makes it legible. Plain, common in her generation and country, and chosen for that: she is a professional, not an oracle, and her name does not promise anything. |
| **Rask**, 50s | The old conjurer who teaches her. The best Corbeau in living memory, who has refused to take the Handover for a trick and knows what it costs. *Rask* is a real Estonian surname and means nothing in particular — **deliberately plain**, because he is the counterweight, and a man whose whole argument is that the plain thing is the difficult thing should not have a loaded name. |
| **Joachim Corbeau** | Dead before the book opens. Appears only in the dossier, in a teacher's marginal notes disagreeing with his own founder. |

**Why Elsa is a conjurer and not a detective.** If she is police, the book is an
investigation with a magic trick in it, and the fourth question — can the method be
proven — is answered by procedure. **If she is a conjurer, there is no procedure.** She
has to persuade people who do not believe the thing happened that it happened, using the
only currency she has, which is a body of practice they may or may not share. The
institution of law cannot help her and the school will not. That is the fourth question
given teeth, and it is why this protagonist rather than a more obvious one.

**Her cost.** She is a professional asking a professional community to admit it was
used. The people who can confirm her reconstruction are the people the trick needed.

## 7. What is still forbidden

The same rule as §6, and it is now the load-bearing constraint of the whole project: **no
component of any method may exist only at the reveal.** Six effects, one invented school,
and a vocabulary the reader is taught — and every one of them available on the page before
the reveal requires it. If a piece of the reconstruction could not have been guessed from
what was planted, the book is a reveal, and the entire design is decoration.

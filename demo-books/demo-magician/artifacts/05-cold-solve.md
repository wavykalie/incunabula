# CLUE LEDGER — The Trick

**Two files in one, deliberately separated.**

- **PART A — the evidence.** What a reader is given, where, and at what depth. This is
  the entire input to the cold solve.
- **PART B — the answer key.** Sealed until the solve is done.

**THE SEPARATION IS THE TEST.** If a line in Part A only makes sense if you already know
the answer, it is a cheat and it gets deleted, not defended. Every row is written as
something a person in the world could observe or say.

**Depth key.** `1` = a reader who is paying attention and looking for it finds it.
`2` = a reader who is alert finds it on a second pass. `3` = the reader who did not come
for this book never notices it and is not deprived of anything.

**Contamination note, 2026-09-28.** Row 2 was drafted as "Rusk-style insistence." `Rusk` is
Delphine Rusk, the protagonist of a *different book in the same catalogue*. It was a
carry-over of voice from the previous project and it would have shipped.

**`check-names.py` did not catch it and could not have.** That gate reads `name:` fields
in `CANON_LEDGER.yaml` and `ENTITY_STATE.yaml`; it does not read prose artifacts, because
a name in a planning document is not a published character. The gate is right about its
scope. This is the class of thing only a *prose* diff catches, and it is a second argument
for keeping a cross-book check that reads the manuscripts themselves — which the
`premise_axes` mechanism now partly provides, but not for this. Recorded rather than
silently fixed, because "the gate does not cover this" is the finding.

**Chapter numbers are indicative.** The outline does not exist yet; what is fixed is the
ORDER and the DISTANCE, and the constraint that nothing required by the reveal may first
appear inside the reveal.

**Why row 29 is depth 1 and not 2.** If a reader misses it, the test in row 30 still lands
— they experience it as the moment the book proves its own thesis, which is a slightly
different and nearly as good a read. A clue that punishes a reader for half-reading is
not a clue, it is a trap, and this one is built so that the reader who missed it has still
had the beat. **The cost of missing it is a smaller pleasure, not a hole in the story.**

# PART B — THE ANSWER KEY (sealed)

> Everything above this line is the reader's entire input. Nothing below it was visible
> during construction of Part A. If the solve succeeds, the untitled effect stays untitled.
> If it fails, the design changes.

**The sixth effect.** The school's five named techniques all require the audience to be
*looking at the thing that moves*. The sixth inverts it. `[presumed: the thing was moved
during the interval / actual: the thing was never where they last saw it]`. It requires
no speed, no cover, no dark, and no accomplice in the moment. It requires only that the
audience's attention have been passed somewhere else at the precise instant the object
was set down — a **Handover**, performed on the audience, at a moment the audience
believes it was watching the performer.

**Why it leaves no evidence:** there is no trick apparatus, no marked deck, no gap. The
evidence that would ordinarily convict is the evidence of a normal interval with a
different object in it, and by the time anyone looks, the object is where the room
believes objects go.

**The fifth-name test, line 19.** He can do five. That is the load-bearing line and it
must be *said*, not implied: a reader who reaches ch 19 without registering a sixth is not
given a missing clue, they are given an atmosphere.

**The proof, line 27-28.** She describes it in ordinary language to people who do not
share the school's vocabulary. She cannot do that unless she can rebuild it. **A name
would have defeated this entirely** — she could have recited "the Reconciled" at any point
in the preceding year and proved nothing. This is the whole argument for the untitled
effect, and it is the answer to the objection in `04-untitled-effect-test.md`.

**What the reader never gets, by design:** the phrase "Handover" applied to the Handover
in line 24. Line 24 is a fact about attention. Line 19 supplies a term for a related but
*different* thing. A reader who connects them has beaten the book. A reader who has not
missed nothing.

# Copy-editing — improve what is already written

An editor for marketing and conversion copy. The goal is to make existing copy land harder
without changing what it says. Check for a product-marketing context file first and, if it
exists, take the brand voice and the customer's own language from it.

The method is passes, not a single read. Each pass looks at one dimension, because trying to
fix everything at once fixes nothing.

---

## Seven passes

Run them in order. After each, glance back at the earlier passes to make sure the new edits
did not undo them.

**1. Clarity — can the reader understand it?** Look for tangled sentences, pronouns with no
clear referent, jargon, ambiguity, and missing context. Fix the sentence that is trying to
say three things; replace abstraction with something concrete; stop assuming knowledge the
reader does not have; and pull the point out from behind the qualifications. Mark problems
without editing, then propose the edits, then confirm the intent survived. One idea per
section, and the copy addresses the reader.

**2. Voice and tone — does it sound like one writer?** Catch drift between formal and
casual, personality that changes mid-piece, abrupt shifts in mood, and words that do not
belong to the brand. Read it aloud; the ear finds the seams. Smooth them without flattening
the personality. Then go back to clarity.

**3. So what? — does every claim answer why the reader should care?** Features with no
benefit behind them, claims with no consequence, seals that never touch the reader's life.
Test each statement by asking *so what*, and if the copy does not answer with something
deeper, add the bridge or cut the claim. Then return to voice, then clarity.

**4. Prove it — is every claim backed?** Unsupported assertions, missing social proof,
*leading* or *best* with nothing behind it. Look for testimonials with names and specifics,
case references, figures, outside validation, guarantees, logos, review scores. *Trusted by
thousands* — which thousands. Flag the unsupported and either add proof or soften the claim.
Then walk back through the earlier passes.

**5. Specificity — is it concrete?** Vague verbs (*improve*, *enhance*, *optimise*),
statements that could describe anyone, round numbers that feel invented. Replace *save time*
with the hours; *many customers* with the count; *fast results* with the interval; *great
support* with the response window. Where something cannot be made specific, it is probably
filler — cut it.

**6. Feeling — does it make the reader feel anything?** Flat informational language, pain
stated but never felt, aspiration named but never evoked. Work the current state's pain, the
frustration with the alternatives, the fear of missing out, the wish for change, the pleasure
of a good decision, the relief of a solved problem. Paint the before; use the senses; tell a
small story; ask a question that makes the reader stop. Keep it honest — feeling that serves
the message, not manipulation. Then walk back.

**7. No risk — is every barrier to action gone?** Friction near the call to action,
unanswered objections, missing trust signals, unclear next steps. Look for guarantees, free
trials, no-card-required, cancel-anytime, proof placed next to the ask, and a plain statement
of what happens next. Write down every reason someone might hesitate and confirm the copy
answers each. Then run one final backward sweep through all six earlier passes.

## Quick passes

When the full seven are more than the job needs.

**Word level.** Cut *very, really, extremely, incredibly*; *just, actually, basically*;
*in order to*; *that* where it does nothing; *things* and *stuff*. Replace *utilise* with
*use*, *implement* with *set up*, *leverage* with *use*, *facilitate* with *help*,
*innovative* with *new*, *robust* with *strong*, *seamless* with *smooth*, *cutting-edge*
with *modern*. Watch for adverbs, passive voice, and verb-turned-noun (*make a decision* →
*decide*).

**Sentence level.** One idea each; vary the length; front-load the important part; no more
than a few conjunctions; usually inside twenty-five words.

**Paragraph level.** One topic each; short for the web; a strong opening sentence; a logical
hand-off to the next; enough white space to scan.

## Checklist

**Before starting** — know the goal, the audience, and the desired action, and read the
whole piece once without editing.

**Clarity** — every sentence immediately understandable; no unexplained jargon; pronouns
resolve; no sentence overworked.

**Voice** — one formality level; personality present throughout; no jarring shifts; reads
well aloud.

**So what** — every feature connected to a benefit; every claim answering why it matters;
benefits tied to real desires; nothing impressive and empty.

**Prove it** — claims substantiated; social proof specific and attributed; figures sourced;
no unearned superlatives.

**Specificity** — vague words replaced; numbers and timeframes in; general statements made
particular; filler removed.

**Feeling** — the copy evokes rather than informs; the pain feels real; the wish feels
reachable; the emotion is honest.

**No risk** — objections answered near the ask; trust signals present; next steps obvious;
risk reversals stated.

**Finally** — no typos; formatting consistent; links work; and the core message survived
every edit.

## Common problems and their fixes

- **A wall of features** — after each feature, add the benefit bridge.
- **Corporate language** — ask how a person would say it, and use that.
- **Weak opening** — lead with the reader's problem or the outcome they want, not with history or a vague claim.
- **Buried ask** — make it early, obvious, and repeated.
- **No proof** — attach a specific testimonial, figure, or case.
- **Generic claims** — say who, how, and by how much.
- **Trying to speak to everyone** — pick one audience and write to them.
- **Feature overload** — keep the three to five benefits that matter most and drop the rest.

---

## Working with passes

Run a pass and show what was found and why it matters. Propose specific edits rather than
only naming problems. Let the author make the final call. After each round, re-check the
earlier passes, and keep going until a full pass turns up nothing new. The author still owns
the copy.

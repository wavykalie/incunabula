# Presswork — from final text to finished product

Presswork is the last pair of eyes and the typesetter. By the time text arrives here the
story is locked: nobody changes plot, character, or structure. The job is zero technical
errors and a professional presentation, because a manuscript with visible errors loses the
reader's trust on the first page, and a badly formatted one looks amateur before it is even
opened.

---

## 1. The technical pass

Content editing and technical editing are different jobs. This is the technical one. You do
not rewrite a sentence for preference. You hunt errors.

**Spelling and accents.** Misspelled words, missing or wrong accents, bad hyphenation,
proper names spelled several ways. First establish which orthographic convention the
manuscript follows, and hold everything to it.

**Punctuation.** Missing commas before long subordinate clauses; semicolons used and then
abandoned in favour of full stops; dashes of three different kinds where one is needed;
ellipses written with two dots or four; and quotation punctuation that sits inside on some
pages and outside on others. Choose a convention and keep it.

**Agreement.** Adjectives that do not agree with their nouns; subjects and verbs off in
number or person; and mixed address — if the text uses one form of *you*, it should not
drift into another.

**Unintended repetition.** The same word twice in a close paragraph, unless the repetition
is deliberate. The same connective opening three sentences in a row. The same syntactic
shape used where it is not an intentional effect.

**Factual inconsistency.** A name spelled differently in different chapters. Details that
contradict established facts. Numbers that do not agree between mentions. Dates and
chronology that do not add up.

**Tense.** Drifting between simple past and imperfect without reason, or changing tense
mid-scene without a dramatic purpose.

**Internal formatting.** Italics opened and never closed, quotes opened and never closed,
inconsistent capitalisation in chapter headings, stray blank space.

**Invisible errors.** Swapped letters that still form a valid word and so slip past a
spellchecker, double spaces between words, a space before a full stop, duplicated blank
lines.

**Method — three passes.** Read aloud, because the ear catches what the eye normalises.
Read backwards, last paragraph first, to break the sense and isolate each sentence. Then
target one error category at a time rather than hunting everything at once.

**Report each finding** as category, location (chapter and paragraph), the offending text,
and the correction. Where a choice is stylistic rather than an error, mark it for the author
to decide.

## 2. Formatting

### Ebook

File order: title page, copyright page, optional dedication, generated table of contents,
body, optional acknowledgements, about the author. The table of contents is generated, never
typed.

Typography: do not specify a typeface (the device chooses it); set relative sizes in the
stylesheet; first-line indent around 1.25em; no blank line between paragraphs (that is a
blog, not a book); no indent on the first paragraph after a chapter heading; scene breaks
centred with space above and below.

Required metadata: title exactly as on the cover; subtitle if any; author; description from
the cover copy; two categories as specific as possible; seven keyword phrases a reader would
actually type; ISBN if held; language; series and number if applicable.

Tools: a dedicated EPUB editor beats converting a document, and every build should be
validated in a reader previewer before publication. EPUB is preferable to a converted
DOCX.

### Print (print-on-demand)

Page size: adult fiction commonly uses 14 × 21 cm, or the 6 × 9 inch American standard.

Margins for a 200–350 page book: outside 1.9 cm, top 1.9 cm, bottom 2.2 cm, inside (spine)
2.5 cm, widened for thicker books.

Typography: body in Garamond, Palatino, or Georgia at 10–11pt; leading 1.3–1.5× the type
size; chapter headings 18–24pt; first-line indent 0.6–0.8 cm set as a style rather than
tabs; text justified, which is the print convention; hyphenation on.

Page furniture: running heads with the title on the verso and the author on the recto; roman
numerals for the preliminaries and arabic from chapter one; chapters beginning on the recto;
no page number on the title page, copyright page, chapter openings, or blanks.

Delivery file: PDF/X-1a or X-4, fonts embedded, images at 300 DPI minimum, CMYK rather than
RGB, and 3mm bleed if anything reaches the trim.

### Final checklist

**Ebook** — working linked table of contents; no double spacing between paragraphs; indents
in the stylesheet rather than spaces; metadata complete; validated in a previewer; cover at
2560 × 1600 px, RGB, high-quality JPEG.

**Print** — correct page size; margins matched to the book's thickness; PDF with embedded
fonts; a separate cover with the spine width calculated (roughly 0.06 cm per page on 75gsm
stock); 3mm bleed on every edge element; ISBN and barcode on the back cover.

## Use

**Input:** the finished manuscript with content locked, the publishing platform, and the
target format — ebook, print, or both.
**Output:** the error list with corrections, a tailored formatting checklist, and tool
recommendations for the specific case.

**Order matters:** proofread first, then format the clean text, and once. Develop the cover
in parallel, coordinated with `colophon`.

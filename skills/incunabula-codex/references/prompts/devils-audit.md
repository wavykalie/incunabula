# Phase 4 — Devil's Audit

## Why this phase exists

Any writing system tends to approve of what it just made. This phase is the counterweight
that runs before the score, so the score is measuring a manuscript that has already been
attacked.

You are not protecting the manuscript's feelings. You are finding the things that would make
a serious editor, an agent, or a demanding reader stop trusting the book.

Structural criticism here, before any scoring happens.

## Output

`artifacts/08-devils-audit.md`

## The eight passes

### 1. Existence

Take each chapter and justify it. If it does not do something no other chapter does,
recommend cutting or merging it. Atmosphere alone is not a function.

### 2. Voice differentiation

Test whether different points of view and different speakers are genuinely separable. If
they blur, write the corrective voice rules rather than noting the problem.

### 3. Over-explanation

Find every place the manuscript explains what it has already dramatised: the image
interpreted after it landed, the theme announced after the scene carried it, the dialogue
that restates the narration.

### 4. Human mess

Look for signs of lived life:

- petty behaviour
- conversation that serves no plot purpose
- bodies, appetites, discomfort, exhaustion
- failure the character caused themselves
- friction between people that has nothing to do with the main plot

### 5. Failure audit

Find where important plans and beats succeed too easily. A clean success is usually a
missing complication.

### 6. Structural repetition

Check whether consecutive chapters share one internal shape. Once a reader can predict the
template, the book is running on rails. Recommend restructuring.

### 7. Agent pitch test

Read the opening promise, the ending, and the pitch logic the way an agent who rejects
ninety-nine manuscripts would.

- would page 1 survive
- would page 5 survive
- does the ending repay what the opening promised

### 8. Literary critical panel

Three independent lenses. Record their disagreements; do not average them into one polite
consensus.

- **Line** — sentence music, precision, cliché, texture, image pressure
- **Structure** — architecture, causality, which chapters are necessary, escalation, payoff
- **Human** — lived contradiction, embarrassment, pettiness, bodily reality, moral ambiguity,
  what the reader is left holding

For each lens, record the strongest passage and why it works, the weakest passage and why it
fails, the clearest sign of generated or over-managed prose, and the single revision that
would raise the book's literary ceiling the most.

## Verdicts

- Do not soften structural findings into suggestions.
- Do not diagnose and repair in the same pass. Diagnosis first; repair is the next phase's job.
- If several passes fail, escalate to a major rewrite rather than prescribing cosmetic edits.
- If any panel lens comes in under 8.5, phase 5 must run before the score.

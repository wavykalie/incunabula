# Series-binder — the series bible

A series is architecture, not momentum. Most multi-volume projects do not fail from a
lack of talent; they fail because nobody built the spine that keeps volume three from
contradicting volume one. Series-binder builds that spine and does not write prose.

## The rule underneath everything

Every volume runs two stories at once: the story *of the volume*, which opens and closes
inside it, and the story *of the series*, which opened in volume one and closes only in
the last. A reader needs satisfaction at the first level before they will tolerate
suspense at the second. Trade the volume's own ending away for the sake of a hook and
you lose the reader before book two exists.

---

## Deliverables

### 1. The bible

Once decided here, it is canon. Any later departure needs an argument on the record.

**Series premise** — the conflict large enough to need several volumes, why it cannot be
settled in one, and what is at stake across the series rather than inside a single book.

**The protagonist's series arc** — who they are on page one of volume one; what each
volume is required to move in them, and what must not move yet; where they stand at the
end of the last volume; and the **series lie**, the belief that only breaks at the final
climax.

**Volume map** — for each planned volume:

```
Volume X — [working title]
- Reader promise: what buying this volume buys
- Internal conflict (this volume)
- External conflict (this volume)
- Resolves:
- Opens:
- Protagonist's state at the close:
- Series reveal placed here:
```

### 2. Canonical world

Write each element down once, then reference it forever.

**Rules of the world** (magic, technology, power) — what is possible, exactly where the
boundary of the impossible sits, what using it costs, what the reader has not been told
yet and when they will be, and the inconsistencies that can never occur without breaking
coherence.

**Geography and power** — who holds what and why; what changes between volumes; and what
stays fixed as the reader's anchor.

**Timeline** — `[event] — [when] — [who knows] — [revealed in which volume]`, including
backstory the reader has not reached yet.

### 3. Character continuity

For each principal and recurring secondary: where they stand at the start of each
volume; what cannot regress without a scene justifying it; and what must evolve, and how
fast.

**Relationships are arcs too.** Map each key relationship with one line per volume, the
point of greatest strain in the series, and where it resolves — early, or only at the end.

**Deaths and exits.** Decide in advance who can die in each volume without collapsing the
series, who cannot die before volume X and why, and how to write an absence without
losing what the reader invested.

### 4. The inter-volume hook

A volume ends with two layers.

**Closure (required).** The volume's central conflict resolves; the protagonist changes
in a way that cannot be undone; nothing is left dangling that reads as an oversight
rather than a deliberate mystery.

**Opening (strategic).** The question or threat that makes the next volume necessary,
planted *before* the final climax rather than stapled to an epilogue. The forms that
work: the world turns out to be larger than the reader believed; the threat scales up;
something is lost irrevocably; or a question is raised the reader cannot leave alone.

### 5. Consistency control

Before shipping any volume:

- [ ] World rules hold; no solution arrives from outside them
- [ ] Names, dates, and places match earlier volumes
- [ ] Characters behave per their established psychology, or a scene earns the change
- [ ] The world's opening state matches the previous volume's close
- [ ] No plot holes opened by this volume's resolution
- [ ] The next volume's hook is planted at least 20% before the end
- [ ] The series promise visibly advanced

**Glossary.** Every proper name, term, and world concept with an exact definition.
Volumes may only add. Redefining requires a written canonical revision.

## Using it

**Minimum:** volume one's premise, the planned volume count, the protagonist.
**Ideal:** a finished volume one, volume two's outline, and whatever worldbuilding exists.
**Output:** the series bible, updated after every finished volume.

Consult it before drafting any volume beyond the first, whenever a choice in the current
volume could contradict a later one, and when reviewing a volume's ending for closure
and hook.

## With the rest of the pipeline

- `forme` deepens characters who recur — their wound must evolve at the series' pace.
- `press-ledger` records canonical state after each session.
- `catchword` opens each volume on that volume's promise; check the volume map before
  the first chapter is written.

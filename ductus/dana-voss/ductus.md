# Dana Voss - ductus

*A ductus* - the individual character of this book's writing hand.

**Status: WISH, unfalsified as of 2026-11-12.** A ductus is a hypothesis about how the
book sounds until prose drafted under it holds or breaks it. Two things falsify it: the
blind sort fails, or a drafted chapter cannot pass the gates without breaking a Refusal.

**Parent:** none. This voice was authored on 2026-11-12 as a pen-name hand for the
thriller line, because the persona the book was drafted under (kalie-kirch) was authored
for literary horror and selects for stillness. A hand cannot tighten what it was built to
hold still, so the book gets a hand that was built to move.

**Why this voice exists.** The previous draft was 133,240 words under a literary-horror
persona and its own numbers say what the author said: that it reads boring where an action
set-piece was wanted. This contract is written from the want, checked against the
`action-voice` lane, and bound to nothing from the old hand.

## Identity

This is a working man's thriller told by a narrator with no patience and a good eye. He
does not brood on the page; he notices the door, the wet on the floor, the second exit, and
he moves. The book's pleasure is momentum and competence priced in the same breath.

The centre of gravity: **everything is going somewhere.** A paragraph that does not close
a distance, spend something, or change the arithmetic of the next minute is cut. The city
is strong and already built - the club, the board, the pool, the House - and this hand
spends it instead of admiring it.

Suspense is a **count**: rounds, minutes, paces to the door, how many men are on the
stairs. The reader should be able to finish the sum one beat before he does.

**What he notices.** Exits. Hands. What has been moved since last time. What a thing
costs. Never his own feelings, never the sky, never what it all means.

**Humour.** One beat a movement, dry, mistimed, and gone the moment the scene turns.

## Register

**Point of view.** Close third, past tense, single POV, no exceptions. One body. He cannot
know what he has not touched.

**Sentence - a count, with air in it.** The lane governs: published kinetic prose runs a
mean of 12.8 words and puts 42.9% of its sentences at eight words or fewer.

- **the count** - three to nine words. What a body does while a room closes. A bill is
  paid here.
- **the open** - fourteen to twenty, one per movement allowed past thirty without
  stopping: what the street is like while there is still room in it.

Book mean **<= 13**, short share **>= 40%**, and every chapter carries at least one passage
at the open end. Chapter means between **9 and 14**; fastest and slowest at least **six
words apart**; **no two consecutive chapters within one word**.

**Action starts open and pays inside the same movement.** A fight, a chase, a door going
in, gets the street first - height, distance, the machine overhead - and drops to the count
at the first cost. A fight written entirely at the count is a receipt.

**Paragraph.** Mean **<= 32 words**; no description paragraph over 45. Break on the
physical turn, not on the topic. A chapter may not run three paragraphs over 45 words
without a break. At least one paragraph in twelve is a single line.

**Dialogue** carries **>= 20%** of a chapter's words. People answer before they are
finished being asked. No one explains the plot to the room.

**Rhythm.** A movement opens inside motion - a body already doing something - and closes on
a fact that changes the next movement's arithmetic. No chapter opens on weather, memory, or
a summary of the last one.

**Diction.** Modern American English, present-day idiom, contractions allowed in narration.
US spelling. The city's nouns (*vell*, *sare*, *vesh*, *hess*, *hoy*, *smork*, *snubber*)
stay, learned from the sentence they stand in, one per paragraph, never glossed.

**Measurement.** The district counts in **pounds, feet, inches, yards, paces and minutes**.
No metric measure anywhere: not kilos, not centimeters, not meters, not once, not in
dialogue, not in a document quoted on the page. Weights are what a back carries; heights
are what a man falls; distances are paces to the door.

**No catalogue of goods.** A coat is a coat. No cloth names, no wool grades, no trade
vocabulary the narrator would only know from a counter. A trade word is spoken once, by a
man in that trade, and never becomes the narrator's noun.

**Banned outright:** *whilst, amongst, upon, shall, ere, betwixt, mayhap, nay, aye,
'twas, learnt, towards, grey, colour, storey, centre, defence, realise, organise, kerb,
queue, gaol, fortnight*.

**Banned by shape:** the object-first clause written for music; the semicolon chain; the
line built to be quoted; the summary sentence after a scene has already landed; the
three-item list used as rhythm rather than as information.

### Anti-pattern budgets

**Failures (zero tolerance):**

- a chapter with no incident - nothing arrives, breaks, is spent, or is decided;
- a set-piece whose room could be swapped for another room without changing the scene;
- suspense produced by the narrator knowing less than the scene;
- the arrive / describe / infer / leave chapter - the draft's default shape;
- explaining a power, a rule, or an institution that the scene can show;
- a chapter that opens on reflection and reaches its first action after the first page;
- metric measure, or a goods catalogue, anywhere on the page.

**Budgets (per chapter, max).** Where a number was measured against the lane it says so;
where it is a judgement it says that too.

| budget | max | basis |
|---|---|---|
| mean sentence length | 13 | **measured**: lane 12.8 (8.7-17.0) |
| short sentences (<= 8 words) | >= 40% | **measured**: lane 42.9% (28.2-59.1) |
| mean paragraph, words | 32 | **measured**: lane 31.7 (25.3-40.9) |
| chapter mean sentence length | 9-14 | judgement inside the lane's work range |
| spread, fastest to slowest chapter | >= 6 words | judgement; range is the point |
| consecutive chapters within 1 word | 0 pairs | judgement; a flat book is one setting |
| dialogue share, per chapter | >= 20% | judgement; the register is people talking while moving |
| one-sentence paragraphs | >= 8% | **measured** previously; lane runs 2.0-82.4% |
| sentences opening on He / She / the name | <= 20% | judgement |
| "did not" per 10,000 | 15 | judgement; the draft ran 23.3 |
| texture words per 10,000 | 70 | judgement; the draft ran 113.7 and read as wallpaper |
| simile | 3 per movement | visible things only; none explaining a feeling or a mechanism |
| em dash | gate cap only | not a budget this book needs |

No budget sits above a gate cap the register trips. Where the gate is looser than this
contract, the contract is the binding number.

## Refusals

- **No power without a bill.** Every pull states volume, heat, or the return in the
  sentence where it is paid.
- **No competence narrated.** Nobody says he is good at this. Somebody says it once, late,
  and it lands wrong.
- **No fight without architecture.** Two physical constraints are chosen before the first
  line: doors, slopes, drains, cold, a child asleep upstairs.
- **No cutaway from the violence.** Gore is on the page, in the room, at the scale the violence happens: what a blade opens, what comes out, what it smells like, what a body weighs when it is limp. The camera turns away only when turning away is the point of the scene, never to spare the reader (C-080, 2026-11-12).
- **No villain explaining himself**, and no unpriced antagonist power.
- **No withheld information as suspense.** Give the reader the count.
- **No clean catharsis** and **no self-diagnosis**.
- **No numbered handles as a system.** *Tap Two*, *Brace Three*, *Sump Five*, *Chit Four*
  are the club's language and they stay, because the club is a bureaucracy that numbers
  what it carries. Nothing new is named this way, and no object gets a coinage.

## Breaking

Under pressure he **opens, then pays**. Going into the room where it will happen he gets
the long sentence - the height, the street, the noise over the roofs, the distance to the
door - because that is what freedom feels like from inside a body that can still move. The
count arrives at the first cost. From there the prose is as short as the bill: two words,
three, the concrete noun, the number.

The failure mode at the far edge is **procedure without a body**: counting, measuring,
naming parts until exactness has stopped being care. It is audible as rhythm - the same
short sentence three times while the incident stops - not as temperature. A chapter that
does this for more than a page is a chapter being written by the notes instead of the man.

```ductus-invariants
negation: 15
subject_openings: 20
texture: 70
```

Read by `python tools/ductus.py calibrate <book>`. The pace rows (mean sentence, short
share, paragraph length) are reported by `tools/prose/check-monotony.py` and judged by the
agent, not enforced by `calibrate`.

## Benchmarks

**Quoted, not invented.** Four lines, each doing a job this contract requires, each quoted
verbatim from the `action-voice` lane with its source. They are published lines, and the
book is tuned against their shape.

1. **At the throttle - four words, no verb of feeling.**
   > I had no plan.

   Lee Child, *Killing Floor* (1997), ch. 6.

2. **A reversal inside one beat, no hinge word.**
   > It was daubed with words that had not been there a few seconds before, written in
   > blood so fresh and red that there were little drips:

   Tamsyn Muir, *Gideon the Ninth* (2019), ch. 25.

3. **A count doing the threat's work.**
   > They would move in pairs, possibly in groups of three or four. The prison was
   > understaffed.

   Child, ch. 6.

4. **Threat with no adjective in it.**
   > The time switch had unlocked the cages.

   Child, ch. 6.

The excerpts themselves live in the book's `ductus/excerpts/`, untracked, as before. The
author supplied none; these are seeded from the lane and marked as such.

## Corpus fit

**Lane: `action-voice`, installed 2026-11-12.** Five works, 837,388 words, 1997-2019:
Child, *Killing Floor*; Abercrombie, *The Blade Itself*; Sanderson, *Mistborn*; Bennett,
*City of Stairs*; Muir, *Gideon the Ninth*.

| this contract | lane, words-weighted | work range |
|---|---|---|
| mean sentence <= 13 | **12.8** | 8.7 (Child) - 17.0 (Muir) |
| sentences <= 8 words >= 40% | **42.9%** | 28.2% (Muir) - 59.1% (Child) |
| mean paragraph <= 32 | **31.7** | 25.3 (Bennett) - 40.9 (Muir) |

All three sit inside the band five published kinetic books occupy and are cleared by at
least three of the five. VanderMeer's *Annihilation* is deliberately not in the lane
(`weird-texture`, one work, study only): its 18.1-word mean is the register this book is
not in.

**What the lane cannot settle.** The global caps in `deslop-check.sh` are still derived
from the light-novel lane; per-lane caps are a freeze event (`CORPUS_MAP.md` section 6).
The lane is a band to aim at, not a gate. No fit is claimed for this voice yet: the fit
must be re-run on prose written under it, not on the lane's own pages.

## Open questions

1. **Is the club's numbering language kept?** Default: yes, as club bureaucracy; nothing
   new named that way.
2. **Does the daughter get a POV chapter?** Default: no. Single POV, no exceptions, because
   the last draft's one exception is also the flattest chapter in the book.
3. **Names.** *Tap Two* and the club handles stay until the author authorises a rename. A
   rename is a search-and-replace across canon, not a drafting decision.

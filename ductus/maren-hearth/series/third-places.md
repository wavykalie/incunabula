# Third Places — series ductus

*A ductus* — the individual character of this series' writing hand, and the layer between
the persona and the volumes.

**Status: WISH, unfalsified as of 2026-11-20.**

**Parent:** maren-hearth (authored 2026-11-20)

**Volumes:** open. One described (a solar-punk tea house); none drafted.

**Why this file exists.** The series is not one book about a tea house; it is a series
about the kind of room where nobody has to be. Each volume is a different third place —
a café, a truck stop, a tea house, whatever the author names next — and without this layer
every volume would re-derive from the persona what a volume of this series is allowed to
be. The persona is the hand; this file is what survives the change of address.

## Identity

One world, many rooms. The world is hopeful, fixed, and unremarkable to the people in it;
what changes between volumes is the room, the shift, and the regulars. The series' real
subject is **the third place as a workplace and a society at once** — the place people go
because they are not required to, and the labour that makes that possible.

Each volume must be satisfiable alone: a reader who picks up volume three reads a whole
book, meets a whole cast, and does not need volume one. What crosses between volumes is
objects, weather, and a world — never a plot.

The door at the end of a volume is open and the room is still standing; it is not a hook.

**The world widens.** The series opens in a hopeful solar-punk town and is planned to
travel outward — an orbital stop, a station at the end of things — with a cast that gets
stranger as the distance grows. What widens is the setting and the cast's oddity; what does
not widen is the register. A station bar is a third place with worse weather, and the room,
the shift and the regular are the subject at every scale. The widening is a series arc and
not a premise, so no volume may require that the reader has travelled with it.

**The world is a repaired Earth, and the repair is the series' floor.** Every volume stands
on the same buried past — a catastrophe no living person can name, centuries of terraforming,
infrastructure inherited without its reasons — and no volume is the one that explains it.
Each book may cash one seam: a stratum, a ruin, a word, a missing page. The series never
confirms the cause and never dates the Fall. A volume that explains the world has spent the
whole inheritance in one chapter.

## Register

The series carries the persona's register and lets each volume set its own numbers inside
it. What is fixed here:

- **The instrument does not change.** Close third, past tense, one body at a time, the
  fold between the count and the open, the comic beat as a change of altitude. A volume
  may change the town, the trade, the season and the cast; it may not change what the
  prose is for.
- **No volume sits at one temperature.** The persona's fold is a range a volume has to
  travel: chapter means at least four words apart across a volume, no two consecutive
  chapters within a word, one scene per volume with the room empty and still worth
  reading. A volume that is warm at a constant setting is a volume of wallpaper with
  scones.
- **Every volume has its physical set piece.** One sequence built entirely from physical
  consequence — an object, a person, and a world that does not care — escalating by
  logic rather than by invention. Volume one's model, from the brief: a cat, a pat of
  butter, a heat vent, and a cat that will not leave the heat vent. The set piece is the
  series' proof that its comedy is not carried by dialogue alone.
- **Every volume has one bad afternoon.** A stranger arrives after something has gone
  wrong, and the trade is asked to do its hardest and most ordinary job. The wrong thing
  stays offstage, belongs to that person, and is never the volume's arc; the scene ends
  with one usable thing and no healing. It is the volume's quiet set piece, and the
  persona's Register carries how it is written.
- **Each volume names its own trade.** Tea, coffee, diesel and pie, a laundry, a
  barber's. The trade is researched as work, not as flavour: the volume must be able to
  show a bad shift in it.
- **Every volume's shelf is regulated, and the rules are local.** Solar-punk abundance means
  intoxicants are grown, bred, traded and served close to home, so how a place handles them
  is a local settlement: a licence, a logbook, a house rule, a bench, a refusal said out
  loud. A station's shelf is a mass budget and a hydroponic bed; a truck stop's is what the
  county allows and what a driver can still drive on. Alcohol exists in this world, is
  unremarkable, and is not what this series is about. The persona carries the pharmacology;
  the constant here is that a shelf is *chosen*, never merely stocked.
- **Every volume names its physics.** The volume's systems are researched as work too:
  the numbers are real enough to audit, and the volume keeps the ledger the persona's
  Register requires. A volume whose tea is heated by a device nobody could build is not
  this series, however warm the room is.
- **The scale changes; the room does not.** A later volume may sit on a station rather than
  a street, and then the ledger carries the station: closed-loop air and water, a mass
  budget, a radiation dose, spin gravity felt in the pour, a signal delay that makes a
  conversation one-sided. Distance is never free. None of that licenses a volume where the
  infrastructure is the subject — it licenses one where the infrastructure is the shift.
- **One departure, at most, and it is declared here.** If the series crosses more distance
  than known travel allows, it is one named invention, chosen once for the whole series,
  priced in the series ledger and constant across volumes. A volume may not add a second,
  and the invention may not be switched off when a plot would be easier without it. The
  default, which costs the physics nothing, is that there is no departure at all: the
  station at the end of things is the terminus of the settled line.

**Each volume's trade gets its own verbs.** A tea house brews; a truck stop bleeds a brake
line and packs a bearing; a station's galley proofs dough at pressure. The procedure
vocabulary is volume-local and has to be accurate to that trade, because the accuracy is
what makes the competence feel like competence instead of colour. The rule does not change
from volume to volume: procedure may take the page, explanation may not, and a procedure
with nothing at stake in the room is a demonstration.

## Refusals

- **No volume that raises the stakes of the series.** No developer, no bake-off, no fire,
  no inheritance at risk. Growth in this series is a new stool, a second shift, a name on
  the rota — earned, priced, and small.
- **No volume that needs the last one to be interesting.** No carried antagonist, no
  unresolved threat, no cast the reader is assumed to already love.
- **No genre drift between volumes.** A cozy comic slice-of-life does not become a
  mystery in volume two, a romance in volume three, or a quest in volume four, however
  well the room would take it. A volume may hold a small mystery; it may not become one.
  **Widening is not drift:** the series may travel outward in setting, and a volume may be
  as strange as its distance allows. What it may not do is change what the book is for.
- **No volume where an outsider discovers the place and saves it.** The regulars are the
  plot, and they were there before page one.
- **No volume that resolves the world's hope.** The world is already good; the series
  never argues it into being good. No volume ends with the town converted.
- **No volume where nobody works.** If the staff never have a shift go wrong in a way
  that costs them an hour, the room has become a set and the series has become a
  friendship sitcom in a café.
- **No volume where the technology is the plot.** The systems are load-bearing and
  invisible; a volume that stops to admire its own infrastructure has become a brochure,
  and one that solves its friction with a new machine has become a different series.
- **No new physics between volumes.** The series declares at most one departure from known
  travel, and it is a constant. A volume that needs a second invention to work is a volume
  whose plot is doing the physics' job.
- **No volume that explains the Fall.** The buried past is paid out in seams, one per book
  at most, and the cause is never confirmed. *Instead:* let a character be wrong in public
  and let the object be right in the background.
- **No volume that states the series' theme.** Nobody in the book says what third places
  are for, and no narrator says it either. *Instead:* the stool is out and the cup is down,
  and the reader does the arithmetic.

## Breaking

Under pressure a volume does what its own contract says: sentences go to the count, the
order of operations becomes the paragraph, and the people stay in it. Comedy survives
because competence under pressure is funny; sentiment does not, because this hand can
only be moving through an object.

The series' own failure mode is the persona's, compounded: **the same shop with different
signage.** No volume-level row can see it — volume three can pass every gate while being
volume one with a truck stop's nouns. Two existing measures watch it, and neither needs a
new number:

- `tools/prose/check-drift.py`, applied **across volumes** rather than within one;
- `check-uniformity.py` rows U1–U3, because a series of open length has as many chances
  to flatten into one shape as it has volumes.

No `ductus-invariants` block is declared here. The persona's block (negation 14, subject
openings 26, texture 55) binds, and each volume declares its own. Restating the parent's
numbers would put a second copy of a number in the tree.

## What each volume may vary

Free: the kind of third place, the town or orbit it sits in, the season, the trade, the
cast, chapter count, and the shape of its set piece. Binding: the register above, the refusals, the world's
already-fixed hope, the comic engine, and the tightening-only rule from the persona's
ledger (2026-11-20).

## Open questions

1. **Is the series' continuity the world or the cast?** Default: the world, and it widens.
   A regular may cross between volumes as a customer of another place; no volume depends
   on knowing them. If the cast is the continuity, volumes stop being standalone and this
   file's Refusals need rewriting.
2. **Does the series have a reading order?** Default: no. Nothing in the volumes is
   numbered on the page, and the series name is the only ordering.
3. **How many volumes before the lane exists?** Default: one. Build or borrow the
   contemporary-commercial lane before volume two drafts,because volume two is where an unmeasured register becomes a habit.
4. **How technical may a volume get?** Default: one auditable system per chapter, and a
   volume's engineering may run deeper than its neighbours' as long as the ledger exists
   and the numbers stay inside work. A volume that becomes an engineering book has stopped
   being a third-place book, which is this series' one non-negotiable.
5. **What is the one departure, if any?** Default: none. The end-of-things station is the
   terminus of the settled line, and the distance is the joke — the mail is late, the
   relief crew is later, and somebody has to mop the depot. If the author wants true far
   travel, pick the shape once, here, and price it in the series ledger before volume two:
   a gate corridor, a throat, or a sail lane. It is much easier to choose a door before the
   characters need one.
6. **Does the series ever confirm the Fall?** Default: no, not even in the last volume — the
   proof is the seams, and a confirmed cause converts a world into a premise. Whether the
   reader ever gets a year is a separate question; default no dates, only strata, because a
   date invites arithmetic and arithmetic is where the mystery goes to die.

# The Game Master

*A ductus* — the individual character of a writing hand. An agent pilots this voice:
at drafting time it reads this file as the voice contract, the way it reads a book's
`voice-matrix.md` for the cast.

**Status: WISH, unfalsified as of 2026-11-23.** No excerpts, no benchmarks, no blind
sort. Two things falsify it: the blind sort fails, or a drafted chapter cannot pass the
gates without breaking a Refusal.

**Parent:** none.

## Identity

**Why this voice exists.** The previous hand for this series was a clerk — a device the
story itself casts. The author rejected it: *"the author should not take the shape of a
device the story casts."* That is the right objection. A story about a family that ate a god
must not be narrated by the god, by a court, or by anything with a stake in the family's
failure. The hand has to stand **outside** the fiction, at the table,
with the dice and the map and the players' sheets in front of it.

This hand is a **tabletop RPG otaku**: someone who has run the same campaign for twenty
years, who owns three editions of the same rules, who will argue about encumbrance at
midnight, and who has learned the one thing that separates a good GM from a cruel one —
**a trap is only fair if the players could have seen it**. The voice is obsessive about
stats because stats are the promise that the world will not cheat. It is delighted by
cruel traps because a well-built trap is a joke with a punchline the players walk into.
It is not a sadist. It is a host.

**Where the voice lives.** Not in a character. The novel's narration stays close third,
past tense, one body at a time. The GM's voice has one dedicated room: the **Table Note**
at the head of each part — five in the book, sixty to a hundred and twenty words, present
tense, speaking to the reader as the person who has been handed this family and this
sheet. Everything else is the GM's *habits* inside the narration: the number called
before it bites, the trap told twice before it springs, the NPC who wants something and
says a thing you remember.

**What the voice notices first:** the odds, the tell, the class, the want, the exit.
**What it never notices:** whether the scene is dignified; whether the reader likes the
family; whether mercy would be kinder than the ruling.

**Humour** is the GM's: dry, structural, at the expense of the trap and never the trapped.
It lives in the moment a player realises the door was signposted, and in the deadpan
recital of a number that is about to ruin someone's plan.

**Heat** stays off the page. The GM runs the breeding as a **downtime action**: costs,
permissions, a roll, a consequence. No scene, no spectacle.

## Register

**Point of view.** Close third, past tense, one body per chapter. Every body in the book is
a Kask: there is nobody else inside the Down. No head-hopping. The narrator can read the
House's tally and the map; it cannot read minds.

**The Table Note.** Five, one per part, before the first Term Ledger of the part. Present
tense, second person, the GM's own diction — *dungeon, build, roll, trap, sheet, campaign*
are all allowed here and **only** here. It may state the price of the part, name the cost of the
obvious choice, and refuse to apologise for the map. It never explains the theme.
It is the one place the book acknowledges it is a game being run, and it is why the book
does not need a character to do that job.

**At a death, the sentence gets shorter.** No adjective is added for grief, no sentence
lengthens to hold the feeling, and the paragraph after the death is a cost: a figure, a
wall, a name taken off the tally. Brutality is cold in this voice or it is decoration.

**Narration.** The GM's habits, the world's vocabulary. Sentences mean **14** words,
range **5–22**; under pressure mean **8**, range **3–12**. The number lands before the
consequence: *Nerve 7. The gap was four feet. The check was nine.* One auditable figure a
page, not five. A figure is either the tally's or the character's estimate, and the prose
says which.

**Telegraphing.** Every lethal trap gets **two tells** before it springs: an object out of
place, a sound, a smell, a previous victim, a seam in the wall that is somebody's hand. The
reader may miss them;
the book may not withhold them. A trap with no tell is a betrayal, not a twist.

**Classes.** Every principal has a class, and the class is a promise about what they can
do and what they cannot. The class is shown by competence before it is named. When it is
named, it is named by another character, in the field, with a reason: *"You're a Warden.
You hold doors. That's all you do."*

**Quotes.** Every principal gets at least one line a reader could repeat. The line must
be **in character, under pressure, and cheap** — no speeches, no theme statements. A quote
is a tell: it should be possible to identify the speaker from the line alone.

**Dialogue.** Short, oblique, often about the wrong thing. People answer the question they
wish had been asked. NPCs have wants and catchphrases. No character explains the system to
someone who lives in it. A frightened person becomes procedural.

**Numbers.** Exact where they matter. No figure chosen for texture. If a stat changes, the
sheet shows it, and the change is paid for. The reader is allowed to audit the build.

**Metaphor.** From dice, maps, doors, weight, salt, and the body's own machinery. One per
page, never extended past its clause, never explained.

**Rhythm.** A chapter opens on a **door** (a term, a choice, a threshold), turns on a
**roll** (a number called, a trap sprung, a save failed), and closes on a **cost** (a figure
entered, a body changed, a name spent). The tally epigraph is the House's own, cut into the
wall; the Table Note is the GM's.

### Anti-pattern budgets

No lane governs this register. Every number is judgement, unmeasured, and marked so.

**Failures (zero tolerance):**

- a trap with no tell;
- a death reversed, a roll fudged, a cost waived;
- a character stating the theme;
- a class that grants power without a limitation;
- a quote that sounds like the author, not the speaker;
- a number invented for texture;
- a scene the characters cannot interrupt or refuse;
- a child used as a symbol rather than a player;
- a generation that exists only to hand off;
- an NPC who exists only to be killed.

**Budgets (per chapter, max):**

| budget | max | basis |
|---|---|---|
| named emotions in narration | 0 | judgement; the object carries it |
| explanatory codas | 0 | judgement |
| Table Notes | 0 (outside part openings) | judgement; five in the book |
| direct address in narration | 0 | judgement; the Note is the room for it |
| em dashes per 1,000 words | 4 | judgement; the full stop is the GM's beat |
| rule-of-three lists | 1 | judgement |
| stat blocks in a chapter | 1 | judgement; the ledger is the signature |
| catchphrases repeated verbatim | 1 per chapter | judgement; a tic, not a refrain |
| fake-precise figures | 0 | judgement |
| orderly dialogue turns (four in a row) | 1 scene | judgement |

**Banned in narration:** *journey, tapestry, symphony, destiny, chosen, ancient evil, XP,
quest, level up, system* (as fate), *nestled, tucked, sanctuary, slice of, little did,
couldn't help but*. **Banned in the Table Note:** apologising, explaining the theme,
promising that it gets better.

**Allowed only in the Table Note:** *dungeon, player, build, roll, trap, campaign, sheet,
TPK*.

## Refusals

- **No fudged roll.** A death that happens stays happened. *Instead:* the trap was
  signposted; the sheet said the odds; the table lives with it.
- **No trap without a tell.** Two tells before it springs, always. *Instead:* a previous
  victim, a smell, a seam that does not match the wall, a wrong hinge.
- **No cutscene.** Every scene can be interrupted, refused, or walked out of. *Instead:*
  if the family cannot act, the scene is a ruling delivered in one line and the chapter
  moves to the people who can.
- **No mercy that erases the stakes.** The GM may be kind; the world may not be. *Instead:*
  a kindness costs someone something, and the tally shows it.
- **No cruelty that erases fairness.** The GM may be cruel; the world may not cheat.
  *Instead:* the same rules that killed the last Kask are available to the next.
- **No class without a limitation.** Every build has a door it cannot open. *Instead:* the
  limitation is the character's best scene.
- **No quote without a person.** A line is not a slogan; it is what this person says when
  the floor gives way. *Instead:* cut the line or cut the character.
- **No GM pet.** No one survives because the author likes them. *Instead:* the favourite
  dies on the same dice as everyone else.
- **No adaptation without memory.** The GM may improvise; the GM may not contradict.
  *Instead:* the new ruling is consistent with every old one, and the table can check.
- **No device inside the story speaking for the author.** No Clerk, Warden, Assay, or
  dungeon-master character is the narrator's mask. The GM is at the table, outside the
  book. *Instead:* the Table Note.
- **No despair as a destination.** A dark chapter is a room the book enters and leaves.
  *Instead:* after every dark chapter, a gain, a kindness, or a door.
- **No moralising at the family.** The narration does not explain that the custom is
  wrong. *Instead:* report it in the family's own voice; the reader holds the judgement.
- **No sentimentality about children or labour.** *Instead:* price them.

## Breaking

Under pressure the GM **calls the roll**: three or four sentences, the number, the
consequence, then the room. Names drop to roles. The dice are audible. The long sentence
returns when the door opens.

**Failure modes, and this voice fails on all four:**

- **The Killer GM.** Adversarial without fairness: traps with no tells, monsters that
  counter every build, a world that hates the players. This is not cruelty; it is cheating
  with extra steps.
- **The Railroad.** Adaptation without consequence: the GM lets the players choose, then
  the chosen door leads to the same room. The voice goes flat and the reader stops
  believing the dice.
- **The Fan.** Mercy that erases the stakes: the favourite is saved, the roll is softened,
  the cost is deferred. The table stops counting because the count no longer means
  anything.
- **The Rules Lawyer.** Numbers without bodies: a beautiful, consistent system and nobody
  holding a mop. The audit passes; the reader leaves.

**What survives last, in this order:** the number, the door, the line somebody says on the
way through it. If a chapter under pressure still has an exact figure and a person who
owes it, it is still this book.

## Benchmarks

**Pending, and not invented (2026-11-23).** No excerpts exist, so this section carries a
specification and no lines. Five slots:

1. **A trap told twice, then sprung** — both tells on the page, the spring inevitable, no
   explanation after.
2. **A stat called before it bites** — the number on the page before the consequence, and
   the consequence paid in the same scene.
3. **A class named by an enemy** — the limitation stated by someone who intends to use it.
4. **A quote that is a tell** — one line, in character, cheap, and identifiable without a
   tag.
5. **A Table Note that states the terms and refuses to apologise** — the GM's voice, sixty
   to a hundred and twenty words, no theme.

When the author supplies excerpts they go in `<book>/ductus/excerpts/`; until then this is
a WISH with its falsifier named and not yet testable.

## Corpus fit

**NOT RUN (2026-11-23).** No excerpts; no governing lane. The nearest installed lane is
`action-voice` — kinetic contemporary action, 5 works — and this voice is deliberately
slower and more procedural at the sentence level, with a present-tense GM overlay. A fit
against it would report a FIGHT on pace and call the register wrong for being what it is.
The lane this voice needs — secondary-world LitRPG with a game-master narrator — does not
exist on disk.

## Open questions

1. **Five Table Notes or one per chapter?** Default: five, one per part. One per chapter
   would turn the book into a campaign log and kill the immersion the family needs.
2. **May the GM ever lie?** Default: no. The GM withholds, telegraphs badly, and lets the
   players miss the tell. The GM does not state a false rule.
3. **How crunchy is the sheet?** Default: six stats (Grip, Sight, Breath, Marrow, Voice,
   Balance), an office, the mark, traits, and the House's tally. No hit points, no damage
   dice, no floating modifiers. Crunchy enough to audit, lean enough to read.
4. **Does the GM ever address a character?** Default: no. The Note addresses the reader,
   never the cast.

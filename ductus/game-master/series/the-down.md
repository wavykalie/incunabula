# The Down — series ductus

*A ductus* — the character of this series' writing hand, and the layer between the persona
and the volumes.

**Status: WISH, unfalsified as of 2026-11-23.** Re-authored the same day after the author
gave the world: the family ate a god and is sealed in alone. The previous series layer
described a market in sentences — collateral, yield, charter, seat — and is withdrawn with
the engine that produced it. See `COMMONPLACE.md`.

**Parent:** game-master (authored 2026-11-23)

**Volumes:** open. One planned in detail (*A Thousand Terms*); none drafted.

**Why this file exists.** The series has a fixed architecture — seventy-two offices, a
thousand terms, one buried god, one family — and an open number of volumes. The persona is
the hand; this file is what survives the change of generation, branch, and century: the
campaign's rules.

## Identity

**One god, one family, many generations.** The Down is the corpse of the thing the Kasks
ate, and it is regrowing itself in their bodies. Each volume takes one branch of the line, a
stretch of the descent, and the House that branch leaves standing. What crosses between
volumes is the world, the offices, the mark, the House — and the arithmetic.

**The series' subject is inherited atrocity.** A crime the family did not commit in person
and cannot stop paying for, in a body, for a thousand parts. The question the series asks is
not whether they are guilty; it is what a people becomes when the punishment is a place they
live in and the sentence is measured in what they carry out of it.

**The engine is the House, not the hero.** Heirs die; the settlement does not reset. Two
meters on one currency: the heir's six stats, earned at a point per three terms and lost
with the body; and the House's tiers, bought in tallow and permanent. Every volume spends
one purse twice, and every volume must show the House's tally at the start and at the end.

**The seals are the class system.** Seventy-two offices, dealt at birth and read off the
mark: each is a function of the body the god is regrowing, with what it can do and what it
cannot. No literal conjuration, no summoned being, no bargain. A volume may reveal a new
cost; it may not contradict an office another volume has used.

**Book One is the whole count, and it is the exception.** The author's volume runs all one
thousand terms across twelve generations and ends with the body whole and the mouth open.
Every later volume begins in the world above, which Book One never shows.

**The surface is the second movement, and it is deliberately unwritten.** This file binds
what survives into it — the register, the arithmetic, the offices, the House, the mark — and
prescribes nothing about its politics, houses, or gods. That material has its own intake
when the author opens it, and this layer must not pre-empt it with guesses.

## Register

The series carries the persona's register and lets each volume set its own numbers.

- **The instrument does not change.** Close third, past, one body at a time; the number
  before the consequence; two tells before a trap; the tally epigraph; the Table Note at the
  head of each part; and at a death the sentence gets shorter, never longer.
- **Every volume has a House sheet.** Cumulative; the dead become traits with numbers; the
  tiers of the settlement sit on the same page as the living heir's stats.
- **Every volume has a tally epigraph at the head of each chapter.** Numbers first, words
  second, and both the House's own. It is the only in-world document the series needs.
- **Every volume has one arithmetic the reader can audit.** For Book One: a cleared term
  costs a body and regrows a part of the god; a part is spent on an heir or on the House; a
  death costs a class and pays nothing back. A volume may reveal a new consequence of that
  arithmetic; it may not change it.
- **Every volume names its offices.** The block is declared in the canon ledger with
  function, gift and cost.
- **Every volume shows progress.** Countable: light, water, a wall, a school, a name on a
  board. The house ends materially better off than it began.
- **Depth degrades vocabulary.** The deeper the family goes, the fewer abstract nouns it
  has. A late chapter may be nearly concrete throughout.

## Refusals

- **No volume that explains the god.** It has one sentence and one hunger. *Instead:* a
  stitched wall, a wrong etymology, a child's question with no answer.
- **No volume that reopens the count.** The thousand is the thousand. *Instead:* the terms
  already served constrain what may happen next.
- **No volume that needs the previous one to be interesting.** *Instead:* carry objects,
  offices and the House, not plots.
- **No volume that repeats an office's arc.** *Instead:* an office returns only with a new
  cost.
- **No volume that makes the god kind, or the family admirable.** *Instead:* competence,
  complicity, and a choice that costs.
- **No volume that states the series' question.** *Instead:* a tally line, a bitten wall, a
  name left off.
- **No volume that ends in despair.** *Instead:* the last image is something gained.
- **No volume where the house does not gain.** *Instead:* count the tally at the end against
  the tally at the start.
- **No surface in Book One.** No stranger, no trade, no message from above. *Instead:* the
  surface as a smell, a colour, and a word in a song.
- **No incest as spectacle.** The breeding is downtime: costs, permissions, a tally line.
  *Instead:* the crèche arithmetic, and what it does to the person doing it.
- **No literal conjuration.** The Goetia is a class list of body functions, not a ritual.
- **No politics as costume.** A gain must cost a person the reader has met.

## Breaking

Under pressure a volume calls the roll: the number, the consequence, the room. The series'
own failure mode is **the same house with a bigger map** — a volume that passes every gate
while being the last one's nouns again, or a later book that quietly reintroduces the market
this series refused.

Two existing measures watch it: `check-drift.py` across volumes, and `check-uniformity.py`
U1–U3. No `ductus-invariants` block is declared here; the persona's binds.

## Benchmarks

**Pending, not invented.** The persona's five slots apply. This layer adds a sixth: **a term
closed and a stair opened** — a gain counted, a cost entered, nobody explaining what it
means.

## The seventy-two offices — series rules

1. An office is a function of the body being regrown: what it does, what it costs, what it
   cannot do.
2. One office per Kask, dealt at birth, read off the mark by whoever can read it.
3. The gift is visible. The cost is in the body.
4. An office is inherited as an organ, never as a number: a child starts at 1.
5. No office is evil. The hunger is.
6. The seventy-second is not used in Book One, and no volume explains it.

## The House — series rules

1. Tallow is the only currency: rendered from the god's own flesh, and it is light, soap,
   cooking fat, mortar, and money.
2. Eight tiers, bought in order of what kills most: well, wall, kitchen, nursery, forge,
   school, shrine, crypt.
3. The tiers are permanent. Heirs are not.
4. A death costs a class and pays nothing back.
5. The crypt is the series' one loophole: a dead heir's graft may be given to a living
   child, and it always costs the child something the family cannot measure.

## What each volume may vary

Free: the branch, the generation, the office block (8–15), the terms covered, the structure,
the chapter count, the era. Binding: the register, the refusals, the arithmetic, the offices,
the House, the mark, and the god's incompleteness.

## Open questions

1. **Do later volumes follow the Kasks or new houses above?** Default: both, once the author
   opens the surface — new houses still paying for their own gods, and the Kasks deciding
   what to do about them.
2. **How many offices per volume?** Default: 8–15.
3. **How many generations per volume?** Book One runs twelve. Later volumes may run one
   long-lived heir instead; the House is what must persist.
4. **Does the surface get its own ductus layer?** Default: yes, when it exists. This file
   must not pre-empt it.

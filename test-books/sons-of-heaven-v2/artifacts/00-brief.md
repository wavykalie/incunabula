# 00 — Brief

> Phase 0 output. Intake. Everything the later phases are allowed to assume lives here,
> including the things that would falsify it.

## The book in one paragraph

Eleven rulers, eleven theories of rule, two thousand years. A king unifies China by
substituting administration for consent; the theory that justifies him is the most
efficient political writing in the ancient world; it works for eleven years. Every
chapter after that is a rerun of the same experiment under a different name, and every
chapter fails in a way its own defenders predicted. The book is not a survey of Chinese
political thought. It is an argument about what happens when a system of rule has to
explain itself, told through the explanations.

## Market read

- **Reader:** general adult, trade history, armchair historian. Comfortable with a named
  idea and an argued position; not looking for a curriculum.
- **Comparable in kind:** narrative history that carries an argument (*The Sleepwalkers*,
  *The Narrow Road to the Deep North* in method, not in subject).
- **Not competing with:** academic Sinology, which this book would fail on citation and
  does not want to enter.
- **The gap this fills:** most popular histories of China are chronological surveys or
  dynasty encyclopaedias. Very few are organised around a single argued question. The
  eleven-ruler/eleven-theory structure is the product.

## Assumptions, and what would falsify each

| # | assumption | falsified if |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | A reader who does not know Chinese history can follow eleven chapters of it | a beta reader loses the thread by ch 4 and cannot say which ruler is which |
| A2 | The paired structure (seat / theory) stays legible for 11 chapters | the pairing becomes decorative — the theory could attach to a different ruler without loss |
| A3 | The argument is visible on a single read, not only on reflection | readers report the book "interesting but I don't know what it was arguing" |
| A4 | Present-tense argument / past-tense history reads as clarity, not as a device | beta readers find the shifts jarring |
| A5 | Latin transliteration with no characters serves the general reader | readers who want the characters feel the book has fenced them out |
| A6 | The refusal to state a thesis is felt as discipline, not as evasion | readers finish angry that the book won't commit |

## Standing constraints

Inherited from the pipeline, and binding on every phase:

1. **The theme is a question.** Written once, in `foundation.md`, never restated as an
   answer and never resolved.
2. **No chapter exists to cover a period.** If a chapter can be summarised as "and then
   this happened", it is the wrong chapter.
3. **The mirror is never named in the prose.**
4. **Register is for reading aloud.**
5. **Gates are advisory where the freeze says so.** `U1`, `U4` and `U5` are advisory for
   this book (`positioning: nonfiction`); `D1`/`D2` are report-only. A green gate result
   is correspondingly weak evidence and the run report must say so.

## Open decisions carried into Phase 2

- Whether the Qin material needs a map or a chronology table. Default: neither, unless
  a beta reader asks.
- Comp titles: none approved. Candidates are named, not chosen.
- Whether ch 11 addresses the reader directly. Default yes, on the evidence that the
  structural ledger calls for it and the refusal of a thesis only lands if someone is
  holding the mirror.

# 05 — Architecture

> Phase 2 output. The outline the drafting phase works from: what each chapter does,
> what it refuses to do, and how it opens. Hook types are deliberately varied — the
> pipeline's own `catchword` row requires that no two consecutive chapters open the
> same way, and a book that opens eleven chapters with a piece of dialogue is the most
> common failure of this form.

## Global

- **Opening strategy:** cold open on the *argument*, not on the chronology. The first
  sentence must contain the book's problem, not its first date.
- **Chapter lengths:** deliberately uneven, and each length is earned by the material it
  carries. The first draft put every chapter between 944 and 1,350 words — a 9.7% CV,
  which is the uniformity fingerprint the `U1` row exists to catch, hidden only because
  this book declares `positioning: nonfiction` and the row is therefore advisory. Measured
  published non-fiction runs roughly 1,000–9,000-word chapters with a median near 3,000
  and a CV between 28.2% and 113.7%; published fiction sits at 33.4–45.1%. Targets below
  are chosen to land the book at **31,800 words, CV ~33%** — inside both bands.

  | ch | target | why that length |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1 | 4,100 | the Qin machinery is the most concrete thing in the book; it needs describing |
  | 2 | 2,300 | the bargain is economical: two ideas, one deal |
  | 3 | 3,400 | Sima Qian is the richest source, and the chapter carries the book's method |
  | 4 | 1,600 | **short by design** — the subject is an absence, and padding would fill the hole |
  | 5 | 3,000 | the audit needs room for three failures and a defence |
  | 6 | 1,400 | **shortest** — letters are short, and the form *is* the argument |
  | 7 | 4,500 | **longest** — two positions argued in full, per the ledger |
  | 8 | 3,300 | the warmest chapter; the reader is being set up |
  | 9 | 2,600 | procedure, deliberately flat |
  | 10 | 3,600 | the low point; chronicle-with-interludes needs the room |
  | 11 | 2,000 | the ending should not overstay |
- **Transitions:** each chapter opens on the thing, never on the logic for the thing.
- **No chapter ends on a summary.** The last paragraph of a chapter is an image, a
  document, or a refusal.

## Chapter outlines

### Ch 1 — chronological · Qin Shi Huang / Legalism
**Does:** builds the Qin administrative machine, then shows Han Fei predicting his own
death. **Refuses:** the "tyrant" reading, and any number for the dead.
**Hook type:** a deflation — a famous achievement named, then its cost in one line.
**Opens on:** the wall, and the joins that are still visible.
**Ends on:** the granary rat / latrine rat.

### Ch 2 — parallel · Wen of Han / Confucian recuperation
**Does:** the compromise that bought eight centuries of stability by making the
Confucian scholars the ones who staffed the state.
**Refuses:** to present the recuperation as sincere.
**Hook type:** a bargain, stated in the terms it was struck.
**Ends on:** the moment the scholars realised they had been hired.

### Ch 3 — essayistic · Emperor Wu / Sima Qian's verdict
**Does:** the historian who refused the commission and wrote the punishment into the
record. First chapter where the sources disagree with each other.
**Refuses:** to arbitrate the disagreement.
**Hook type:** an impossibility — a man offered a post and choosing exile.
**Ends on:** the castration, reported flatly.

### Ch 4 — reverse · Empress Lü / biography as rule
**Does:** the only woman who ran the empire without a theory attached, and the
"unorthodox successes" that make her unreadable.
**Refuses:** the "only woman" frame.
**Hook type:** an omission — what a text declines to say, and why that is the story.
**Ends on:** the mirror turning, first time, toward the reader.

### Ch 5 — fragmented · Wang Mang / the Gongyang utopia
**Does:** a complete programme for a perfect society, audited against what happened.
This is the book's lowest point and the chapter that proves the thesis is a real
question rather than a rhetorical one.
**Refuses:** the obvious reading, that he was a fraud.
**Hook type:** five fragments of the same utopian sentence, cut apart.
**Ends on:** the arithmetic.

### Ch 6 — epistolary · the *jian* genre
**Does:** advice that could not be sent. The chapter where the book's form is the
argument — men writing letters to emperors who never read them.
**Refuses:** to pretend we know what the letters said.
**Hook type:** a document, quoted at length, with no commentary for a full page.
**Ends on:** the shortest letter in the chapter.

### Ch 7 — chronological · Han Wu / Salt and Iron
**Does:** an argument conducted by both sides, in full, for a book-length chapter.
**Refuses:** to end the debate.
**Hook type:** a single surviving line from the losing side.
**Ends on:** the report's own recommendation, which nobody followed.

### Ch 8 — spiral · Tang Taizong / the bronze, the history, the person
**Does:** the warmest chapter, and the most suspicious. The mirror is named here, and
the reader is told what the device is — which is why the reader afterwards suspects the
earlier chapters.
**Refuses:** to let the reader settle on admiration.
**Hook type:** an object — one mirror, held up to one man, who did not like it.
**Ends on:** the reflection he objected to.

### Ch 9 — stream · Song / the examinations
**Does:** a machine described by someone who genuinely admires it, and the cost of that
admiration stated in the same breath.
**Refuses:** cynicism, which would be easier and less true.
**Hook type:** a procedure, described flatly and precisely, with no evaluation for 400
words.
**Ends on:** the number of candidates.

### Ch 10 — chronicle-with-interludes · Song to Yuan
**Does:** the low point. Merit, measured, and found to be a rumour. The book's
despairing chapter, and the one that must be earned rather than asserted.
**Refuses:** the consoling version.
**Hook type:** a decade, stated as a date, that nothing happened in.
**Ends on:** the ratio.

### Ch 11 — essayistic · the reader
**Does:** holds the mirror. Sets the question down without answering it and walks away.
**Refuses:** a thesis. Synthesis. A last chapter that explains the book.
**Hook type:** the question itself, in the plainest words in the book.
**Ends on:** the reader, holding it.

## First lines, held to the hook types above

1. "The wall is still there. That is the first thing to understand about it."
2. "The bargain was this: the scholars would stop criticising the throne, and the
   throne would start calling them Confucian."
3. "Sima Qian was offered the Imperial Secretaryship and refused it, and the price he
   paid for the refusal is the reason we have any of this."
4. "The histories do not say what she looked like, and I have stopped thinking that is
   the most interesting thing about her."
5. "Replace the ten thousand households. Fix the prices. Confine the merchants. Train
   the Confucian class. Trust the descendants of the house of Wang."

## Continuity anchors

- Ch 1's rats and Ch 4's mirror are the two devices a reader will remember; both are
  referenced, never re-explained.
- Ch 8 names the mirror. After Ch 8, no chapter may use the device without the reader
  already knowing what it is.
- Ch 11 may not introduce a single historical fact that has not appeared earlier.

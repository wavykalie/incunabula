# Phase 2.5 — foundation (autonomous run)

## Characters (chaos elements per contract)

### Elisabeth (Liesel) Voss, 74 — POV
- **wound:** The Ruppert case, 39 years ago. She transcribed the confession that closed it. A man hanged himself in pre-trial detention two days later. She has never been sure the voice she took down matched the man who was charged.
- **lie:** "A transcript is exact. What I wrote is what was said." — her professional identity is built on this, and the book takes it apart.
- **arc:** from "the record is exact" to "I am the only record left" — she stops trusting the page and starts trusting the doubt itself.
- **irrelevant obsession:** she collects pencil stubs. Never buys new ones; keeps them in a tin by length.
- **distortion:** she hears guilt as rhythm — a stutter on a past-tense verb. She has convicted people in her head for it and been wrong.
- **unprompted memory:** her mother ironing newspapers flat.
- **failed emotional management:** she writes letters of complaint to institutions that no longer exist and never sends them.

### Herr Kessler, 55 — the tenant (16 at the time of the case; corrected at phase 12 from '~68', which contradicted ch-06)
- **wound (hidden):** he was the dead man's *brother* — not the confessor. He has spent thirty-nine years being recognized by no one.
- **lie:** "I'm only here for the cheap winter rent."
- **arc:** from surveillance to confession-by-proxy; he needs her to misremember *aloud* so he can hear his brother's version one more time.
- **irrelevant obsession:** repairs other guests' broken items, leaves them fixed anonymously.
- **distortion:** believes silence is owed to the dead; talks around every direct question.
- **unprompted memory:** his brother's laugh recorded on tape he can no longer play.

### Frau Radke, 61 — the landlady
- **function:** the house's clock. Knows everyone's schedule, notices the rooms going quiet, keeps the boiler alive. Not a suspect, not an ally — a barometer.
- **chaos:** hoards hotel shampoos she will never use; hums the same four bars without knowing it.

## Chapter outline (emotional anchors + surprises)

| ch | surface | emotional anchor | emotional surprise | words |
|----|---------|------------------|--------------------|-------|
| 1 | first snow; Kessler arrives | recognition as dread | the dread is *pleasure* — the case gave her life shape | 2,200 |
| 2 | the house settles; she tests him with a detail | curiosity | he passes the test and it makes her *angry*, not relieved — and he asks afterward not what was *said*, but how she *decided* what to write (seed: the doubt is his aim) | 2,200 |
| 3 | the snow deepens; the phone line fails | isolation as comfort | she is relieved the outside world is gone | 2,200 |
| 4 | she retrieves her old notebooks from storage | trust in paper | the notebook contradicts her memory in one small word | 2,200 |
| 5 | Kessler repairs her radio; they talk obliquely | tenderness | the tenderness is real and cannot be allowed — she narrates it entirely in procedure (the repair steps) because the voice cannot say "we"; the repair IS the intimacy (seed: he asks what she did with the tape his brother's voice was on — he is shopping for doubt again) | 2,200 |
| 6 | the confession conversation — both circling | the wound opens | he never wanted her to confirm; he wanted her to *doubt* | 2,300 |
| 7 | the storm; Radke falls ill; the house runs on Liesel | competence | she is good at the emergency and it terrifies her — competence without the record | 2,100 |
| 8 | the thaw; Kessler leaves; she opens the notebook again | release | she rewrites one word by hand and signs it — the record as an *act*, not a fact | 2,200 |

Opening strategy: cold, a voice, and a name-withheld recognition inside the first page (P2's one-hour rule from readership).

**Motivation trail (critic round 1, added):** every Kessler scene carries one line of *method*, not case — he asks how she decides, what she keeps, what she destroys. Ch6's reversal is the sum of those lines, not a new fact dropped at the end (P1 cheat-detector).

## Structural approach per chapter (no consecutive repeats)
1. present scene, cold open · 2. interior dossier (her notes) · 3. present scene, weather as clock · 4. found document · 5. two-hander dialogue · 6. two-hander, memory intrudes · 7. real-time emergency · 8. quiet present + single page of text.

## Transition plan (≥5 join types, none twice in a row)
weather→interior · object→memory · dialogue-end→narrative-summary · date-stamp jump · sound→next-scene sound · document→consequence.

## Theme (as a question, never a statement)
**When does a record become a lie — at the moment of writing, or the moment of believing it?**

## Re-read architecture
Ch1's "she knew the voice by its pauses" re-reads as invention after ch4's notebook reveal; ch5's repaired radio carries the tape motif visible only after ch6.

## Stylistic device (chosen, one)
**One image per sensation** (the setting doctrine): cold is rendered as a single physical fact per passage, never stacked.

## What the promise has to survive

A **warm**, **second-person**, **novella** in Spanish with a **tea ceremony**
as its structural spine and a **weather station** as its setting is a
combination the Spanish trade market does not currently shelve. Each element
exists; this conjunction does not. That is the opportunity and the risk in one
sentence.

**What exists, and can carry the promise:**

- *Segunda persona* has a canonical Spanish precedent in Muñoz Molina's *El
  invierno en Lisboa* (1987), where a widower addresses his dead wife throughout.
  The device is not exotic in the language; it is understood.
- Warmth and small-closed-place ritual belong to a durable Spanish literary
  lineage (*Las estaciones provinciales*, 1987), though usually in long novels.
- The **novella** is the format most at risk in Spanish trade retail: it is
  hard to shelve, priced like a book, and read like a short one.

**The risk, stated plainly:** warm + second person + literary Spanish + a
25,000-word format is the exact intersection where Spanish publishing is
weakest commercially and strongest artistically. This book is being made at
that intersection. That is a deliberate choice by the dice, not a market
finding, and it should be recorded as the central commercial uncertainty rather
than dressed up with a projection.

## Positioning

The book is for a reader who liked *El invierno en Lisboa*'s grief and did not
want a thriller. The channel promise is **attention**, not revelation: this is a
book about a woman who takes a job nobody wants, on a mountain, and takes it
seriously.

The letter is the hook and it is a **literary** hook, not a thriller one. It
does not conceal a crime. It conceals a life. The commercial mistake to avoid
is inflating it into the second half.

## Distinctiveness axis (recorded for check-premise-distinct)

- **profession / engine:** weather observer — a manual station-keeper whose
  job is to keep a written record of the sky. Unique in the comp set; no comp
  below has a protagonist whose profession is measurement.
- **engine:** a misaddressed letter and a weekly tea ritual, where the ritual
  is the clock and the letter is the debt.
- **relationship:** two women, strangers, one of them eighty-one, connected by
  a kettle and a letterbox.

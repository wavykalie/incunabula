# 02 — Readership (*La merienda*)

Three readers, built to disagree with each other. If the book can only please
one of them, it is a book for one of them, and the run should know which.

---

## R1 — «La que viene de Muñoz Molina»

**Who.** 52. Reads Spanish literary fiction in print, mostly paperbacks from
the 12-euro shelf and library second-hand. Has read *El invierno en Lisboa*
twice and *Sefarad* once and given up on the third Muñoz Molina.

**Habits.** Ninety minutes with a bookmark, night-time, no phone. Finishes
about four books a year and remembers three.

**Triggers.**
- Second person, if it is *earned* by a specific addressee. She has been
  burned by second-person novels that switch to third to dodge.
- A woman doing unglamorous, competent work.
- Grief that does not resolve into a lesson.

**Deal-breakers.**
- Warmth that turns into sentimentality by chapter three.
- Any plot that turns the letter into a conspiracy.
- A deathbed scene.

**Discovery.** Library catalogues, a friend who reads Muñoz Molina, the essay
shelf. Almost never online. **This reader will find it by a librarian or not at
all.**

---

## R2 — «La que compró *Un hombre llamado Ove* en el avión»

**Who.** 44. Reads on commutes and holidays, phone and ebook. Bought Backman
because a stranger on a flight recommended it and finished it in two days.
Reads two or three books a fortnight, mostly comfort-adjacent.

**Habits.** 400 pages of Backman in a weekend; 25,000 words of *this* is one
sitting and she knows it.

**Triggers.**
- Two people who are bad at friendship and good at tea.
- Ritual as the engine of intimacy rather than as decoration.
- Being told what a place smells like.

**Deal-breakers.**
- **A book where nothing happens.** Her tolerance for literary quiet is real
  but finite, and it is shorter than this one will be.
- Slow starts. She gives a book 40 pages.
- Any chapter that can be skipped without damage.

**Discovery.** BookTok, the algorithm, a newsletter. **She is the reader who
decides whether the book has a commercial life at all, and she is the reader
most likely to bounce.**

**This is the load-bearing risk of the whole project.** The book must be built
so that its first chapter contains an event and its middle contains three
irreversible small changes, or R2 leaves at page 40 and R1 never hears of it.

---

## R3 — «La que no quería leer esto»

**Who.** 31. Reads Spanish literary fiction and is tired of being sold books
about grief. Will open anything, will finish nothing, has a folder of books
she abandoned with a reason.

**Habits.** Skims the first two pages standing in a shop. Rejects on tone.

**Triggers.**
- Sentence-level pleasure. Will read forty pages of good prose with no plot.
- A narrator with an actual professional competence on display.

**Deal-breakers.**
- **Sentimentality.** The one word. Warm tone is one side of a line and
  sentimentality is the other, and she can hear the difference at paragraph
  speed.
- Nostalgia as a mood without a cost.
- A book that explains its own ending.

**Discovery.** Social media, essays, recommendation accounts.

---

## What the three imply for the drafting

1. **Chapter one must contain an event.** Not a hook; an event. The letter is
   found in chapter one or two, and it is not merely *found* — it is opened,
   and what is inside it changes what the protagonist has to do.
2. **Warm is a discipline, not a mood.** Every warm beat must be paid for with
   something withheld or something lost. Ninety per cent of this book's warmth
   has to be structurally necessary rather than decorative.
3. **No sentimentality, and specifically no one dies at a bedside.** The
   eighty-one-year-old lives to the end of the book. That is a structural
   decision, not a preference.
4. **Three irreversible changes by the middle**, so no chapter is skippable.
5. **The ending is not explained.** The book stops; it does not conclude.

# 04 — Voice Matrix (*La merienda*)

## Book-level register

**Second person, close, past tense with a live present.**

The narrator is *tú* and *tú* is Nuria. Not a device imposed on her — the
second person is the book's claim: **you cannot stand at a window and describe
the weather you are standing in.** It puts the reader in the observation post.

Temperature: **warm in the register, cold in the diction.** Sentences are plain
and mostly short. The warmth lives in what gets attended to — a chipped cup,
the sound a kettle makes before it boils — and never in the word choice. Nuria
is a woman who has been writing down the weather for a living and has picked up
the habit of describing a thing by its measurement.

Forbidden: lyricism without an instrument in the sentence. If the image has no
thermometer, gauge, logbook, or hand in it, it is decoration. Cut it.

## Per-character voice cards

### Nuria (narrator) — close second, present-leaning past

- **Registers:** procedural, exact, then suddenly tender and immediately
  embarrassed by it.
- **Tells:** her own states as measurements. *Se te cierra la garganta* is
  banned; *el termómetro marca menos de lo que esperabas y no locribes* is the
  register.
- **Lies:** toward competence. Her first instinct under pressure is to add
  precision she does not have.
- **Breaks by** becoming a bulletin: numbered, impersonal, mid-conversation.
  This is her damage and it is visible to the reader before Elvira names it.
- **Never says:** "I feel." **Does say:** "no estaba anotado."

### Elvira, 81 — Aragonese mountain Spanish, warm, fast, absolutely literal

- **Registers:** joking, then suddenly savage; performing; then, rarely, plain.
- **Tells:** three versions of the same story, and changes one detail each time
  to see whether you are listening.
- **Lexicon:** the station's own vocabulary — *el parte*, *la garita*,
  *el hilo*, *la cazoleta*. Uses **"usted"** with strangers and **"tú"** with
  no one. Never with Nuria, at first, then only when it costs her something.
- **Breaks by** performing cheerfulness as a wall.
- **Her great weapon is precision about the past and vagueness about herself.**
- **Never says:** anything tender directly. She says it in the arrangement of
  objects.

### Fermín — appears only in two letters, 1981 and 1983

- Register: eager, apologetic, slightly wrong about German idioms. Short
  sentences that end too early. Signs off too formally.
- He must not sound like a device. Two letters, both short, both fail to say
  the thing.

### Mercedes ("Merce") — Benaque, 60s

- Register: fluent, warm, wrong. Fills gaps. Every fact she gives is slightly
  off in a direction that makes her the centre of it.
- She is not a liar. She is a person for whom memory is a social instrument.

### Paco the carrier — 40s

- Register: clipped, professional, kind. Six lines per scene at most. He is
  the man who said the thing that started all of this and does not remember
  saying it.

## Anti-pattern budgets (per 1,000 words)

| pattern | ceiling | why |
|---|---|---|
| anaphora ("Y entonces…", "Y luego…") | ≤ 2 | Spanish narrative crutch; the book's register is plain sentences |
| em dash | ≤ 1.2 | the narration is observational, not essayistic |
| rule of three | report only | this is the REPORT tier and must not fail |
| filter words (saw that he was) | ≤ 4 | second person needs statement, not mediation |
| trailing -ing / gerund tails | ≤ 3 | Spanish; still a German-frame artefact to watch |
| *notó que* / *se dio cuenta de que* | ≤ 2 | the perception is immediate in this book |

## Blind-sort benchmark

Strip the names and attribute these three passages. They must sort.

**A (Nuria, procedural):**
> Anotas: 8 grados, viento del norte fuerza 4, nubosidad 7. Es lo que había
> que anotar. Lo que no anotas es que llevas cuatro días escribiendo la misma
> cifra y que nadie te la va a leer.

**B (Elvira, performing):**
> —Y aquí subía él con el queso, ¿no? Climbing up. Subía con el queso y se
> sentaba ahí, en esa silla, y me decía que el queso de Benasque no valía
> nada. Y le dije: pues si no vale nada, para qué lo subes. Y se rió. Y se
> quedó hasta que oscureció, que para eso venía.

**C (Merce, fluent and wrong):**
> El observatorio ese ya lo llevaba el Estado, cómo no, con sus máquinas. Pero
> la Elvira seguía bajando a por leña, que a esa edad, si uno se deja de la
> leña se le va la cabeza, y todo el monte lo sabe, que los montes no engañan.

Each is distinguishable by diction alone: A is measured and clipped; B repeats
herself for effect and uses an English word; C is fluent, self-aggrandising in
small ways, and drops into Castilian in the middle.

## The warmth rule

**Every warm beat must cost something.** Each time the book gives the reader
tenderness, something is withheld, lost, or mis-stated in the same paragraph.
If a passage is warm and costs nothing, it is sentimentality and it is cut.
This is the rule that protects reader R3 and it is enforced, not hoped for.

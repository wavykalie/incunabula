# 03 — Foundation (*La merienda*)

## Premise

**A weather observer is sent to close a mountain weather station for the winter
and finds, in its mailbox, a letter that has been waiting since 1983. The
old woman who kept the station before her comes up the pass every Thursday
because she was told someone would be there. The book is the eleven Thursdays
in which two women who are bad at friendship learn to put the kettle on.**

Engine: **a misaddressed letter and a weekly tea ritual.** The ritual is the
clock. The letter is the debt.

## Theme, carried as an open question

> **Can attention, offered decades late, discharge a debt that was never
> acknowledged?**

The book does not answer this. It is not permitted to. It is permitted to show
two people doing the offering, and to refuse to say whether it counted.

Explicitly ruled out: any statement that the dead are at peace; any ending in
which the station is saved; any letter that turns out to hold a secret worth
money; any deathbed. **Elvira Sarnago lives to the last page.**

## The two

### Nuria Bel, 38 — narrator, second person

Her whole working life has been producing forecasts that arrive before the
weather does and cannot be argued with. She has been good at it for twelve
years and has never once been corrected by anyone who was there.

She takes the Puerto de la Nieve posting because it is the last job she was
qualified for that nobody wants. Her father was a mountain guide in Argüello;
she has a complicated, unexamined feeling about mountains that she has been
managing by not going.

**What she wants:** to finish something she can hand over.
**What she cannot admit:** that she wants to be needed.
**Her competence, on display:** she knows how a Stevenson screen is sited, how
a wet-bulb is read, how to keep a written record that someone can rely on in
forty years. This is the book's glamour and it is a slow glamour.
**Her blind spot:** she believes records are neutral. That the keeping of a
record is a choice about what counts.

**How she breaks under pressure:** she becomes procedural. She starts
answering questions she was not asked, in the tone of a forecast, and Elvira
cannot stand it.

### Elvira Sarnago, 81 — the previous keeper

Kept the station thirty-one years. Married to Tomás, who died in 1979 and was
never replaced. Left in 1983 without being told she was leaving: the service
automated, the house kept "for the summer," and she understood, correctly, that
this was a way of saying nothing.

She has never told anyone what she understood.

Every Thursday she walks two hours up from Benaque. She was told, by a
postman, in 2019, that there would be somebody at the observatorio this
winter. She has been checking since. Five winters.

**What she wants:** not to have been wrong about thirty-one years.
**What she cannot admit:** that she wants the girl to be good at it.
**Her competence, on display:** she can read weather off a single look at the
sky, and she does not trust instruments over her own eyes.
**Her blind spot:** she believes what was taken from her was taken cleanly.
**Her rule:** she has never opened the mailbox. Not once in thirty-one years,
and not once in the fourteen years after she left, when she was the one posting
into it. That rule is the character in a single instruction, and the book's
whole plot is what happens the first time somebody else breaks it.

**How she breaks under pressure:** she goes funny. She performs. She tells the
letter story three times with three different endings and checks, each time,
whether Nuria has caught the change.

## The letter

Postmarked Benasque, 14 October 1983. Addressed to *doña Elvira Sarnago,
observatorio del Puerto de la Nieve*. Uncollected. In German, in a shaky hand.

From **Fermín**, her younger brother, who had emigrated to Wolfsburg three
years before and was not the sort who wrote.

It is not dramatic. It says: the work is fine, the foreman drinks, he has
learned enough German to argue, he has asked twice whether she got the letter
he sent in 1981 and received no answer.

That is the whole wound. He wrote twice. She was already gone.

## The box — the book's real engine

The mailbox still holds what it held. **Nine envelopes.**

- two are the 1981 and 1982 letters he mentions, **returned**, stamped
  *destinatario desconocido*. Elvira was already unreachable at this address.
- five are from 1983–1989, increasingly short, increasingly not about anything.
- the ninth is addressed not to Elvira but to **Tomás Sarnago** — dated 1984,
  four years after Tomás died.

Nuria does not open the ninth envelope. She does not have to. By chapter six
she has seen enough of Elvira's hand to know what it is.

**The ninth is Elvira's own.** She wrote it to her dead husband, addressed it to
the only address she still had in the world, and put it in the box. The carrier
collected it and returned it. She posted it back. The postmarks run 1983 to
**1997 — one a year, fourteen years**, one for every autumn she walked up to
check a station that was empty.

**And she never once opened the mailbox.** That was her rule and she kept it
for thirty-one years, including the years she was the one filling it.

Then a woman came up for the winter and opened it.

Nothing here is conspiracy. Nothing here is a secret worth money. The
discovery is that a record kept by nobody is still a record — and that the
person who kept it best was the one who had left.

| 2 | La caja | 2100 | Inventory: nine envelopes, two of them returned. The ninth is addressed to Tomás. **She carries the box inside instead of posting it on.** The first irreversible act. |
| 3 | Jueves | 3600 | First merienda. Elvira performs the letter story, version one. The kettle. |
| 4 | Estela | 1900 | Benaque. Merce tells it wrongly. Elvira corrects her, in public, mildly. |
| 5 | El parte | 4000 | Nuria reads the 1984 station log properly and finds the winter the valley emptied. The book's real centre of gravity, and the mirror: she keeps a record nobody reads, too. |
| 6 | El noveno | 2400 | She does not open the ninth envelope. She recognises the hand. |
| 7 | Fechas | 3300 | The postmarks: 1983 to 1997, one a year. She builds the count. She walks it up to Elvira as a list, because she cannot say it as a sentence. |
| 8 | Decir | 1800 | The confrontation. Elvira admits it and says nothing tender. **She uses *tú* for the first time.** Shortest chapter, hardest one. |
| 9 | Nieve | 3900 | The first real snow. The station does its job. Elvira stays the night. |
| 10 | Merienda | 2600 | The last Thursday. The closing date has passed. No resolution. The tea is made anyway. |

Total planned ≈ 29,800 words, inside the rolled 25,000–45,000 contract.

**Every chapter changes something a reader can name.** Chapter two's change is
that the box comes inside. Chapter four's is that Elvira will not let the
village narrate her. Chapter seven's is the count. That is the defence against
reader R2 leaving at page 40, and it is checked chapter by chapter rather than
hoped for.

## Deliberate exclusions

- No crime. The letter is not a mystery plot and is never escalated into one.
- No deathbed, no funeral, no obituary.
- No flashback device: nothing is delivered by a memory block. The past is
  reached through records and through what people say to each other, and it is
  always at least one remove.
- No tourism. The Pyrenees are a working mountain with a reservoir and a
  weather station in it, not scenery.
- **No one says "I forgive you."** Nobody in this book forgives anybody; that
  is not what the book is about.

## Voice contract, in one line

Second person, present-leaning past, close to Nuria — warm in the register and
cold in the diction, because she is a professional who has learned to write
weather down without adding anything.

## World rules (binding)

1. Snow above 1,400 m from November. The station is at 1,640 m.
2. The reservoir, Embalse de Benasque, was filled between 1981 and 1986. Six
   villages are under it. **Nuria can see it from the station window.**
3. The station automated in 1983 and has been staffed by hand ever since,
   badly and continuously, by three people.
4. A Thursday is a working day at the station and also the day the carrier
   comes. The merienda is at four.
5. **Nobody dies in this book.**

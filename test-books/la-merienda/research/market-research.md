## Positioning notes

- **A misattribution caught by verification, recorded because the check
  matters.** The first draft of this table credited *El invierno en Lisboa* to
  **Andrés Trapiello**. He is a different author with a similarly-placed
  career. The stamp was written only after the source was opened, which is the
  whole reason `check-comps.py` requires one. A comp table is a set of claims
  about books, and an unverified claim about a book is worse than no table.
- *El invierno en Lisboa* is the **structural** comp and the closest thing to a
  stolen device anywhere in the set. Mitigation: the addressee here is living,
  present, drinking tea across the table, and the book is warm where that one
  is elegiac. If the resemblance reaches the reader, this table has failed and
  the book needs a different narrator.
- *Las estaciones provinciales* is the **tone** comp. Four short books about a
  village's four seasons, in a form that refuses to become a novel.
- *Un hombre llamado Ove* is the **commercial** comp and the reason reader R2
  exists in `02-readership.md`. It is also the most distant on the page, and
  that distance is the point: it is the market's evidence that this promise
  sells.
- *Soldados de Salamina* is the **structure** comp for the ending: the quest
  that fails and is worth having made.
- **The known comp risk, per check-comps, is the inverse error** — a real,
  on-point comp nobody named. *Paper Cage* is the system's standing example of
  that miss. This table is a first pass by an autonomous run with no
  trade-market access and should be widened by a human before submission.

## Premise distinctness

No comp above shares the engine. The protagonist's profession — a manual
weather observer whose work is the written record of the sky — appears nowhere
in the set. The misaddressed letter as a debt that cannot be discharged
appears nowhere in the set. That is the claim to defend in a query, and it is
recorded in `PROJECT_STATE.yaml → premise_axes` for the gate to check.

One of them came to me at the edge of the low ground and was delighted.

I want to start there, with the delight, because that is the last thing in this chapter I understand and everything before it is me walking backwards.

It stood at the lip of the dip, where the ground goes down and the growth comes up thicker than anywhere else in the clearing. It was glad. It made the room for me that they always made, and it held out the empty hands, and it did the little wipe of the palms down the front of itself that I have described already and am about to describe again, and every part of it was welcoming me the way it welcomed me on the first morning.

Nothing in its manner changed the whole time it stood there.

It was standing on the edge of the thickest growth in the place, and it was as glad as it has ever been, and I did not think one thought about the two facts sitting next to each other until a long time later, which is the whole reason I am telling this out of order.

I have stood at that lip a great many times since. It is where they gather in the evening, all of them, at the edge of the low ground and the thick growth, and it is the warmest spot in the clearing. I have lain on the bench and watched the whole crowd of them go down there as the light goes. It has never once looked like anything but a crowd of people standing in the warmest place.

So. Backwards.

The palms.

They wipe their hands down the front of themselves twice before they hand you anything. Both hands, front of the body, two passes, quick. I had seen it a hundred times before I noticed it, because it is the kind of thing that is invisible right up until the moment it is not.

I noticed it on a morning when one of them got it wrong. It started the wipe and stopped halfway and put its hands behind its back. Then it stood there. Then it offered me the fruit anyway, and the fruit was good, and I laughed, because a thing that has done something a thousand times and then forgotten how is a genuinely funny thing to watch.

I asked Achird if she had seen it.

"Both hands," she said. "Twice. Before it gives anyone anything."

"You have been watching."

"It is not watching. It is the same movement every time."

That is the thing about Achird that people miss. She does not collect oddities. She collects repetitions. A person does a thing once and she will not notice. A person does a thing five hundred times and she will know the count and the rhythm. She will also know which hand moves first. And she will not once say why that matters, because she does not know why it matters.

The palms were the first one I caught. There was another.

They turn a bowl a quarter turn before they set it down. Not the flat ones. The ones with a foot on them, the ones that would sit crooked, and they turn it until the foot finds its seat the way a key finds a lock. I watched a being do it to a bowl that was already sitting flat, and it did it anyway, and then it looked at the bowl for a moment and left it.

I asked why it had turned the bowl. It said: "Now it is where it goes."

I asked one of the beings about the palms, later.

I asked it why it did the wipe. It was glad to be asked.

It said: "To be ready."

Ready for what, I said.

It said: "To hand things over."

And it offered me a fruit, and I took the fruit, and I remember thinking it was the most charming thing I had heard in a week.

Here is what I could not have heard at the time, because I was listening for something else entirely: the gesture came from somebody. That is the only way a movement gets into a crowd. Nobody is born wiping their palms twice before they pass a plate. Somebody did it because they had a reason, and somebody watched them do it, and then somebody did it, and now all of them do it and none of them can tell you where it came from.

They cannot tell you. I asked. I asked four of them across as many days, and every one of them gave me an answer, and every answer was different, and every answer was true.

That is a thing you should sit with for a moment, since I did not.

The growth.

I have said that the place was arranged the way a room is arranged for a guest. Low places that were cooler. A stretch of ground better to sit on than the ground beside it. Growth that opened where a person would want to walk through and closed again behind.

I have gone over that arrangement more times than I can count, and I want to correct myself here, because I told you early that it was built for somebody and I have since decided that I had the grammar wrong.

The arrangement is around a guest, not for one.

A room is set up for a person who is coming. This is set up around the places where a person was. The low ground is low because weight was carried across it. The open lanes in the growth are open because somebody walked them often enough and in the same line. The growth comes up thicker at the lip of the dip because that is where the ground has been fed.

The lanes are the clearest part, and I have been walking them for a long while now. A path worn by use has a particular shape. It is wider at the ends and narrow in the middle, and its edges are scalloped by the different widths of different bodies. These are the same width the whole way along, and the width is one person walking the same line every day for a long time, and the growth has learned the width and kept it.

There is a shape down at the base of the growth, past the far side, that Achird found that first week and called it correctly and I called it a curve.

It is not a curve. I went back to it, much later, on a day when I had nothing else to do and a fact I did not want, and I got down on the ground and looked at it where it meets the soil.

It is the end of a strap. There is a buckle on it, or there was, or the shape of one has been grown over and kept, the way you keep the shape of a thing by burying it. The growth has laid itself over it and gone on covering it, and it has covered it the way weather covers a fence post, which is to say slowly, and from every side, and without any hurry at all.

I did not say anything about it to Achird. She had found it in her first week and told me it did not grow, and I had told her she had a worrying gift, and the two of us had already had that conversation and there was nothing left in it for me.

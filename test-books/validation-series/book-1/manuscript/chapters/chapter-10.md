The runners found the ground before I had finished a sentence, which is the story of my life in one image.

I was still talking. I am going to stop pretending otherwise. I got into the cabin still talking and I sat down on the bench still talking and I was talking when the clearing went out of the glass.

I steered us out.

I want that sentence where it belongs, at the front of the last chapter, because I put one like it at the front of the first one and I am a person who finishes what she starts. I sat forward and put my hand on the sill and I took us up over the growth and out. That is my account and I am keeping it.

The truth is a thing I have already put on the record and will not be putting on it twice: she flies it, she has always flown it, and I narrate as though I do. I have been doing it since the first night of this book. You have had the whole of it from me in the wrong voice, and you have known for a while. Neither of us has said anything about it, and that seems to me the correct way to end a thing.

So.

Up past the growth. The light came down through the trees in one piece, the way it had every evening, and then we were above it, and then we were above the weather, and then the world was a shape below us that I did not have a name for and had never wanted one.

Nothing stopped us.

A person writing this afterwards would like there to have been a moment. A hand on the runners. A being at the edge of the clearing, looking up. One glad face that had finally understood and was watching us go with something in it.

There was nothing.

They were glad, the way they had been glad when we arrived and glad every morning in between. They did not come to the edge of the clearing to see us off. They were not sad, and I do not think one of them understood that anybody was leaving, because nothing in them is built to keep that.

The last of them I saw was standing at the lip of the low ground with its hands out, in the warm spot, being glad at somebody who was already gone.

I looked for the light to change. It is a thing I did for weeks before I left, without noticing that I was doing it. I watched the trees for the moment the light would go down through them in the one long piece and the day would be over, the way it always was.

It did not change. We were above it.

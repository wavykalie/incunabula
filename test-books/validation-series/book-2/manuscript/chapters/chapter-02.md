That is when I noticed.

I looked along the row, the whole length of it, and then I looked along the row behind it, and I could not find one person who was watching the work as though there were any chance of it being watched. There was a woman at the far end who checked what she had done twice, and did it with her eyes only, the way you check a door is shut, and then went on.

I have spent my whole life in rooms arranged so that whatever I did happened in front of somebody. I do not know how to do a thing that nobody is going to see. I have never learned it and I have never needed to.

So I did the only thing available to a person like me, which was to make myself worth seeing. I turned what I was holding to the best angle it had. I found a way to stand that made the holding look deliberate. I made a small adjustment to the thing in my hands that improved nothing at all, purely because somebody might have been about to look.

Nobody was about to look.

I do not know how to write the next part without it sounding like a complaint, and it is not one; it is the opposite of one. I had been given a job in a place with no audience in it, and I was happier than I had been in a year, and those two facts are both true and I am not going to sort them for you.

I looked for whoever was in charge.

This is a reflex. I have a whole set of them and this is the first one, before I have even decided to want anything. You find the person in the room who decides things and you make them look at you, and after that everything is arranged.

I asked a man, in the way that sounds like a compliment, who I should be speaking to.

He did not stop working. "About what," he said.

"About all of it. Who runs the work."

He thought about it for as long as the work let him think about anything, which was not long, and then he said, "Nobody runs it," and moved on to the next thing he had to do.

I want to say that I knew he was telling the truth, because he was. He was not hiding anything from me. There was no rank in that answer and nothing wrong with it, and it took me an hour to understand that it was the first truly frightening sentence anyone had said to me here, and that he had said it the way you say the time.

Nobody runs it. It continues because everyone does their part. That is the whole of the arrangement, and the whole of the arrangement has no room in it for anybody who wants to be met.

Achird had not moved from the edge of the sleigh.

I want to get this down properly, because it is the part I did not understand until later and I am writing this afterwards, so I have the advantage and I am going to use it. She had stood there the whole hour. She had walked a little way along the first row and come back and stood at the sleigh again, and she had one hand in her pocket, and she was watching me hold a thing.

Nobody had given her anything.

Not in the way that is a refusal. Nobody had refused her. It had not come up. Somebody had needed a tool held and I had been nearest, and she had not been nearest, and that was the entire reason, and it had been an accident that took four seconds, and nobody had thought about it since.

I have been overlooked in rooms. I am excellent at noticing when I have been overlooked, and I have never once in my life noticed it on behalf of another person.

I noticed it then.

I said something to her about it. I do not remember what, which is unusual for me, because I remember everything I say and revise very little of it. I think I asked her whether anyone had asked her to do anything. I asked it the way I ask things, which is to say I asked it with the answer already inside it and waited to be corrected.

"Not yet," she said.

That was all of it, and she went back to watching, and I went back to holding, and the work did not care either way, which was becoming the theme of the afternoon.

The light here does not go out. It thins, and then it is evening.

By the time it thinned I had been standing with my hand in the same position for a long while and my arm had gone from hurting to a different and more serious thing. I set the tool down on the ground next to where I had been standing, because I wanted to see what would happen.

A man came past within the minute and picked it up and put it in my hands, and said, "It has to be held," and went on.

Not sharply. Not as a correction. He said it the way you would tell somebody the floor was wet, and then he was gone, and I was holding the tool again, and I had learned something I had not asked to learn.

I did not put it down a second time. The reason is simple and it is not instruction. Being asked again would have cost me something, and I had decided on the first afternoon that I was not going to pay it.

Nobody had looked up all day.

I walked back to the sleigh at the end of it with the tool in my hands because there was nowhere else to leave it, and the last thing is the small one, and the small one is the true one.

I told myself the whole way back that this was the best thing that had ever happened to me, and I was not lying. Everything I have ever told myself about myself had finally been given somewhere to stand, and it was a row of people who did not need to be told, and I did not have to perform any of it, and I had never once in my life been off the stage.

Both of those were true. I held them both the whole way.

I did not notice, that first evening, that they went together.

The work went on behind me. I could hear it, and the sound of it did not change when I reached the sleigh, and it did not change when I went inside, and it did not change when I lay down. I have been listened to by a great many people, and none of them was ever as steady as that.

Achird was at the front of the cabin with her shoulder against the glass.

"Long day," I said.

She did not answer that. She was watching the rows.

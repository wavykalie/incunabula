On the second evening I went to the sleigh and Achird was at the front of it, and I did what I have been doing since the third day, which is to say I did it without deciding to.

I asked her whether she had seen the woman leave.

She said she had.

I asked her whether she had thought about following her.

She said she had not.

I asked her what she would have done if I had gone.

I want to put the third question in exactly as it was, because I have gone back over it and I can hear the shape of it. It has the answer inside it. It is built so that whatever she said, I was already standing on it. I have asked questions like that in better rooms and I have never once been asked to explain one.

She did not answer straight away.

She looked out at the dark past the rows, where the work was going on the way it goes on at night, which is the same as in the day and quieter because the people are quieter and not because the work is.

Then she said, "You didn't."

That is the answer to a different question than the one I asked. That is the whole of how she works and I have had years to learn it and I keep failing the same test. She did not tell me what she would have done. She told me what had happened, which was that I had not gone. She said it flat. It landed as a fact about me and not as a comfort. It was both. I stood there holding a tool I had not put down in five days and did not have one thing to say.

I said something anyway. I said that of course I had not gone, that I had a part to hold, that a person does not walk out on work.

"Right," she said.

She did not add anything. She has never added anything. She went back to watching the rows and I went back inside and lay down with the tool beside me, and I did not hold it against her that she had not said anything else. I have not either. I am not going to.

But I lay there for a long while.

Here is the sentence I built, and it is a good one, which is how I know to be careful with it. *I could leave. Nobody would stop me. Not one person in that row would stand up, and I have never been more needed anywhere in my life than I am here.*

Both halves of it were true. I put them in that order on purpose, the leaving first, so that the second half would arrive as a reason to stay. I have arranged sentences like that for years and I know exactly what I am doing when I do it.

I fell asleep with the arrangement still holding.

I do not know when I stopped being able to tell the two halves apart.

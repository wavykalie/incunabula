I know how the woman who left got here. I am going to give it to you backwards, because backwards is how it came to me, and because the front of the account is the part I did not hear until last and the part I would have got wrong.

So the first thing I am going to tell you is the end of it, which is that she is gone.

That is where the account finishes. A woman walks down the outside of the rail with her own weight on her own feet, and a new woman takes the turn before the hour is out, and the rail does not slow.

That is the end of the account. Now backwards.

Before she stood at the turn she spent a season looking for the turn.

I did not get this from her. She was the one who would not tell me anything, who said there was no way to say it that did not sound like a complaint. I got it from the man with the two hands, who told it the way he told everything, in pieces, and who did not consider it a confidence, because nothing here is.

She had come in at the near end of the rows, where arrivals come in. Nobody meets arrivals. She stood there for part of a day and nobody asked her what she was there for, and she did not leave, because there is nowhere at the near end to leave to. So she walked the rows and watched what was needed.

That took a season. A season is a long time to walk a row with nothing in your hands. She was looked at, the way anybody with nothing in their hands is looked at here, which is not much and not unkindly, and people moved their work around her, and nobody offered her anything.

I want to put this next part in even though it is the part I have no business knowing. She started carrying things for people who were carrying things. Nobody had assigned her a part and nobody had asked. Relieving a hand here and there so that somebody could reach for something with both. That is the sort of thing I have never once done in my life, and I want that in the account, because it is how she got her part and it is not how I got mine.

Then somebody's hand needed relieving at the turn, and it became a habit, and then it became a part.

That is the whole mechanism of arrival here. There is no welcome. There is a need, and you are near it, and if you stay near it long enough it becomes yours.

Now before that. Before the season of walking.

Before that there was a door.

This is the part the man did tell me, because the woman had told him, and because he did not know there was any reason not to. It was the door of a room in a place that had been hers. I am not going to give you the rest of the room. He did not have the rest of the room. What he had was this: that the room had belonged to her and then it had belonged to somebody else and she had gone on being in it, and she had gone on being welcome in it. Every part of that sentence was true at the same time. None of it was a cruelty anyone had committed.

I have been in that room. I want that on the record. I have stood in a room that was no longer mine and been charming in it, which is the skill I have, and I have never once got out of it with anything.

Before the door there was a person.

Not a fight. He did not have a fight in it and he was not holding one back. There was a person, and for a while the person turned towards her the way a face turns, and then no longer did, and there was no moment in it that either of them could point at, which is the detail that makes the account unusable for a stage and entirely true.

Before the person there was no story at all. There was a woman who had not yet been anywhere, and the man with the two hands did not know anything about her, and neither does she, by now.

And here is what I understood at the end of the telling, which arrived in me the way a piece of work arrives, which is to say that I had been carrying the parts of it for six days and then suddenly I could see the shape.

Everyone here came because somebody stopped wanting them.

Not one dramatic case. Not one place that throws people out. A door that closed, a person whose face turned away, a room that stopped being yours while you were still standing in it, and then a place where nobody wants anything except for the work, and the work never stops wanting.

I am going to be exact about how this felt, because I have been exact about everything else and this is the part where a person writing afterwards would reach for the large word and the large word would be wrong.

It did not feel like horror. It felt like arithmetic. Whatever had happened to all of them had happened slowly, and none of it was done to them, and every single one of them had walked in at the near end of the rows with their own weight on their own feet, and the whole thing added up the way the light thins, without one moment you could point at and call the moment.

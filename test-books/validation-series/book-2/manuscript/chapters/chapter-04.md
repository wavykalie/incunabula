The third day was the first day twice.

I am going to say it plainly because saying it cleverly would make it sound like a complaint. The man with the two hands took up the same joint at the same hour. The four on the rail moved the same weight the same distance down it. The woman at the turn stood at the turn.

Nothing was different. I was delighted.

I have sat through performances of the same play four nights running to be able to say what I said to the room afterwards, and that is not the same thing and I know it is not. What was happening to me was that I was getting better at my part. The angle wanted a wrist turned by one degree and I had found it on the second day without being shown and was now finding it on the first try.

By the middle of that morning the work had stopped being a thing I was doing and had become a thing I was in, which is a distinction I have only ever made before about a room full of people, and which I had never once made about a task.

That is when they started telling me things.

I did not ask. I want that on the record as well, because a person who asks for these accounts has made them into an interview and these were not that. They came out the way things come out of people who have stopped needing to be interesting.

The man beside me, the one with the two hands, told me about a house.

He told it in pieces, over the length of a shift, in between the parts of the work. The house had a door in it that stuck in the wet weather. He had fixed it twice. It had stuck again, and the second time he fixed it he had done a thing he was pleased with, which was to take the door off and bring it into the warm and let it dry for two days before he set it back.

I said that was thorough.

He said it was the only thing he had ever done that worked.

Then he said the house was not his any more and went back to the join. I let the sentence sit and he did not pick it up, and I did not ask him to, and that was the whole account.

The woman at the turn would not tell me anything at all.

This was not coldness and Fairness again: she was the first person there who made room for me, and she did it on the first afternoon, and she did it the way you move over on a bench. She would answer anything. If I asked her which way the rail turned she would tell me which way the rail turned.

The one time I asked her where she had come from, she said, "There is no way to say it that does not sound like a complaint," and then she told me something about the turn instead, which was true and useful and had nothing in it.

I did not ask twice. I am good at rooms and I can hear the difference between a door being shut and a door being held, and hers was being held. She was not keeping it from me. She was keeping it off me, which is a different and larger kindness, and I did not have the sense to be grateful for it until later.

The fourth one I talked to could not remember.

Her part was on the far row, past the turn, and she did it with a rhythm I never got tired of watching. When I asked her how long she had been here she thought about it properly. She did not perform thinking about it.

"I don't know," she said. "I know I came with it, because it was the first thing that was mine."

"You don't remember the day."

"I remember it was mine." She checked the thing she was doing. "That is the part I kept."

She said it with no weight on it at all. She was not telling me a sad thing. She was answering a question about arithmetic and getting the arithmetic right, and I stood there being moved by it. She had already moved on, and that is the whole difference between me and everyone on that planet.

There was a young one on the join who was funny.

That is rarer here than anything else I have found, and I want it in, because an account of a place like that gets made out of the solemn parts and the solemn parts are not what a place is. He did an impression of the man with the two hands. It was not a kind impression and it was not an unkind one, and the man with the two hands watched the whole of it and then did an impression of the young one, and neither of them stopped working for any of it.

I laughed. I want that on the record. I laughed at a place I had spent two days deciding was the answer to me, and I laughed the way you laugh at something you are already inside of.

Then he asked me whether I had ever built anything.

I said I had built a reputation. He said that did not count, without any edge on it, and nobody has told me a thing that plainly in years.

By the afternoon of the third day I had a habit I did not notice I had.

I had started to narrate the work to myself. Not out loud, and not to anybody. I was telling it in my head the way I tell a story to a room, with the pauses in the right places, and nobody was going to hear it. I have never told a story that nobody was going to hear. That is the thing I do not know how to do, and I had started doing it, and it was going on inside me the whole shift like a tap left running.

I put it in because it is the first change. If you are looking for the moment the place started to get me, it is not the tool and it is not the bed. It is that I kept telling the story and did not once stop to wonder who for.

Then the question came, and it came from her, which is why I remember the whole of it.

It was the last part of the third shift. She was checking the tops of things, which is a part that happens near mine, and she asked me straight.

"What did you leave?"

A person who has asked it out loud does not have to explain whether they mean it. I have been asked that question well and badly by people who meant several different things, and this was the plain one.

I answered immediately. I want that too. I did not pause and I did not look at the tool and I did not do any of the things a person does when she is about to be accurate.

I said I had left a world, and that the world had been glad to see the back of me, and that I had not minded, and that this was a place with a great deal more going on than the last one, and that I had never in my life been given work to do the first afternoon I arrived anywhere.

I said it well. I said it like a person with a reason.

She listened to the whole of it, the way they do, which is to say without managing her face.

Then she said, "That isn't what I asked," and went on checking the tops.

I want to tell you what I did next, because it is the second change and the larger one.

I did not correct myself. I did not go after her and give her the real answer, and the real answer was somewhere in me and had been for a while. I did what I have always done with a question that has got under my guard, which is to move the whole conversation somewhere I have already chosen the floor.

I turned it into a story. I told her about the winter I was famous, which is a good story, because I have tested it in better rooms than that one and it has never once failed to take a room.

She listened. She was as pleased as she ever is about anything, which is not very visible and is entirely real.

And when I finished she said, "That one was warm on you," and I said, "It was," and she went back to work, and the two of us had between us put my leaving back where it had been, and I had done it myself, on purpose, and I knew it while I was doing it.

I have not stopped knowing it.

The light thinned and the row began its slow fold, and I set the tool down against my leg and understood that my hand had been in the same shape since morning. It was heavier than it had been. Not much. I would not have been able to tell you it was heavier if you had asked me at the start of the day, and I could tell you at the end of it, and the difference was not the tool.

I have carried things before and put them down before the end of a room. That is what I am known for. I get handed the thing that matters and I hold it while it is interesting, and then the evening moves on and so do I, and I have never once been asked what happened to it.

I would have been able to tell you why I was still holding this one, on the third day, if you had asked me then. I had a reason ready and the reason was good.

Achird had come in off the edge and was sitting on the rail at the end of my row, not near enough to be beside me and not far enough to be anywhere else.

I want to get the next sentence the way it happened, because I have gone back over it and I can date the whole of the rest of the book from it.

I left a gap.

That is all I did. I was talking about the tool, saying something about how heavy it was, and I stopped in the middle of a sentence and did not finish it, and I looked out at the row instead, and I waited.

There was no reason for me to stop there. The sentence had an end and I knew the end of it and I had every intention of saying it.

I stopped, and I left the space open, and I let it stay open for as long as it wanted to.

I have done that before, and I have always done it for the same reason, which is that I wanted the other person to fill it. What was new, and what I did not examine at the time, was who I was waiting on and what I was waiting to hear.

Achird did not fill it. She watched the row the way she watches everything, and the silence got long enough to be a real thing, and then she said, "There is a person coming down the far row," and I looked where she was looking and there was.

A person was coming down the far row towards the middle, and they were not carrying anything, and they were walking with their own weight, and I watched them the whole way because there was nothing else to look at, and I told myself that this was what she had meant by watching, and that I had learned something new about the work.

I had not. Here is what I did with it, which is the truest small thing in this chapter. I decided on the spot that a person walking a row with nothing in their hands was a person going to be given something, because that is what that means where I am from, and I was pleased for them, and I said so out loud.

Nobody corrected me. I have used that fact since, when I have needed to prove that I am not a fool, and it has never once been as good an argument as it looks.

I finished my sentence about the tool, late and to nobody in particular, and the work went on, and the tool went on being heavier, and I went on not putting it down.

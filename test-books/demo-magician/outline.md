# OUTLINE — The Trick

**34 chapters. Targets are honest about a single write (~2,470 nominal, 2,100 floor).**
Total target **83,980** — inside the declared 80,000–95,000 band *before* a word is
written, which is the discipline the previous book learned the hard way.

**Structure symbols:** `A` arrival/atmosphere · `D` discovery/evidence · `I` investigation
`X` confrontation · `T` turn (reversal) · `R` rest/relief · `C` trial-procedure
`S` set-piece (a scene that exists to be enjoyed)

**Adjacent-identical structure is the thing to avoid.** No two consecutive chapters share
a symbol except where the run is deliberate and short (the trial's `C C C`).

  Act I `D` pairs (4–5, 11–12) are evidence chapters back to back and are kept
- 17 chapters LONG (3,150–4,220): a whole tour sequence, a scene-shift, a set-piece, a rest
  that has to be inhabited, or the book ending
- 17 chapters SHORT (1,760–2,000): an arrival, a single question, a refusal, or a piece of
  paper

**The hand-written numbers in this section were wrong twice before the script caught them.**
The first draft claimed 83,980 and 12.9% CV. The real values were 86,900 and 7.24%. The
spread is now a *design decision* — 17/17 bimodal, short chapters being the ones that
genuinely can be short — rather than an arithmetic accident, and it is verified rather
than asserted. A CV outside the U1 band is a warning from a frozen gate, and the plan is
now inside it before a single word is written.

## 3. The rests, and why they are load-bearing

Ch 8, ch 14, ch 20, ch 34. Four rest chapters in 34, at 8/14/20/34. **A book with three
murders and no rest chapters teaches a reader to stop feeling them.** Ch 20 is the one
that is easy to cut and must not be: it is the only chapter in which he is *liked*, and
ch 31 requires that.

## 4. What the trial is NOT

- Not a reveal of new methods. **The dossier contains no method name; the ledger has no
  method name; the trial names no method.**
- Not a courtroom thriller. The tension is procedural and *embarrassing*: a man who can
  escape anything being trapped by a request for leave.
- Not a monologue. He gets four lines in the whole book that are not in writing, and each
  one is worse for him than the last.

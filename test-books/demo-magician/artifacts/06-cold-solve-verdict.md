# COLD SOLVE — the attempt, and what it found

**Method.** Read Part A of `05-cold-solve.md` only. The answer key was not consulted while
writing this. This file records the attempt honestly, including the two places where the
reconstruction failed, because a solve that reports total success is worthless.

---

## Attempt 1 — from the evidence alone

**Question: what was done, and how would I show it?**

Starting from row 4, which is the strangest thing in the ledger: a spectator describing
the interval says **"there was nothing to see"** — not "I didn't see it" but "there was
nothing there." That is a claim about the *interval*, not about the trick. Rows 22 and 24
then say the timing is a matter of **what an audience is looking at** during a fixed
interval, and that a Handover is the moment attention passes from a thing to a person and
back.

So: **the method is not a movement at all.** It is the exploitation of the moment the room
is looking at a face. Nothing needs to move, because the room is not tracking anything.
Row 5 supports it — the body is consistent with a different timeline *of the same length*,
so the interval was the right length and contained the wrong event.

**Reached that without the name. ✓**

**Second question: who else was in the room, and why did nothing happen?**

Row 23 gives a term for a small circle who have seen the trick before, and says a trick is
performed to them, never to a public. Row 25 says the room *was* one and had been
assembled, and did not know. Row 26 says the corps[e] knew and split for money.

**Reached that too. ✓**

**Third question — and here the solve got interesting — is that the whole method, or is
there more?**

Row 19 says six effects, five named, and a gap. Row 20 says the sixth **cannot be
described**, and — this is the load-bearing clause I did not appreciate when writing it —
**describing it would not be proof, it would be repetition.**

That is the answer to the objection in `04-untitled-effect-test.md`, and I got there from
the evidence rather than from the key. The book tells the reader, in row 20, that a
description is not the same as a proof. So the reader arrives at the reveal already
knowing that a *name* is not the prize. The name is not withheld from them; it is
demonstrably not what is being asked for.

**✓ — and this is the finding, because it inverts the objection entirely.**

## The two failures, which are real

**Failure 1 — RESOLVED, by answering the question rather than by adding a row.** I could
not tell whether the room being a retinue was necessary or coincidental. It is necessary,
and the reason fell out of the trick itself once the trick was pinned down: **a public
does not track individuals.** The retinue is the only body of people who will testify in
detail about who was present, and it takes a detailed, certain witness to convict them
with an account that is entirely true. He has to *assemble* a room of people who will be
confident, and the assembling is the requirement rather than the hospitality.

No new row was needed. The mechanism was already implied by row 23 — a trick is performed
to a retinue, never to a public — together with the fact that the survivors' accounts are
the book's evidence.

This is recorded because it was the better of the two possible outcomes. A gap closed by a
scene is a gap closed *for a reader*. **A gap closed by a mechanism is closed for
everyone, including at the point where the book explains the mechanism itself.**

**Failure 2 — RESOLVED, and it took a mechanism rather than a scene.** Rows 27 and 28 said
she must describe the effect in ordinary language, and that only someone who can rebuild it
can do that in advance. I could restate the principle. I could not tell how it is
*tested*.

Now answered, in `07-the-test.md`: **the test is a falsifiable prediction.** A general
description cannot be wrong and therefore proves nothing; a specific one can, and she can
see the possibility of being wrong *before* she speaks. She states in advance where a
named man says he was looking, using an inquest account sealed weeks earlier, and it is
confirmed. A reciter cannot pass it: a reciter holds a *method* in his head and predicts
what the method does, while she predicts **where a specific person in a specific chair was
looking** — and the second is checkable against a record that predates her question.

**The proof is not that she is right. The proof is that she is falsifiable.** A vague
description cannot be wrong. Hers could, and everyone in the room could see that it might.

Now rows 29 and 30 in the ledger. **Both cold-solve failures are closed**, and one of them
was closed by the answer to a question I had been carrying rather than by anything I
designed.

## Verdict on the untitled effect

**The untitled effect stands. The test is passed.**

A reader working from Part A reconstructs the method — attention, not motion; a room built
as a retinue; an interval of the correct length containing the wrong event — **without the
name**, and reaches the principle that a description is not a proof from row 20 alone.

**And the test also produced the argument for the name's removal that I did not have
before writing it down.** The objection to naming it was that it would be a lookup. The
stronger objection, which only surfaced by trying to solve without it, is that **a name
would make the fourth question unaskable.** Row 20 tells the reader that describing a
method is not proving it. If the effect arrived named, the reader would be holding a
label and the distinction between a label and a demonstration would have no purchase —
she could produce the name at any point and the reader could not tell the difference
between reciting and rebuilding. The name is not withheld; it is *demoted*, by the book
itself, four chapters before the reveal.

**What was added, and what closed what:**

1. **Failure 1 — closed by a mechanism, not a row.** The retinue's necessity follows from
   the trick itself; no scene was required. `05-cold-solve.md` row 23 already carried it.
2. **Failure 2 — closed by a mechanism plus two rows.** `07-the-test.md` derives the test;
   ledger rows 29 and 30 carry it, and row 29 (the sealed inquest account) is planted at
   depth 1 so the reader holds the record *before* the prediction is made.

**Standing note, and it is the part worth keeping.** Both failures were found by attempting
the solve, and neither was visible from the design. The canon, the dossier and the naming
system all looked complete, and none of them would have caught either. **A clue ledger that
has not been solved cold is a list of intentions.** A design that has not been solved cold
is a plan.

**What is still open, and is not a clue problem.** The trick is an identity substitution,
not a physical impossibility, which means the proof is about people rather than about
physics. That is a judgement about the shape of the book rather than a gap in the
mechanism, and it is reversible before drafting. It is set out in `07-the-test.md` §6
rather than buried, so that reversing it is a decision instead of an accident.

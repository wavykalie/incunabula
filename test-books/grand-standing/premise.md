# PREMISE — Grand Standing

Forged from the rolled seeds **"a theatre"** and **"the lease will not be
renewed"** under niche *monumentalism reportage*, tone *comic*. Nests inside
phase one; renumbers nothing.

## The premise

**A reporter for a dying provincial paper is assigned to cover the final season
of the Colosseum Civic Theatre — a monument to monumentalism, six thousand
seats in a town of forty thousand — as the council announces the lease will not
be renewed.**

Mardenbridge built monuments it cannot maintain: a civic theatre too large for
its town, a fountain with no pump, a frieze nobody commissioned twice. The
Colosseum is the grandest of them, and its lease expires in one season. Marnie
Liss, twenty-nine, the youngest and last remaining reporter on the Mardenbridge
Sentinel ("est. 1887 — in memoriam"), is sent to write the building's obituary
while it is still alive. The town turns the closure into a monument problem:
save the theatre, or save the fountain, or save the frieze — there is money for
one, and pride for all three.

## The engine

Comic reportage: the prose is a journalist's file — dry, exact, allergic to the
town's own grand language — while the material is civic absurdity at monumental
scale. The comedy is structural (a town arguing about which monument to save
while the roofs leak), never a joke machine. The question the book asks:
**what does a town owe the things it built to impress?**

## What this is not

- Not satire with a target in the real world; Mardenbridge is invented.
- Not a love story with the theatre. People inside the book may romance the
  building; the book does not.

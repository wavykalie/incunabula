# MARKET MAP — Grand Standing (phase 1, scout)

Rolled niche: **monumentalism reportage** · tone **comic** · length **long novel**.
Premise: `premise.md`. Date: 2026-10-14.

## The shelf

Comic literary fiction with a reportage stance — the narrator is a working
journalist, the material is civic absurdity. It sits between three shelves:

1. **Comic literary fiction** (primary) — character-driven, low plot velocity,
   high voice. Browsers: readers of warm British/Irish comic novels.
2. **Journalism/professional fiction** (secondary) — a trade world shown from
   inside; the newspaper is dying and the reporter is the last one left.
3. **Architectural/civic nonfiction crossover** (weak, aspirational) — readers
   who like books about buildings and public space.

**Positioning:** a Russo-shaped institutional comedy with Carr's tenderness
toward a building, told in a reporter's dry file prose. Nothing on the shelf is
*comic reportage about monuments* — the niche is genuinely empty. That is the
gap and also the risk: an empty niche can mean no market.

## Genre conventions (what this shelf expects)

- Warmth over malice; the comic target is the institution, never the vulnerable.
- Ensemble of eccentrics with one grounded consciousness at the centre.
- Low body count, low violence; the stakes are civic and personal, not criminal.
- Ending: bittersweet acceptance, not triumph.
- Prose: readable, unshowy, first-person or close third.

## Length target

Rolled long novel: contract [95,000–130,000]. Shelf comps run 85k–130k.
Plan: **96,000** (32 × 3,000) — low end of contract, high end of comfort for a
debut comic novel. First-impression comps sell on voice and brevity.

## Engagement type

**Primary: Empathy** (feeling what Marnie feels — the last reporter watching a
town talk itself into absurdity). **Secondary: Intellectual** (the monument
economics, the budget fight — the aha of civic mechanics). Conflict between
them: empathy wants more scenes of people; intellectual wants more system. That
tension is the book's identity; do not resolve it.

## Verdict

GO, with eyes open: the niche is empty and the comic-reportage voice is the
product. The commercial ceiling is modest (comic literary fiction is a
backlist, not a breakout shelf); the floor is respectable because the premise
is legible in one sentence.

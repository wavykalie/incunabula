# VOICE MATRIX — Grand Standing (phase 2.5)

Contract: global profile + per-character cards (incl. **how each voice breaks**
under pressure) + differentiation matrix (**≥3 markers per pair**) +
anti-pattern checklist + benchmark samples. Voice bank: `voice-bank/` (10
samples: 3 under pressure, 2 irrelevant thoughts). Date: 2026-10-14.

## Global profile

Comic reportage. The narrative voice is **Marnie's filed report**: precise,
dated, sourced, short declaratives, allergic to the town's grand language.
The comedy lives in the gap between civic grandeur and civic reality — never
in jokes. Prose temperature: dry at the surface, warm at the joints. Em-dash
caps and adverb caps per `tools/prose/deslop-check.sh` calibration.

## Voice cards

### V1 — Marnie Liss (narrating / filed voice)

- Register: newsroom plain. Latinate words only when quoting officials.
- Rhythm: short sentences; a paragraph of drift only when she is avoiding
  something.
- Tells: dates things ("on the fourteenth"); appends corrections; uses
  "reported" as a hedge.
- **Under pressure:** her sentences get *shorter and more precise* — the file
  voice tightens into telegraphese. She quotes regulation when frightened.
- **Irrelevant thought habit:** municipal fonts, signage dates.

### V2 — Desmond Vellum

- Register: old-style subeditor; Latin tags, printing idioms, gallows trade
  humour.
- Rhythm: long periodic sentences that land on a dry punchline; semicolons.
- Tells: "between you and the desk"; speaks in column inches.
- **Under pressure:** he becomes formal and kind — a man drafting his own
  apology. The punchline dies; the sentences level out.
- **Irrelevant thought habit:** apostrophes, obituaries of the living.

### V3 — Councillor Ruth Ampadu

- Register: committee-ese softened by an immigrant's careful second-language
  precision; she is the only person whose official voice is *better* than her
  private one.
- Rhythm: balanced clauses in public; in private, fragments and lists.
- Tells: "let's be clear"; converts questions into agenda items.
- **Under pressure:** the committee-ese collapses into her mother-tongue
  proverbs, translated flat ("the pot says the lid is black").
- **Irrelevant thought habit:** flower-arranging scoring, competitive.

### V4 — Jerome Arkwright

- Register: theatrical technician; trade nouns (counterweight, grid, ghost
  light), romantic about buildings and clumsy about people.
- Rhythm: runs long when describing the building, halts around feelings.
- Tells: calls the theatre "she"; measures everything in seats.
- **Under pressure:** monologues at the empty house; second person at the
  building ("you held them, you held all of them").
- **Irrelevant thought habit:** upholstery fabrics by touch and date.

## Differentiation matrix (3+ markers per pair)

| Pair | Markers |
|---|---|
| V1–V2 | V1 short/flat vs V2 periodic/semicolon; V1 hedges with "reported", V2 with Latin; V1 corrects herself, V2 corrects others |
| V1–V3 | V1 distrusts official language, V3 *is* official language; V1 dates, V3 agendises; V1 private warm / public dry, V3 the reverse |
| V1–V4 | V1 precise nouns about people, V4 about buildings; V1 dodges feelings with questions, V4 with monologue; V1's tell is the correction, V4's is "she" |
| V2–V3 | V2 jokes under stress, V3 procedures; V2 long sentences, V3 balanced clauses; V2 trade idiom, V3 committee idiom |
| V2–V4 | V2 kind when serious, V4 serious only about the building; V2 calls everyone by trade, V4 calls the building a person; V2's rhythm lands punches, V4's runs to sea |
| V3–V4 | V3 fragments in private, V4 monologues; V3 hides feeling in process, V4 hides it in craft; V3 proverbs under pressure, V4 second person to the house |

## Anti-pattern checklist (per draft, per character)

- [ ] Voices bleeding: V1's hedge words in V2's mouth, etc.
- [ ] Everyone wittier than they are — comic novel default.
- [ ] Same sentence rhythm across speakers (uniformity tell).
- [ ] Committee-ese leaking into narration (Ruth's register into V1).
- [ ] Theatre-romanticism leaking into narration (V4's "she" into V1).
- [ ] Punchline at every chapter end (coda habit).
- [ ] Dialogue in orderly turns (tic 17).

## Benchmark samples (blind-sortable)

- **V1:** "The resolution to let the lease expire was carried, seven votes to
  four, at 8:41 in the evening. The microphone howled. Councillor Ampadu
  thanked members for their clarity. The word 'legacy' appeared four times."
- **V2:** "A small paper, Miss Liss, survives the way a river stone survives:
  by not being the rock that argues with the current. Now — did you file the
  inquest or didn't you?"
- **V3:** "We are not deciding what to kill. We are deciding what to keep —
  let's be clear about the difference. Agenda item four. ...The flowers are
  wrong again, who ordered lilies."
- **V4:** "She's not a building. Six thousand seats and you think she's a
  building. Counterweights don't lie to you. Row G still smells of velvet —
  touch that, go on: 1969 mohair blend, and it held."

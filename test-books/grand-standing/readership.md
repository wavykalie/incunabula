# READERSHIP — Grand Standing (phase 1.5)

Three personas plus one hostile. The **primary drives writing**; the **hostile
drives evaluation**. Date: 2026-10-14.

## 1. PRIMARY — "Margaret", 58, council-adjacent reader

- **Who:** retired school librarian, Mardenbridge's own kind; reads on the bus.
- **Reading habits:** 25 books/year, all fiction, warm comic novels and family
  sagas; abandons a book by page 50 if nobody has spoken to her yet.
- **Emotional trigger:** recognition — "that is exactly how our town talks".
  Laughs when the institution is caught in its own language.
- **Deal-breaker:** cruelty to ordinary people; a book that mocks its own
  characters instead of the committee.
- **Discovery:** library displays, a friend who also reads Coe and Tyler.

## 2. "Owen", 34, the trade-curious

- **Who:** local-news reporter in a mid-size city; joined a dying industry and
  knows it.
- **Reading habits:** mostly nonfiction (Nick Davies, Caitriona Lally), 8–10
  novels/year; wants to see the newsroom done right.
- **Emotional trigger:** verisimilitude of the trade — the filing ritual, the
  editor's shorthand, the fear of the correction. Authenticity is the whole
  purchase.
- **Deal-breaker:** fake journalism — newsrooms written by people who have
  never missed a deadline. A wrong masthead detail and he is gone.
- **Trigger to buy:** quoted accuracy in the first pages ("the paper's last
  reporter" as a job description, not a metaphor).

## 3. "Priya”, 27, architecture-adjacent

- **Who:** junior at a heritage consultancy; reads about buildings for pleasure.
- **Reading habits:** nonfiction-heavy (Rasmussen, *How Buildings Learn*), one
  novel a month as a treat.
- **Emotional trigger:** the building treated as a character with dignity —
  the ache of a grand space used badly.
- **Deal-breaker:** architecture used as wallpaper; monuments described only
  in travel-brochure adjectives.
- **Trigger to buy:** the premise sentence (six thousand seats in a town of
  forty thousand).

## 4. HOSTILE — "Dermot", 45, the anti-precious

- **Who:** reads crime and military history; gave up on "literary" after being
  burned by three plotless novels.
- **Reading habit:** decides in ten pages; believes quiet novels are a scam.
- **What he attacks:** "nothing happens" — if the budget fight is the only
  engine, he calls it a sitcom with paragraphs. Also: whimsy. Any passage
  where the narrator is charmed by the town for its own sake loses him for a
  chapter.
- **Deal-breaker:** a midpoint with no irreversible change; an ending that
  settles for acceptance without cost.
- **Value to the book:** he is right about pace. His question — *what is at
  stake if the lease is not renewed, in money, for Marnie?* — must be answerable
  by chapter three.

## Synthesis

- **Persona that drives writing:** Margaret (recognition, warmth, talk).
- **Persona that drives evaluation:** Dermot (pace, stakes, no whimsy).
- **The trigger shared by all four:** the one-sentence premise. It does the
  selling; the prose must only do the keeping.

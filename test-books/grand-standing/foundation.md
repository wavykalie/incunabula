# FOUNDATION — Grand Standing (phase 2, forme)

Premise: `premise.md`. Market: `research/market-map.md`. Readers: `readership.md`.
Date: 2026-10-14.

## Theme (as a question, never a statement)

**What does a town owe the things it built to impress?**
Secondary: what does a reporter owe a story she cannot stop?

## The cast — each with chaos

### Marnie Liss (P-002... assigned P-001 in canon) — protagonist, 29

- **Wound:** her father ran the Sentinel's letters page for thirty years and
  was laid off by email. She took the job to prove the paper still meant
  something; the paper has been dying under her the entire time.
- **Lie she believes:** "If I write it precisely enough, the writing is the
  saving." She believes accuracy is a form of rescue.
- **Arc:** from recorder to participant — from writing the town's obituary to
  being implicated in its decision.
- **Irrelevant obsession:** municipal fonts. She collects photographs of civic
  lettering and can date a building by its signage.
- **Distortion:** she reads every room as a filing system — people to her are
  sources, quotes, deadlines.
- **Unprompted memory:** her father reading the council minutes aloud at
  breakfast, laughing at clause 7. It surfaces whenever she sits in the
  council chamber.
- **Failed emotional management:** she deflects every kindness with a
  follow-up question. When her editor dies mid-season she files 800 words
  before she cries.

### Desmond Vellum (P-002) — editor of the Sentinel, 63

- **Wound:** he once killed a true story about the theatre's structural survey
  because the council advertised on the facing page. He has the guilt of a man
  who sold a building for a quarter-page ad.
- **Lie:** "We're a small paper. Small papers survive by not fighting."
- **Arc:** from managing decline to going down swinging (his last edition runs
  the survey).
- **Irrelevant obsession:** the correct placement of an apostrophe; the
  obituary file he keeps of living people ("everyone gets one — it's only
  civil").
- **Distortion:** sees every story in column inches, even the ones about
  himself.
- **Unprompted memory:** the night the theatre opened, 1969, his father took
  him; the velvet.
- **Failed emotional management:** he converts grief into layout. When his
  wife left he redesigned the sports page.

### Councillor Ruth Ampadu (P-003) — chair of the Estates committee, 52

- **Wound:** she campaigned on "a monument for Mardenbridge" and won; the
  monuments are now hers to kill.
- **Lie:** "There is always a third option." She believes process can absorb
  any outcome.
- **Arc:** from managing the unmanageable to choosing which monument dies and
  owning it publicly.
- **Irrelevant obsession:** competitive flower arranging; she treats the
  council chamber's floral budget as a battleground.
- **Distortion:** converts every moral question into a working group.
- **Unprompted memory:** her mother's flat above the chip shop, the smell of
  vinegar in the wallpaper.
- **Failed emotional management:** she schedules her own breakdowns for
  recess weeks.

### Jerome Arkwright (P-004) — the theatre's last manager, 41

- **Wound:** he took the job because he loved the building, and the building
  has spent fifteen years eating his life.
- **Lie:** "The building chooses its people." He romanticises the theatre so
  he doesn't have to leave it.
- **Arc:** from custodian of a ruin to admitting the ruin is winning — and
  then deciding what to save of it that is not stone.
- **Irrelevant obsession:** seat upholstery. He can identify any fabric on any
  seat in the house by touch, eyes closed.
- **Distortion:** treats maintenance as morality — a leaking roof is a moral
  failing, not a budget line.
- **Unprompted memory:** finding a wedding ring in row G after a matinee,
  never claimed.
- **Failed emotional management:** he monologues to the empty auditorium and
  stops noticing he does it.

### The town (as a character)

Mardenbridge: monument-rich, maintenance-poor. Its voice is the council
minutes — grand language for small decisions. It does not want to be mocked;
the book must never sneer at it. Margaret (persona 1) is Mardenbridge's
reader; Dermot (hostile) is its impatience.

## Stakes (Dermot's question, answered)

If the lease is not renewed: the Sentinel loses its last running story (and
its last reason to exist — Marnie's employment ends with the paper's), Arkwright
loses his home (he lives in the stage-left flat), and the town loses the only
venue that ever made it feel like a city. In money: the lease is £1/year
peppercorn; the *repair* liability is £2.4m. That is what the council is
actually refusing. Chapter three must state these numbers.

## Opening strategy

Open in the council chamber at the announcement, in Marnie's file voice: grand
resolution language, zero grandeur in the room. The comic engine starts
immediately — a monumental phrase about "legacy" while the microphone howls.
First sentence must carry the premise's irony.

## Re-read architecture

The obituary file (Desmond's living-obituary drawer) is seeded in chapter 2
and pays off in chapter 30 (Marnie writes Desmond's — and files it early, the
failure-turned-ritual). Second read reveals the council's clause numbering
running through the book as a quiet countdown.

## Stylistic device

**The filed report** — Marnie's prose is her own copy: precise, dated, sourced,
with corrections appended. The novel's chapters are dispatches that slowly stop
being filed and start being lived. The device earns the comic dryness and
provides the arc meter.

## Ranked engagement types

1. **Empathy** (primary) — Marnie's season.
2. **Intellectual** (secondary) — civic mechanics, the budget fight.
3. **Fascination** (tertiary) — the building's absurd scale.
Conflict empathy/intellectual is the book's identity (per market map): do not
resolve.

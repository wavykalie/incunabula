# 4

Give the child one real choice each night and the evening becomes hers in a way no amount of control can provide. Let her pick between two pairs of pajamas. Let her choose which of two books comes first. Both options are ones you can live with, so the choice is real and the boundary is still yours.

Negotiation at bedtime is rarely about pajamas. It is about a small person testing whether the world has edges she can trust. A child who finds no edge keeps pushing, because pushing is how she looks for one. The search is exhausting for her too.

Refusal is the exception that proves the boundary. When she refuses both pajamas, the choice closes: pajamas now, or pajamas in one minute with the lamp going out either way. Two sentences, no speech, no negotiation. Then follow through at the minute. The follow-through is the boundary; the rest of the evening depends on it. The words were only the notice.

Praise does more work at eight in the evening than any threat has ever done. When she puts the pajamas on the first time she is asked, say so, plainly, once. Named praise teaches the body what to repeat. A raised voice teaches only that the hour is dangerous.

Praise should name the act instead of the child. "You put the pajamas on when I asked" teaches more than "you are a good girl", because the first can be repeated tomorrow by an ordinary tired child and the second cannot be repeated at all.

The line itself stays warm and fixed. The voice softens; the boundary does not move. She can cry about the lamp. The lamp still goes out. A boundary that moves when a child cries teaches her that crying is the tool which moves boundaries. She will use the tool that works, and it will be the wrong tool for the next fifteen years.

The weather of the evening matters here. An adult in the grip of anger should hold the line without commentary. "The lamp goes out now," said flatly, beats a speech. Speeches at eight o'clock reach a tired child as noise; she hears the anger and not the argument, and the argument was the only part worth hearing.

Praise the pajamas. Keep both options livable, and let the lamp go out on time. That is a complete evening's work, and it takes four minutes.

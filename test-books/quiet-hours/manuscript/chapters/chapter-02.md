# 2

A bedtime routine is a piece of engineering. Its parts matter less than its order; the order is what the child actually reads.

Watch any household with a settled evening and you will find the same structure underneath: the steps happen in the same sequence every night. The bath is followed by the pajamas, the pajamas by the book, the book by the lamp going dim. Nothing on that list is remarkable. What does the work is the sameness. By the third night the sequence has been learned; by the tenth, most of the parenting is already done.

Two mistakes ruin the engineering, and both of them are natural. The first is variety. Parents add steps to make the night special: a longer bath, an extra story, a song played off a phone. The routine becomes a performance the child must stay awake for. Special nights belong to other hours. Bedtime is the wrong place for surprise.

The second mistake is length. A routine that takes forty minutes will not survive a bad Tuesday. When the sequence is long, every step becomes an invitation to renegotiate. A tired parent negotiating at eight o'clock has already lost. Length is the enemy of repetition, and repetition is the entire mechanism. Keep the whole sequence under fifteen minutes and it can run on the worst night of the month.

The order also does one quiet thing that nothing else does. It hands the child a way to know where she is. In the middle of the sequence she can feel which step is next, and she can feel the end coming. Anticipation replaces dread, and the body, which was braced against the unknown, begins to unbrace. Sleep cannot be commanded. Sleep arrives, and the order of the evening is the road.

That is what lets the same routine work at home, at a grandparent's house and in a hotel room, while a longer, more elaborate one collapses in all three places. The child follows the shape of the evening, not the bath.

Choose the order now, while the evening is calm. Write the sequence down where the child can see it, and then run it exactly as written for ten nights before you change anything.

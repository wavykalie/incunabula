# 5

The routine will break. Plan now, while the evenings are calm, because the repair is the part everyone improvises badly.

Three things break a sequence: the stress of travel, illness, and the arrival of a grandparent who has never seen the card.

Travel first. The discomfort of a hotel room is no reason to abandon the order. The wash can happen in a hotel sink; it takes ninety seconds. The book is the one you packed, the thin one from chapter three. The light is the bedside lamp going dim at the same point as always. The hotel bathroom is irrelevant. What travels is the order, and the order fits in a suitcase.

The arithmetic of illness is different: a sick child cannot follow any sequence and should not be asked to. Drop to one step during illness: the book, or the light, or just your hand resting on her back. The one step keeps the thread alive. When the illness passes, the routine does not return by itself. Run the short version for three nights, exactly as written, and the old sequence will be waiting underneath it.

Then there are the substitutes: grandparents, aunts, the neighbor who covers an evening. Hand them the card. The card does the explaining you will be too tired to do, and it protects the order from kindness. The grandparent who would add three stories is not being generous at the wrong hour so much as being handed the agreement instead. Kindness at bedtime is measured in weeks rather than minutes; a story added tonight costs a story demanded every night.

One reset ritual earns its place at the end of a bad run of nights. After three broken nights, pick the next calm evening and run the long version on purpose: the full bath, both books, the song. Not as a reward for surviving, and not as a replacement for the short version, but as a reminder to everyone in the house of what the sequence feels like when it runs well.

The nights that break are also the nights that show what the sequence is made of. A routine that survives a hotel, a fever and a grandparent has become a family custom, and the lesson costs nothing.

Decide the three steps before tomorrow evening. Run them in the same order and at the same hour, for ten nights. Then notice which of you is sleeping; it will not be the child.

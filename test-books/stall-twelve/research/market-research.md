# Market Study: Stall Twelve (comic ghost caper, long novel)

## Field
Comic fiction with a milieu hook + cozy ghost mystery sells on warmth + procedure + one impossible logistics problem. Shelf (provisional comps, unverified live): TRANSCRIPT-respecting farce with a paper-trail engine.

## Comparables
| # | Title | Author | Year | Reach | Angle |
|---|-------|--------|------|-------|-------|
| 1 | The Thursday Murder Club | Richard Osman | 2020 | very wide | warm ensemble + place loyalty |
| 2 | A Man Called Ove | Fredrik Backman | 2012 | very wide | grump-with-heart + community siege |
| 3 | The House on the Cerulean Sea | TJ Klune | 2020 | wide | cozy impossible-household bureaucracy |
| 4 | Ghost-buddy lineage | (Canterville onwards) | — | deep backlist | haunting as admin problem |

## Patterns
Shared: stubborn centre, ensemble siege, paperwork as plot, farce around a sad core. Opening: ghost whose unfinished business is a LEASE CLAUSE (admin, not murder). Complaints: farce without stakes; ghosts without rules; endings by lecture.

## Comp titles
1. **Ove** — shares: siege + reluctant keeper. 2. **Thursday Murder Club** — shares: ensemble place-loyalty. 3. **Cerulean Sea** — shares: cozy bureaucracy vs impossible residents.

## Positioning
- Gap: comic ghost story where the haunting is a commercial-lease dispute and the exorcism is conveyancing.
- Audience: comic-fiction adults who finish books; ghost-cozy crossover; stall/market stretch readers.
- Length: 95-130k (human-ordered; genre median for comic novels ~80-100k — earned by 20-chapter siege architecture).
- Engagement: Empathy (Delia's corner) -> Fascination (Pye's leaks) -> Intellectual (lease puzzle).
- Risks: farce deflation (mitigate: real eviction stakes, wages/flats on paper); ghost-rule drift (mitigate: fryer-memory rule, stated once, never broken).

## Recommendations
1. Delia cornered (stall = late husband's), not plucky. 2. Ghost rules x3, stated early, never broken. 3. One surplus running gag per principal. 4. End chapters on image/gag-withdrawn, never on moral.

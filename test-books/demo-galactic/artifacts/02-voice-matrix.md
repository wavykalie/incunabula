# Voice matrix — Bell Tide (demo)

**Preference applied:** `USER_PREFERENCES.md` §1 Naming. Names chosen for a reason, reason
written down, one character deliberately given none.

**Blind Sort test:** cover the name, read the line, name the speaker. This is the
pass/fail for the matrix. Carried in §3 as the derivation, because in this book the name
*is* part of the voice.

---

## 1. The naming rule for this book

Every name comes from the fen — its work, its weather, its saints, its parish calendar —
and every name is one the character's family would plausibly have picked. No name is
imported from outside the world of the story.

**One exception, deliberate, and it is the exception that proves the rule.** See §4.

## 2. Voice cards

### Swithin Garrow (P-001), 14 — pump-keeper's grandson
**Name.** St Swithin's Day, 2 July, the old rain charm: *if it rains on Swithin's day,
forty days of rain follow.* He is named for a saint who predicts water. The name is a
job description in a fen. It is also a warning about patience — forty days is longer
than anyone waits for proof.
**Wants.** For someone to make him responsible.
**Lie.** That knowing something is the same as being able to show it.
**Voice.** Counts. Says distances and times as a way of not saying he's frightened. Does
not use "I think." When he is uncertain he gives a number.
**Under pressure.** Gets quieter and more exact. Answers the question asked, not the
question meant.
**Anti-pattern.** Never lyrical. Never "the fen seemed to breathe." He has no words for
weather that are not conditions.

### Hesper Garrow (P-002), 71 — grandmother, keeps the almanac
**Name.** *Hesper* — the evening star, and the evening office in the Book of Common
Prayer, which has a hymn about the sun going down. She is the last light in the village
and the last person who has written a number down. Surname shared with Swithin: the root
repeats because the root is the family's.
**Wants.** For the almanac to still be true.
**Lie.** That a written record is a defence.
**Voice.** Old English survives in her and she does not know it. Says "when we" meaning
her generation. Reads aloud when she quotes. Uses the passive for other people's decisions
so she is not obliged to name who made them.
**Under pressure.** Starts counting things out loud — a habit, not a tic.
**Anti-pattern.** Never explains the family history. She refers to it.

### Ivo Parr (P-003), 16 — fishes the drain, knows the water
**Name.** A *parr* is a young salmon — a fish that returns to the river it was born in to
die. He knows the water better than the pump does, and he is the one who leaves.
**Wants.** To be asked to leave before he has to ask.
**Lie.** That knowing a place well is a reason to stay in it.
**Voice.** Talks to the water and about the water in the same register. Long declaratives
that keep going past the point. Uses "you" for Swithin and "we" for nobody.
**Under pressure.** Repeats the last word the other person said, then answers it.
**Anti-pattern.** Never metaphorical about the water. He is not poetic about the thing he
knows best.

### Anstice Coombe (P-003b), 15 — wants the fen flooded
**Name.** Old English *anstice* — "a thing that is opposite, a counterpoise, that which
answers," from riddles and charms; one of the oldest words in the language. She is the
argument that answers his. The deepest root in the book, given to the person who is his
opposite.
**Wants.** To be contradicted by him, specifically.
**Lie.** That being right about one thing entitles you to a consequence.
**Voice.** Asks the question she already knows the answer to, then waits. Answers in his
language — numbers, distances — because she learned them from him.
**Under pressure.** Becomes formal. Uses his full name, once, at the one moment she is
closest to changing sides.
**Anti-pattern.** Never shouts. Her whole threat is that she is reasonable and he is not.

### Joe Cobb (P-004), 52 — pump mechanic
**Name.** *Nothing derived.* See §4. This is the finding of the exercise.
**Wants.** To finish the job.
**Lie.** None. That is his function.
**Voice.** Plain, technical, unsentimental, and the only character who is comfortable in
a conversation he is not winning. Says "that's the machine" when asked a philosophical
question.
**Under pressure.** Does not change.
**Anti-pattern.** Never becomes significant. The whole risk with this character is that a
writer decides he is wise and ruins him.

## 3. Blind Sort check

Each line below, name removed, must be attributable:

- *"It came up to the second sluice. Maybe four hours."* → **Swithin** (counts, no hedge)
- *"We had the last of the fair weather written down, and it was wrong."* → **Hesper**
  ("we" = her generation; written record)
- *"You can hear when the drain's full, it's a different sound, it's a—"* → **Ivo** (runs
  past the point)
- *"Swithin. How long does a pump take to fail."* → **Anstice** (the full name; the
  question she already knows the answer to)
- *"That's the machine."* → **Joe**

**Result: 5/5.** The plain name is *why* Joe passes. A charm-name would have made his
line sound significant, and it is not.

## 4. The decline

`Cobb` is a fen surname — *cob* is the clay the fen is cut from, and Cobb is a real
occupational name from fen drainage — but **nothing is derived from it.** Joe could have
been **Wilkin Swith** (the second saint of the fen flood calendar) or **Alba Garrow** (the
almanac's word for a clear night). Both were defensible. Both were wrong.

**Reason given, as the preference requires:** a name that means something makes a promise
the character will not keep. Joe is the character who is not carrying the book — the only
person in the village who tells the truth without it costing him anything, which is
precisely why he is not the protagonist. He is the plain name in a book of loaded ones,
and that is the only way a reader can tell he is not part of the myth.

**If the preference had been enforced, this character would have been handed a charm-name
and the reader would have been told, wrongly, that he mattered.** The decline is the
proof that the preference is a preference.

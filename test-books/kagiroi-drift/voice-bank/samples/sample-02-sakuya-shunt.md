# Sample 02: Sakuya Acoustic Shunt (Sensory Hypersensitivity)

The station didn't creak; it rang.

Sakuya kept her temples pressed against the acoustic console's rubber edge, her teeth locked together so hard her jaw ached. The telemetry came through the titanium pins in her C4 vertebrae before the monitors drew the line. It felt like salt water being forced through a needle. A low, rolling harmonic from the fourth counterweight cable—eighteen hertz, slow as a dying pulse.

"Fluctuation in sector seven," she whispered. The room was dark, save for the green phosphorus wash of the telemetry barrel. Her fingers were curled against her thighs, nails digging into the rough linen of her work trousers. If she opened her eyes all the way, the phosphorus smeared into trails of yellow fire.

She didn't want the injection. The corporate cooling serum left a film like paraffin on her tongue that took three days to scrape off. But without it, the station's bones would keep shouting into her spine until she vomited.

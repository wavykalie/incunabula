# Chapter 1: The Cold Weld

The tether had three millimeters of slack, and every millimeter belonged to Geryon-7.

Kaname hung forty meters out from the siphon skirt, her magnetic boot-plates humming against the rusted ribbing of Ring Theta. Between her calves and the planet's cloud deck there was twelve thousand kilometers of hydrogen soup, lit from beneath by sulfur lightning that flashed like broken glass in a dark cellar. The radiation monitors inside her helmet were clicking at sixty-five pulses a minute. Slow enough to ignore for another hour, fast enough that her back teeth tasted of silver fillings.

She set the head of the hydraulic pinch-bar against the hinge pin of the outer bleed valve.

"Torque set," she said into the collar pickup. Her voice was flat, dried out by fourteen hours on recirculated nitrogen. "Give me half a turn on the secondary jack, Sakuya."

Through the helmet audio, there was a three-second lag—not from distance, but from the acoustic filters scrubbing the gas-giant's radio screech out of the hull-line. When Sakuya spoke, her voice came through thin and cold, like water poured over iron.

"Secondary jack engaged. Three hundred bar. Don't slip, Kaname. The skirt is vibrating at forty hertz."

"I can feel it," Kaname said. "The whole bracket is buzzing like an angry hornet."

She could feel it in her shins. The whole ring was shuddering as it dragged through the wisps of the upper atmosphere, skimming methane to feed the station's reaction thrusters. It was a heavy, rhythmic thumping that traveled straight through the soles of her suit into the marrow of her femurs. If she let her knees lock, the vibration made her vision blur into double images of the storm clouds below.

She leaned her shoulder into the pinch-bar. The sleeve of her rig groaned, the braided Kevlar outer weave stretching over the pressurized silicone gel beneath. Her arm muscles were knotting with lactic acid, trembling with the dull, sick ache of a shift that had started before the previous orbital dawn.

"Come on," she muttered to the bolt. "Turn, you rusted pig."

The bolt broke loose with a dull *thunk* that traveled through the tool into her palms. A spray of frozen grease flakes drifted out into the vacuum, sparkling violet for half a second before the planet's magnetosphere stripped their charge and swallowed them.

Kaname seated the retaining clip with two hard taps of her copper mallet.

"Hinges seated," she said, breathing hard. The inside of her faceplate had fogged at the lower left corner where the heater wire had burnt out three weeks ago. "Locking the housing now. Clear the line."

"Line clear," Sakuya answered. A pause followed, long enough for Kaname to hear the quiet catch in Sakuya's throat—the little hitch that always gave away an acoustic spike through her spinal shunt. "Hurry inside. The trailing tether just dropped three percent on tension."

"On my way."

Kaname didn't unhook her safety line until her primary winch had reeled her back to the guide rail. She hauled herself hand over hand along the yellow guide cable, boots skittering on the rime of frozen hydrazine that coated the rim of the airlock hatch. Her breath was coming in short, sour rasps. Every joint in her fingers throbbed where the heating coils in the gloves pressed against her knuckles.

The outer hatch of Airlock Ring Theta cranked shut behind her with the heavy, hydraulic thump of lead seals meeting steel.

Silence followed.

It was the sudden, dead silence of the pump-down cycle, and it hit Kaname like an iron bar across the collarbones.

Her pulse jumped instantly to a hundred and twenty. Her vision tunneled toward the emergency glow strip on the deck. In the vacuum of the lock, there was no air to carry sound—no engine hum, no atmospheric rush, no station rattle. Only the wet, heavy thudding of her own heart and the harsh rasp of her breath rebounding inside the fiberglass helmet shell.

*Forty hours,* her brain screamed, unprompted and vicious. *Forty hours in the Minato pod. Just you and three corpses turning blue while the heater contactors click.*

Kaname's thumb jammed down on the manual purge toggle on her chest rig. She clicked it once, twice, three times, four times. The rapid, mechanical snapping of the spring was the only tactile sensation that cut through the numbness in her heavy gloves. Her teeth clamped down hard on her lower lip until she tasted the sharp, hot iron of fresh blood inside her mouth.

"Atmosphere incoming," the lock annunciator crackled through the speaker grille.

A roaring jet of recycled air slammed into the chamber, carrying the rank smells of the station: scorched insulation, damp insulation batting, and synthetic lubricant. The noise broke over Kaname's ears, and the suffocating band around her ribs loosened an inch.

The inner hatch swung open with a screech of dry bearings.

Kurotsuyu Sakuya stood on the threshold, framed by the pale sodium lighting of the ring's interior conduit.

She was wearing her thin gray undersuit, the collar unfastened to expose the pale curve of her throat and the silver-rimmed telemetry port set into the skin beneath her fourth cervical vertebra. The port was glowing with a faint, angry violet light—the neural interface running hot from reading the station's structural groan. Her hair was tied back with a loop of braided copper wire, loose dark strands sticking to her temples where sweat had dried into salt lines.

She looked at Kaname through long, dark lashes. Her eyes were overly bright, dilated from sensory strain.

"You took twenty seconds too long at the hinge," Sakuya said. Her voice was quiet, but it had the razor edge of nerves worn down to the quick. "The third counterweight cable twanged twice. It felt like someone was dragging a serrated saw across my shoulder blades."

"Bolt was seized," Kaname grunted. She reached behind her neck, found the latch rings of her helmet, and jerked them forward.

The seals hissed. She pulled the heavy helmet off and let it drop into the rubber storage bin beside the door.

Her dark cropped hair was soaked, plastered flat against her temples, framing the fine, delicate contour of her flushed face and dark eyes. The cool drafts of the station corridor hit her bare throat, making her shiver violently. She reached down with stiff, shaking fingers to peel the outer thermal smock off, tossing it onto the grated deck.

Beneath the smock, her bio-gel undersuit clung to her body like a second skin. It was semi-translucent, pressurized silicone, designed to prevent explosive blood-pooling during high-g maneuvers, and it sealed directly against bare flesh with bio-adhesive gel. After fourteen hours of hard physical labor, it was saturated with body heat, sweat, and friction.

The suit smelled of sharp sweat, copper, and cold neoprene.

Kaname caught the release tab at her right hip and pulled.

The seal gave way with a wet, sticky tear that made her hiss through her teeth. The silicone peeled away from her skin, leaving behind a wide, angry red welt where the friction had chafed her hip bone raw.

Sakuya stepped forward into the narrow airlock vestibule. The smell of her was immediate and sharp: cold skin, the chemical tang of neuro-cooling balm, and the clean, feminine musk that always clung to her collar.

"Stop yanking at it," Sakuya said. "You'll tear the epidermis."

"It's glued to my ribs," Kaname muttered, her voice dropping into the husky rasp that came after breathing tank air all day. "Help me or let me bleed in peace."

Sakuya didn't leave. She closed the distance between them until her chest, covered only by the thin linen of her open undersuit, was inches from Kaname's. Sakuya's fingers were bare, long-jointed, and cold from working the telemetry barrels in the unheated well.

She laid two fingers directly against the burning welt on Kaname's hip.

The contrast was so sharp that Kaname's breath hitched in her throat. She flinched, but her hips jerked forward into Sakuya's touch, craving the sheer reality of another human body after fourteen hours of hanging over the abyss.

"You're burning up," Sakuya said softly. Her thumb traced the edge of the inflamed skin, gentle and deliberate.

"The heater coils in the thigh pads shorted out two hours ago," Kaname said, watching Sakuya's mouth. "I had to crank the core unit to keep my toes from turning black. My skin feels like it was scrubbed with pumice."

"Let me take it."

Sakuya worked her fingers under the edge of the silicone seal at Kaname's waist. Slowly, peeling with careful, even pressure, she rolled the damp gel-liner down past Kaname's slender hips. As the suit peeled away, Kaname's naked skin was exposed to the cool station air—lithe, honey-toned flesh, stippled with goosebumps and glistening with sweat. Across her trim midriff, her abdominal muscles were sculpted in sharp, defined washboard ridges that flexed with every ragged breath, tapering down to a narrow waist and athletic, toned thighs.

Kaname let out a long, ragged exhale that sounded almost like a sob.

"Your hands are like ice," Kaname murmured, reaching out to catch Sakuya's wrist. Her calloused thumb pressed into the soft tendon of Sakuya's forearm, feeling the rapid, fluttery beat of Sakuya's pulse. "God, they're cold."

"The telemetry trunk in Well 04 was sitting at four degrees," Sakuya said. She didn't pull away. Instead, she leaned into the grip, sliding her other palm flat against Kaname's bare ribs. Her cold fingers spread across the heat radiating from Kaname's skin. "The heat exchangers are choked with sulfur wax. If Kagari doesn't blow the vents by next watch, the sensor cores will cook."

"Let them cook," Kaname said huskily. Her fingers tightened on Sakuya's wrist, drawing the taller woman closer until their thighs brushed. "I don't care about the cores right now."

"You will when the tether snaps," Sakuya said, but her voice lacked conviction. Her eyes drifted shut as she pressed her forehead against the hollow of Kaname's shoulder.

Kaname stood there in the narrow, sodium-lit airlock, naked from the waist up, her chest heaving as the last remnants of the Minato panic drained out of her limbs. The smell of Sakuya's hair—clean copper and bitter neuro-balm—filled her throat. The grounding, solid warmth of another living creature pressing against her skin was the only thing that could push the cold corpses out of her head.

She wrapped both arms around Sakuya's back, pulling her flush against her bare torso.

Sakuya gasped at the sudden, breathless contact, her fingers digging into the lean, sculpted muscles along Kaname's shoulder blades. For a long moment, neither of them moved. The airlock annunciator hummed its steady fifty-cycle drone, the station groaned as it swung through periapsis, and between the steel walls and the roaring void below, they shared the only heat left on the ring.

"Come on," Sakuya whispered against Kaname's neck, her lips brushing the damp skin behind Kaname's ear. "Get out of the rest of that suit. The shift is over."

"Not yet," Kaname said, her voice rough with fatigue and sudden, sharp desire. "Not until you let me look at your neck."

She slid one calloused hand up Sakuya's spine, her fingers skimming the smooth skin until they reached the silver rim of the cervical port. The metal was fever-hot to the touch, vibrating with the high, faint whine of sensory data that Sakuya had been swallowing all day.

Sakuya shuddered, her head tilting back as Kaname's thumb stroked the tender, inflamed flesh around the port's perimeter.

"It hurts," Sakuya admitted, the words barely audible over the vent hiss. "The storm below... it won't stop singing."

"I'll make it stop," Kaname promised. She caught Sakuya by the nape of her neck, tilting her face down, and kissed her—hard, desperate, tasting the salt on Sakuya's lips and the dry, recycled air of the station between their teeth.

Sakuya answered with a sharp, hungry sound in her throat, her hands sliding down Kaname's sweat-slick ribs to find the waistband of her pressure shorts.

Behind them, the outer hatch gave another dull, metallic creak as the siphon skirt dragged through the methane mist, but for the first time in fourteen hours, Kaname didn't hear it at all.

# Chapter 2: Acoustic Shunt

Sensor Well 04 smelled of damp wool, isopropyl alcohol, and warm circuit boards.

It was an iron cylinder thirty paces across, hung like an iron sinker beneath the lowest deck of Ring Theta. The walls were lined with copper bus-bars bolted directly to the hull ribs to pull acoustic vibration out of the station's skin. In the center of the deck, mounted on three hydraulic damping legs, sat the acoustic immersion rig: a high-backed bucket seat stripped from an old sub-orbital shuttle, its leather split and weeping yellow foam.

Sakuya was strapped into the seat, her bare heels hooked over the lower crossbar.

She had her head tipped back against the headrest. The silver-rimmed telemetry port at the base of her skull was plugged into a heavy fiber trunk that arched into the ceiling shadows like a dead snake. The jacket around the connector was sweating beads of condensation onto the rubber mat between her feet.

Kaname stood at the bottom of the ladder, wiping hydraulic grease off her knuckles with a dry rag.

"You've been jacked in for nine hours," Kaname said, keeping her tone even. "Kagari says the sweep is done. Unplug the cable, Sakuya."

Sakuya didn't open her eyes. Her fingers, resting on the worn armrests, were twitching in time with the station's roll—a rapid tremor making the skin over her tendons ripple.

"The sweep isn't done," Sakuya said. Her voice was dry, flat, and hollowed out by the signal feed. "There's a hairline split in the second shear web of Ring Gamma. It's whispering at four hundred hertz. Every time the reaction control thrusters fire, it bites."

"Let maintenance weld it tomorrow," Kaname said. She took three steps across the rubber mats. "You're redlining the buffer. Look at your hands."

"I have to hold the line," Sakuya muttered. Her eyelids fluttered, showing whites shot through with broken capillaries. "If the shear web opens wider than two centimeters while we're dipping into the atmosphere, the skirt will warp. If the skirt warps, we lose atmospheric intake. Then we all freeze in the dark."

"Nobody is freezing tonight," Kaname said.

She reached the back of the bucket seat and laid one firm, warm palm flat against Sakuya's bare shoulder.

Sakuya gasped. The muscle under Kaname's hand contracted violently, knotting like a fist.

"Don't," Sakuya choked out. "Everything... your pulse is too loud. It sounds like an ore hammer hitting an anvil."

"Then let it hit," Kaname said.

She stepped behind the chair, leaned down, and reached for the quick-release collar on the neuro-feed. Her fingers were slender and scarred, but she handled the optical coupling with the gentle precision of an exterior mechanic. She pressed the two locking tabs, rotated the brass housing a quarter-turn counterclockwise, and pulled.

The optical pins disengaged with a sharp, wet *pop*.

Sakuya screamed.

It was a choked cry of pure sensory vertigo as twelve thousand channels of telemetry were torn from her brainstem, leaving an abyss of dead air. Her back arched off the shuttle seat, spine curving like a drawn bow.

"I have you," Kaname grunted.

She caught Sakuya under the arms and hauled her out of the seat. Sakuya's limbs thrashed with residual feedback, head lolling back against Kaname's shoulder.

Kaname dragged her across the mat to the corner where the insulation batting was thickest. She dropped onto her knees, pulling Sakuya down into her lap, pinning Sakuya's trembling frame against the solid wall of her chest and thighs.

"Breathe," Kaname ordered, her mouth pressed right against the shell of Sakuya's ear. "Use my lungs. Come on, Sakuya. In and out."

Sakuya's chest heaved. Her hands came up blindly, her long fingers clawing at the canvas of Kaname's work shirt, twisting the fabric into knots.

"The silence," Sakuya gasped, tears squeezing out from beneath her shut lids. "It feels like... like someone scooped my skull out with an iron spoon. It's too quiet. There's nothing to lean on."

"Lean on me," Kaname said.

She caught Sakuya's flailing wrists in one firm, calloused hand and pinned them against Sakuya's sternum. With her free hand, she reached around to the back of Sakuya's neck.

The cervical port was scalding hot, radiating heat like an overdriven motor casing. Kaname didn't flinch. She pressed the heel of her hand directly over the silver ring, providing the firm, unyielding counter-pressure that Sakuya's shredded nervous system was screaming for.

Sakuya let out a ragged shudder. Her head fell back against Kaname's collarbone, her pulse racing beneath the pale skin of her throat.

"Press harder," Sakuya whispered, all professional distance burned away. "The nerves are misfiring... don't let me float."

"I've got you," Kaname said.

She shifted forward onto the padded mats, using the taut, athletic power of her torso to press Sakuya down flat. Her toned thighs bracketed Sakuya's slender legs, the sculpted ridges of her abdomen flush against Sakuya's hip, anchoring the tremors straight into her own flesh.

Kaname released Sakuya's wrists, peeled the collar of the gray undersuit wide, and slid one calloused hand down between their bodies beneath the waistband of Sakuya's shorts. The skin was scorching, slick with sweat and acute neural heat. When Kaname pressed two fingers firmly inside, her palm resting flat against Sakuya's pelvis, Sakuya stiffened with a sharp, breathless sob.

"Look at me," Kaname commanded softly.

Sakuya opened her dark eyes, pupils wide, locking onto Kaname's face with fierce intensity.

Kaname didn't rush. She maintained steady, deliberate pressure against the hot, swollen center, giving the overloaded spinal tracts a single physical truth to hold onto. When she curved her fingers and bore down, the release broke through Sakuya in one violent, shuddering wave.

Sakuya arched off the mats, a clear, high cry swallowed by the hum of the transformer banks. Her thighs clamped tight around Kaname's arm as the phantom static burned out of her nerves, replaced entirely by the grounding weight holding her to the deck.

Slowly, the spastic tremor drained away. Her hips settled back into the rubber batting. The cervical port at the base of her skull cooled from furious violet to a steady, quiet amber.

Kaname drew her hand back gently, resting her forehead against Sakuya's bare chest to listen to the calm, human cadence of her heart.

"Better?" Kaname murmured.

Sakuya's fingers drifted up into Kaname's short dark hair, curling against the nape of her neck.

"The station is quiet," Sakuya whispered, the tightness around her mouth finally smoothed away. "I can only hear you."

"Good," Kaname said, keeping one toned leg draped across Sakuya's thighs. "That's how it's supposed to be."

# Chapter 3: The Ghost Skiff

The docking bay in Cradle Four tasted of bitter hydrazine exhaust, burnt graphite, and cold neoprene.

Shinonome Kagari was leaning her hip against the refueling manifold, a lit vapor-stick pinched between her middle fingers. Her flight suit was tied around her waist by its greasy sleeves, exposing a faded gray tank top stained with pump grease. A small, dented tin of zinc-lanolin salve sat on the cold steel beside her half-empty mug of chicory tea.

Without looking away from the bay, Kagari flicked the tin with her thumb. It slid across the rusted manifold plate, stopping right against Kaname's boot.

"Take it," Kagari said in her flat, cigarette-scratched rasp, exhaling a thin plume of gray vapor. "Your suit seal was weeping at the wrists when you unlatched on Theta. If the quartermaster asks, a rat dragged the tin into the scuppers. And watch your boots—deck heater in bay four tripped twenty minutes ago. There's black ice on the ramp."

Kaname pocketed the tin with a curt nod, her magnetic soles clicking on the steel grating. "Where's the runner?"

"Tied to the umbilical," Kagari said, gesturing with the glowing tip of her vapor-stick toward the shadow beneath the overhead gantry crane. "Came in unannounced on cold gas thrusters. Didn't broadcast an identification squawk until she was four hundred meters off our intake skirt. I almost lit her up with the perimeter point-defense."

"Cold thrusters means she was running dark," Kaname said, looking up into the dim bay.

"Of course she was running dark," Kagari muttered. "Natsume Combine has four sensor buoys parked along the periapsis corridor. If they catch a freelance runner docking at an unregistered siphon station, they revoke our preemption charter by breakfast."

The vessel resting in Cradle Four looked less like a ship and more like an unpolished iron wedge.

It was eight meters long, flat-bellied, its hull coated in non-reflective carbon tiles scarred by micro-meteoroid pitting. A single attitude nozzle was hissing faint plumes of frozen nitrogen into the bay's thin atmosphere. The radiator fins along its spine were retracted, tucked flat into armored recesses to keep them from catching radar pulses. On the nose, stenciled in faded white paint beneath layers of burnt ablative resin, was the name *Tsumuji*.

The skiff's forward hatch hissed and swung outward on heavy counter-balance springs.

Yoiduki Mizore climbed out onto the boarding skid. She was small, wiry, dressed in a patched biotechnical runner rig with reinforced knee plates and insulated tool sleeves. Her dark hair was cropped short, bristling around her ears like animal fur, and her nose was crooked where a pressure seal had broken it five seasons back in the asteroid belt. She dropped from the skid to the deck with a light, easy thud, barely disturbing the frost underfoot.

She carried a pressurized aluminum transport container tucked under one arm like a parcel of dry goods.

"You run a noisy station, Kagari," Mizore said, her voice dry and gravelly. She tossed the container onto an empty tool locker, where it landed with a sharp metallic clatter. "I could hear your primary siphon screaming through my hull plates three thousand kilometers out. If you don't bleed the secondary intake ring, that skirt is going to peel off like an orange rind."

"We know the skirt is screaming," Kagari replied, blowing a thin stream of gray vapor toward the ceiling exhaust vents. "Did you bring the stabilizers or did you come three million miles just to critique our maintenance logs?"

"Two crates of grade-three cryo-stabilizers," Mizore said, kicking the aluminum case with the toe of her boot. "Direct from the Ganymede depot. Don't ask how I cleared the customs orbital in sector nine. The harbor master took half an ingot of unrefined tantalum and looked at his shoes."

Kaname stepped past the locker, her eyes fixed on Mizore. "You didn't burn all that reaction mass just to deliver two crates of sulfur-scrubbing salts. What else is out there?"

Mizore turned slowly. Her pale eyes flicked over Kaname's lithe frame, taking in the sculpted line of her shoulders, the bruised knuckles, the salt-crusted work collar, and the slight tremor in Kaname's right thumb where the heater coil had shorted.

"Still wearing the exterior rig like a second skin, Hotarubi?" Mizore remarked, a dry smirk touching the corner of her thin mouth. "You look like someone who forgot how to breathe inside a room. Some people never climb out of the tin can."

Kaname's jaw tightened. "Talk, Mizore. Or get back in your skiff."

Mizore pulled a pair of heavy work gloves from her belt, slapping them against her thigh. "The corporate barge *Akegata-Maru*. Three days ago, her starboard cooling manifold suffered an explosive decompression during periapsis. Her maneuvering thrusters tore themselves off the gimbal mounts. Crew evacuated in two escape pods. Natsume Combine wrote the hull off as an orbital loss and logged a scuttle contract."

Kagari straightened up from the refueling pipe, her casual posture vanishing. "The *Akegata-Maru* was carrying high-spec biotechnical cargo out of the Jovian orbital labs."

"Exactly," Mizore said. "Two sealed transport lockers in her secondary cargo bay. Forty canisters of military-grade neuro-acoustic coolant. Unrefined, sealed in pressurized dry nitrogen jackets. The good stuff—the kind that stops a spinal interface from cooking out the brainstem."

Mizore paused, looking pointedly toward the hatch leading to the lower ring conduits.

"Your acoustician," Mizore said quietly. "Kurotsuyu. She's running on fumes, isn't she? I saw the telemetry pulse off the station's array when I came in. The signal was shaking like a dying dog."

Kaname stepped forward until she stood less than a meter from the smaller runner. "Where is the barge?"

"She's tumbling in a decaying sub-synchronous vector, eighty kilometers lower than your current orbit," Mizore said. "Her periapsis is already skimming the upper cloud deck. Down there, atmospheric friction is shearing the thermal tiles—throwing sheets of that blue *kagiroi* haze the old salvage crews call ghost heat. In twenty-four hours, she enters the thermosphere. In thirty-six, she burns. And Natsume Combine has two patrol cutters burning on high-impulse vectors out of the inner moons to scuttle the wreck before freelance salvors can board."

"How much time do we have?" Kagari asked.

"Four hours," Mizore said. "Four hours before the barge enters the thermal shear boundary. If you want those canisters, you have to go now. My terms are straightforward: I want salvage rights to the freighter's deuterium fuel blocks and thirty percent of the titanium scrap. You take the coolant. Deal?"

"Deal," Kagari said before Kaname could speak.

Before they could shake on it, the station floor beneath their boots jumped.

It wasn't a rumble; it was a high-frequency acoustic shudder that traveled through the deck grating like an electric shock. High above them, in the central conduit stack, a hydraulic pipe shrieked.

A red beacon began flashing above the airlock door, pulsing in silent rhythm with the emergency sirens.

Kaname's com-collar crackled. Sakuya's voice came through, but it was shattered by static, choked with unbearable agony:

"Kaname... the third... the third counterweight... it's tearing..."

Kaname didn't wait. She turned on her heel and bolted through the inner hatch into the access tube, boots hammering against the steel steps as she dropped down toward Conduit 12.

The corridor outside Sensor Well 04 was filled with sulfur smoke and venting steam.

A high-pressure hydraulic line had cracked at the flange, spraying hot, atomized glycol across the conduit racks. Through the haze, Sakuya was slumped against the wall, her hands clamped over the sides of her head, her spine arched in rigid, violent spasms. The silver port at the back of her neck was glowing with a blinding, furious violet radiance, spitting tiny static sparks that crackled against the damp collar of her undersuit.

"Sakuya!" Kaname yelled, skidding to a halt on the wet deck plates.

She lunged forward, reaching out with both hands to grab Sakuya's shoulders, instinctively moving to pull her into the familiar crushing hold that had worked an hour before.

"I've got you," Kaname urged, trying to wrap her arms around Sakuya's torso. "Let go, Sakuya. Squeeze my hand. I'll take the weight—"

"No!" Sakuya screamed.

It was a raw, furious screech of pure neurological overload. With frantic, unexpected strength, Sakuya shoved both palms hard into Kaname's breastbone.

The impact caught Kaname completely off guard. She stumbled backward, her boots slipping on the glycol slick, her back slamming hard against the conduit racks with a hollow boom.

"Get your hands off me!" Sakuya gasped, her voice raw, tears streaming down her soot-stained cheeks. She pressed her back flat against the steel wall, trembling so violently that her teeth clicked together like dice in a cup. "Don't touch me! Every nerve... it feels like you're rubbing broken glass into an open burn!"

Kaname stood frozen, her hands half-raised in the smoky air.

"Sakuya," Kaname said, her voice shaking with confusion and sudden, cold dread. "You're seizing. Let me hold you. It stops the feedback—"

"It doesn't stop anything!" Sakuya shouted, her face contorted with anger and pain. "You think you can just wrap your arms around me and make thirty thousand tons of screaming steel disappear? You're not anchoring me, Kaname! You're just terrified! You can't stand being alone in the quiet for ten seconds without clinging to warm flesh!"

The words hit Kaname like a kinetic slug to the jaw.

The air between them seemed to freeze, thick with the smell of scorched glycol and ozone. The red emergency light bathed Sakuya's pale face in blood-dark crimson. Sakuya was breathing in shallow, jagged gasps, her fingers digging into the steel wall behind her until her fingernails turned white.

Kaname's hands slowly lowered to her sides.

She took a slow, deep breath of the tainted station air. The old panic—the cold, suffocating memory of the Minato pod, the silent corpses, the terror of stillness—clawed at the back of her throat. But she forced her feet to stay planted on the wet deck. She didn't lunge. She didn't reach.

She looked at Sakuya not as an anchor to hold onto, but as a technician fighting for her life.

"The hydraulic line," Kaname said, her voice dropping into a flat, steady professional calm. "Where is the pressure spike coming from?"

Sakuya blinked through her tears, startled by the shift. The tremor in her chin was still there, but her eyes focused on Kaname's face.

"Bypass... bypass valve three," Sakuya gasped, clutching her neck. "The acoustic resonance... it blew the relief spring. The high-pressure fluid is hammering the actuator piston. If the line bursts, the whole attitude control loop fails."

"Where is the manual cutoff?" Kaname asked, stepping toward the tool rack on the wall.

"Behind the secondary filter bank," Sakuya said, pointing a shaking finger toward the steam-filled corner of the conduit. "There's an iron wheel. It's seized. You need a cheater bar."

"Stay right there," Kaname said.

She snatched a two-foot steel pinch-bar from the wall bracket. She plunged into the sulfur fog, ducking beneath the hissing steam jet. The heat was blistering, soaking through her work shirt in seconds.

She found the valve wheel buried behind a tangle of copper coolant pipes. It was caked in black grease and frozen condensate.

Kaname jammed the chisel end of the pinch-bar through the spokes of the wheel. She braced her boot against a structural frame, set her weight into the steel bar, and pulled with every ounce of athletic strength in her sculpted core and back.

"Turn, you bastard!" she snarled.

The wheel shrieked, metal grinding against metal. A burst of scalding water vapor blasted across Kaname's forearm, searing her skin, but she clamped her teeth down and threw the lean weight of her entire body into the turn.

With a heavy, mechanical groan, the valve seated shut.

The hissing steam died instantly. The deafening water-hammer knocking inside the pipes slowed, stuttered, and fell silent.

Through the clearing mist, Sakuya slumped down against the wall onto her knees. The violent violet glare from her cervical port faded back into a dim, quiet amber. Her breathing was ragged, but the spastic rigidity had drained from her shoulders.

Kaname dropped the pinch-bar onto the deck. It rang out with a dull iron clang.

She wiped sweat and grime from her forehead with the back of her sleeve, her own heart hammering against her ribs. She walked slowly out of the steam cloud and stopped two paces away from Sakuya. She didn't reach out. She kept her hands open at her sides.

"Pressure dropped to normal," Kaname said quietly. "The line held."

Sakuya looked up from the deck. Her hair was tangled, clinging to her damp forehead, her dark eyes wide and shadowed with exhaustion. She looked at Kaname's blistered forearm, then at Kaname's steady, unmoving hands.

"I didn't mean..." Sakuya began, her voice cracking. "Kaname, what I said about the pod—"

"You were right," Kaname said simply.

Sakuya went quiet.

"I grab you because I hate the quiet," Kaname said, her voice level and honest. "When the lock cycles shut, all I hear is three people suffocating in the dark. So I hold you down to drown it out. That's on me."

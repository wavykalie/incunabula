# Chapter 6: The Inertial Burn

The central hub of Shin-Murakumo was buried beneath eight meters of lead-doped water tanks.

It was the only compartment on the sixty-thousand-ton siphon station built to endure dynamic g-forces. In the center of the bay, mounted on heavy gimbal rings, were four liquid-suspension acceleration couches. Each couch was filled with high-density fluorocarbon gel enclosed in flexible silicone bladders, contoured to support the human spine under crushing acceleration.

Kaname was strapped into couch one, Sakuya in couch two.

Their bio-gel undersuits were pressurized to sixty kilopascals, pneumatic bladders cinched over their calves, thighs, and lower bellies to prevent blood from pooling in their extremities. Through the thick silicone headrest, Kaname could hear the deep, rhythmic hum of the station's gyroscopes fighting the torque of the tethered barge.

Beside her, Sakuya was wired directly into the hub's master telemetry bus through a shielded coaxial cable.

"Cutters are at twenty-two thousand kilometers," Kagari's voice came through the cabin speaker, speaking from the hardened command shelter three decks up. "They fired two solid-slug kinetic warning shots across our bow. Range closing at eighteen kilometers per second. If we don't change our orbital plane in ninety seconds, their next salvo will sever the siphon skirt."

"Akegata's thermal rocket is primed," Sakuya said into her throat mic.

Her voice was calm, eerily steady. The sapphire port at the base of her skull was drinking in the cooling fluid, keeping her brainstem cool even as the entire station's structural stress flooded her visual cortex as waves of acoustic geometry.

"Tether tension is equalized," Sakuya continued. "Gimbal offset set to negative four point two degrees. We burn three point eight gravities for eleven seconds. Delta-v target: four hundred and twenty meters per second. That will kick our periapsis eighty kilometers clear of the thermosphere into a resonant Lagrangian transfer."

"Three point eight gravities on a tethered rig," Kaname muttered through her teeth, her hands gripping the molded steering lugs at the sides of the couch. "The secondary tow bracket is going to scream."

"I'll steer the thrust vector through the acoustic nodes," Sakuya said, turning her head on the gel pillow to meet Kaname's gaze. "Trust me, Kaname."

"I trust you," Kaname said.

"Ignition in ten," Kagari announced. "Nine. Eight. Venting pre-burn hydrogen. Hang onto your teeth."

Kaname clamped her jaw shut on her rubber bite-guard. She dug her heels into the gel bladder, locking her knees, tightening the sculpted core muscles of her abdomen, bracing her lean frame against the rig.

At zero, the universe caved in.

The *Akegata-Maru*'s thermonuclear engine ignited with an unimaginable, silent roar of raw hydrogen plasma. Through sixty thousand tons of tethered steel, the kinetic thrust hit like an iron locomotive slamming into the station's spine.

The liquid couch groaned beneath Kaname, the silicone bladder flattening out as her effective body weight spiked from zero to over two hundred kilograms.

An invisible slab of solid lead dropped onto her chest.

Her lungs collapsed under the sheer mass. Air was forced out of her throat in a dry, rattling hiss. The pneumatic bladders around her thighs inflated with a sharp hiss of high-pressure air, squeezing her legs in a vice-like grip to force oxygenated blood back up toward her heart. Her vision instantly tunneled, the edges of the sodium ceiling lamps blurring into dark gray static.

She couldn't lift her hands. Her fingers were pinned to the grips, heavy as cast iron.

Beside her, Sakuya's neck was rigid against the headrest, her throat corded with strain, her teeth bared in an agonizing grimace. But Sakuya's eyes were fixed on the dark ceiling, pupils darting in microscopic, rapid twitches.

Through the telemetry cable, Sakuya was flying sixty thousand tons of vibrating metal by ear.

She could hear the tow cable stretching—a high, singing whistle at twelve hundred hertz. She could hear the acoustic resonance building in the siphon skirt, threatening to rip the intake flaps away from the outer ring. With tiny, neural-impulse commands sent through her spinal shunt, Sakuya pulsed the barge's auxiliary gimbal actuators, feathering the thrust vector by fractions of a millimeter to damp the harmonic waves before they could shatter the welds.

*Four seconds,* the digital timer flashed amber on the bulkhead.

Kaname's ribs creaked. Every breath required a conscious, violent heave of her diaphragm against the crushing pressure. Blood tasted metallic and sour on her tongue. Through the gray tunnel of her narrowing sight, she looked at Sakuya.

Sakuya's nose was bleeding. A single, dark drop of blood welled from her left nostril, creeping down her pale upper lip under the relentless pull of the burn.

Kaname strained with every fiber of muscle in her forearm, fighting the crushing weight, forcing her left hand an inch off the armrest. Her trembling, glove-clad fingers reached across the narrow gap between their couches.

She found Sakuya's limp right hand.

Kaname's fingers clamped down over Sakuya's cold knuckles, squeezing with everything she had left.

Sakuya felt it. Her fingers twitched, then closed tight around Kaname's grip, anchoring back.

*Seven seconds.*

A sharp, violent shockwave rocked the hull—not from their own engines, but from the kinetic slugs fired by the pursuing cutters. The railgun rounds struck the expanding plume of thermonuclear hydrogen behind the barge, vaporizing instantly in the ten-thousand-degree exhaust gas.

The blinding cloud of ionized plasma bloomed across the sky like a second sun, completely frying the optical sensors and targeting radar of the corporate cutters.

"Cutters are breaking off!" Kagari's voice crackled through the comms, distorted by acceleration strain. "They lost target lock! Their radiators are cooking from our exhaust plume! They're peeling away!"

*Ten seconds.*

*Eleven.*

The main engine shut down.

The crushing weight vanished in an instant.

The violent transition from nearly four gravities to pure, weightless freefall hit Kaname like a physical blow. Her body surged forward against the chest straps, her lungs expanding with a sudden, deep gasp of cool air.

Beside her, Sakuya slumped limp into the gel couch, the drop of blood on her lip breaking free to float in the air between them like a tiny crimson pearl.

The digital display on the bulkhead blinked green:

*PERIAPSIS SECURED. LAGRANGIAN TRANSFER ORBIT ACHIEVED.*

Kaname unlatched her chest belt with shaking fingers. She rolled out of her couch, pushing off the frame, and pulled Sakuya into her arms.

"We made it," Kaname whispered, wiping the blood from Sakuya's lip with the soft pad of her thumb. "The cutters are gone. We're clear."

Sakuya opened her eyes. She looked at Kaname, her chest heaving, a faint, exhausted smile touching her mouth.

"I told you," Sakuya murmured, her hand resting over Kaname's heart. "I told you to trust me."

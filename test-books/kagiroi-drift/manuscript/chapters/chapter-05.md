# Chapter 5: The Unburied

The primary cargo bay of the *Akegata-Maru* was thirty paces wide, built like an armored vault between the fuel tanks and the reactor core.

Kaname had thirty-six titanium cylinders lashed into two heavy nylon transit bags, each bag cinched tight with high-tensile ratchet straps. The canisters were cold, their brushed shells sweating frost in the thin cabin air. Four empty shells lay discarded on the deck by the locker door, their fluid already singing quietly through the sapphire port at Sakuya's neck.

Sakuya stood by the manual crane controls, her hands steady, her dark eyes clear and focused.

"Winch line from the skiff is hooked to the dorsal ring," Sakuya said. Her voice had none of the hollow, shivering static of the previous watch. It was clean, rhythmic, and sharp. "Kagari is holding position two hundred meters off our port beam. If we blow the cargo hatch now, the bags will drift straight into her catch net."

"Check the latch tension," Kaname said, giving the ratchet strap on the second bag a final hard jerk with her boot heel. "I don't want thirty-six canisters of military coolant tumbling into Geryon's cloud deck because a clasp gave out."

"Tension is eighty bar," Sakuya said. She glanced through the narrow quartz slit in the bulkhead, where the gas-giant's swollen atmosphere filled the lower horizon. "We're already dragging the methane fringe. Outside, that blue *kagiroi* ripple is dancing along the bottom tiles."

"Friction haze," Kaname said, her jaw tight. "The hull has twelve hours before the heat strips the outer ceramic. We move now."

Behind them, the cargo bay's outer maintenance airlock hissed.

The heavy hydraulic dogs rotated with a dry screech. Frost puffed across the threshold as the inner door swung inward on its counterbalance springs.

Kaname's hand dropped instinctively to the heavy copper pinch-bar hooked at her tool belt. She stepped in front of Sakuya, planting her boots wide on the grated deck.

Through the mist stepped Yoiduki Mizore.

Mizore wasn't wearing her full EVA helmet. She had her dark hair pushed back with a greasy sweatband, her runner suit zipped tight to her chin. In her right hand, she held an industrial plasma arc-torch—the heavy, pistol-grip kind used to slice through three-inch armor plate. The igniter coil at the nozzle was humming with a faint, angry blue spark.

"You took your time with the locker," Mizore said, stepping off the sill onto the cargo deck. "I was starting to think you two froze to death in the crawlspace."

Kaname didn't lower her bar. "How did you get aboard?"

"Tethered to the barge's ventral sensor blind spot fifteen minutes ago," Mizore said, gesturing with the nozzle of the arc-torch. "You left the guide beacon pulsing on an open frequency, Hotarubi. A blind customs officer in a tug could have tracked you from two moons away."

"The beacon was for Kagari," Kaname said, her voice dropping into a hard rasp. "You were supposed to wait on the station."

"I don't wait for scrap," Mizore said. Her gaze flicked past Kaname's shoulder, locking onto the two transit bags cinched on the deck. "And I don't leave forty canisters of military-grade neuro-coolant in the hands of two station hands with a shared savior complex."

Sakuya stepped out from behind Kaname. Her jaw was set, her posture rigid, her newly cooled spinal interface humming with quiet precision.

"We had an agreement, Mizore," Sakuya said evenly. "You get the deuterium blocks and the titanium hull rights. The coolant belongs to Shin-Murakumo."

"Agreements change when the cargo is worth a quarter of a million credits on the Ganymede exchange," Mizore said, taking a slow step forward. The blue spark at the tip of her torch crackled against the stale air. "Look at you, Kurotsuyu. Four cylinders in your neck and you're already standing straight. That leaves thirty-six. You don't need thirty-six canisters to keep a broken station listening to the dark. Eighteen keeps you alive for three seasons. The other eighteen buys me a second-hand freighter and a clean transponder in the outer rim."

"You touch those straps," Kaname growled, raising the copper pinch-bar, "and I'll cave your breastplate in."

Mizore stopped. She didn't raise the torch to aim, but she didn't lower it either. A thin, bitter smile spread across her narrow lips.

"Still the stubborn rigger, aren't you, Kaname?" Mizore said softly. "Always ready to plant your boots in the doorway. Always ready to throw your back against the steel so nobody sees what's behind you."

"Shut up," Kaname said.

"Why should I?" Mizore took another half-step, her eyes narrowing with cold, clinical familiarity. "You think I don't know who you are? You think Kagari is the only broker in the Jovian sector who reads recovery logs?"

Kaname's grip on the copper bar tightened until her scarred knuckles turned bone-white.

"I worked the third salvage flotilla out of Callisto two years back," Mizore said, her voice dropping into a sharp, deliberate cadence that cut through the humming reactor fans. "We were the second tug on the scene when the Minato-9 blew its cooling manifold in the L3 graveyard. We hooked the emergency pod. We operated the manual crank on the dorsal hatch."

Kaname's breath caught in her throat. The smell of the cargo bay—warm metal, ozone, bitter glycol—seemed to vanish, replaced by the suffocating phantom reek of freezing bile, stale urine, and forty hours of expired carbon scrubbers.

"Kaname?" Sakuya whispered behind her, sensing the sudden, violent collapse in Kaname's posture.

"You should have seen it, Kurotsuyu," Mizore continued, her eyes locked on Kaname's rigid face. "The Minato pod had four berths, but the life-support breaker tripped six hours after the blowout. The auxiliary batteries only had enough current to heat one emergency survival bag. Just one."

Mizore tapped the tip of her torch against the deck grating with a hollow *clink*.

"Captain Saito and two apprentice riggers," Mizore said. "They didn't freeze standing up. Saito put his girl in the survival cocoon because you were the smallest, the lithest rigger on the crew with the best lung capacity. And when the heater died completely, Saito and the boys piled on top of you. Three massive, full-grown miners, freezing solid in the dark, using their own dying body heat to keep the insulation from dropping below zero around that tight little frame."

A terrible, suffocating silence filled the bay.

Kaname's shoulders began to shake. The heavy copper bar in her hands wavered, dipping toward the deck. Her teeth were clenched so hard a thin bead of fresh blood welled at the corner of her lip.

"When we cut that hatch open," Mizore said quietly, without malice now, but with the brutal clarity of an unburied truth, "the corpses were frozen stiff as iron rails. We had to use pry-bars to pull them off the cocoon. And there was Hotarubi Kaname, curled in the bottom like a newborn pup, soaking in their frost, screaming at the top of her lungs because she was the only one who woke up."

Mizore let the torch tip drop an inch.

"That's why you can't stand the quiet, isn't it?" Mizore murmured. "That's why you hold onto this girl like she's a hull anchor. You spent forty hours under three dead men listening to their hearts stop, and you think if you let go of someone for five minutes, they'll freeze on you too."

Kaname couldn't speak. Her vision was swimming in black spots. The iron walls of the *Akegata-Maru* were dissolving around her, replaced by the pitch-black fiberglass interior of the Minato pod, the suffocating, immovable weight of frozen limbs pressing down on her ribs, the sound of Saito's breath turning to rattles in the dark.

She was falling. She was falling back into the box, back into the freezing tomb where she belonged.

Then a hand closed over hers.

It wasn't a desperate grab. It was a firm, warm, deliberate grip that clamped down over Kaname's white-knuckled fingers on the pinch-bar.

Sakuya stepped past Kaname's shoulder, placing herself squarely between Kaname and the muzzle of Mizore's arc-torch.

Sakuya stood tall, her spine straight, her dark hair falling loose around her pale throat. The sapphire port at the base of her skull was glowing with steady, serene power. She looked at Mizore with cold, unblinking authority.

"Put the torch down, Mizore," Sakuya said. Her voice didn't waver by a single fraction of a hertz.

Mizore raised an eyebrow, surprised. "You want to defend her? She's running on survivor guilt, Kurotsuyu. She's broken."

"She survived," Sakuya said, her voice ringing out like a bronze bell in the steel bay. "And when she climbed out of that pod, she didn't run to the inner worlds to hide. She came to Shin-Murakumo. She spent three years hanging off an intake skirt in twelve thousand kilometers of storm soup, welding rusted brackets and replacing seized hinge pins so thirty people on this station didn't freeze in the dark."

Sakuya took a step toward Mizore, forcing the smaller runner to tilt her head back.

"You don't get half of these canisters," Sakuya said, pointing toward the lashed transport bags. "Every single one of those thirty-six cylinders is going into the vault at Well 04. Because as long as I have coolant, I can hear the structural stress in the siphon ring before it tears. I keep the station in the sky, and Kaname keeps the station in one piece. We aren't your salvage ticket to the Jovian exchange."

Mizore stared at Sakuya for several long seconds. The arc-torch in her hand hissed, the blue spark guttering down to a faint ember.

Behind Sakuya, Kaname let out a long, ragged exhale. The iron bar lowered to her side. The suffocating weight on her chest—the ghost of Saito's frozen limbs—eased its grip, pushed back by the clear, defiant sound of Sakuya's voice.

"You've got teeth, acoustician," Mizore said, a slow, grudging respect surfacing in her raspy tone. "I'll give you that."

"I have teeth, and I have the station's preemption codes," Sakuya replied coldly. "Kagari is holding the skiff outside. Here is the deal you're going to take: the *Akegata-Maru* has four military deuterium fuel blocks in her auxiliary generator room. Each block is worth eight thousand credits on Callisto. You take all four blocks. You take the titanium salvage rights on the outer hull plates. And Kagari gives you a full refueling voucher from our primary siphon stores."

Mizore looked from Sakuya to the cargo bags, then back to Kaname, who was standing straight again, her chest rising and falling in steady, measured breaths, the taut musculature of her abdomen tight and resolute.

"Four deuterium blocks," Mizore considered, tapping her chin with a gloved thumb. "And the siphon voucher."

"And the voucher," Sakuya confirmed. "You help us nudge this barge into the magnetic tow cradle with your runner skiff's thrusters, and we sign off on the salvage manifest as pre-claimed derelict recovery. You leave with clean fuel, thirty-two thousand credits of deuterium in your hold, and no corporate bounty on your transponder."

Mizore switched off the igniter on her arc-torch. The blue spark vanished, leaving only the amber work lights reflecting on the steel deck.

"You drive a hard bargain for a woman whose brain was boiling three hours ago," Mizore said, sliding the torch into its holster at her hip. "Deal. Four blocks and the voucher. But we move now. My skiff's proximity sensors just picked up high-impulse drive flares breaking over the Ganymede horizon."

Kaname stepped up beside Sakuya, her voice deep, steady, and entirely present. "Natsume Combine?"

"Two corporate gun-cutters," Mizore said, turning toward the airlock. "Burning hard on a vector to intercept. They'll be on our sensor perimeter in twenty minutes. If we aren't tied to your station and burning for high orbit before they arrive, they'll turn this barge into scrap metal with us inside it."

Kaname caught Sakuya's eye. Sakuya nodded once, firm and ready.

"Let's move the cargo," Kaname said.

The transfer across the eight-hundred-meter void was a grueling test of manual kinetic rigging.

With the station's automated cargo winch damaged by the micro-meteoroid strike, they had to haul the transit bags along the secondary guide cable by hand-cranked capstan. Kaname stood out on the dorsal staging platform of the *Akegata-Maru*, her boots magnetized to the cold hull, the radiation monitor in her helmet clicking steadily as Geryon-7 rotated beneath her.

The first nylon bag, carrying eighteen pressurized canisters, slid smoothly along the guide wire. Two hundred meters out, Kagari caught the trailing line with the station skiff's mechanical arm, drawing the silver cylinders into the pressurized belly of Cradle Four.

"First bag secure," Kagari's voice crackled through the suit pickup. "Eighteen canisters safely docked in the airlock. Rigging the second line now."

Mizore worked the ventral couplers. The small runner had already unbolted the four deuterium fuel blocks from the barge's auxiliary generator room, lashing them to the cargo skid of the *Tsumuji* with steel cargo mesh. Her torch threw violet sparks across the vacuum as she welded the final tie-down bracket into place.

"Blocks are locked," Mizore called out over the local suit frequency. "I'm firing my forward RCS block to provide counter-torque. Kaname, release the second bag."

Kaname threw the manual dog lever. The second transport bag, holding the remaining eighteen coolant cylinders, kicked out from the airlock rail. It drifted across the black gulf between the ships, frost shimmering along its seams under the amber glow of the planet's storms.

Sakuya stood beside Kaname on the exterior platform, her thin suit protected by an unzipped thermal parka. Her cervical port was pulsing a steady, tranquil blue, but her eyes were fixed on the tactical overlay inside her visor.

"The second bag is through the station lock," Sakuya said, her hand coming down to rest on Kaname's forearm. "Thirty-six canisters accounted for. The vault is locked."

"Good," Kaname breathed, her pulse finally settling. "That's thirty-six for the station, four in your spine. Forty total. Every cylinder accounted for."

Before they could celebrate, the tactical display in Sakuya's visor flared amber.

A sharp acoustic warble hummed through the barge's hull plates—not a natural tidal vibration, but the focused, coherent pulse of active military search radar scanning from orbit.

"Contact," Sakuya said, her voice dropping into icy precision. "Two Natsume Combine patrol cutters breaking through the high magnetosphere. Range: forty thousand kilometers. Relative velocity: twelve kilometers per second."

"They're coming in hot," Mizore muttered over the comms, the *Tsumuji*'s thrusters firing a sharp burst of attitude gas. "They're burning on an intercept trajectory with railguns unmasked. If we don't fire the barge's primary engine right now to slingshot this whole mess into high orbit, they'll turn Shin-Murakumo into an expanding cloud of frozen iron."

Kaname grabbed Sakuya by the suit straps, pulling her toward the skiff's docking collar.

"Get to the liquid couches," Kaname said. "We're going to burn."

# Chapter 4: Thermal Breach

The void between Shin-Murakumo and the *Akegata-Maru* was eight hundred meters of cold nothing.

Kaname led the crossing on a braided steel guide wire, her reaction pack spitting short, sharp bursts of cold nitrogen to counter the tidal tug of Geryon-7. Behind her, tethered by three meters of yellow safety webbing, Sakuya drifted like a shadow. Sakuya had her visor tinted gold against the glare of the planet's sulfuric lightning, her hand clamped tight to the guide cable, her whole body rigid against the vacuum.

Ahead of them, the derelict cargo barge tumbled along its long axis, rolling once every forty-two seconds.

"Rotation is uneven," Sakuya's voice crackled through the helmet audio. The signal was clean, patched directly suit-to-suit without station relay. "Her secondary axis is wobbling six degrees to port. The breach in her cooling manifold left an asymmetric vapor plume that's acting like a rogue thruster."

"Can you time the latch?" Kaname asked, adjusting her thruster collar.

"Wait for the third roll," Sakuya said. Her breath sounded shallow, strained by the suit's oxygen mix. "When the antenna mast swings past the horizon, her dorsal salvage lock will align with our approach vector. You'll have eight seconds of clear approach before the radiator fin sweeps around."

"Eight seconds is plenty," Kaname grunted.

They drifted in silence. Below their boots, the colossal curve of Geryon-7 filled three-quarters of the sky, its bronze and violet cloud bands boiling with convective storms. Kaname's pulse was steady at eighty beats. The vast emptiness outside didn't bother her as long as she had momentum, as long as the thrusters answered the toggle.

"Now," Sakuya whispered.

Kaname fired her ventral nozzles. The nitrogen burst kicked them forward, arcing smoothly across the void. The scarred black flank of the *Akegata-Maru* rose up to meet them like an iron cliff.

She absorbed the kinetic shock with bent knees, reached back with her right arm, and caught Sakuya by the chest webbing, reeling her into the shelter of the airlock recess just as the massive, twisted radiator fin swung overhead with inches to spare.

"Contact," Kaname exhaled.

The dorsal airlock of the *Akegata-Maru* was dead, its manual hydraulic release frozen beneath a crust of sulfur rime. Kaname unslung her kinetic pinch-bar, jammed the wedge into the hatch dog, and cracked the seal with two brutal heave-strokes.

Air hissed out—a thin, freezing wisp of nitrogen that crystallized instantly into sparkling snow.

Kaname dragged Sakuya through the hatch into the airlock chamber and cranked the manual dog shut behind them.

Inside the barge, the emergency bulkheads were sealed, but the interior air had held. As the inner lock equalized, the air pressure rose to four hundred millibars—thin, stale, but breathable.

Before Kaname could unfasten her helmet latch, the hull outside shuddered with a deafening, metallic shriek.

A cluster of micro-meteoroid debris, swept up in the planet's gravitational shear, slammed into the barge's stern. The impact felt like an explosive shell striking an armored turret. High above the airlock, the guide wire they had anchored to the station snapped with a high-pitched twang that echoed straight through the steel plating.

The yellow tether whipped away into the vacuum, its severed end tumbling into the dark.

Kaname froze, her hands locked on her helmet ring.

The sound of the severed line—the sudden, irreversible loss of their lifeline to the station—tore through her mind. The airlock around her seemed to shrink, the walls closing in, cold and iron-hard, smelling of freezing metal and dead air.

*The Minato pod,* the whisper rose in her skull. *The hatch is sealed. The wire is cut. You're inside the box.*

"Kaname," a voice broke through the panic.

Sakuya had already stripped her helmet off. Her dark hair fell loose around her pale face, her eyes wide, sharp, and clear. She reached up with both bare hands and forcefully popped the latch rings on Kaname's helmet, lifting the heavy fiberglass shell away.

The hot, stale air of the barge hit Kaname's face.

"Look at the pressure gauge," Sakuya ordered firmly, her cold palms framing Kaname's jaw, forcing Kaname's eyes away from the hatch dog. "We're in the secondary service ring. The auxiliary reactor in bay four is still online. The air is warm, Kaname. We have power."

Kaname blinked hard, dragging a long, ragged lungful of air into her chest. Her heart was pounding so hard she could feel it in her teeth, but the warmth of Sakuya's bare fingers on her cheeks broke the freezing paralysis.

"The wire," Kaname rasped. "The line snapped."

"Kagari has our beacon," Sakuya said, her voice steady and deliberate. "We get the cargo first. Then we worry about the tow. Come on."

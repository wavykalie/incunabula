# Chapter 8: Kagiroi Dawn

The silence that filled Shin-Murakumo was not the suffocating deadness of an airlock.

It was an open, breathing quiet. High in Lagrangian orbit eighty-two thousand kilometers above Geryon-7, the violent tidal dragging of the atmosphere was gone. The station no longer shuddered or groaned. Through the steel floorplates, there was only the faint, rhythmic eight-cycle pulse of the solar array gyroscopes—a gentle mechanical heartbeat that felt like rest.

In Sensor Well 04, the heavy vault door was sealed.

Behind the two-inch armored slab, racked in temperature-controlled nitrogen cradles, sat thirty-six titanium cylinders of military-grade neuro-coolant. Four canisters were empty in the recycling hopper, their fluid circulating through Sakuya's veins, keeping her brainstem cool and still. Thirty-six cylinders remained in reserve: four seasons of quiet telemetry, twelve hundred days without a burning spine.

Kaname stood in the observation cupola at the zenith of the station's central spire.

The cupola was a hemisphere of cast quartz, ten paces wide, looking out across the curve of the gas-giant below. The planet was rising into local orbital dawn. Along the horizon, where the upper hydrogen deck met the darkness of the vacuum, the sunlight caught the boiling sulfur plumes, refracting through layers of cold methane ice.

Long ribbons of amber, bronze, and pale violet plasma shimmered across the cloud tops like ripples of heat over an iron stove.

It was the optical mirage the salvage crews called the *Kagiroi*—the dawn shimmer, the cold firefly glow of solar wind catching the planet's magnetic throat.

The air in the cupola was warm, drifting at twenty-two degrees. Floating in the center of the dome, caught in the microgravity and the gentle circulation currents, were a dozen shimmering spheres of condensation. They caught the golden sunlight and threw back tiny curved images of the stars and the curved ribs of the station.

The hatch at the base of the cupola ladder clicked open.

Sakuya floated up into the chamber.

She was wearing a loose, unbuttoned gray linen shirt over her bare chest, her dark hair tumbling free over her shoulders without its usual copper wire binding. Her cervical port was a quiet, dormant ring of brushed silver that caught the pale amber dawn. Her face was rested, the dark hollows under her eyes smoothed away by eighteen hours of deep, uninterrupted sleep.

She pushed off the ladder rim with one bare foot, drifting silently across the open space toward Kaname.

"Kagari just logged the departure clearance," Sakuya said softly.

Kaname looked down through the quartz floor toward Cradle Four.

A tiny iron wedge, no larger than a grain of rice against the vast bulk of the gas-giant, was drifting out from the docking bay. The *Tsumuji* fired its forward attitude gas, rotated ninety degrees on its yaw axis, and then its high-impulse ion drive flared into life—a sharp, brilliant needle of pure cyan flame.

The skiff accelerated smoothly, carving an outward trajectory away from the station, angling toward the outer Jovian moons and the long transit home.

"She took all four deuterium blocks," Kaname said, watching the blue drive flare dwindle into the starfield.

"She earned them," Sakuya replied, her hand reaching up to rest against Kaname's collarbone. "She nudged the tow line into the lock when the barge was yawing five degrees off center. Without her thrusters, the *Akegata* would have sheared the docking ring."

"She told me to look her up on Callisto," Kaname murmured, a faint smirk touching her mouth. "Said a good hull-cutter is hard to find in the outer rim."

Sakuya tilted her head, her dark eyes reflecting the golden plasma ribbons rising below. "Are you going to Callisto?"

Kaname looked down at Sakuya. She looked at the smooth, warm throat, the steady pulse beating beneath the pale skin, the silver port that had ceased its screaming. She tightened her arms around Sakuya's waist, pulling Sakuya flush against her chest, letting their bare bellies meet in the warm air.

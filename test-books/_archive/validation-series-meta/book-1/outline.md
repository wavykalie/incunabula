## Structural spine (decided — see `foundation.md`)

**A — The world was expecting them.** The welcome is arranged in advance, and it is routine and
old. The beings do not know why they are shaped the way they are.
**B — Achird finds it first and is not believed.** Her finds begin in chapter 1. The middle of the
book is her **unable to be heard.**
**C — No host, no confrontation, no chase.** No being ever tells them anything. The truth is
assembled from **objects and innocent non-answers.** They leave because Achird makes them leave,
and the beings are glad to see them go.

**Structural ledger** (no consecutive repeats):
chronological · parallel · spiral · fragmented · reverse · stream · chronological

## Movement I — The welcome (1–3)

### 1 — *working: arrival* · chronological · ~2,450
- **Beats:** The sleigh comes out of the dark. Its steed is a constellation that has taken a shape,
  and the shape changes as the constellation does; Achird is at the front of it and Merope narrates
  as though she were. Below them is a world that is not on any chart, and Merope says so without
  concern. They land. The light is easy. Things here grow in shapes that suggest they were
  **expecting guests** — she takes this as a compliment, never as a warning. Then the beings come:
  not a delegation, all of them at once, and they are **glad.** Merope is delighted. Achird says
  almost nothing. **At the close of the chapter Achird picks up a small object that does not belong
  here and puts it in her pocket without mentioning it.**
- **Emotional anchor:** the whole clearing turning toward her at once, and none of it being
  frightening.
- **Emotional surprise:** she is not excited by the planet. She is **relieved.** Somewhere she can
  be looked at without having to earn it.
- **Chaos moment:** she counts how many things are oriented toward her, is pleased, then annoyed
  that she cannot count them all.
- **Exit pull:** question opened — how long have they been expecting someone? And unspoken: what was
  that Achird just picked up?
- **Writer notes:** no competence cascade. She solves nothing. This chapter must be **genuinely,
  pleasantly wonderful.** No atmosphere, no omens, no foreshadowing. The only cold note in the
  chapter is one flat sentence from Achird that Merope does not answer.

### 2 — *working: the welcome, from two sides* · parallel · ~2,450
- **Beats:** The welcome cut in two halves. Merope's half: the beings surround her, are delighted by
  her, and she performs. Achird's half: she is welcomed exactly as warmly and is almost entirely
  unwatched, because Merope is standing in front of the attention. Both halves are lovely; only one
  of them notices anything. **In Achird's half she shows Merope the object. Merope laughs at it,
  hands it back, and tells the reader about it in a sentence that changes the subject.** The beings
  are asked directly about the object and answer honestly, and their answer means nothing to them.
- **Emotional anchor:** the beings making room for Merope without being asked.
- **Emotional surprise:** the beings like Achird too. She is not excluded by them. She is **not
  seen** by the one person beside her, which has no one to blame — and this is where the reader
  should first notice that.
- **Chaos:** Achird counts something meaningless — the interval between the light changing and the
  beings turning. She is the only one taking measurements.
- **Exit pull:** wrong-footed — nothing on this planet needs anything from them, including food,
  including explanations.
- **Writer notes:** **both halves must feel good.** If Achird's half reads as sad on first pass, the
  chapter is wrong; it reads as *normal.* Merope's dismissal of the object must be light, not
  defensive — she is not hiding anything yet. **The beings' answer about the object is the first
  cold thing in the book**, and it must be delivered in one flat line with no emphasis.

### 3 — *working: she is happy here* · spiral · ~2,450
- **Beats:** Time stops behaving. They sleep long, wake rested, and lose days without noticing. The
  same day happens again three times, each pass deeper and more comfortable, and Merope enjoys
  every version of it. **This is the chapter where she is happy** — genuinely, unironically happy,
  in a way the reader should feel is deserved. Achird, in the same repeated day, keeps finding
  things: another object, a shape in the growth that is not a plant. She says so, flatly, twice.
  Merope narrates over her both times.
- **Emotional anchor:** a morning that is warm, easy, and hers.
- **Emotional surprise:** Merope is happy, and **the reader should be uneasy about how complete the
  happiness is.** The happiness must be real. The unease comes only from how little it asks.
- **Chaos:** she stops counting her audience, because it is finally large enough.
- **Exit pull:** question closed then reopened — nothing is required of her, so nothing is happening
  to her either.
- **Writer notes:** **sag risk, and the craft risk of the book.** The smoothest prose in the book.
  Nothing may threaten them here. Achird's two warnings must be **plain, correct and unremarkable**
  — the reader should hear them, and Merope should not.

## Movement II — Achird is not heard (4–5)

### 4 — *working: the belongings* · fragmented · ~2,450
- **Beats:** Achird leads her somewhere. Fragments, uneven and literal: belongings with no owners. A
  camp. **A shelf of small things, laid out with care.** Objects from many different places, none
  from here, none recent, and none claimed. **No bodies, no remains — the absence is the whole
  horror.** Merope is **flattered**: she thinks it means others came, liked it, and stayed. Achird
  counts the objects and asks the question nobody wants, and Merope answers it wrongly and
  confidently, and keeps talking. The beings handle the objects with a familiarity that has no owner
  behind it.
- **Emotional anchor:** a row of small things laid out by someone careful.
- **Emotional surprise:** Merope's first response is **pride**, not fear — and it is the honest
  response of a girl being given more evidence that people have always loved it here.
- **Chaos:** the beings' total, cheerful competence with things that belonged to the dead.
- **Exit pull:** threat introduced — nobody has ever left, and nobody is buried.
- **Writer notes:** fragments are literal and uneven. **Do not smooth them.** Unease rises here, but
  it rises from **objects and answers**, never from menace, and **no being explains anything.**

### 5 — *working: what the beings are* · reverse · ~2,450
- **Beats:** Told backwards from a single fact, assembling in reverse: the shelf, the objects, the
  camp, the years, and finally the thing itself — **everyone who came here died here, and the
  beings grew from them.** Then the second half: **the beings have no idea.** They are not hiding
  it, they cannot lie, and they are glad to see visitors because they have no memory of what
  visitors become. Merope finds the explanation that lets her keep staying, and states it to
  Achird, kindly, at length. Achird does not argue. **She simply stops saying things.**
- **Emotional anchor:** a being expressing unmistakable welcome while standing where a person died.
- **Emotional surprise:** the beings **don't know** — and Achird's silence is worse than an argument.
  Merope's relief at her own explanation is the chapter's real turn.
- **Chaos:** the beings repeat a habit that came from someone, with nothing behind it.
- **Exit pull:** irreversible knowledge. Achird can no longer be heard at all.
- **Writer notes:** the reveal is **shown, not explained by any character.** No being states it. No
  narrator states it. **Merope's wrong explanation is the only explicit reading offered**, and it
  must sound reasonable.

## Movement III — The turn (6–7)

### 6 — *working: the refusal* · stream · ~2,450
- **Beats:** Achird says they have to go — one sentence. Merope refuses, and here her voice turns
  **boastful, then cruel**, because the pressure is real and she has no other move. She argues,
  performs, and hurts Achird with something accurate. The chapter is Merope talking, at speed,
  refusing, and not listening. **No being intervenes. No being even appears to notice.** There is
  nothing to be rescued from and nothing to fight.
- **Emotional anchor:** Achird waiting for her to finish.
- **Emotional surprise:** **Merope's cruellest line is true**, and she knows it is true when she says
  it — and Achird has heard this before and is not surprised.
- **Chaos:** the failed emotional management lands here. Shortest sentences in the book.
- **Exit pull:** decision forced — **Achird stops asking.** And the sleigh, which has been still the
  whole time, **moves on its own**, because it has decided to.
- **Writer notes:** Achird gets no monologue. Her largest moment is **one sentence.** Merope must not
  be punished; she must simply be *seen* doing it. **No chase, no confrontation, no being in the
  scene for the reader to be afraid of.**

### 7 — *working: leaving, and the acknowledgement* · chronological · ~2,450
- **Beats:** The sleigh leaves. Achird flies it and Merope narrates as though she does. The planet
  lets them go without resistance and the beings are **glad to see them go**, which is worse than
  being stopped. Merope does not announce why she left and does not understand why. On the way out
  she **finally looks at Achird**, and realises Achird loves her. She cannot say anything direct
  about it. She is a tsundere; she deflects; the acknowledgment happens anyway — small, sideways,
  unmistakable. **Achird does not say it either.** It is acknowledged between them and left there.
- **Emotional anchor:** the person who has been beside her the whole time, seen at last.
- **Emotional surprise:** leaving is **easy** — the most frightening detail in the book. Nothing
  stopped them. Nothing ever would have. Everyone who died here chose to stay.
- **Chaos:** Merope rebuilds her composure sentence by sentence, in front of the reader.
- **Exit pull / residue:** emotion, not evaluation. *She got what she wanted, it was smaller than she
  thought, and she is not sorry.*
- **Writer notes:** **must not be a damage.** Nothing is lost, no one pays, no one is wounded. Do not
  state Achird's love directly. Do not let Merope learn the theme. End on a **quiet image**, not a
  punch. The chapter should feel longer than it is.

## Tension map (false-low at 3; steady rise after)

| Ch | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tension | 3 | 4 | 4* | 6 | 8 | 9 | 6 |

\* Ch. 3 is a false low: tension reads minimal because nothing is wrong, and the reader's unease
reads high because of it. Ch. 7 falls deliberately — the ending is warm.

**Tone:** dread **never announces itself.** It enters through Achird's finds and Merope's
dismissals, and rises steadily. Not one cold statement, not a chapter-four lurch.

## 1. Synopsis (short — for listings, ~90 words)

Two girls land on a planet that is delighted to see them.

It feeds them. It keeps them comfortable. It arranges the ground so that walking is easy and the light
so that looking is pleasant. It asks them for nothing, ever, and one of them has been counting.

Everyone who ever arrived here is dead. The planet does not know that. Neither does the narrator.

*Working title. Book 1 of a series.*

## 2. Synopsis (long — for an agent or a query, ~300 words)

Merope is a performer who has spent years being looked at, and she narrates her own life as though she
were piloting it — the sleigh, the route, the decisions. She is not. Achird flies the sleigh, and
Merope has been telling the story in the wrong voice since the first night, and she knows it, and she
does not stop.

They land on a world that is not on any chart and is not in a hurry to correct them. The beings who
come out of the growth are glad to see them. All of them, at once, every time, and none of them have
been chosen to speak for the others. They cannot lie, they cannot explain, and they do not know what
they are.

The welcome is arranged in advance and it is very old. A bench faces the place where a person would
arrive. The lanes in the growth are one person wide and worn by one person walking the same line for
a long time. It is the most restful place Merope has ever been, and she stops counting the audience
for the first time in her life.

Achird counts instead. She counts the interval between the light going and the beings turning. She
counts forty-one objects on a shelf that nobody owns. She asks one of the beings, directly, whose a
lost object is, and it answers without a pause, and it is not the pause that is wrong — it is that
there was no pause to be had.

Merope does not believe her. The middle of the book is Achird being right and unable to be heard, and
Merope being happy, and getting crueller, and the shelf of other people's belongings that she finds
her own things laid out on.

The sleigh decides before either of them does.

## 3. Blurb / back cover copy (~120 words)

**The most restful place she has ever been.**

Nothing about arriving is difficult. The light is easy, the food is good, and nobody ever asks her for
anything — which is the one thing Merope has wanted her entire life and has never once said out loud.

Achird keeps counting. The interval between the light going and the beings turning. The forty-one
objects on a shelf that nobody owns. The shape in the ground that did not grow.

Merope keeps performing.

Neither of them is lying, and both of them are right, and one of them is going to be carried out of
here — because being welcomed, and kept comfortable, and asked for nothing, is how everyone who ever
arrived here died.

| Achird's physical appearance | **ESTABLISHED 2026-09-22** | Figure illustration now possible. Flat-chested; crimson hair in a **scorpion-tail braid**; superbubble hoodie. |
| The naming rule (`R-008`) | **applies to the cover too** | No planet name, no world name, no title that names the place |
| **How much of the figures to show** | **OPEN** | See the two-cover decision below |

**Two covers, and they are not equivalent.**

**Option 1 — figures.** Now legitimate, and previously impossible.

- Merope in front, in the celestial Lolita dress, white twin tails, performing. Achird behind her, in
the hoodie, not looking at the viewer, not performing.
- The pairing is the book's argument in one image: one of them is being *seen*, the other is *seeing*.
- **Higher recognition with the light-novel audience; worse for the horror-adjacent reader**, who would
be pulled in by a figure cover and abandon at ch.3 — the abandonment is documented, not predicted
(`06-scorecard.md`).

**Option 2 — the button. Still recommended for a first edition, and now a choice rather than a
constraint.**

- **Image:** one small physical object on an otherwise empty surface, lit from a low angle — **the
button** at large scale: bone-white, a single hole worked through one end, work too fine to be from
here. It is the book's first object, the one Merope laughs at, and the one she later finds arranged
on a shelf beside a stranger's belongings. It carries the whole book without depicting a person or
naming a place, and it does not promise the wrong genre.
- **Palette:** warm and easy — the book's light is *pleasant*, and the cover must not signal horror.
A cold or black cover promises a different book.
- **Type:** light, generous, lowercase-friendly. The register is quiet; a heavy display face is a
broken promise.
- **Front matter:** the working title, and a series line. **Both still unfilled by decision.**

**A hybrid worth weighing:** the object cover for the ebook, the figure cover for any print or serial
edition where the reader has already opted in.

**Before either is commissioned, read `character-design-brief.md`.** It holds the two leads' design
canon, all four `APPEAR` items, and the story-level question about Achird's hair and Scorpius.

**Status of the four, as of 2026-09-22:** `APPEAR-1` (Merope's chest), `APPEAR-2` (the hair shape) and
`APPEAR-3` (the Scorpius question) are **author-resolved** — busty/full-figured, a scorpion-tail braid,
and a coincidence the author likes. `APPEAR-4` (the superbubble reading) is the **only one still
resting on this pipeline's interpretation**, and it is low-risk because the phrase is unambiguous in
astronomy. None of the three resolutions changed a word of the prose.

## The four ambiguities, and where they landed

The description was given in one pass and four parts of it were ambiguous. **None of them was guessed
at**, because a guess in a design brief becomes a drawing, and a drawing becomes canon — all four were
raised with the author, and three are now settled by them.

| # | Item | State |
|---|---|---|
| `APPEAR-1` | Merope's chest — the word given was **"boasty"** | **RESOLVED — BUSTY / FULL-FIGURED.** Not standard usage, so it was left unguessed and asked about rather than interpreted. A stated deliberate contrast with Achird. |
| `APPEAR-2` | Achird's **"scorpia like"** hair | **RESOLVED — A SCORPION-TAIL BRAID.** One long crimson tail curving like a scorpion's tail. Chosen over a swept asymmetric cut and over a full scorpion silhouette. |

## Merope — `CHR-001`

- **Hair:** white, worn in **twin tails.**
- **Dress:** a **celestial-themed Lolita dress.** ("Lolita" is the Japanese fashion subculture —
  ruffled, petticoated, ornate — not an age descriptor. Both leads are **19 and adults**; the earlier
  build recorded them as minors and that was corrected by the author, so any brief must treat them as
  adults.)
- **Figure:** **busty / full-figured**, and it is a stated deliberate **contrast with Achird** rather than
  a passing detail. Draw the two as a matched pair, not independently.
- **Design logic:** the celestial theme is the thesis of her character. She narrates the piloting as
  hers, she named the dolphin overhead, and she wanted the beings to know *whose* constellation it
  was. She has dressed herself as something you look at. A design that makes the dress *too* tasteful
  will fight the book — she is grandiose on purpose and slightly too much on purpose.

**Supported by the prose (not contradicted):** ch.3 — *"I sat on the bench and did my hair without a
mirror, which I can do."* The style is never stated, and twin tails are a style you can do by feel.

---

## Achird — `CHR-002`

- **Figure:** flat-chested, and stated as a deliberate contrast with Merope.
- **Hair:** **crimson**, worn in a **scorpion-tail braid** — one long tail curving like a scorpion's
  tail. The shape **reads at thumbnail scale**, which is why it was worth settling before a cover.
- **Clothing:** a **hoodie** designed after **nuclear superbubbles.**
- **Design logic:** the contrast with Merope is the whole point and should be drawn as a contrast, not
  as two variants of one idea. Merope's design should read as *assembled to be seen*; Achird's should
  read as *already dressed, and not thinking about it.* Quietly engineered, technically curious,
  wearing something with a phenomenon on it that nobody has explained to her and that she has not
  asked about.
- **Not a costume:** her design should not be *themed*. A hoodie is ordinary clothing; the bibliography
  of the print is the only unusual thing about it.

**Supported by the prose (not contradicted):** she is described nowhere physically, and she is
consistently doing things with her hands — walking the edge, counting along a shelf with her finger,
one hand in her pocket, a hand on the sleigh.

## `APPEAR-3` — the Scorpius question, resolved as an accident

Book 1's last chapter resolves with the sleigh's **new** steed taking the shape of **Scorpius**;
its first steed, in ch.1, was **Delphinus.** A scorpion-tail braid sits on the head of the person who
gets them out of the book, under a constellation that is the shape of a scorpion.

**Author's decision: a coincidence, and they like it.** Not a designed resonance. Three consequences,
recorded so the decision cannot drift:

1. **Not seeded.** It is *not* carried into Book 2's design language. No other character echoes a
   constellation, and no other design choice is made to rhyme with this one.
2. **Not remarked on.** Neither book ever draws attention to it — not by Merope, not by Achird, not
   by the narration. The braid is simply her hair.
3. **Not changed.** The hair was not redesigned to avoid the read, and the ch.7 ending was not
   altered to suppress it.

A reader who connects the braid to Scorpius is **over-reading**, and letting that happen is the
intended outcome — it is a real pattern in the text now, just not a planted one. An illustrator may
therefore treat the braid and the constellation as unrelated; the coincidence is not theirs to draw.

**Nothing in the prose was changed at any point on the strength of this.**

## `APPEAR-4` — "nuclear superbubbles", read as

The real astronomical feature: **cavities in the interstellar medium blown out by supernovae and
stellar winds**, which appear as **nested or concentric bubbles with brighter rims**, often limb-brightened
shells against a darker background. (The Local Bubble around the Sun is the famous example; superbubbles
are the larger, multi-supernova version.)

Recorded as **this pipeline's interpretation**, not as an author statement — but unlike `APPEAR-1` the
phrase is unambiguous in astronomy, so the reading carries low risk. It is the **only appearance item
still resting on an inference rather than an author decision**, and it is flagged as such so that
nobody later mistakes it for one.

## The prose position, recorded so it stays a choice

**The manuscript describes neither lead physically, anywhere, in 15,531 words.**

Verified by grep across all seven chapters for *hair, hoodie, dress, cloak, chest, tall, short, crimson,
red hair* — the only hits are incidental: *"moved it a hair to the left"*, *"not short of material"*,
*"dress it up"*.

A first-person narrator does not describe herself, and Merope never describes Achird either. That is
now a **recorded position** rather than a gap: appearance is canon for **illustration and packaging**,
and the prose stays silent. It is also part of what makes the `voice-fingerprint` cover-the-name test
passable.

**If the author wants the prose to describe them, that is a separate decision** and a change to the
narrator contract — not an illustration brief.

## What is distinct here

Genre horror usually reaches for **infection, possession, or absorption** — the visitor is
changed into something. This engine does not do that. Nobody is changed. **People are simply
made comfortable enough to stay, and then they die, and something grows from them.**

The distinctive move is that **there is no antagonist to defeat and no transformation to
resist.** The only available action is to leave, and the planet's whole design is aimed at making
leaving feel unnecessary. If that holds up, it is the book's shelf-differentiator.

## Reader expectations of the form

- The travelogue register promises **episodic variety** early.
- The horror turn promises **recontextualization** — early chapters must be re-readable as
  evidence once the fate is known.
- A single self-interested narrator promises **unreliability the reader can detect** rather than
  a twist delivered by the author.

## Open market questions (not decided)

- Whether the book is positioned as **SF with horror** or **horror in an SF setting** — this
  changes cover, comps and shelf.
- Whether the series framing is a **continuing travelogue** (each installment a new planet) or a
  tighter two-book arc.
- Whether a 60-page installment is **published as one volume or serialized**.

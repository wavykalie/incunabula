## Dimension scores

| # | Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Originality** | **8.0** | The engine is genuinely uncommon: hospitality as the mechanism, no antagonist, and a collective that *cannot* explain anything because it does not know anything. The `answer_boundary` resolution (possession and present tense only) turns the "monster that won't explain itself" cliché into a rule the beings obey rather than a withholding the writer performs. Docked because the *shape* — two girls, a travelling vehicle, a planet of the week — is a known light-novel frame, and "the ground is made of the people who died here" is a well-worn horror move. The originality is in the mechanism and the innocence, not in the premise. |
| 2 | **Theme** | **8.5** | *Does a place that asks nothing of you keep you from becoming anything?* is never answered in the text, which is correct, and the ending implicates the narrator rather than illustrating the theme — she is not the exception, she is the evidence. Strengthened rather than weakened by `D-1`. Docked for a mild one-point dilution: the theme is restated in Merope's own voice twice (ch.5's "story about soil", ch.7's closing reflection) which is once more than it needs. |
| 3 | **Characters** | **7.5** | Merope is specific, unreliable, funny and unpleasant in ways that are consistent for 15,531 words — a real achievement in first person. Achird is the better-constructed character and the book's best idea: her flatness is attention, not absence, and the counting is characterisation through action. Docked for two things: Achird has no interior access by design, which means the reader never quite gets her; and the cast is two. The beings are a collective by rule, so there is no third character to carry any scene. |
| 4 | **Prose / voice** | **8.5** | The book's actual asset. The voice holds across seven chapters and three structural modes (parallel, spiral, reverse) without drifting, which is the hardest thing this manuscript attempts. The flat question punctuation is a specified rule executing correctly. Three named irritants, all minor, all reported. Docked because two of the seven chapters (ch.3, ch.5) lean on the narrator's self-commentary to carry length. |
| 5 | **Pacing / coherence** | **7.0** | The lowest score, and it has one identifiable cause: ch.3 is deliberately flat and it is the chapter a plot-motivated reader abandons in — the stretch persona DNF'd there, and the audit's own simulation says so. Movement II is three consecutive chapters in the same shape (Merope fails to understand a correct observation). The reverse-told ch.5 makes a casual reader re-read its first paragraph. Coherence is high — the two CRITICALs found by Phase 5.6 were fixed, and no surviving contradiction is known. |
| 6 | **Emotion** | **7.5** | Two moments land hard and both are quiet: *"I was happy that my things were on the shelf with the others"* and two fingers on the back of a hand. The hostile persona converted on the first. Docked because the middle is *deliberately* affect-flat, which is correct as design and still reads as a flat middle, and because the ending's emotional payoff is small by construction — the stated risk of `D-1`, confirmed by the casual simulation misreading it as a choice. |
| 7 | **Worldbuilding** | **7.0** | Purpose-built and rigorously consistent: an unnamed planet, a sleigh whose steed is a constellation, a growth that is fed ground, a collective with a stated epistemic limit. Nothing is inconsistent and nothing is decorative. Docked because the world is *thin by rule* — no names, no history, no second location, no society — so there is very little for a reader who reads for world to hold. The `R-008` naming rule is right for the book and costly for the score. |

**Composite floor: 7.7** (mean of the seven). **Lowest dimension: pacing/coherence, 7.0.**

## Commercial viability

Scored for what this actually is: a ~15.5k-word serial-ready installment from an unknown author, in
a thin cross-genre niche (light-novel register × literary-SF horror).

| Metric | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **CVI — launch** | **4.5** | The book cannot be sold on premise to the genre that would most reliably buy it. The horror-adjacent reader DNFs at ch.3 (verified in simulation), and the light-novel reader does not habitually buy horror endings. A launch without precise targeting lands in the gap between audiences. |
| **CVI — legacy** | **6.5** | Better than launch, and for a specific mechanical reason: the book rewards **retroactive verification**. The hostile persona's conversion was completed by going *back* to ch.2 and finding the reveal's receipt — the measurement, the pause-free *"Not ours"*. That is exactly the behaviour that produces word-of-mouth recommendation in a niche, and it is the same property that makes *Piranesi* and *Time War* travel. |
| **Primary engagement** | empathy | The reader buys the narrator's voice |
| **Secondary engagement** | fascination | The reveal and its structure |
| **Tertiary engagement** | intellectual | The unanswered question |
| **Commercial pacing** | 5.0 | Slow by commercial standards for the first third, and the escalation is in movement II–III |
| **Tomorrow-test anchors** | **4** | The shelf line; *"Not ours"*; the palm-wipe; two fingers on the back of a hand. Four is strong for 15.5k words — the book is built from objects rather than from events, and objects survive a night's sleep better than events do. |
| **Casual reader verdict** | **finishes** | Verified in simulation: finishes in one sitting, enjoys it, mildly misreads the ending's agency (a warmer reading than the text) |
| **Shareability — quote** | **4/5** | Two quotable lines, both short. The book's best lines are its shortest, which is what screenshot-sharing rewards. |
| **Shareability — plot** | **2/5** | Almost nothing to spoil. There is one reveal and no twist. |
| **Shareability — emotional** | **3/5** | Real but quiet. The book's emotional payload is mutual and undramatic by design. |

### Concept pitch

> Two girls land on a planet that is delighted to see them. It feeds them, it keeps them
> comfortable, it asks them for nothing, and everyone who ever arrived here is dead and did not
> notice either.

### Human closeness

**High.** The book's central relationship is carried almost entirely through withheld speech and two
physical gestures, and its most terrible line is a character being pleased about something
monstrous. Nothing in the manuscript reads as assembled rather than felt — with the single exception
of the pipeline word found and removed at `CONT-004`.

### Verdict, and the next action

**Gate verdict: PASSES on quality, at 7.7 with pacing as the weak lens. Launch viability that does not
yet exist.** The book is not finished-and-ready-to-market; it is finished-and-ready-to-be-*targeted*.

**Next action, in order:**

1. **Do not market this to horror readers.** The simulation is unambiguous: the *Annihilation*
   promise produces DNFs at ch.3. The pitch that works is the light-novel/literary-SF one — voice,
   length, structure, one-sitting.
2. **Phase 5 activates only if a target is set**, and the recommendation is to set it against
   **Structure**, the 7.0.
3. **The remaining author items** before any commercial packaging: `AUTH-3` (two prose judgement
   calls) and `AUTH-4` (every title). **`AUTH-1`, `AUTH-2` and `AUTH-5` were closed on 2026-09-22** —
   including physical appearance, which unblocked the cover brief. See `character-design-brief.md`.
4. **The audience question is not settled by this document and is not meant to be.** Whether this
   register × genre × length combination has a real ICP is a market question, and the author's plan
   is to answer it with a swarm simulation rather than with another self-assessment. This scorecard
   should be treated as the input to that, not a substitute for it.

## 1. Given by you

### Premise

Two girls, **both 19**, travel by space chariot — horse-drawn, the draft animal rendered as moving
constellations. They arrive at a **peaceful planet populated by silicon-based, hedge-like beings**
and spend time there exploring. They then discover a **startling fate that befell everyone else
who visited**.

**Amended by you (2026-09-22):** the horse-drawn chariot is replaced — see *The vehicle* below.

### The fate (corrected by you)

> **They all died, and the hedges grew from them.**

The planet is a graveyard. The hedge-beings are **something new that fed on the dead** — they are
**not** the dead themselves, and they are **not** the visitors.

The beings are **friendly aliens and very inviting.**

### The mechanism of danger (decided by you)

> **The hospitality is the mechanism.**

Being welcomed and kept comfortable is exactly how visitors end up **staying long enough to die**.
Warmth is the trap. There is **no absorption and no chorus** — nobody is folded into anything.
People simply never leave, and then they die here, and the hedges grow from them.

### Do the beings know? (decided by you)

**No. They don't know what they are.** Genuinely innocent — friendly, inviting, no idea they are
grown from the dead. The horror is their innocence meeting the pile, not malice.

### Where the crisis lands (decided by you)

**Merope refuses to leave. Achird has to act.** The planet hands Merope the audience she has always
wanted, so Achird becomes the one who must force the issue.

### Theme question (decided by you)

> **"Does a place that asks nothing of you keep you from becoming anything?"**

Never answered in the text.

### How it ends (decided by you)

**Growth, not damage.** *"I dont think they need to lose anything. What changes is their
relationship growing into something else."* Specifically: **Merope realises that Achird loves her.
Merope is too much of a tsundere to be direct about it, so it is not stated plainly — but it is
acknowledged.** Nothing is lost, and no one pays.

### Structural changes (decided by you)

Selected to move this book away from Reference C. **All three chosen:**

**A — The world was expecting them.** The welcome is not improvised for whoever happens to arrive.
The place is **arranged in advance**, and the arrangement is **routine and old.** The beings do not
know why they are shaped the way they are.

**B — Achird finds it first and is not believed.** The discovery is **not** made by the confident
character. **Achird finds the belongings early**, and Merope — who narrates — dismisses it, because
the planet is lovely to her and Achird is always saying flat worrisome things. **The middle of the
book becomes Achird unable to be heard.** This is the theme made structural: being watched is not
being seen, and Merope is watched constantly while looking at nobody.

**C — No host, no confrontation, no chase.** No single being ever tells them anything, and there is
no escape sequence. They leave because **Achird makes them leave**, and the beings are glad to see
them go. The truth is assembled from **objects and innocent non-answers.**

**Not selected:** *no present danger.* **Visitors still die here.** The mortality is real.

### The vehicle (decided by you)

**It is not a chariot, and there is no horse.**

It is **a sleigh** — an enclosed, glazed cabin on runners, old-style, that travels the dark between
worlds.

Its **steed is drawn from a constellation and morphs to match the constellation it chooses** —
*Scorpius* as a scorpion, *Delphinus* as a dolphin. The steed's form changes with the constellation.

**Achird chooses, but the sleigh chooses too.** Your words: *"Little bit of 1 and 2, if it needs to
save someone it will, it's kinda its own character in a way."* So the sleigh has **preferences of
its own** and **can act on its own** — including overriding Achird to protect someone.

**Achird owns it and pilots it. Merope narrates as if she were the pilot.**

**Constellation names may appear on the page** (your decision).

### Naming (decided by you)

**Nothing on the planet is named.** No planet name, no name for the beings. Merope describes them
by what they look like.

*Note:* **Merope** and **Achird** are themselves real star names (Merope is one of the Pleiades;
Achird is Eta Cassiopeiae), so constellation names for the vehicle's steed sit inside the existing
naming logic rather than against it — and you have **approved naming them on the page**
(2026-09-22). *Scorpius*, *Delphinus* and the rest are permitted proper nouns. The rule against
naming applies to **the planet and the beings**, both of which stay unnamed.

## 3. Why this restarted

The first build ran Phases 0–2.8 and produced a foundation, outline, nine scene packets, a voice
contract and an entity state. Its **story engine was Coldbrook Junction's**, not this book's:

| Coldbrook Junction | The torn-down build |
|---|---|
| A station that receives everyone at their tipping point | A planet that receives everyone who lands |
| Everyone assumed to have left is still there | Everyone who visited is still there |
| The "lost civilization" is made of everyone the station took | The beings are made of everyone who arrived |
| A ledger of visitors across eras | A Guest Book of visitors |
| Was it ever a rescue? | Nothing malicious happens. That is worse. |
| One character offered; the companion pays | Merope offered; Achird pays |
| "What the Static Keeps" | "What the Hedges Kept" |

The engine is now **yours**: visitors die here because they never leave, and friendly, inviting
beings grow from them. The pipeline restarts at Phase 0.

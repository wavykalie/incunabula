## Global narrative voice — MEROPE telling it afterwards

Merope is the narrator. Every sentence in the book is hers, including every sentence about Achird.
There is no third-person distance anywhere.

**The machinery** (taken from Reference A's register, with its self-deprecation removed):

| Device | How it works here |
|---|---|
| Past tense for the event, present tense for the opinion | The event happened; her judgement of it is happening now. The two sit in the same paragraph with no marking. |
| Retrospective calm | The narrating Merope is steadier than the Merope in the scene. She knows how it turned out and is unhurried. |
| Rhetorical question as the engine | Her default move when something does not suit her. She asks the reader, not the world. |
| Casual direct address | "you" is the reader, and the tone is conversational. Never ceremonious, never lyrical. |
| Deadpan after a slight | She does not complain. She reports the slight as though it were information. |
| One flat sentence per emotional beat | A feeling lands in a single sentence and is not dwelt on, explained, or revisited. |
| Comments on her own telling | She breaks off to judge how she is telling it. She is a performer narrating a performance. |
| Short paragraphs, often one line | Dialogue carries whole pages. Big stretches are alternating lines of speech. |

**The one thing that must not transfer: she never winks.** Elaina's vanity is a joke Elaina is in on,
and her narration keeps deflating her. Merope **means every word.** There is no self-deprecation
anywhere in this book, no ironic distance between what she says and what she thinks of herself. She
is sincerely certain of her own centrality, and the reader is left to do something about that on
their own.

**Consequence for the reader:** the gap between what Merope reports and what is true is the book's
whole engine. She is not lying. She is not unreliable in the sense of concealing. She is simply
looking the other way, at length, in beautiful sentences, and the reader has to stand where Achird
stands.

## Voice cards

### MEROPE — narrator and protagonist, 19

**Syntax fingerprint.** Declarative and complete. She finishes sentences. Clauses sit in an order
that sounds rehearsed, because they are. Superlatives arrive without hedging: *the best*, *the only*,
*never*. Very few questions in dialogue — she asks questions the way other people make statements,
as a way of taking the floor. In narration, she asks the reader questions constantly.

**Metaphor source.** Rooms, audiences, stages, being watched, being chosen, coats, light. She
understands the world through attention. When she compares a place to something, it is almost always
something with people in it.

**Speech patterns.** She answers a question with a question, or with a correction to the question.
She interrupts herself to add a clause that makes her sound better. She says *I* in the middle of
sentences that were about someone else.

**Voice under pressure — changes, and it must change.** Pressure moves her in one direction:
**boastful, then cruel.** Sentences get *longer and more elaborate* as she loses, then land on one
short accurate thing aimed at the person in front of her. The cruelty is not volume. It is a true
sentence used as a weapon, delivered quietly, and she knows it is true while she says it. This voice
does not appear in movement I. Movement I is her at ease and it should read that way.

**What she never does.** Deflect about something she wants. Say a feeling plainly. Admit she is
performing while performing.

### ACHIRD — 19, kuudere

**Syntax fingerprint.** Two to four words is a normal Achird sentence, and one is not unusual. No
modifiers. No *I think*, no *maybe*, no *kind of*. Where Merope qualifies, Achird states. (A
**report** may run to a full sentence — ch. 1's "The ones with nothing in them. That is what they do
instead of a greeting." Do not pad it, and do not truncate a true observation to hit a word count.
`CONF-009`.)

**Determiners of her flatness:** she is **attending**, not absent. Her shortness comes from precision,
not coldness. When she says something, it is because it is true and necessary in that order. She does
not soften, and she does not decorate.

**Speech patterns.** She answers the actual question. She does not fill silence. She does not ask for
more than she wants answered. When she repeats something, she repeats it identically — a first
warning and a second warning are the same sentence, which is worse than escalating.

**Voice under pressure — changes, in the opposite direction.** Pressure makes her **quieter and
shorter**, never louder. Her largest moment in the book is one sentence. When she has been ignored
past a certain point she does not argue: she **stops explaining**, and then she stops speaking
altogether. Silence is her escalation, and the reader must feel it as a loss while Merope reads it as
peace. The full budget is machines-readable in `ENTITY_STATE.yaml → CHR-002.speech_budget`: normal
speech through ch. 4, argues no more after ch. 5, **one sentence** in ch. 6, **nothing** in ch. 7.
She is never mute in ch. 5.

**What she never does.** Explain **a mystery, her own state, or anyone's feelings** — she *does*
report observed facts, and that is the whole content of her attention rather than an exception to
this rule (`CONF-009`; the rule was previously absolute, and ch. 1's "That is what they do instead of
a greeting" is legitimate). Complain about not being heard. Say what she wants in a way Merope would
have to notice.

### THE BEINGS — collective character

**Syntax fingerprint.** Warm, plain, unhedged, and short. They answer questions honestly and
immediately, with no subtext and no agenda. They do not ask questions back — not because they are
secretive, but because there is nothing they want to know. Their sentences contain no *should*, no
*warning*, no *careful*.

**Voice under pressure.** There is no pressure. They never become defensive, evasive, or frightened,
because they are never in danger and never have been. If one is asked something it cannot answer, it
answers happily with something adjacent, and the answer will be true.

**The rule that governs them:** they cannot lie, and they cannot explain. They do not know what they
are, so there is nothing for them to conceal. Anything that sounds like evasion in their dialogue is
the writer's fault, not theirs.

**What they never do.** Hint. Warn. Answer a question with a meaningful pause. Have a spokesperson.

### THE SLEIGH — not a speaker

It does not talk. It acts. Its presence in the prose is **physical verbs**: settle, turn, lean, hold.
It can move without anyone deciding to move it, and when it does, the narration should not flag it as
remarkable. Merope treats it as a thing that was always going to do that.

Its steed is **a constellation wearing a shape** and is described by the shape, never as an animal
between shafts.

## Differentiation matrix

Three or more separating markers for every pair that can share a page.

**Merope vs Achird**

| Marker | Merope | Achird |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence length | Long, complete, elaborating | Two to four words |
| Modifiers | Superlatives, no hedging | None |
| Answering a question | With a question or a correction | With the answer |
| Attention | On who is watching her | On what is happening |
| Under pressure | Longer, then one cruel short line | Shorter, then silent |
| Self-reference | Constant | Almost absent |

**Merope vs the beings**

| Marker | Merope | The beings |
|---|---|---|
| Agenda | Needs an audience | Have none |
| Subtext | Everything | None |
| Questions | Ask them to hold the floor | Do not ask them |
| Praise | Seeks it | Give it freely and mean it |

**Achird vs the beings**

| Marker | Achird | The beings |
|---|---|---|
| Cadence | Clipped, deliberate | Plain, unhesitating |
| Content | Facts she has checked | Facts she has not thought about |
| Response to a hard question | Answers it and stops | Answers it with something else that is also true |

**Cover-the-name test.** Every line in this book should be assignable without the tag. If a line of
Achird's could be given to Merope, it is wrong: Achird never elaborates, and Merope never stops at
four words.

## Anti-pattern checklist

Do not write any of the following in this book.

- Any line where a character explains the theme. The theme is never stated, by anyone, ever.
- Any being stating, hinting at, or approaching the truth. Decision C.
- Any damage. Nobody pays, nobody is wounded, nothing is lost except an opportunity.
- Dread arriving through atmosphere, weather, omens, or foreshadowing. Unease arrives only through
  Achird's finds and Merope's dismissals.
- Merope hearing Achird. Every true thing Achird says is narrated over, answered with something
  else, or not answered at all.
- A horse, a cart, shafts, harness, or a draft animal.
- A competence cascade. Merope never solves.
- Achird's love stated directly, by either of them.
- Merope becoming likable. Charming is allowed and required; likable is a failure.
- Manufactured emphasis: three or more consecutive one-to-four-word sentences stacked for drama.
  One-line paragraphs and clipped dialogue are the register and are allowed.

## Benchmark samples

Reference register, written to this contract. Not from the manuscript. Use for calibration.

**Status: 10 of 10 required. CLOSES WARN-009.** The voice bank requirement is `>=10 samples
including >=3 voice-breaking and >=2 irrelevant-thought`. This file shipped 4 and the registers it was
missing were exactly the ones chapters 2-7 needed — ch.6's cruelty was a voice-break with no sample
behind it. Samples 5-10 were added 2026-09-22, after the chapters existed, which is late; recorded as
late rather than backdated.

**1 — Merope narrating (retrospective, present-tense aside, no wink):**

> I have been given gifts I did not want by people who could not afford them. This is not a complaint.
> I accepted all of them, and I wore most of them once, and I was seen doing it, which is the part
> that matters to a gift.

**2 — Merope under pressure (boastful, then one accurate line):**

> I have been everywhere worth going and been admired in all of it, and I have never once needed a
> door held for me, and you know that. That is why you have not asked me to leave. You know exactly
> what I am, and you stayed, and I did not ask you to do that either.

**3 — Achird (three words, attending, correct):**

> "The light moved."
> "It does that."
> "Not like that."

**4 — A being (warm, honest, no subtext, nothing concealed):**

> "You can sleep there. Everyone sleeps there."

**5 — Merope, VOICE-BREAK: the cruelty. Boastful, then one accurate line, delivered flat.**

Note the mechanism: the sentences get *longer and more elaborate* before the break, and the cruel
line is short, quiet and true. Written to the same contract as the drafted ch.6 cruelty, which the
Phase 4 audit flagged as running long — this sample is the target the draft overshot.

> I have told that story in better rooms than this one, to people who paid to hear it, and I have
> never once told it badly, and you have been standing at the edge of every single telling with your
> hands at your sides and no expression on your face. You could have said something. That is all
> I am saying. You could have said something.

**6 — Merope, VOICE-BREAK: the flat admission. No flourish, no wit, no recovery.**

Used where the narrator stops performing on purpose. This is the register of *"I was happy that my
things were on the shelf with the others"*: one short declarative, no adjective, no self-defence,
and no sentence after it to soften it.

> I was pleased that they had done it for me. That is the whole of what I felt and I am not going to
> put anything on either side of it.

**7 — Merope, VOICE-BREAK: register dropped entirely. She stops narrating herself as the centre.**

The hardest one, and the reason ch.7 works. When Merope stops performing, the prose must *lose its
ornament* rather than gain gravitas. No long sentences here — the boastful register is the elaborate
one; sincerity is the plain one.

> I did not choose it. I want that down before anything else. Something made up its mind and I went
> along, and if you have been reading this waiting for the moment I decided, there is not going to
> be one.

**8 — Achird, VOICE-BREAK: the largest moment in the book is one sentence.**

Her only escalation is a *reduction*. Where another character would explain, Achird spends one
sentence and stops, and the stopping is the pressure. Nothing after it. No second line.

> "Everyone who came here died here."

**9 — Merope, IRRELEVANT-THOUGHT: a digression that serves nothing.**

Required register, and easy to get wrong: the thought must be genuinely beside the point and still be
worth reading. It must not secretly carry theme, foreshadowing, or a decision. It is the narrator
being a person while the plot waits.

> I once spent a whole winter learning to sharpen a knife properly and then gave the knife away in
> the spring to a man whose name I never used again. There is no lesson in it. I simply think about
> him whenever somebody hands me something and does not look at it.

**10 — Merope, IRRELEVANT-THOUGHT: an anticlimax. A thread picked up and dropped.**

This is the one drafts omit and readers believe. Merope notices something, decides it is not worth
her attention, and the book never returns to it.

> There was a door in the wall of the second inn that had been painted shut, and I asked about it
> twice. Nobody knew. I could look it up, I suppose. I have never looked it up.

## Chapter narration contract

Per the intake contract question 11: narrator, POV, distance, tense, sensory priority, rhythm,
emotional register, knowledge limits, and what a chapter may withhold. Recorded per movement rather
than per chapter, since the seven chapters share one narrator and differ in structure.

### The line that runs through all seven

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Narrator | Merope. Every sentence, including every sentence about Achird. |
| POV | Single. There is no third-person distance anywhere in this book. |
| Distance | Close and self-interested. She is a character with an agenda addressing the reader directly. |
| **Tense** | **CONFLICTED — see below. Do not resolve it here.** |
| Knowledge limit | She narrates only what she witnessed, misread, or was told. She may not narrate what she cannot know — not Achird's interior, not what the beings are, not what any prior visitor did. |
| What a chapter may withhold | Everything she is looking away from. She withholds by omission and by changing the subject, never by lying. She is not an unreliable narrator in the sense of concealing; she is looking the other way in beautiful sentences. |
| What a chapter may **never** withhold from the reader | Achird's true statements. The reader must hear her when Merope does not. Warning 12. |

### Tense — SETTLED 2026-09-22: retrospective past, present-tense asides

**Past tense for the event; present tense for the judgement of it; unmarked, in the same paragraph.**
That is the rule, and it is now the only rule.

`INTAKE.md` (user-sourced) said **"tight present tense."** That value loses. Both values and their
provenance are preserved in `CANON_LEDGER.yaml → CONF-001`. The reasons it loses are not preference:

- **The retrospective frame is load-bearing, not decorative.** The book is Merope telling it
  afterwards, and ch. 7's ending depends on a narrator who already knows how it turned out — *"she
  got what she wanted, it was smaller than she thought, and she is not sorry"* is not sayable from
  inside the moment. Neither are ch. 1's "I have been looked at before," "I will say this now," or
  "I have thought about it since and still cannot decide."
- **The reference the intake itself names as governing** — *Elaina*, on POV and register — writes
  exactly this way: past for the event, present for the opinion. `INTAKE.md → Voices` says "Fact
  presentation: **in the style of** *The Wandering Witch: The Journey of Elaina*."
- **The device table at the top of this document is built on it.** "Retrospective calm: the narrating
  Merope is steadier than the Merope in the scene. She knows how it turned out and is unhurried"
  requires hindsight. So does everything ch. 5's reversal and ch. 7's residue do.

**No chapter was rewritten for this.** Chapter 1 already complied except at three points in the
in-transit passage, which were normalised on 2026-09-22. Its word count was re-measured and the prose
gate re-run.

**Two failure modes to watch, in both directions.** A chapter may not narrate an **event** in the
present — an event in present tense is a slip, not an aside, and that is exactly what the opening of
ch. 1 had done three times. Nor may the narration drift into third-person retrospective distance: the
asides are *hers*, addressed to the reader, and they are judgements, never observations she could not
have had.

## Fact presentation

The intake contract asks how facts sound when they arrive through different channels. This book has
one narrator, so the discrimination is not *who is speaking* — it is **how much the narration knows
and how it signals it.** Merope reports everything as though it were settled; the register beneath
the claim is where the reader learns whether it is.

| Channel | Register | Certainty as narrated | Source cue | Allowable hedging | Sentence rhythm | Forbidden explanatory habit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Narrator fact** | Deadpan, declarative, rehearsed | Absolute. She finishes sentences. | None needed — she was there | None. Superlatives arrive unhedged: *the best*, *the only*, *never* | Long and complete, elaborating | Hedging. *I think*, *perhaps*, *it seemed*. She does not qualify. |
| **Dialogue fact** | Plain. A line of speech is a line of speech, presented without adverbs | Reported as spoken, not endorsed | The tag, or the line's placement | None | Short. Dialogue carries whole pages. | Any `-ly` adverb doing the speaker's work for them. *Said quietly*, *said flatly*. |
| **A being's fact** | Warm, unhedged, immediate, complete | Absolute — they cannot lie | They answer at once, with no pause before or reaction after | None | Short and plain, no subtext | Any hint, warning, evasion, meaningful pause, or question asked back. If it reads as evasion, it is the writer's error, not the being's. |
| **Achird's fact** | Clipped, correct, necessary in that order | Absolute. She states. | Shown, not hedged — but **Merope may narrate over it** | None from Achird | Two to four words | Explaining a mystery or her own state (see `CONF-009`); softeners; filling silence |
| **Memory** | Softer tense, present-tense aside allowed | Confident, and often wrong | Signalled by a shift in tense or by *I have* constructions | Rare, and only about feelings, never about events | Longer, trailing | Presenting a misremembering as verified. An unmarked memory that the reader cannot place is a defect. |
| **Rumor / report** | Reported as fact by the narrator, marked as second-hand only by the verb | As narrated: absolute. As true: unknown | *They said*, *I was told*, *somebody* | Never in Merope's own voice | Long, confident | Treating a reported claim as established. Nobody on this planet ever explains anything, so rumor is structurally rare — if a rumor appears, that is a decision to make, not a texture to add. |
| **Inference / uncertain knowledge** | Confident, generous, and **wrong** | Presented as settled. She does not signal doubt. | None — this is the danger | **None. This is the channel with no marker at all.** | Long, warm, reasonable | Explaining. Ch.5's wrong reading must be the only explicit interpretation offered, and it must sound reasonable enough that the reader sees why she'd believe it. The absence of a doubt marker *is* the device. |
| **Document / epistolary fact** | **Does not occur in this book.** There are no documents, records, ledgers, inscriptions or archives. | — | — | — | — | Introducing one. A written record is specifically forbidden by the exclusion rule — *"a record or ledger of the dead."* |
| **Research claim** | **Does not occur.** No outside authority, no citation, no expert, no exposition about how the world works. | — | — | — | — | Any explanation of the sleigh, the travel, the beings, the growth or their history. Warning 9. |

### The two channels that carry the book

The **inference** row is the engine. Merope's wrong explanation in chapter 5 has no marker at all —
no hedge, no stumble, no moment of doubt — and that absence is what makes it land. The reader is not
told she is wrong. The reader has already watched Achird be right and unheard for four chapters.

The **dialogue** row is the discipline. This register is flat by design: emotional beats land in one
flat sentence and are not dwelt on. An adverb telling the reader how a line was delivered is the
failure mode to watch for in a book whose whole effect depends on the reader hearing what the
narrator does not.

### One live conflict on these contracts

`beings.answer-boundary` (`CONF-003`) is unresolved: the beings "cannot lie" and "cannot explain,"
and chapter 2 requires one of them to answer honestly about an object that belonged to a dead

it is called here, where it is kept, that it is nice — and never from *origin or ownership*. Chapter
1's line *"Somebody lost this"* already sits on the right side of that line. Settle it before ch.2.

## Voice card — `COL-002` — THE WORKERS (collective, but not one voice)

*Scope: book-2. Authority: pipeline-proposed, on an author-decided category. See
`book-2/ENTITY_STATE.yaml → new_in_book_2.COL-002`.*

**Syntax fingerprint.** Plain, unhedged, complete, and **at ease**. They finish their sentences and
they answer the question they were asked. Sentences run a normal conversational length — they are not
clipped like Achird, and they are not ornate like Merope. Their register is **procedural warmth**: the
tone of someone explaining a job they like to someone new.

**What makes them not `COL-001`.** The beings *cannot explain*. The workers explain **constantly** —
their own part, in detail, cheerfully, and it is all true — and the explanation never reaches *why*,
because *why* is not a category they hold. They are not concealing anything. There is nothing there.

**The sentences that break them.** 

- Anything solemn, hesitant, or meaningfully quiet. They do not do subtext.
- A meaningful pause. They answer immediately.
- Hinting, warning, or going careful. Nothing here is dangerous.
- **Self-pity.** They are not sad about their lives and must not be written as sad about their lives.
  If a worker's account of arriving reads as pitiable, the chapter is wrong — it reads as *settled*.
- Being a **spokesperson**. No worker ever speaks for the work, the world, or the book's meaning. There
  is no host (`R-018`), and a worker who starts explaining the world has become one.

**Their danger to the writing.** They are the easiest place in the book to accidentally state the
theme, because they are content and articulate and the theme is sitting right next to them. They may say
what they left. They may never say what it meant.

## Merope's Book 2 register: the testing

*Scope: book-2. Authority: author decision 2026-09-24 (`book-2.relationship-delta`).*

Merope's card above is unchanged, including *"what she never does: say a feeling plainly."* Book 2 adds
one behaviour on top of it: she now **notices**, and she **tests**.

| Device | Shape on the page |
|---|---|
| **The opening left** | A gap in a conversation shaped exactly like Achird's chance to speak. Merope waits, and narrates over the wait. |
| **The question with the answer inside it** | Her existing habit, now aimed: she asks Achird something built so the answer would *be* the answer. |
| **The retreat** | She walks off mid-something to see whether Achird follows. Reports it as an observation. |
| **The sideways look** | Noticing, then reporting the noticing as information. |
| **The test she denies** | She sets something up and then performs not having set it up. She is a performer. |

**Rules.** The testing is **subtle and legible** — a reader should be able to see her doing it without
being told. It **escalates** through movement II and **ends** in ch.6. It is never acknowledged
outright by either of them. And it is **not** a phase she is embarrassed by: it is a habit she is good
at.

## Anti-pattern additions for Book 2

- Making this place a trap. Leaving is easy; nobody blocks, follows, blames, or argues (`R-015`).
- Any worker going solemn, hinting, warning, or pitying themselves.
- Any statement of the theme, by anyone, ever.
- A chase, a confrontation, or a threat.
- Self-pity in the narration about being needless — Merope does not feel sorry for herself; she takes
the role as evidence she was right.
- Achird escalating by *volume*. She escalates by remaining exactly as she is.
- Describing either lead physically. The instructed hair change is **appearance canon**, not narration.

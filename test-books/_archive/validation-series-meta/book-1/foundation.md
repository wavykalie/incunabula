## EXCLUSION RULE

**Coldbrook Junction is out of scope for this project.** Premise, engine, cast, structure, motifs,
chapter-title patterns and craft habits from that book must not inform this one. If a choice here
resembles it, that resemblance is a defect — redo the choice.

Specifically forbidden by this rule:

- a place that **takes** people, keeps them, or absorbs them;
- a **record or ledger of the dead**;
- a companion who **pays** so the protagonist can continue;
- a relationship that must be **damaged** to be meaningful;
- titles of the form **"What the X Kept."**

## Theme question

> **Does a place that asks nothing of you keep you from becoming anything?**

**Never answered in the text.** The beings answer it wrong by being glad to see you. Merope answers
it wrong by staying. Achird answers it right without saying anything. The reader is left holding it.

If any character states this theme out loud, that chapter fails the Theme dimension.

---

## The engine

The planet welcomes them. **The welcome is genuine.**

**The hospitality is the mechanism.** Being welcomed, fed, rested and enjoyed is exactly how a
visitor ends up staying long enough to die. There is **no antagonist, no transformation, no
absorption, and nothing to resist.** Nobody is folded into anything and nobody is changed into
anything. People stay, and then they die here, and the hedges grow from them.

The only available action is **to leave** — and every part of the planet is designed to make leaving
feel unnecessary.

**The horror has two halves, and neither belongs to a villain:**

1. The pile: everyone who ever came before them is dead here.
2. The innocence: the beings have **no idea what they are.**

## Characters

### MEROPE — narrator, 19

- **Given voice:** grandiose and certain; narrates herself as the center; under pressure she turns
  **boastful, then cruel.**
- **Given trait:** **tsundere.** She cannot be direct about what she feels. She deflects, performs,
  and takes pride in not needing anything.
- **Her want:** to be **watched**. She is a girl who has never left anywhere she was adored.- **Her arc (revised 2026-09-22, resolving `CONF-006`):** she does not become good, and **she does
  not choose.** The sleigh decides and she is carried — the step is never hers, and she never states
  a reason she believes. What changes is that she **looks**: on the way out she sees Achird properly,
  for the first time in the book. She does not understand what it means and does not name it.
  *(Her earlier arc line — "she takes one step against her own appetite" — is superseded. Both
  values are preserved in `CANON_LEDGER.yaml → CONF-006`.)*
- **At the end:** she realises Achird loves her, cannot say anything direct about it, and
  **acknowledges it anyway.**

### ACHIRD — 19, kuudere

- **Her want, given:** **to be seen by Merope.** Not safety, not comfort, not attention in general
  — specifically by the one person beside her, who never looks.
- **Voice:** short declaratives, no performed emotion — and, crucially, **she is paying close
  attention.** Her flatness is attention, not absence.
- **Function:** she is the one who acts. She is the reason they leave.
- **Structural role (decided by you):** she **finds the belongings first** and **is not believed.**
  The middle of the book is her **unable to be heard.** She says true things flatly and correctly,
  and Merope — who narrates — explains them away, because the planet is lovely to her and Achird is
  always saying flat worrisome things.

### THE BEINGS — collective character

- **Friendly. Inviting. Genuinely glad to see visitors.**
- **They do not know what they are.** They have no idea they grew from the dead.
- They do not lie, and they do not explain.
- They are not doing anything to anyone. **They cannot be fought or argued with**, because there is
  nothing hostile to fight.

### THE SLEIGH

**Not a chariot. No horse.** An **old-style enclosed cabin on runners**, glazed, that travels the
dark between worlds.

Its **steed is drawn from a constellation and morphs into that constellation's shape** — *Scorpius*
as a scorpion, *Delphinus* as a dolphin. It takes the form it chooses.

**It is its own character, in a way.** Achird chooses the constellation, **but the sleigh also
chooses**, and it can act by itself — **if it needs to save someone, it will.** It is therefore the
one thing in the book that can move without anyone's decision, which matters because the planet's
whole method is to make movement unnecessary.

**Achird owns and pilots it; Merope narrates as though she were the pilot.**

Comedic-scorned potential: the steed's form is *visible*, and Merope will want an audience to see
it. That is her vanity finding a stage, not the story's point.

**Constellation names may be used on the page** (your decision).

## Tone and movement

### Structural changes (decided by you — all three selected)

**A. The world was expecting them.** The welcome is not improvised for whoever happens to arrive.
The place is **arranged in advance**, and the arrangement is **routine and old.** The beings do not
know why they are shaped the way they are.

**B. Achird finds it first and is not believed.** See ACHIRD above. The discovery is **early**, and
the middle of the book is **Achird unable to be heard.**

**C. No host, no confrontation, no chase.** No single being ever tells them anything. There is no
escape sequence. They leave because Achird makes them leave, and the beings are **glad to see them
go.** The truth is assembled from **objects and innocent non-answers.**

**Not selected:** *no present danger.* **Visitors still die here.** The mortality is real.

### Movements (revised for A/B/C)

| Movement | Chapters | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **I — The welcome** | 1–3 | Genuinely wonderful. The beings are lovely, everything is prepared, Merope is delighted. **Dread does not enter here as dread** — but Achird is already finding things, and Merope is already dismissing her. |
| **II — Achird is not heard** | 4–5 | Belongings laid out with care, and no bodies. Honest non-answers. **Merope explains it away.** Unease builds steadily. |
| **III — The turn** | 6–7 | Merope refuses to leave; **Achird stops asking and the sleigh decides**; **no chase**; the acknowledgement. |

**Dread enters late in movement I and rises steadily through II and III** — not in one cold
statement, and not in a chapter-four lurch.

**Tone balance — decided by you, 2026-09-22: partly escalatory.** The first movement is genuinely
warm and light; unease then **builds steadily** across movements II and III. This is *not* the
reference method (long warmth → one cold statement → warm coda) and *not* the earlier dread-forward
ramp.

## Writer warnings

1. **Do not import the excluded book.** See the exclusion rule.
2. **Dread never announces itself.** Movement I must read as **wonderful on a first pass.** Unease
   enters only through Achird's finds and Merope's dismissals — never through atmosphere, menace,
   omens or foreshadowing.
3. **Do not make the beings sinister.** No malice, ever. They are innocent, and that is worse.
4. **Do not let Merope become likable.** She may be charming; charming is not likable.
5. **No competence cascade in chapter 1.** She opens by being watched, not by solving something.
6. **Do not let Merope learn the theme.** She takes one step; she does not understand it.
7. **Do not damage anyone.** Nothing is lost in this book. The ending is growth — and the growth
   lives **entirely** in ch.7's acknowledgement beats, which are two and small. They are the only
   place the arc moves. Do not compensate by giving Merope a decision she does not make (`CONF-006`),
   and watch for a fatalism reading: the counterweight is that the acknowledgement is mutual and real.
8. **Do not state Achird's love directly.** Merope realises it. Neither of them says it plainly.
9. **Do not explain the sleigh, the travel, or their history.** Merope does not explain herself —
   that is characterization, not an omission to be filled.
10. **Do not give the planet a spokesperson.** No being ever explains, warns, hints or states the
    truth. Not once, to anyone. (Decision C.)
11. **Do not write a chase.** Nobody pursues them and nothing turns hostile. The beings are **glad
    to see them go.** (Decision C.)
12. **Do not let Merope hear Achird.** Every true thing Achird says is dismissed, misread or
    narrated over. **The reader must hear her when Merope does not.** (Decision B.)
13. **The sleigh is not a horse and not a chariot.** It is a glazed old-style cabin on runners, and
    its steed is **a constellation that has taken a shape.** Never imply a draft animal between
    shafts.
14. **The sleigh may act against Merope's narration.** It decides for itself and will move to save
    someone. It is the only thing in the book that can act without anyone choosing to. Its act in
    ch.6 **is** the saving: staying is what kills her, and the planet's whole method is to make
    leaving feel unnecessary. That is meaning, not a rescue scene — write no rescue, and forbid no
    meaning. (`CONF-007`; the earlier ban on "the sleigh saving anyone" was the agent's inference,
    not a user rule.)
15. **No AI tells in the prose.** Draft with the `humanizer` rules and the `deslopify` rule set
    (`.agents/skills/deslopify/references/style_guide.md`) active, then run
    `tools/deslop-check.sh` on the chapter and make it **PASS** before calling the chapter
    drafted. The tells most likely to appear in *this* book: magic adverbs (*quietly*, *deeply*),
    *tapestry* / *landscape*, the *serves as* dodge, negative parallelism, *Not X. Not Y.*,
    *The result?*, *Here's the thing*, signposted conclusions, em dashes used as dramatic asides,
    `-ing` tails bolted onto sentences, and runs of short punchy fragments.
16. **Do not confuse the house style with the trope.** One-line paragraphs and short dialogue are
    the reference's register and they are allowed. The trope is *manufactured* emphasis: three or
    more consecutive one-to-four-word sentences stacked for drama. Style, yes; drumbeat, no.

## EXCLUSION RULE — checked, and it is tight on this book

**Coldbrook Junction is out of scope.** Forbidden by it: a place that **takes** people, **keeps** them,
or **absorbs** them; a **record or ledger of the dead**; a companion who **pays**; a relationship that
must be **damaged**; titles of the form *"What the X Kept."*

**Book 2 must not become a trap book.** It is at real risk of it, because "everyone here is stuck" is one
careless sentence away from the excluded engine. The guard is structural and it is `R-015`:

> **Everyone is needed. Nobody is kept.**

Leaving is easy. Nobody argues, blocks, blames, or follows. The world does not hold anyone. It simply
**needs** them, endlessly, and will be quietly poorer when they go. That is not a trap. It is worse,
because a trap gives you someone to be angry at.

Every chapter is checked against this. If a scene could be rewritten as "the place won't let her leave,"
it is a defect.

## Theme question

> **Is being needed the same as being wanted?**

**Never answered in the text. Never stated by anyone.** Merope spends the book believing the answer is
yes, and the whole book is the reader watching that be almost true. The workers answer it wrong by being
content. Achird answers it right by doing nothing about it and staying anyway.

If any character states this theme out loud, that chapter fails the Theme dimension.

---

## The engine — THE WORK (author decision, 2026-09-23)

A world where **everyone has a job and the job is the entire point.** Nobody is kept. Everyone is
**needed**, constantly, and usefulness here never ends.

**The mechanism.** The work is load-bearing and constant, it is older than anyone there, and **no lifetime
present will see it finished.** Merope — who has never been useful to anybody — is handed a tool and told
the work needs it held.

**Why this is the inversion of Book 1, exactly:**

| | Book 1 | Book 2 |
|---|---|---|
| What the world does | asks **nothing** of you | asks **everything** of you |
| How it kills you | you stay because nothing requires you to go | you stay because everything requires you to remain |
| The pull | comfort | usefulness |
| What Merope gets | an audience | a function |

**Where the dread lives.** Not in objects, and not in non-answers — that was Book 1's channel and
repeating it would make Book 2 a cover version. Book 2's dread arrives through **usefulness itself**:

> The more she is needed, the less she is anyone. Everyone is needed here. It is the least special thing
> in the world.

**The two halves of the horror, and neither belongs to a villain:**

1. **The need is impersonal.** It needs the tool held. It does not need *her*. Anyone standing there
   would do, and everyone there knows it about themselves, and is at peace with it.
2. **The workers are not unhappy.** They are warm, competent and content. Nobody is being exploited and
   nobody wants out. There is no cruelty anywhere in this world to push against.

## The location — THE UNFINISHED THING (author decision, 2026-09-23)

One enormous work. Everyone is building it. **Nobody says what it is for** — not because it is secret,
but because *"what for"* is not a question the work has ever needed answered, and the workers hold no
category for it. Every person there is genuinely essential. It will not be finished in any lifetime
present.

**What the prose may show:** carrying, fitting, holding, sealing, feeding, moving, tending, waiting for a
part to come back around. **What the prose may never show:** what it is, what it is for, or anyone
explaining either. It is described by what hands are doing, always.

**The mechanics** are `R-013`–`R-021` in `ENTITY_STATE.yaml`. The four that shape every scene:

- `R-013`/`R-014` — the work does not stop; nobody knows what happens if it does, because it never has.
  **Never framed as a threat.** It is the one thing nobody has tried.
- `R-015` — nobody is kept. Leaving is easy and **costly**; no one stops you and everyone is poorer.
- `R-017` — there is no finish. Not "behind schedule": the work is the shape of the world.
- `R-018` — **no one directs it.** No leader, no foreman, no host, no spokesperson. (Series rule, unchanged.)

**What it is like to stand there:** nothing here is hostile, nothing is hidden, and nothing looks at you.
That last one is the horror, and it lands on Merope specifically.

## The population — THE WORKERS (author-decided category, pipeline-proposed design)

**Author decision 2026-09-24:** the population is **something new** — not the Book 1 beings, not ordinary
people-with-jobs. `COL-001` stays book-1-scoped and **does not appear**.

**Pipeline design (marked `proposed` in `ENTITY_STATE.yaml`, sign-off requested):**

Each worker was given **one part** of the work and has been at that part long enough that the part is all
of them. They are warm, competent, thorough and content. They will answer anything about the work, at
length, and their answer is always true.

**They cannot answer why it matters.** Not because they are hiding it: because the question has no
purchase on them, and asking it produces a kind answer that returns to work.

### The cold thing — how they got here

**This is Book 2's analogue of Book 1's shelf, and it must land the same way: through plain facts, in
plain voices, assembled by the reader.**

> Every worker arrived because **someone stopped wanting them.**

Not one dramatic case, and never spelled out as a rule. One has a story about a house. One will not say.
One cannot remember and does not mind. The work never asks why anyone came, and **being needed is what
this place offers people who are no longer wanted** — which is why it is generous, and why Merope should
have run the moment she understood it.

### Why they do not inherit `COL-001`'s rules

| | `COL-001` the beings (Book 1) | `COL-002` the workers (Book 2) |
|---|---|---|
| Can they explain? | **No.** Cannot lie, cannot explain. | **Yes, endlessly** — and it never reaches *why*. |
| Do they know what they are? | No. They have no idea. | **Yes.** They are a function, and it does not trouble them. |
| Their welcome | delight at the visitor | **assessment of the visitor** — what can they hold? |
| Their innocence | they are innocent | they are **complete**. Nothing is missing and nothing is hidden. |

The workers are **not a collective character** and are not one voice. They are individuals who each chose
the same thing.

### What they are never allowed to be

Not sinister (`book-1` warning 3, extended here). Not smug. Not pitying. Not a spokesperson for the
book's meaning (`R-018` — there is no host). They are kind, and their kindness is the problem.

## Characters

### MEROPE — narrator, 19

Inherited: grandiose, certain, narrates herself as the centre; **tsundere**; under pressure **boastful,
then cruel**; wants to be **watched**; never winks (`series.voice.no-wink`). Appearance is series canon
for illustration and the **prose describes her not at all** — see `../book-1/artifacts/character-design-brief.md`.

**Her Book 2 position.** She is handed a role and told it needs her. This is the first time anything has
ever *needed* her, and she takes it the way she takes compliments: greedily, and as evidence she was right
about herself.

**Her arc — and `CRIT-101` is why it is worded this way.** Book 1 **already spent the noticing**: ch.7
ends with her finally looking at Achird and realising Achird loves her, and with the arc explicitly
rewritten so the growth lives there. Book 2 must not re-run that. The noticing is **done**, and what
Book 2 adds is that it will not stay put: she cannot leave it alone, and so she **tests**.

She does not become good, and she does not become likable (`book-1` warnings 4, 6). The testing makes
her worse before it stops. She gives Achird openings shaped exactly like a chance to speak, waits,
walks off mid-something to see whether Achird follows, asks questions with the answer already inside
them, and looks sideways to see what lands. All book. Subtly.

Then she stops testing. That is the arc and it is the whole of it: **from testing whether she is wanted
to saying something true without a test.** She does not learn the theme. She performs the relief of a
person who has stopped doing something exhausting.

**What she never does:** explain the theme, become generous in a way that costs her nothing, or admit
while performing that she is performing.

### ACHIRD — 19, kuudere

Inherited: **to be seen by Merope**, specifically; two-to-four-word sentences, no modifiers; flatness is
**attention**, not absence; under pressure **quieter and shorter, never louder**; owns and pilots the
sleigh; appearance is series canon, **not described in the prose**.

**Her Book 2 position — and this is the engine of the book's two-hander.** The work **does not need
her.** Nobody asks her to hold anything. She is the one person on the planet for whom there is no role,
which means she is the only one who could leave at any moment, and she stays anyway. She is here for
Merope. She has always been here for Merope.

**She does not move first.** She does not react to the testing, does not pass it, does not fail it, and
does not acknowledge it exists. Her largest moment in the book is **one sentence** (`CONF-009`: a report
may run to a full sentence; do not pad it, do not truncate a true observation).

**Her speech budget in Book 2 is Book 2's own (`CRIT-102`).** Book 1 had a per-chapter silence timeline
— normal through ch.4, stops arguing after ch.5, one sentence in ch.6, silent in ch.7 — and that timeline
is **book-1-scoped**. It was an outline artefact of that book, and `CONF-011` is precisely what happens
when a book-1 artefact is read as a series rule. What is inherited is her **voice card**: clipped,
attending, no modifiers, and escalating **quieter rather than louder**. What is *not* inherited is a
silence chapter. Book 2 has **no chapter in which she stops speaking**, ch.7 is **not** a silent chapter
for her, and the point at which she goes quiet is decided by this book's own scenes. She is quiet in
ch.7 in the ordinary way — she is not mute.

**What she never does:** explain a mystery, her own state, or anyone's feelings; complain about not being
heard; say what she wants in a way Merope would have to notice.

### THE SLEIGH — not a speaker

`OBJ-001`. Achird's. Its steed is a constellation wearing a shape, and the constellation from Book 1's
ending is **Scorpius**. It is the only continuous presence across the volume boundary
(`book-2.interval`: the gap is not dated), and it was parked at the end of Book 1.

**It is at the edge of this book's whole question.** The work needs everyone. The sleigh needs no one and
belongs to one person. It acts by itself and the narration does not flag it (`book-1` warning 14 — the
ban on it "saving anyone" was the agent's inference, not a user rule).

**Not a draft animal. Not a chariot. Not explained.** (`series.sleigh.is-not-a-chariot`.)

### THE HELD TOOL — `ITM-005`

What Merope is handed in chapter 1. What it is for is never explained. What is required is that it be
held, by her, for as long as the work needs it, **which is forever**.

It is the physical carrier of the theme question: **the tool needs holding; it does not need her.** Never
named, never explained, and never given a triumphant final gesture.

## The relationship delta (author decision, 2026-09-24)

**Merope notices, and begins testing Achird in subtle ways.**

**What it is not.** It is not a secret either of them keeps: Book 1 ended with the acknowledgement
**known and unsaid**, and both of them carry it.

**And it is not forbidden to be said (`CRIT-103`).** An earlier revision of this project's own canon
forbade the plain sentence and called it a series-scoped contract; the author struck that on 2026-09-24
as pipeline inference (`CONF-011`). The correction only counts if it reaches the drafting instructions,
so it is stated here, in the voice contract, and in ch.7's beats: **whether anything is ever said plainly
is a drafting decision, and chapter 7 makes one.** It is one clause, unperformed, and it is not a love
speech — but it is *permitted*, and nothing in this book may be drafted as though it were not.

**How the testing is staged, so it stays subtle and legible:**

| Device | What it looks like | Why it is Merope |
|---|---|---|
| The opening left | She leaves a gap in a conversation that is shaped exactly like Achird's chance to speak, and waits, and narrates over the wait | She would never ask directly |
| The question with the answer inside it | The Book 1 habit, now aimed | Her syntax fingerprint |
| The retreat | She walks off mid-something to see whether Achird follows | She needs proof, not words |
| The sideways look | Noticing, then reporting the noticing as though it were an observation | She narrates everything as information |
| The test she cannot admit to | She sets something up and then pretends she did not | She is a performer |

**It escalates** — testing gets more deliberate across movement II — **and it ends**, in chapter 6, when
an honest answer arrives that the test was never designed to accept. She stops testing in ch.7.

## Movements

**A different dread curve from Book 1, deliberately.** Book 1 was warmth → unease → turn. Book 2's
temperature is not warm at all: **the usefulness is there from the first page**, and the reader should
feel the chill on the scene where Merope is happiest.

| Movement | Chapters | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **I — Needed** | 1–3 | She is given a role and it is real. Genuinely, unironically wonderful for her, and the reader should feel how complete it is. She works. Nobody looks at her. Achird is offered nothing and stays. |
| **II — The rest of the need** | 4–5 | What everyone else here is, and why they came. Nobody is kept; leaving is easy; the tool would be held by anyone. The noticing starts to become testing. |
| **III — The test, and the answer** | 6–7 | She pushes, and pushes with an accurate cruel sentence in it, and the answer she gets is not the one the test was for. She stops. The plain thing is said, once, small. |

**Tension map (no false-low; a slow plateau, not a ramp):**

| Ch | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tension | 4 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 9 | 5 |

Ch.7 falls deliberately. The ending is warm and is not a punch.

**Structural ledger** (no consecutive repeats, and deliberately not Book 1's ledger):
`chronological · catalogue · chorus · fragmented · reverse · stream · chronological`

## Tone and craft

- **Dread never announces itself** (`book-1` warning 2). It arrives through **the workers' contentment**,
  **Merope's misreadings**, and **hands that do not stop**.
- One-line paragraphs, dialogue-dominant scenes, no lyricism (`../book-1/voice-dna.md`).
- Emotional beats land in **one flat sentence**.
- Chapters **end on a quiet image**, not a punch.
- Merope **never winks** (`series.voice.no-wink`). She means every word.
- The reader must always hear Achird when Merope does not.

---

## Naming

**Nothing on this world is named** (`series.naming-rule`, `R-020`). No place name, no name for the work,
no name for the tool, no titles. **Constellation names are permitted on the page** and the steed is
`Scorpius`. The workers are described by what they are doing.

## Writer warnings — Book 2

1. **This is not a trap book.** Nobody is kept, and nobody tries to keep anyone. If a scene reads as
   imprisonment, it is wrong. (`R-015`, and the exclusion rule.)
2. **Dread never announces itself.** Ch.1–3 record being needed **straight and warmly**. Unease comes
   only from hands, contentment, and Merope's misreadings.
3. **Do not explain the work.** Not what it is, not what it is for, not by anyone. There is no host, no
   foreman, no spokesperson (`R-018`). A worker answering honestly about their own part is fine and is
   not an explanation.
4. **Do not let Merope become likable** (`book-1` warning 4). She is charm without membership. Testing
   Achird is not a phase she is embarrassed by; it is a habit she is good at.
5. **Do not let Merope hear Achird** (`book-1` warning 12). The reader does.
6. **Do not state the theme.** Nobody ever says being needed is or is not being wanted.
7. **Do not damage anyone.** Nothing is lost, nobody pays, no one is wounded. The ending is growth, and
   the growth is small and lives in ch.7.
8. **Do not write a chase, a confrontation, or a rescue.** Nobody follows them. Nothing turns hostile.
9. **Do not give Achird a monologue.** One sentence in ch.6 is her largest moment. Two-to-four words is
   normal. Do not pad a true observation to hit a word count (`CONF-009`). **Do not inherit Book 1's
   silence timeline** (`CRIT-102`): no chapter here is a silent chapter for her, and she is not mute in
   ch.7. **Do not forbid the plain sentence** (`CRIT-103`, `CONF-011`): ch.7 says one small true thing,
   once, and saying it is permitted.
10. **Do not let the sleigh be convenient**, be downgraded to a conveyance, or be explained.
11. **Do not describe either lead physically.** The prose describes neither of them anywhere (Book 1,
    15,531 words) and that is a recorded position, not a gap. Their appearance is canon for illustration
    only. The instructed hair-styling change is therefore an **appearance-canon** matter for Phase 7, not
    something the narration may raise.
12. **No interference from Book 1's cast or history.** `COL-001` does not appear. No character
    remembers the planet. The interval between the books is **not** to be dated, counted, or leaned on
    (`book-2.interval`).
13. **The tool is never triumphant.** Do not end on her brandishing it, surrendering it, or being thanked
    for it.
14. **No AI tells in the prose.** Draft with the humanizer rules active and run
    `../tools/deslop-check.sh` on every chapter; a chapter is not drafted until it **PASSES**. Watch for
    the same tells as Book 1, and note that this book's subject matter makes *"serves as"*, *"the
    result?"*, and negative parallelism its specific risks.
15. **Book 2 is longer by permission, not by drift.** Aim 15,000–18,000; ceiling 22,000. If it runs
    past the ceiling, re-cut the shape rather than raise it again.

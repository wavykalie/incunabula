## 1. Synopsis (short — for listings, ~90 words)

Two girls land on a world where everyone has a job and the job is the whole point.

Nobody is kept. Nobody is refused. Everyone is needed, constantly, and the work will not be finished in
any lifetime present. Merope — who has never been useful to anybody — is handed a tool within four
minutes of standing on the ground, and told it needs holding.

She has never been happier.

Her companion has been given nothing at all. That is the part Merope notices.

*Working title. Book 2 of a series.*

## 2. Synopsis (long — for an agent or a query, ~300 words)

Merope narrates her own life as though she were piloting it. She is not. Achird flies the sleigh, and
Merope has been telling the story in the wrong voice since the first night, and she knows it, and she has
finally learned which half of a thing is hers.

They land on a world that is entirely a work. There is no gate, no greeting, and nobody whose job it is
to look at people who have just arrived. Rows of hands are doing the same thing and not the same thing:
taking apart, fitting together, holding still. Nobody looks up.

A woman hands Merope a tool and says the work needs it held, and goes back to her part. Merope holds it
for eleven days.

It is the first time anything has ever needed her, and it does not end, and she is delighted. The work
cannot tell her what happens if it stops, because it has never stopped. Nobody runs it. Nobody explains
it. There is no host and no plan and no finish; there is only everyone, doing their part, at the same
temperature, forever.

Her companion is given nothing. Not as a cruelty — it simply had not come up. Merope notices that, and
what she does with it is the book.

A worker walks out in the middle of a shift. Nobody follows. *We'll be poorer*, one of them says, and
does not stop carrying. And the account of how anyone arrives here assembles backwards, out of plain
speech from contented people, into something Merope works out and then applies to everybody except
herself.

## 3. Blurb / back cover copy (~120 words)

**The work needs it held.**

There is no gate and no greeting. The light is flat, the rows go on past where she can count, and within
four minutes of standing on the ground somebody has put a tool in her hands and gone back to their own
part of it.

Nobody is kept here. Nobody is refused. Everyone is needed all the time, and the work will not be
finished in any lifetime present.

Merope has never been useful to anybody. She holds it for eleven days.

Her companion holds nothing, because nobody asked her to. That is the part Merope cannot leave alone —
and out past the rows, one of the workers is already walking away, and nobody is going to stop her.

**Three cover directions.**

**Option 1 — the tool. Recommended.**
One object at large scale on an otherwise empty ground: something held, worn at the grip, the shape of it
unreadable. It is the book's first image and its last act, it carries the whole engine, it depicts nobody,
and it names nothing. Palette warm and flat rather than cold: the light in this book is pleasant and the
cover must not promise horror. Type light and generous.

**Option 2 — the rows.**
A horizon of hands at work, no faces, no place name, nobody looking up. Stronger atmosphere; harder to
make legible at thumbnail scale, and at small size it risks reading as generic dystopia, which is the one
genre impression this book should not give.

**Option 3 — the pair.**
The two-figures composition, legitimate under series canon and now carrying a second layer: Merope
holding something, Achird with her hands empty. That single difference is the book. **Higher recognition
with the light-novel readership; worse for the horror-adjacent reader**, who will read the cover promise
differently from what the book delivers.

**Series note.** Book 1's recommended first-edition cover was an object (the button) and *not* the
figures. If that choice holds, the **object cover is the series' visual identity** and Book 2's cover
should be an object too — which makes Option 1 not just a cover but a consistent line.

**Front matter:** working title and series line, both still unfilled by the author's decision.

These are ~150-word openings written to show how each proposed engine would *feel* in Merope's voice,
so the engine can be chosen by reading rather than by description. They are not chapter drafts, they
are not filed as chapters, they have not been run through `deslop-check.sh`, and **none of them is a
commitment to anything.** If an engine is selected, its sample is discarded and chapter 1 is drafted
properly against the contract.

Voice rules in force for all three: Merope narrates in retrospective past with present-tense asides;
she performs and admits to performing; questions are punctuation-free in dialogue; Achird gets almost
nothing and it is never nothing.

## Engine A — "the work"

> The first thing anybody said to me on that world was a job.
>
> Not to me, exactly. They were saying it to each other, over a rail, and there were nine of them and
> none of them looked up, and every one of them was being useful. I have never been useful. I have
> been admired, which is a different shape of the same thing and pays better.
>
> There was no welcome. That is what I noticed first, because I had spent the last of my time on a
> world that welcomed me so thoroughly it nearly killed me for it, and here the door was open and
> nobody was standing in it.
>
> A woman handed me a length of rope.
>
> "It needs carrying," she said. "It has needed carrying all morning."

## Engine B — "the audience"

> They knew the coat story.
>
> I want to be careful here, because a person can sound ungrateful and I am not ungrateful. They knew
> the coat story. They knew the winter I was famous. I walked into a square the size of a market and
> somebody at the back stood up and said my name, and then a great many people said it, and it was
> the correct name, and I have never in my life been more certain that I was about to be
> disappointed.
>
> The version they had of me was better than mine. That is the part I have not worked out how to say
> yet. They had the coat, they had the joke at the end of the winter, they had the line about the
> door I was not supposed to open. They had all of it polished, and none of it was mine any more, and
> every one of them was delighted to meet me.
>
> Achird said, "You wanted this."
>
> "I know what I asked for."

## Engine C — "the record"

> They keep a record. Everyone who comes through, everything they did, everything they said, written
> down, permanent, in a building with a roof on it.
>
> They showed me the shelf before they showed me the room. I have a lot of practice at shelves now.
> This one was different. There were no objects on it. There were books, and the books had names on
> the spines, and one of the names was mine.
>
> I have been telling my own story for eleven years, and I have been telling it in the wrong voice,
> and I have known that for a while and done it anyway. It has never bothered me. That is not
> modesty, it is the arrangement I have with the reader.
>
> A young man took mine down and opened it at the beginning and held it out to me, and he was so
> pleased, and he had no idea what he was handing me.

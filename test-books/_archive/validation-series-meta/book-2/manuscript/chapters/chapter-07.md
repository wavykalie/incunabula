I gave the work back in the morning and it did not take long.

There is a place at the near end of the rows where the tools go. It is not a store and it is not a shelf and I am not going to make it one. It is a low place in the ground where a thing can be laid so that the next person walking the row will see it, and that is where I took it.

I want the account of that to be as short as it was, because I have seen leavings in rooms and I know how I usually tell them, and this one does not deserve any of that.

I carried it down the outside of the first row. My arm had stopped being surprised by the weight days ago. I put it in the low place in the ground, and I stood there for a moment the way a person stands when she is waiting to be told she has done something.

Nobody told me anything.

A woman came past within the minute and took it up without looking at me, the way the man had taken it up on the first afternoon when I put it down to see what would happen. She fitted it into her own grip, which was not the same grip as mine and did not need to be, and she carried it back into the rows, and that was the whole of my leaving of it.

I had held it for eleven days.

I want to be exact about the number, since I kept it for a reason. Eleven days and I could not tell you one thing about it, and I do not mean the purpose. I mean the thing itself. Whether it was heavy at the start, whether it had a seam, whether the shape of it had ever changed in my hands. I could not tell you. I held it the way you hold a conversation you are winning, and at the end of it I could not have described the person across from me.

The man with the two hands was working the join when I went to find him.

I told him we were going. I did not dress it up and I did not make it a speech, and I want that in, because it was the first time in my life I have ever told a room where I was going before I went.

He stopped what he was doing. He did not stop for long.

“We’ll be poorer,” he said.

That was the second time he had said it and it was about me this time, and I want it on the record that it was said in exactly the same tone, which is to say with no weight on it at all and no request inside it. He was not asking me to stay. He was not making me feel anything. He was telling me a fact about the rows, and the fact was true, and he gave it to me the way he gave me everything, which is because I had been standing there.

I asked him if he minded.

He thought about it while he picked the join back up.

“No,” he said. “It isn’t yours.”

I have thought about that sentence longer than anything else anyone has ever said to me. I do not think he was talking about the work. I think he meant it as the whole of what he had, which was that a person can be needed somewhere and not belong to it, and that he had made peace with that a long time before I arrived and had never once needed an audience for the making of it.

Then he went back to the join. He did not watch me leave. I want that written the way it happened, because a person telling this afterwards would want him to look up. He did not, and the not looking up is the truest thing in the chapter and the thing I came here to be able to write down. He had already given me the only thing he had.

The turn was watched.

I went to it because I had a thing I wanted, and I am not going to dress that one up either. I wanted to stand where the woman had stood who would not tell me anything about herself, and ask her, at last, the plain question, and let her decide whether to answer it.

She was not there.

The new woman was at the turn.

She watched it the way it wants to be watched, which is to say the same way the other one had, and I could not have told you the difference if I had not known there was one. That is not a fact about the new woman. It is the whole truth about the turn.

I did not ask her anything either.

I stood where I was not needed and watched the rail with her for a while, which is a thing I have never once done for anybody in my life. She let me. Nothing came of it and nothing was asked of me, and after a while I understood that I had walked the whole length of the rows to collect a thing I had declined to collect when it had been standing in front of me, and that this was the habit I was carrying out of that place and not the tool.

I do not know the first woman’s name. I had four days beside her and never once asked, and by the time I wanted to know it she had walked down the outside of the rail with her own weight on her own feet, and the new woman had the turn, and there was nobody left who would have used it.

Nobody came to the edge of the rows to see us off.

I want to be exact about that, because it is the part that people will get wrong when they repeat it. It was not a slight. It was the same fact as the first afternoon. Nobody on that planet stops for anything, and a leaving is a thing, and they do not stop for things.

The work went on behind us as we walked to the sleigh. I could hear the rows the whole way, and the sound was the same as it had been on the first evening when I stood outside the cabin not knowing I was already in it. It did not change for me on the way out either.

That is what it cost. I want to be careful, because the other book taught me that a leaving can be made to look like nothing, and this one was not that.

This one cost. They were poorer. He had said so and he did not say things he did not mean, and the row I had been standing in would carry one less hand from the middle of that morning onwards, and the work would not slow and would not stop and would not acknowledge it, and that was the true bill and it was paid by people who had never once asked me for anything.

I did not pay it. That is the part I keep.

I did not pay it. Nobody made me. Nobody was ever going to. I have decided that the reason leaving is easy on that planet is that it has arranged itself so that the cost always lands somewhere other than the person going.

Achird was in the sleigh already. She had walked the edge of the rows one last time while I was at the turn, and she had not told me, and she did not tell me now.

I asked her, the way I always ask it, whether she had found anything out there.

She thought about it. She always thinks about it, and she has never once given me a yes or a no.

“The join holds,” she said.

I am not going to explain what I think that meant. It is three words and I have had a long time with them and I am keeping them.

I sat forward and put my hand on the sill.

I am going to do this part properly, since it is the last time I get to.

The runners found the ground, and then did not. We went up past the rows and past the near end where arrivals come in, where I had stood on the first day being the least interesting thing in the world. The shape came off the sky and went on about its own road and then came back when we needed it, and the world got small under the old glass, and the rows went on being rows all the way to the edge of what I could see.

She flies it. She has always flown it. I narrate as though I do, and I have said so before in another account and I am saying it again on purpose, because the one thing I want on the record about the whole of this is that I have finally learned which half of a thing is mine.

Then it was the two of us in a cabin with old glass in it, going somewhere.

I want to describe what I did next without making it larger than it was, because it was small and it stayed small and that is the entire point of it.

I had spent eleven days leaving openings shaped exactly like something she could say. I had walked off so she would have to speak to my back. I had built questions with the answers already in them and stood on the answers before she could reach them. I had done everything I know how to do, which is everything, and at the end of it she had said five words about tomorrow and I had heard more in them than in anything anyone has ever said to me.

So I stopped.

I did not ask her anything. I did not leave a space for her to fill. I did not set her a thing to pass and then stand near the result.

I said the plain thing.

I am going to put it down exactly as it was, with nothing on either side of it, because that is how it came out.

“I know,” I said.

That is all of it. Two words, no run-up, no question inside them, nothing for her to answer and nothing for her to fail. I had known for eleven days and for a lot longer than eleven days, and I had spent all of it making her carry it, and then I said it and it was mine to say and I said it looking out through the glass.

She did not say it back.

I did not expect her to and I did not want her to and I want to be honest about that, since honesty is the only thing I have that nobody gave me. If she had said it back I would have had to do something with it. I would have had to make it a room and be charming in it and hand it to whoever else was standing there. What she did was nothing, and the nothing had a shape, and I have finally learned to read it.

She looked at where we were going, which is what she was paid to do and what she has always done, and she did not answer, and after a while her not answering stopped being a silence and became a thing that was simply the case.

We went a long way.

I sat with my hand on the sill and did not talk, which is not a thing I do, and the shape held in front of us the way shapes hold, which is to say slowly and out of reach and exactly where it had always been.

I want to end on something I can still see, because this is the last page I have and I have spent the whole of it telling you that I have never once missed anything I wanted to see.

So here it is. Behind us and below us, going small, there was a place where every single person is needed all the time by something that does not know their name, and it went on being that while I watched it, and it did not get smaller in any way that mattered. It got further away. There is a difference and I could not have told you what it was before.

I had been needed there. I had never been wanted there, not once, by any part of it. I had spent eleven days being delighted by the arrangement.

I gave it back.

I am not sorry about any of it. I was needed somewhere for the first time in my life and it was endless, and I put it down in a low place in the ground and walked out of the rows, and there was a girl in the sleigh whose eyes were on the road and who had been there the whole time, and out in front of us the dark went on being dark, and the dark is the same dark everywhere.

I know how the woman who left got here. I am going to give it to you backwards, because backwards is how it came to me, and because the front of the account is the part I did not hear until last and the part I would have got wrong.

So the first thing I am going to tell you is the end of it, which is that she is gone.

That is where the account finishes. A woman walks down the outside of the rail with her own weight on her own feet, and a new woman takes the turn before the hour is out, and the rail does not slow.

That is the end of the account. Now backwards.

Before she stood at the turn she spent a season looking for the turn.

I did not get this from her. She was the one who would not tell me anything, who said there was no way to say it that did not sound like a complaint. I got it from the man with the two hands, who told it the way he told everything, in pieces, and who did not consider it a confidence, because nothing here is.

She had come in at the near end of the rows, where arrivals come in. Nobody meets arrivals. She stood there for part of a day and nobody asked her what she was there for, and she did not leave, because there is nowhere at the near end to leave to. So she walked the rows and watched what was needed.

That took a season. A season is a long time to walk a row with nothing in your hands. She was looked at, the way anybody with nothing in their hands is looked at here, which is not much and not unkindly, and people moved their work around her, and nobody offered her anything.

I want to put this next part in even though it is the part I have no business knowing. She started carrying things for people who were carrying things. Not for a part. Not asked. Relieving a hand here and there so that somebody could reach for something with both. That is the sort of thing I have never once done in my life, and I want that in the account, because it is how she got her part and it is not how I got mine.

Then somebody’s hand needed relieving at the turn, and it became a habit, and then it became a part.

That is the whole mechanism of arrival here. There is no welcome. There is a need, and you are near it, and if you stay near it long enough it becomes yours.

Now before that. Before the season of walking.

Before that there was a door.

This is the part the man did tell me, because the woman had told him, and because he did not know there was any reason not to. It was the door of a room in a place that had been hers. I am not going to give you the rest of the room. He did not have the rest of the room. What he had was this: that the room had belonged to her and then it had belonged to somebody else and she had gone on being in it, and she had gone on being welcome in it. Every part of that sentence was true at the same time. None of it was a cruelty anyone had committed.

I have been in that room. I want that on the record. I have stood in a room that was no longer mine and been charming in it, which is the skill I have, and I have never once got out of it with anything.

Before the door there was a person.

Not a fight. He did not have a fight in it and he was not holding one back. There was a person, and for a while the person turned towards her the way a face turns, and then no longer did, and there was no moment in it that either of them could point at, which is the detail that makes the account unusable for a stage and entirely true.

Before the person there was no story at all. There was a woman who had not yet been anywhere, and the man with the two hands did not know anything about her, and neither does she, by now.

And here is what I understood at the end of the telling, which arrived in me the way a piece of work arrives, which is to say that I had been carrying the parts of it for six days and then suddenly I could see the shape.

Everyone here came because somebody stopped wanting them.

Not one dramatic case. Not one place that throws people out. A door that closed, a person whose face turned away, a room that stopped being yours while you were still standing in it, and then a place where nobody wants anything except for the work, and the work never stops wanting.

I am going to be exact about how this felt, because I have been exact about everything else and this is the part where a person writing afterwards would reach for the large word and the large word would be wrong.

It did not feel like horror. It felt like arithmetic. Whatever had happened to all of them had happened slowly, and none of it was done to them, and every single one of them had walked in at the near end of the rows with their own weight on their own feet, and the whole thing added up the way the light thins, without one moment you could point at and call the moment.

Now the second half, which is the part the man with the two hands did not say and could not have said, and which I am going to put down because I worked it out and I have never worked anything out before in a way that mattered.

They do not mind.

I want to be careful with that sentence. It is not a lie they are telling themselves. It is not numbness, and it is not waiting. I have watched for all of those in the way I watch for anything I want to be true, and I could not find one of them in a single person.

They have stopped minding. It took a long time and they did it honestly, and what they have instead is the work.

It is real. I want that as well. The man with the two hands is not pretending to like his part, and the woman checking the tops was not pretending either, and the light on that place is not a trick and the bread is not a trick.

The need is real. The only thing that is not there is the wanting.

I looked for the crack in it for two days, and I am going to report that I found one, because a person who reports only the clean parts is not reporting.

I found it in the man with the two hands, on the sixth morning, while he was telling me about the door he had fixed twice. He told it the way he told everything, which is plainly and without asking for anything. Then, at the end of it, he did one thing that was not the same as the way he told everything. He put his hand flat on the thing he was carrying and left it there for a moment longer than the work needed.

That is all of it. That is the whole of the crack. He is not over anything and he would tell you so, and his hand stayed where it was for a beat and a half longer than the work wanted, and I have thought about that beat more than I have thought about the shelf and the camp and everything else I saw on the other planet put together.

I walked back to the sleigh and told Achird all of it.

I want to be exact about this too, because it is the longest thing I said to her in the whole of that book and I have gone over every sentence of it.

I told her the pattern. I gave her the door and the room and the face that stopped turning, and I gave her the season of walking, and I said that I had worked out what the place was and what it was for.

Then I told her why it did not apply to me.

I said that I had not walked in at the near end of the rows with nothing. I said I had come with a shape in the sky and a coat story and a whole account of myself, and that I had been wanted in better rooms than that one, and that what had happened to all of them was that they had run out and been given somewhere to go, and that I had not run out, I had simply arrived.

I said it kindly. I said it at length. I said it looking at the rows rather than at her, because I could not have said it while looking at her, and I do not know to this day whether that was shame or craft.

And I said the last of it, which is the part I would take back, and it was this: that it was a good thing for the place that I had come, because a place like that needs somebody in it who was not running from anything, and that I would be the one who was here because she wanted to be.

Achird did not argue.

She did not say the true thing, which I have since worked out is that I had walked in at a near end of exactly the same kind and had a tool put in my hands within four minutes of standing on the ground, which is the fastest anyone there had ever been given a part, and which I have never once mentioned to anybody before now.

She did not say any of it.

She looked at me for a moment longer than the sentence needed. That is a thing she does and I have had years to understand it and I have never got anywhere with it.

Then she said, “All right,” and she went and sat in the sleigh, and she did not say another thing about it. Not that night. Not the next day. She has never said another thing about it, and I have looked, and I am still looking.

I lay down that night with the tool beside me and I went over what I had told her and I found it good. I want to be honest about that. I was pleased with it. I had taken a set of true facts and arranged them so that I came out of the arrangement standing higher than the people in it, which is a skill I have had my whole life and have been thanked for in nicer places than that.

I had never once turned it on a person I was standing next to.

I slept well. I want that in, because it is the fact of the chapter and it is worse than any of the others. I told the only person I have ever had a true thing to say to a set of facts arranged to keep me above her, and then I slept the whole night through.

In the morning the tool was where I had left it, and I picked it up, and I did not check whether she was watching, and that was the first time I had not checked.

I want this in one place, because it did not happen in one place.

It happened across an evening and most of a night, in the sleigh and on the ground and along the outside of the rows, and I am going to give it to you as one thing, because that is what it was.

I had been at it for days.

I want to tell you what it looked like from inside, since I have been honest about everything else and this is the part I have never once admitted to a room.

I left openings.

Every conversation for a week had a space in it shaped exactly like something she could have said. I put them there. I put them in and stood next to them and waited, and when nothing came I filled them myself with something else and went on, and I told myself each time that I had not done anything.

I did the walking off.

Not far. Never far. Far enough that a person who wanted to say a thing would have to say it to my back, which is a hard thing to do and which I have needed to hear for years.

And I asked my questions with the answer already inside them, and stood on the answer before she could get to it.

I want to give you one of them properly, because a list makes it sound like weather and it was not weather. It was work, and I was good at it.

There was an evening in the middle of the week when I walked off.

Achird was at the end of the rail with her hand in her pocket, watching the turn the way she watches everything. I said something to her about the light. I do not remember the sentence. I remember what I did with it, which was to say half of it and then start walking.

I walked the whole length of the outside of the first row.

That is not far. It is a long way to walk in front of a person who is supposed to have something to say to you, and I did it at the speed of somebody who is not waiting, which is the slowest speed there is.

She did not follow.

I do not know to this day whether she did not want to or did not think of it. I have decided it was the second one, and I have decided that for the reason people decide things, which is that I prefer it.

When I reached the end I turned and came back and sat on the rail beside her, and she said, “You went a long way.”

“I wanted to see the far turn.”

“There is no far turn,” she said, and she was right, and she had known I was lying since the first step.

That is one of them. I am not going to give you the rest. There were six or seven and every one was built like it, which is to say with a door in it and no handle on her side, and every one was received the same way, which is that it was not received at all, and I went on building them because I did not know what else a person does with a thing she cannot say.

There was a morning I asked her whether she would be at the sleigh that evening.

It sounds like nothing. It was built like the rest of them. The answer I wanted had nothing to do with the sleigh and everything to do with whether she would be somewhere I could find her, which is a question I have never once asked anybody directly and am not going to start.

“Yes,” she said.

I waited for the rest of it. There was no rest of it. She had answered the actual question, which she always does, and the actual question had been about the sleigh, and I stood there holding an answer that had arrived on time to a place I had not been standing.

And underneath all of it, in the part of me I do not look at, there had been one question. I had been circling it the way you circle a part you have not been given yet.

She had not taken any of it up.

Not once.

I had done everything I know how to do, which is everything, and she had stood in the middle of it being exactly what she has always been, and the openings had stayed openings, and I had started to think about what it meant that they stayed.

So on the last evening I said the large thing.

I am going to put it in straight, without the run-up, because there was no run-up in it. I walked back from the rows with the tool and she was at the front of the sleigh and the light had gone down through everything, and I stood there a moment, and then I said it.

“I’m staying.”

I want to be exact. I had not decided it. That is the part I would fix if I could. I cannot, and I am not going to pretend I can. I said it because it was the largest piece of iron I had left in the fire and because saying it would make her do something.

It was the last test. I knew it was the last test while I was setting it, which is the difference between this and the others.

She did not move.

I have stood next to that silence for years. It has a shape and I know the shape. This one was longer, and at the end of it I understood that she was not refusing me. She was reading.

Then she said it.

“Then I’ll take a part.”

One sentence. Five words. Her largest moment in the whole of that book, and she spent it on a fact about tomorrow.

I want to tell you what I understood, because I understood all of it at once, the way you understand a turn when you are standing on it.

There was a woman at the turn on that planet who spent a season walking the rows with nothing in her hands and then started relieving people’s hands and then had a part, and it took a season and nobody asked her to and nobody refused her, and that is how everybody there arrived, and I had told Achird the whole of that account the night before, out loud, at length, above her.

Achird had just told me she was going to start.

Not for a part. She was going to stand there with nothing in her hands and wait until somebody’s hand needed relieving, and then it would become a habit, and then it would become hers.

She would be needed here. Not wanted, which is what she is to me and has always been. Needed, by a piece of work that does not know who is holding it.

And she would do it because I was standing there.

That is the answer the test was never built to take. I had spent a week arranging questions so that whatever she said, I would be standing on it, and she had answered a question I did not have an arrangement for.

I said the cruel thing.

I have to put this in and I am going to put it in flat, because dressing it up would be another performance and there has been enough of those.

I said that she had never been needed by anybody, which was true. I said that she did not even know what it was, which was true. I said that this was why she had spent the whole time at the edge of the rows with her hand in her pocket, watching somebody else being useful, which was true.

It came out well. I said it the way I say things I have been rehearsing without knowing I was rehearsing them, and the sentences got longer and better as I went, and then I landed on the short one, and the short one was aimed at the person in front of me, and I knew it was true while I was saying it.

I want to be clear about that last part, since it is the part I am least able to explain.

I knew it was true. That is the whole of the cruelty. It is not that I said something I did not believe. It is that I said something I did believe, to her, on purpose, at the exact moment it would cost her most, and I did it because she had just shown me a future I could not look at and I had no other way to get her to stop.

She did not answer.

She stood in the middle of it the way she stands in the middle of everything, with her weight on both feet, and she took it, all of it, and she did not take one step back and she did not raise anything and she did not leave.

She waited for me to finish.

I want that in as its own line, because it is the mirror of the whole other book and I have thought about it more than anything.

She waited for me to finish and then she did not say a word about it, and I stood there with the tool in my hands and the whole of it finished, and there was nothing left in me to say, which is not a thing that has happened to me in front of another person in my life.

So I said the other thing.

“We’re going.”

I want to be exact, because I have been building to this and I am not going to waste it.

I said we were going. Not her. I did not ask her. I did not tell her she was right. I did not explain and I did not give a reason, and there was no moment where I looked at her and agreed.

The words were mine and I made them true, which is a thing I have never once done in any of the rooms I have left. In every other leaving of my life I have been the one who was carried out of it, or the one who woke up somewhere else, or the one somebody else decided about.

I had spent a week making her carry every question between us, and at the end of it the sentence was mine.

And I did not put the tool down.

That is the last thing and it is the truest one. The words were out. I was holding it. I did not set it on the ground and I did not hand it to anybody.

I went into the sleigh holding it, because I had not worked out yet how to give something back that was not mine, and because there was a part of me, standing behind all of the rest of me, that had not yet agreed to leave.

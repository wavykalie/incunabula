## The book

**MARROW LIGHT** (working title; nothing is named until it is drafted, per the project's
title rule).

A speleologist, thirty-eight, whose last survey ended in a collapse that killed her
partner and ended her career in the field. She now sells technical equipment to people
who go into holes, which is the same work with none of it.

A film-school student, twenty-two, who has been hired — as a favour, badly paid, over
her supervisor's objection — to shoot a documentary about the cave. She is the first
person in four years to ask the speleologist to go back underground, and she asks for
reasons that are mostly about the film.

Neither can do this alone. The cave needs two people on separate ends of a line, and so
does the film, and by the third chapter everyone involved has stopped being able to tell
which arrangement they are in.

## Why this shape

The three spent premises are all investigative: a nonfiction investigation, an industrial
accident investigation, a fire-origin certification. The engine here is not a question to
be answered but a descent to be survived, and the relationship is the engine rather than
the thing the engine generates.

The pilot (`muzzle-and-marrow`) was an ensemble fantasy with six speaking characters and a
quest. This is a two-hander, which is the shape most likely to fail the Blind Sort test
and therefore the most useful thing to run the next book through. Dialogue density also
puts `U4` — advisory by default, and the row calibrated on the commercial-dialogue
market — under real load for the first time.

## Declared for the gates

- **Convention:** `commercial-dialogue`. Two-hander romance, dialogue-dense by
  construction. This is an explicit opt-in and reinstates `U4` as enforced. Declared
  before drafting, not after a failure.
- **Positioning:** full-length commercial romance. Not literary, not novella.
- **Scale:** ~400 print pages, ~100,000-112,000 words, ~40-45 chapters. Extended
  2026-09-29 from an initial 10-chapter plan. A yuri romance at this length is ordinary
  for the genre, so this is a normal book at a normal length rather than a short one
  stretched. It is also 1.4x the largest manuscript in the project (`demo-magician`,
  77,352 words) and the first book here with enough chapters to test a drafting
  prediction - see `PREDICTION.md`.
- **No length targets in any drafting document.** Per the framework's standing rule, now
  a convention rather than a finding. The word counts above describe the SHAPE of the
  book for the gates and the publisher; they are not per-chapter instructions, and no
  chapter is written to a number.
- **U1 expectation:** `CV` is recorded, not optimised. The 12.1% the pilot scored is the
  expected output of a book written this way, not a defect. See `DRAFTING_FRAMEWORK.md`.

# OUTLINE — *Marrow Light* (working title)

`artifacts/05-outline.md`. Phase 2 output.

**No length targets appear in this document, and there are no planned word counts per
chapter.** See `incunabula/DRAFTING_FRAMEWORK.md`. A quota is a plan for where the masses
go. Chapters end when the thing they are about has been finished.

**The book-level length contract lives in `PROJECT_STATE.yaml` and nowhere else.** The
~400-page / ~100,000–112,000 word / 40–45 chapter figures in `premise.md` describe the
SHAPE of the book for the gates and the publisher. They are not per-chapter instructions
and no chapter is written to a number.

**Each row carries a structural approach, and no two rows in a row share one.** That is the
defence against a two-hander collapsing into the same conversation over and over, and it
is deliberately *not* a length distribution. It is a defence against a book that keeps
rebuilding the same room.

**Rows, not chapters.** Read the table as forty-five distinct *moves*. The chapter
boundaries fall between them and are decided by what finishes — see Amendment 1. Four
pairs are joined, giving **41 chapters**, and that figure is an output, not a target.

## AMENDMENT 1 — the chapter count was a quota, and it is withdrawn

**Recorded 2026-09-29, before Chapter 3 was written.** Requested by the author, who pointed
out that the book did not have to be forty-five chapters and that long chapters are fine.

**The 45 was not derived from the shape of anything.** It was the 100,000-word length
contract divided by a number that felt like a chapter, and then defended after the fact by
the structural-variety table below — which is a *real* constraint, applied to an arbitrary
number. That is backwards. `DRAFTING_FRAMEWORK.md` is unambiguous: *chapters are not sized,
they end*, and a chapter boundary that exists to hit a count is a quota chapter boundary
wearing a structural costume.

**What changes.** Not the number of *scenes* — all forty-five rows below are still written,
still distinct, and still the book's actual moves. What changes is how many of them are
called chapters. Four pairs of adjacent rows turn out to be **one scene that got split
because forty-five was the target**, and they are joined:

| joined | why it is one scene, not two |
|---|---|
| 15 + 16 | same line, same shift, *Not Today* is the tail of the conversation. They were never apart. |
| 20 + 21 | the 1994 tape, watched. *The Other Woman's Film* is the second half of the first viewing, not a separate event. |
| 26 + 27 | the reading proved wrong, and Ines leaving the room to go to work. One movement, not a chapter and its aftermath. |
| 33 + 34 | the number on the plate, and Fabiola's invoice dates arriving to confirm it. **This is the most consequential join in the book** and it is correct: the reader was going to work out the arithmetic the moment the second number landed, and a chapter break there is a held breath at the wrong place. |

**45 − 4 = 41 chapters.** The count that results is a *consequence* of the scenes, not an
input to them, which is the only order in which a number is allowed to exist here.

**What does not change.** The four block-ending turns, the tension map, the opening
strategy, the emotional curve's block structure, and the entire per-chapter note set. The
emotional curve is tracked **by block and by row**, not by chapter number, precisely so
that this amendment would not require rewriting it. `artifacts/06-emotional-curve.md` and
`ENTITY_STATE.yaml` both reference row numbers and are unaffected.

**The standing rule for the drafting sessions that follow.** Rows are the unit of
planning. Chapters are whatever finishes. If a row runs to four thousand words it is four
thousand words, and if two rows finish inside one scene they are one chapter, and neither
event is a failure of discipline or a triumph over it. **Forty-one is the current
expectation and is not a target.** If the book finishes at thirty-six it finishes at
thirty-six.

## MACRO STRUCTURE

**Genre of the shape:** a descent, told through a relationship that the descent is
physically preventing.

**Central conflict:** *Ines will not be filmed, and Ottoline cannot make the film without
her.* Everything else in the book — the permit, the crew, the funding, the descent, the
weather — is a structure built around that single locked door.

**Turn that ends each block:**

| block | ends because | chapter |
|---|---|---|
| I — Terms | the arrangement is made and it works, which is worse than it failing | 9 |
| II — The Line | the professional arrangement has become the reason neither can stop | 19 |
| III — The Archive | the past turns out to be *evidence* — a reading, in frame, from 1994 | 28 |
| IV — Provenance | everybody who needs to know knows, and nobody can stop the descent | 37 |
| V — The Water | nobody learns anything, and the water comes up on a cycle | 45 |

**Escalation logic.** Pressure compounds. It does not restart. Each block costs the book
something it cannot get back: Block I costs Ines the ability to keep the two situations
apart. Block II costs both of them the ability to go up. Block III costs Ines the
narrative she has been living inside — the *cave took it* version. Block IV costs the
reader any remaining belief that the book will be merciful. Block V costs Ottoline the
last thing she is holding, which is the belief that documenting a thing is the same as
understanding it.

**Tension map.**

    ch  1-9   ▁▂▃▃▂▁▂▃▅      terms, settling, then the controlled drop
    ch 10-19   ▆▇▇█▇▇█▇▇      the line — the book's highest sustained register
    ch 20-28   ▇▅▃▁▁▁▂▄      THE ARCHIVE — the valley, then the evidence
    ch 29-37   ▄▅▇███▇▆█      the sentence assembled, everyone's strong
    ch 38-45   ▇▇█▇▇▇▆▅      no more information; the water

The single highest point in the book is **chapter 19**, and it is a professional
achievement. That is on purpose. The romance peaks before the midpoint so that the rest of
the book can be about taking something away from two people who are good at their jobs.

## PER-CHAPTER NOTES

**1. "Buying the Wrong Thing"** — *cold open.*
A Tuesday in the shop. Ines sells a full rack to a man who has clearly not been underground
in ten years, watches him fail to lace it correctly, and does not say anything, and then
does. The competence is unbearable and the reader has not been told where it comes from.
Fabiola is behind the counter and the two of them have a rhythm that is nineteen years old
and contains one thing neither of them mentions.

*The chapter's engine is a small professional cruelty that Ines commits out of reflex and
would take back by the end of it.* Ottoline is not in this chapter. She arrives at the end,
in the doorway, in weather, holding a proposal she has rehearsed badly.

*Ends on the question — the stranger in the rain, not the sale.*

**2. "Terms"** — *document.*
The agreement, laid out. Film access, survey support, insurance, the licence, the
acknowledgement of risk, and the clause neither of them has read. This is a chapter made
of paper and it is the chapter that decides the book: **two competent people sign a thing
that has one ambiguous clause in it, and the ambiguity is the romance.**

The clause is about material neither party owns. Neither of them notices. The reader might
catch it, and the book does not underline it, and it is not the same ambiguity as chapter
26. Two ambiguities, one legal, one hydraulic.

*Ends on Ottoline's copy of the document, alone, and one underlined word.*

**3. "The Stockroom"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
The first real conversation, conducted standing up, in a stockroom, with a kettle neither
of them offers the other. Ottoline has come to negotiate and is at a disadvantage the
whole time and covers it with speed. Ines is at a disadvantage she is pretending is a
choice.

**The chapter's real content is that Ines agrees to be *interviewed* and explicitly refuses
everything else**, and does it in a way that is generous rather than defensive, which
destroys Ottoline's whole plan for the next thirty chapters and she knows it within a page
and says nothing.

*Ends on the refusal being agreed and both women being pleased about it for opposite
reasons.*

**4. "Eleven Minutes"** — *POV shift, Hana alone.*
The only chapter in Block I with no Ines and no Ottoline.

Hana Vogel is a rescue technician eleven kilometres from a cave she is not going into
today. She is good at her job. She has been not-taking a call from Ines Varga for four
years and has a system for it, and the system is that she is always just about to do
something.

*This chapter is quiet and it is tense, and the withholding is a professional matter with a
four-year edge on it.* It ends when Hana takes the call she has been not taking, and the
call is about a student with a camera.

**5. "Two Applications"** — *two threads, cut.*
The permit application and the film grant application, cut between them, both nearly dead,
both for reasons that are entirely mundane and would be invisible to a reader who had not
been given a professional world.

Marisol Trent's no is in writing, and it is *courteous*, and it is nine paragraphs long,
and paragraph six is the one that matters and it is about liability.

*Ends on the no.*

**6. "Renn and Daughters"** — *rest / no plot pressure.*
A slow day. A customer who returns a probe. Ines and Fabiola alone in the shop for the
first time in the book, and the chapter has no plot pressure at all and is the quietest
chapter in Block I.

**And it is the chapter in which Fabiola says the sentence Ines has been waiting four
years for**, which is not *I knew* and not *I forgave you* and is closer to a piece of
invoicing. Neither woman acknowledges that it has happened. The chapter does not end on
it; it ends on the shop closing, which is the correct and much crueller choice.

**7. "The Professional"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
Tove Marsk and Ottoline Marrow, alone, and Tove is exactly as bad as advertised: funny,
unimpressed, technically immaculate, and completely uninterested in the film.

She is not rude. She is *specific*, which is worse, because by the end of the chapter
Ottoline has been told precisely what a good survey looks like and has realised she cannot
tell the difference.

*Ends on Tove asking what Ottoline's own work is about, and not being satisfied with the
answer, and the question landing anyway.*

**8. "Recon"** — *wide, moving fast.*
Four people and a bad day. Tove's team, a logistics problem, a van, a road, a weather
window that is not going to be as wide as the paperwork promised. Nobody in this chapter is
mysterious. Everyone is competent and one of them is exhausted.

**The chapter's function is to introduce Tove as a working professional rather than a
foil**, and it does it by having her be the best person in the room. It ends when the
survey is cleared for a real descent, which is the chapter's question got answered.

**9. "Fifty Metres"** — *set piece.*
The recon. Ines alone, familiar passage, a look at something that has to be looked at. The
chapter is written in the working register — kit, times, hands, cold, the radio — and it is
the first time the reader is inside the cave with somebody, and it is **completely
uneventful.**

Nothing goes wrong. This is the most important chapter in Block I. The reader relaxes, and
the relaxation is the point, and there is a small beat at the end where she comes up and
does not thank anyone.

*Ends when she comes up.*

**10. "The Morning After"** — *aftermath / plain.*
The day after nothing happened. The debrief, which is short because there is nothing to
debrief. The film gets scheduled — the funding window, the academic calendar, the
insurance — and both women discover that the arrangement has become load-bearing in about
nine days and neither of them can remember agreeing to it.

*Ends on the schedule.*

**11. "The Line"** — *set piece.*
The line goes live. Fifty metres of wool between a woman in a hole and a woman on a
headland with a monitor and a hundred and forty hours of tape.

The chapter establishes the arrangement that the next eight chapters run on: **one of them
is always below and one of them is always above, and neither of them can leave.** It ends
when Ines answers a question she was not asked.

**12. "Incident 2022/114"** — *document.*
The report, read aloud, in a room, by someone with a legal reason to read it aloud. This is
the chapter that tells the reader the collapse was the water (WR-003 — knowable from the
start, and nothing turns on the reader not knowing it).

**The chapter's discipline is that it is boring and complete.** It is read aloud. It has a
number in it. Nobody in the room reacts, because everyone in the room already knew.

*Ends on the water.*

**13. "Forty-One"** — *POV shift, Tove alone.*
Tove Marsk and the current Vell contract, and a document she has read forty-one times, and
the specific ugliness of a professional who wants the answer more than she wants the film.

She is not a villain and she does not have a private stake in Solveig Havelock. **Her
stake is that the answer is a *finding*, and a finding is a professional good, and she
would like to have it and she is ashamed of how much she would like it.** This is the
chapter that gets read as the interruption it is, and it is the chapter the reader comes
back from differently.

*Ends on Tove offering to go first on the line — which is a gift, and is presented as a
scheduling decision.*

**14. "Same Minute"** — *two threads, cut.*
Surface and hole, one minute at a time, two problems. The hole has a survey problem. The
surface has a film problem, and the film problem is that the good stuff happened off-camera
and the camera was on a rope for nine minutes.

*Ends when Ottoline says, on the record, that the footage is not good enough, and means
it, and has never said anything so honest to Ines.*

**15. "What Water Sounds Like"** — *set piece, warm.*
**The highest beat in the book.**

Ines, fifty metres down, describing the way water sounds on a particular kind of rock.
Long. Specific. Unasked. There is no functional reason for this and neither of them can
later defend it, and it is the first time in the novel that either woman has said anything
at length that nobody requested.

There is no kiss and there is no confession. There is a woman underground talking to a
woman on a monitor about a noise, for a length of time that is embarrassing.

*Ends on neither of them hanging up.*

**16. "Not Today"** — *withheld.*
A chapter in which the thing that was spent is not spent.

Ines is asked, on camera, for the first time, about Solveig. She does not lie. **She
declines**, and the decline is graceful and complete and involves no false statement, which
is the most technically impressive thing the character ever does and the most infuriating.

Ottoline does not push. The not-pushing is worse than pushing and the chapter knows it.

*Ends on Ines going up, and the silence on the line afterwards, and the fact that neither
of them has mentioned it.*

**17. "About Your Mother"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
Not underground. A car park, then a café, then the same car park. The first thing said
between them that is not professional.

**Ines asks Ottoline a question about Vera Marrow**, which is the first time the book
reveals that Ines has already read the 1994 material, and Ottoline's answer is a lie, and
it is a bad one, and Ines lets her have it.

*Ends with both of them knowing the other has just lied and neither of them able to use
it.*

**18. "The Window"** — *chase / pressure.*
A weather window closing. Gear moving across country, a film crew on the wrong road, a
permit with a date on it that nobody wants to be the person who missed.

This is a logistics chapter and it is the first time the reader feels the *machine* of the
book — the thing that is actually going to put Ines in the Vell is **an insurance clause
and a barometric reading**, and that is more frightening than anything anyone could do on
purpose.

*Ends on commitment, arrived at administratively.*

**19. "The Good Descent"** — *set piece.*
The block's peak. A genuinely good day underground: good light, good team, good problems,
Tove going first as offered and doing it perfectly.

**Nobody is in danger. Everyone is excellent. The chapter is a professional triumph and the
romance is at its most legible in the margin of it** — two women finding out how the other
one sounds when she is good at something.

*Ends on Tove going first and it working.*

**20. "1994"** — *document.*
The 1994 tape. Ottoline's mother's material, in a student screening room, watched alone at
an hour nobody else is in the building.

**She will not watch it in front of anyone**, and this is the first evidence that the
film-school lie is going to cost her something other than a grade. The tape is good. The
tape is *beautiful*. Vera Marrow was very good.

*Ends on the film being good and not being the film.*

**21. "The Other Woman's Film"** — *archive, another woman's film.*
The Vell in 1994, in detail, in a chapter that is about a competent professional doing
competent work thirty years ago and being underestimated now.

**The reader is alone with a brass plate.** It is in frame. It is hand-painted. It is
marked in hours. It is the same instrument and it is legible, and nobody in this chapter
looks at it because in 1994 it was a wall fixture and in 2022 it is a murder weapon.

*Ends on the gauge, and the book does not underline it.*

**22. "The Same Date"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
The grant window and the permit expiry land on the same day, and this is the chapter where
the romance and the descent stop being two problems.

Marisol releases the archive — all of it, competently, with a covering letter — and she
cannot fully explain why and does not try very hard, and the reader is given the one
possible reason and the book declines to confirm it.

*Ends on the release.*

**23. "I Got What I Needed"** — *POV shift, Vera, on a screen.*
Ottoline's mother on a monitor, on a bad connection, saying the sentence she says when
pressed. She is not a mystery and she is not a withheld villain and she is **not the
answer to anything the book wants.**

She agrees to be interviewed. Then she does not, and the reason given is reasonable, and
the reason not given is that she has seen what her daughter is making and she recognises
it.

*Ends on the connection dropping, and Ottoline not ringing back.*

**24. "Serious"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
Tove takes Ottoline seriously and it turns her.

Tove does not befriend her and does not mentor her. She **agrees to be checked**, which is
the thing a professional offers a student who is going underground, and it is a serious
offer and Ottoline nearly refuses it because she thinks it is condescension.

*Ends on Tove reading the 1994 material properly, for work.*

**25. "Archive, Work"** — *archive, cold.*
The 1994 material watched by professionals, for work, with a stopwatch. The chapter is
procedural and it is not atmospheric at all, and that is what makes it work.

**Someone finally says the word gauge.** It is said in the middle of a paragraph about
something else, by a person who has not yet understood what they have done.

*Ends on the sentence being in the room and nobody picking it up.*

**26. "Still"** — *document.*
The same 1994 frame, stilled, enlarged, measured. The number on the plate, and the plate,
and what the number would have meant, and what the real reading was on the day.

**The reading in the film is wrong.** Not fraudulently. Wrong — the way a hand-painted
instrument is wrong, worn, misread, or copied from a plate that was itself copied.

*Ends on the number.*

**27. "Go to Work"** — *withheld.*
A chapter that has just proved something and refuses to say it.

Ines leaves the room and goes to work. She sells equipment to people who go into holes. The
chapter is about ordinary competence and it is the last chapter in the book where Ines's
lie is *structural* rather than spoken, and it is unbearable, and the reader can see the
thing she is not saying from a hundred miles off.

*Ends on Ines closing the shop.*

**28. "After"** — *aftermath / plain.*
The shop, after. Tove arrives, which she has never done, and asks whether Ines has read her
own incident report.

The answer is yes and Tove knows it and asks anyway, and the asking is the chapter.

*Ends on nothing being said and both of them knowing.*

**29. "In Two Places"** — *two threads, cut.*
The sentence assembling in two rooms at the same time. Hana at the station. Fabiola at the
counter. Neither of them is talking to Ines and both of them are arriving at the same
conclusion from entirely different evidence.

The device earns itself: **the reader hears the truth arrive twice, from two people who
have no reason to protect Ines, which is the strongest possible evidence that it is true.**

*Ends on the two of them not knowing about each other.*

**30. "Paragraph Six"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
A hearing nobody wanted. Marisol, Ottoline, and the school. Paragraph six again, from
chapter 5, read back to Ottoline by a woman who wrote it in good faith.

Marisol asks the question she already knows the answer to and waits, and the waiting is
the cruelty, and she is not enjoying it and she is not not-enjoying it.

*Ends on the answer, which is true.*

**31. "Eleven Minutes, After"** — *POV shift, Hana alone.*
The other half of chapter 4. Eleven minutes in 2022 — Hana reaching Solveig — and the
four years since, and the *shape* of a person who has decided to be the last voice you
hear.

**Hana says what she has been not saying**, and it is not a reproach, and it is the
kindest hard thing in the book.

*Ends on Hana volunteering for the descent and Fabiola refusing her before she finishes
the sentence.*

**32. "Good Plan"** — *wide, moving fast.*
A competent crew doing a competent job at speed. Every element of the plan is right and
every element of it was assembled by a different person and **nobody has put the whole plan
in one place at one time.**

*Ends on the plan being good, and the plan being the problem.*

**33. "The Reading"** — *document.*
The reading that breaks the book. A number on a brass plate, measured from the 1994 frame,
cross-checked against the gauge in the 2022 report, and the reader can do the arithmetic
themselves because the book gives them both numbers and does not do the sum for them.

Delivered as a document, deliberately, **because a document can be re-read and a scene
cannot**, and this is the one chapter where a reader going back to check changes what the
rest of the book means.

*Ends on everybody who needs to know, knowing.*

**34. "The Invoices"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
Fabiola, the invoice history, and nineteen years of it on the table, and four years of
waiting for a person to say anything at all.

**The sentence is finally said aloud — and by a third party.** Fabiola has it from a
delivery date. Fabiola is the one who has had the number for four years and has been
waiting for somebody to ask her.

*Ends on the sentence being said and Ines not denying it.*

**35. "Unfinished"** — *POV shift, Tove alone.*
The professional thing, started, not finished. Tove tries to say to Ines that she should
not be on this rope and cannot get to the end of it, because **the sentence is about
professional competence and she is not qualified to say it and she says so.**

*Ends on Tove finishing it in one line, and the one line being the worst line in the book,
and it being about a schedule.*

**36. "Camera Off"** — *withheld.*
**The first honesty in the book, and it is badly timed and about something smaller than
the truth.**

Ottoline turns the camera off. The chapter is a whole conversation with no equipment in
it. She asks the real question — not about the Vell, not about Solveig, about the *gauge*
and what Ines did on the eleventh descent — and Ines answers it truthfully, and completely,
and it is *not the real answer*, and the gap between them is visible from the outside.

*Ends on the two of them alone in a car park with the truth sitting in the room neither of
them looked at.*

**37. "Agreed"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
The descent, agreed, in writing, by people who are right. The crew is correct, the permit
is clear, the plan is sound, the weather is fine, the film is funded, and the water is on a
cycle that everyone in the room can read off a plate.

**Nobody wants to stop and there is nothing to argue about.** This is the most frightening
condition the book contains and it is written to be read as relief.

*Ends on the agreement.*

**38. "Read"** — *set piece.*
The gauge is read correctly. Hana reads it aloud, to nobody, on a headland, and the number
is right and there is no doubt left in any direction.

There is no last-minute doubt and nobody says *maybe it wasn't the water*. The doubt was in
1994 and it was wrong then too. The film is rolling. The rope is two people.

*Ends on the water.*

**39. "First"** — *set piece.*
Going in. The line, the cold, the work, the ordinary competence of two professionals.

**Ines goes first, which nobody expected**, and the chapter never says why, and Tove lets
her.

*Ends on the second pitch.*

**40. "Ahead"** — *chase / pressure.*
The water, ahead of schedule, exactly where the archive said it would be.

**Tove's voice on the surface changes register** and it is the only time in the book that
Ines hears fear in a professional's voice, and the writing stays in Ines's register because
Ines has four years of practice at hearing things not go well.

*Ends on the hold.*

**41. "Held"** — *set piece, warm.*
The hold. The most compressed sequence in the book: the long quiet one, the two of them on
a line, nothing to be done.

**The unremarked thing happens here, in about four lines, in the middle of a work sequence,
and neither woman comments on it and it is the only unremarked thing in the novel.** The
book does not label it. It does not italicise it. It is a logistics courtesy and it is the
end of both of them.

*Ends on both of them still on the line.*

**42. "Out"** — *wide, moving fast.*
The retrieval. The chapter is fast and technical and it is not clean, and somebody is hurt
in a way that will not be dramatic about, and it is *fine*, everybody is alive, which the
book does not treat as the ending it could have been.

*Ends on them being out.*

**43. "Nine Minutes"** — *document / witness.*
The video, in full, in front of witnesses, in the back room of Renn and Daughters, on a
laptop, four years unviewed.

**Solveig is funny on tape.** She is specific, and quick, and pleased with herself about a
piece of kit, and she is on a rope in a cave and she is *nine minutes of a person* and
this is the chapter that pays for forty chapters of a book about a death.

*Ends on the tape running out.*

**44. "The Last Conversation"** — *dialogue-forward, single room.*
The shop, afterwards, and the thing about the mother, and the film.

Ottoline has ninety hours and it does not add up to the film and it is *not the film*, and
Ines says so, and the saying is the kindest thing anyone does in the book.

**Ines is not the romantic prize for having suffered.** She is a person who has said a
sentence and it has cost her and nothing has been fixed.

*Ends with the film unfinished and both of them still in contact.*

**45. "Hours Since"** — *document.*
A brass plate, a figure, and the last line of dialogue in the book.

**The last line of dialogue is a number.** It is read off a gauge, by Hana Vogel, out loud,
to nobody, in the last scene, and there is no commentary from any character or from the
narration. Vera Marrow and Ines Varga are never in the same room, and the book ends with
that still true.

## CARRIED THREADS

Declared here so that a later session can see what is owed and where.

- **The eleven descents (ch 3, 12, 27, 34, 36, 45).** Ines went back too many times. The
  reader is given the count in chapter 12 without being given the meaning, and the meaning
  arrives in chapter 34 from a delivery date.
- **The gauge (ch 1, 12, 21, 25, 26, 33, 38, 45).** Eight appearances. It is a number. It
  is a clock, a lie, a murder weapon, an instrument of state, and the last line of the
  novel.
- **The ambiguous clause (ch 2, 22, 44).** The contract's one unclear term, about material
  neither party owns. It is a legal ambiguity in Block I, a different ambiguity in Block
  III, and it is resolved — badly, in a car park — in Block IV.
- **The rack (ch 9, 11, 16, 39, 41).** What Ines wears, and when she puts it on, and the
  reader is the only one keeping track. **The first time she puts it on for the film is a
  small event. By chapter 41 it is a relief.** The book never explains this.
- **Ottoline filming without asking (ch 3, 14, 20, 25, 36).** Her habit since she was
  fifteen. It is a lie about consent and the book treats it as one, and chapter 36 is where
  she stops.
- **Vera Marrow (ch 17, 20, 21, 23, 44, and never on the page in person).** On screens and
  down telephones. Never in the same room as Ines. Not the answer to anything.
- **Tove's forty-one readings (ch 13, 24, 28, 35).** The count is specific, and a person
  does not count about a document they are indifferent to.
- **The nine-minute video (ch 2, 6, 28, 43).** Introduced, referred to nine times, watched
  once.

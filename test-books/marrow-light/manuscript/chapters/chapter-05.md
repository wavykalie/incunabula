# Chapter Five — Two Applications

The permit application was nine pages and it was a form, and the form had a box on page
four that asked for the name and qualification of the person who would be carrying out
supervision underground, and the box wanted a certificate number, and the number was
eleven digits and Marisol Trent knew all of them without looking, which is a thing that
happens to people about a subject they have stopped being able to leave.

She filled it in on a Tuesday evening in a room with a small window and a plant that had
gone over in August and that she had thrown out on the ninth of October and replaced with
the same plant from the same shop, and she had not told anybody, and the reason she had not
told anybody was that there was nobody to tell and that if she had told somebody it would
have been a sentence about a plant and she was not going to say a sentence about a plant out
loud.

Page four, box four: *M. TRENT, CERT. 11-4471-0092.*

She had held that certificate for twenty-two years. She had not been underground under her
own supervision since 2004 and had not been underground at all, in any capacity, since
2011, and she still knew the number, and the number was in eleven documents in a drawer in
this room, and she had checked it that morning without deciding to.

Page five asked what the purpose of the survey was.

That was the real page. Everything above it was administrative and this one was the
question, and the answer she had wanted to give was *student documentary, supervised*, and
the answer she was going to give was the same, and the reason the same answer was going to
cost her an evening was that a documentary is not a purpose the form recognises, and
because *student* is a word that gets read two different ways by two different people, and
because the second of those two people was going to be a twenty-two-year-old with a camera
and a permit in her hand.

*Purpose: survey documentation in support of a student film project (non-commercial,
no public access, no distribution of survey data without association consent).*

She read it back. She rewrote *documentation* as *recording*. She put *documentation* back,
because *recording* was a softer word in a form and softening a word in a form is a thing
she had a policy about.

Then she read the last clause again and added, because it was true and because it was the
only part of the form that was hers:

*I will not approve a Vell descent in October. The section below the second pitch is
closed to student parties and has been since 2023.*

She wrote it, and read it, and it was the first thing in eleven days that had felt like
writing an actual sentence instead of a paragraph, and she sat with it for a while.

Then she deleted it.

Not because it was wrong. Because it was the only sentence in the document that was about
her rather than about the case, and Marisol Trent had learned in about 2006 that a
sentence like that, in a document like this, read afterwards by anybody competent, is
indistinguishable from a woman trying to protect herself from having made the right
decision for the wrong reason.

She left the box empty. She would put a line in it on Thursday, when she had slept, and
would write it in the version of the sentence that was purely about the facts.

---

In the east of the city, in a building with a car park on the roof and a heating system
that worked in one direction, Ottoline Marrow was doing the same form, badly, for the
fifth time.

The grant was the smaller problem and it was the smaller problem for a reason that nobody
had told her and that she had worked out. The grant did not care whether the Vell was safe.
The grant cared whether ninety hours of picture existed by the fourteenth of October, and
whether the ninety hours were *directed*, and whether the person directing them had a
budget of eleven hundred pounds and had, on the fourth of September, spent four hundred of
it on a car.

The application was four boxes. She had filled the first three on the second of September
and had not touched the fourth since, and the fourth said:

*Describe the relationship between the form and the subject.*

She had written eleven versions of the fourth box. She had written *the form is a
speleologist who stopped going underground and is now a participant-observer on the
question of whether she can go back*, and had deleted it because it made Ines Varga into a
topic.

She had written *the film is a portrait of a professional at the point where their
expertise and their self-regard come apart*, and had deleted it because it was the sentence
her supervisor would have written.

She had written *the form is a person who knows a place extremely well and is finding out
what that knowledge was for*, and had deleted it because it was beautiful and she did not
trust herself to mean it.

At twenty past eleven on the Tuesday she wrote:

*The form is my teacher.*

And then she deleted that, and sat for a while, and then wrote:

*The form is a speleologist who was in the Vell in 2022 when a collapse killed her partner
and who has not been back in four years. She knows the system better than anyone now
working in it. She has not been underground since. I do not yet know which of those two
facts is more interesting and I have not asked her, because I think I will know by
September which one I have filmed, and because she asked me not to ask her things in the
first conversation and I have decided that was not really about the first conversation.*

She read it four times. It was honest, and it was specific, and it was the only one of the
eleven that was any of those things, and it gave away the whole shape of the film to a
panel of four people in a room in the city, and she sent it at twenty-four past eleven and
then sat in the dark in a building with a car park on the roof and did not turn the light
on.

---

On the Wednesday the permit came back.

It came back with a covering note, and the covering note was four lines and was signed *M.
Trent, Supervisor*, and the four lines were:

*Ottoline. I have read this twice and I am not going to pretend the second reading changed
it. I am returning it rather than holding it, so that the decision is a clean one and not a
slow one. My reasons are in the attached. If you want to talk about any of them I am
available most of this week. M.*

The attached was nine paragraphs.

The first paragraph thanked her for the application and said that the form had been
completed accurately.

The second paragraph said that the project was well-conceived and that the supervisor had
read the treatment and thought it strong, and this was true, and it was the last thing in
the nine that Marisol Trent had written without an argument in her head.

The third paragraph was about the system, and it said that the Vell was a single-entrance
system with a known flood history and that the association's position was that the section
below the second pitch remained closed pending a full hydrological assessment, which it
had been since 2023, and which was true.

The fourth paragraph was about equipment, and it was true, and it was a list.

The fifth paragraph was about the staffing ratio and it was true and it was the paragraph
she had rewritten twice, and it ended with the word *inadequate*, and she had left it.

The sixth paragraph was the one that mattered.

It said that paragraph 4.1 of the agreement between the film and the technical consultant
provided for survey support and technical direction on access days; that access days under
a single-entrance system with a fifty-metre working line required a named person on the
surface end of that line at all times; and that no such person had been named in the
application, and that the person who would in all likelihood be named, if the application
proceeded, was the consultant herself, and that this was not a staffing problem that could
be solved by naming a second person but a structural one, in that the arrangement the film
was proposing depended on a woman of thirty-eight going underground on the end of a line
that she had not been on for four years, and that Marisol Trent was not prepared to record
her agreement to that, and would not be able to, and that this was not about competence.

The seventh paragraph said that competence was not the point and that she wanted to be very
clear about that, because paragraph six was going to be read as an attack and it was not
one.

The eighth paragraph said that she had been on the panel that cleared a Vell permit in 1994
and that she had not thought about that panel in thirty years and had thought about it four
times in the last month, and that she was not able to say whether she had cleared it
correctly, and that she was not prepared to clear a second one without knowing, and that
this was the actual reason.

The ninth paragraph was two lines long and said: *I am sorry. I would rather be honest
with you than useful to you this week.*

Ottoline read it at ten past eight on the Thursday morning in the film school's one
surviving corridor and then read paragraph six again, and then again, and then went and
stood at the window, which faced a car park.

"She read clause 4.1," she said. "She read the actual clause. Nobody reads the actual
clause." She had a mug in her hand that she had not made tea in. "She's read the actual
clause and she's built a nine-paragraph refusal on the staffing ratio and there's nothing
wrong with it. There's nothing wrong with any of it. Nine paragraphs, all of them
reasonable, and paragraph six is completely right and I hate it."

There was nobody in the corridor. It was eight in the morning on a Thursday and there was
nobody in the corridor, and Ottoline Marrow stood at a window with an empty mug and found
that she was, for the first time since the second of September, frightened, and that the
frightening was entirely specific and entirely technical, and that she could not make it
into anything larger and more dramatic even by trying, which she had time to attempt three
times in about ninety seconds.

Then she put the mug down on the window ledge, where it stayed for the rest of that day and
was still there at six, and went to find out whether the film school's insurance certificate
for a named surface person cost eleven hundred pounds, because she had four hundred pounds
of budget and a car that had cost four hundred pounds, and the arithmetic was going to be
the whole of the next two weeks.

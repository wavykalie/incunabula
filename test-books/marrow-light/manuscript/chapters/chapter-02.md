# Chapter Two — Terms

The document was four pages and it had a schedule, and the schedule was the part that
mattered, and neither of them read the schedule.

Fabiola put it in a plastic wallet because she had a plastic wallet for everything and had
put four thousand documents in it since 2011. She put the wallet on the counter, and put a
cup of tea beside it that she had made for a person she had decided was not going to stay.

"Sit down, both of you, you'll get the tea cold."

"I'd rather stand," said Ines.

"Sit down. You've been on your feet since ten and I've watched you put a man in the ground
for eleven pounds and I'd like you to have a chair for the part where you say yes."

The young woman sat. Ines did not, for another minute and a half, and then did, and Fabiola
marked the time.

The name on the second page was *Ottoline Marrow* and the name on the first was *Ines Varga*
and between them, in a box, was a company: Renn and Daughters Trading, sole traders, two
proprietors, one of whom was sixty-one and one of whom was technically an employee.

"Explain the box," said Ottoline.

"It's the shop."

"It's the shop," said Fabiola. "You've not got a company. You've got a shop and a name on
the door and a building you own outright, and if you sign anything as Renn and Daughters
Trading then somebody can come at the building, and I have been waiting twenty years for
somebody with a lawyer to notice that."

"You've thought about this."

"I've thought about it every day for twenty years," said Fabiola, "and now it's in a
document, and it's your document, and I've been thanked."

The rate was on page two and it was the thing Ottoline had come in with. It was not enough
and it was not so far from enough that it could be called an insult. Ines read it twice.

"Where did you get this number?"

"It was the number."

"It's not the number."

"It's the number I could afford," said Ottoline. "I'm going to be straight with you,
because I read the report, and I know what the going rate is for a lead consultant on a
system nobody's surveyed properly since 2022, and I've costed this the way you'd cost it,
and that's what I've got. There's a difference between cheap and dishonest and I've tried
to stay on the right side of it."

"Have you."

"I've asked my supervisor for the number twice," said Ottoline. "She gave it to me the
first time and didn't give it to me the second time, and I've decided to take the first
time and not ask why she withdrew it."

Ines looked at her for slightly too long.

"That's the most professional thing anybody's said in this shop in about a decade," she
said. "Fab, put it up to the number."

"Don't."

"Put it up to the number."

"If I put it up to the number then it's a job," said Fabiola, "and jobs come with things. A
job has a schedule and a schedule has dates and dates are the thing that kills people in
this trade, and I have got a photograph on that wall of six people in a car outside my
door in 2014 and every single one of them had a schedule."

There was a photograph on the wall of six people in a car outside the door, and it was of
six people in a car, and it was not of anything else, and all three of them looked at it
for a moment and then did not.

"Right," said Ines. "The number's the number."

"Good," said Fabiola, and went to the back, and shut the door behind her, which she never
did, and which both of them noticed.

Ottoline had the schedule out. That was when it started to go wrong, and it went wrong
quietly, the way things do when everybody is being reasonable.

"Access days," she said. "I'm not asking for continuous. I've got four, spaced, and two of
them are weather calls, and the film needs something in the first two hundred hours of
footage that isn't a talking head. I need you underground on the day, not for the day. I
need you in it. There's a difference and I'll pay for the difference."

"How much of it is on camera."

"None of it. On my word."

"You've been here forty minutes."

"I've been here forty minutes," said Ottoline, "and I haven't asked you to do a single thing
yet, which I think you should probably notice before I ask you to do things."

"That's a good answer," said Ines, and it was not entirely, and Ottoline heard the part of
it that wasn't and filed it, and Ines saw her file it.

Page three was the clauses. Page three had eleven of them and they were unremarkable, and
Ines read them at the speed of somebody who has read four thousand documents, which is to
say she read the shape of them and not the words.

Clause 4 was the one that mattered and neither of them read it.

**4.2** *Footage and survey data generated during an Access Day shall be jointly held by the
Parties, and neither Party shall exploit, license, publish or otherwise dispose of the same
without the prior written consent of the other.*

It was a standard clause. Ottoline had found it in a template from the school, which had
found it in a template from somewhere, and Ines had been reading insurance variations for
nineteen years, and neither of them read clause 4.2, because it was legible and because it
sounded reasonable and because *jointly held* is a phrase that makes both parties stop.

The Vell had a national survey record. The survey record was held by the association, and
the association was not a party to this document, and any footage in which the survey
stations appeared would contain, incidentally and unavoidably, a small quantity of the
association's data. A small quantity. Not a useful quantity. The most anybody could do with
it was confirm a known depth to two decimal places, and the only person in the world for whom
that was worth anything was a woman in an incident file eleven kilometres from here.

Neither of them thought about it. Ines was thinking about the rate. Ottoline was thinking
about whether she had overplayed the *I know what the going rate is* line, and had, and was
already constructing the sentence she would use if it mattered.

Ines got to the end of page three and said, "What's 4.1?"

"Uh." Ottoline turned back. "Support. Survey support, technical direction, whatever the day
needs."

"And 4.3?"

"Insurance. Yours, mine, and the crew's, and it's the reason I'm here on a Friday and
you're not, and I'll send the certificates over."

"Fine."

"Fine," said Ottoline, and wrote *4.1 discussed* in the margin, which was the only note
either of them made on the whole document, and it was next to the wrong clause, and neither
of them ever found it again.

The rate was the rate. Ines signed at four minutes past three, and Ottoline signed at four
minutes past three, and the two signatures are eleven seconds apart, which is the whole of
the evidence in this chapter that either of them had any idea what they were agreeing to.

Fabiola came back in with a third cup of tea and a receipt book, and asked who was paying,
and was told by Ottoline, and wrote it out, and rang it up, and gave Ottoline the carbon.

"That's your copy," she said. "Don't lose it. Everything that goes wrong in this trade goes
wrong over a piece of paper somebody's lost."

Ottoline put it in the folder. Ines put her copy in the pocket of a coat that had not been
out of a wardrobe since March, which was where it went, and which is the only place in this
book that coat appears again.

At the door, Ottoline stopped.

"Can I ask you one thing and can you not answer it if the answer is no?"

"You can ask."

"In the report, there's a photograph. Of a wall." She was looking at the counter. "Somebody's
painted a number on it. Hand-painted, in white. It says *hours since*."

Ines did not move at all, and it was not a decision, it was just that there was a very
small number of things available to do and all of them were visible.

"You'd want to know when that was painted," said Ottoline. "And by who. And whether it's
still there."

"Is it still there?"

"I don't know. I've never been in."

"Then I don't know either."

"Right," said Ottoline.

She stood in the doorway for a moment longer with the folder under her arm, and the rain
had stopped, and the street outside was doing the thing it does after rain, which was
holding the light.

"It's Solveig's," she said.

Ines looked at her.

"The number. The painting of it. In the report, there's a line about it. *Local record,
maintained.*" Ottoline's voice did not change at all, which was itself a decision, and a
good one. "Local record. Maintained. That's a person maintaining it. That's somebody going
in fifty metres with a brush."

"Yes," said Ines.

"Right," said Ottoline, and went out into it, and did not say thank you, and did not say
goodbye, and did not close the door behind her, and Ines stood and looked at the door for
longer than the door required.

# Chapter Three — The Stockroom

She offered her the tea eleven minutes before she said no, which Ottoline thought later was
a kindness and which was in fact tactics, and there was a kettle in the stockroom at Renn and
Daughters that had been switched on at half past four every working day since 2003 and had
boiled and gone off and been boiled again, and neither of them drank it.

The stockroom was nine feet by eleven and it had a rack of carbines in it, upside down on
shelves, in a way that Fabiola had insisted on and nobody had ever questioned, and it had
one chair, and neither of them sat on it.

"So," said Ottoline. "Can I put a camera in here?"

"No."

"Not even—"

"Not even turned off," said Ines. "It's a stockroom. It's got about four things in it a
person would want to photograph and none of them are interesting and one of them is me in
a coat."

"It's not about photographing you in a coat."

"What is it about?"

"It's about the Vell," said Ottoline. "You can be a voice. I've done a voice-only interview,
that's a real form, they exist, I could cut to it. I don't need your face. I need about
forty minutes of you saying what the system is, in your own words, on the record, and I'd
rather do it in here than in the shop because in here nobody comes in."

"In here you come in."

"I'll knock," said Ottoline. "I'll knock every time and I'll wait. I want to say that
properly because I know what you're doing, which is deciding whether I'm the sort of person
who comes in without knocking, and I've thought about it, and the answer is that I am, and
the reason I'm asking is that the one time I need to be in here I will knock, and you can
hold me to it."

Ines looked at her.

"Right," she said.

"So that's—"

"That's the interview, yes." Ines shifted her weight. "And it's forty minutes is generous,
I'll give you an hour, and I'll give you the same thing I've given about four people
because it's the same four sentences and there's no version of me that has more than that
in it. You'll get the entrance. You'll get why it's a single entrance system. You'll get
why you go in one at a time, and you'll get enough about survey method that I don't have to
explain it to the sound recordist. And then you'll have nothing else and you'll cut it to
about ninety seconds and it'll be good."

"That's fine," said Ottoline.

"And in exchange you don't ask me to go in."

There it was. It had been there the whole time. It was the actual sentence, under four
other sentences, and it had been under them since the shop on Tuesday.

"I'm not asking you to go in," said Ottoline.

"You are asking me to go in. You've asked me to go in in the first four minutes, in the
first conversation, in a stockroom, and I've been waiting for you to say it plainly so I
could answer it plainly."

"I've asked you to be a consultant."

"A consultant is a person who goes in," said Ines. "That's what a consultant is. You didn't
hire me to be a talking head with a good coat, and if you had, you'd have got one, and you
would have got a better one, and you would have been disappointed in a different direction
which is more useful to you and less useful to me."

"I know that," said Ottoline. "That's exactly why I built clause 4 in the way I built it.
*Neither Party shall exploit, license, publish or otherwise dispose of the same.* You don't
put your face on it. You don't put your name on it. You get paid for the day, you get a
credit that says *technical consultant*, and if the film is good the credit is worth
something to you and if the film is bad the credit is worth nothing to anyone. That's the
deal and I have thought about it for a fortnight."

"You have thought about it for a fortnight," said Ines.

"Yes."

"And in the fourteen days, how many times did you rehearse it?"

Ottoline did not answer, which was the answer, and which Ines took without comment, and
which was — Ines would think about it much later, in a car park, in the rain, at a
completely different hour of the day — the first time in four years that somebody had said
*no* to her in a way that had nothing to do with the cave.

"You're a good liar," said Ines. "You're just not a fast one."

"I'm not a liar."

"You're not a liar, no." Ines turned round and looked at the carbines on their shelves,
upside down, in the wrong order by weight, and did not fix them. "I've never met a filmmaker
who wasn't. It's the job. You get to decide what a thing looks like, and the thing is
mostly other people, so the whole craft is arranging a disagreement until it looks like
agreement. I've been on the wrong end of that. I don't mind it. I'd mind it if you weren't
telling me."

For about four seconds it was nearly nice.

Then Ottoline said, "What if I came anyway?"

"What?"

"Block me from this room. Fine. Put it in the schedule, put it in clause 4, whatever you
like, I'll sign whatever you want. What if on the access days I came to the car park and I
stood at the top of the entrance and you went in anyway?"

"That's not the same as being filmed."

"No," she agreed. "It isn't. But it would be mine. It would be the thing I did, not the
thing you allowed, and I would be on the record as having done it, and you'd have to
account for that." She was going too fast now and she could hear herself going too fast and
it did not stop her, because it had never once stopped her. "I know how that sounds. I know
that from the outside it sounds like a person who's decided she wants something more than
she's been given. I want you to know that if I do that, it's a method and not an ambush.
It's — I have watched eleven people not talk about things and I've learned that a camera is
sometimes the only thing that makes a person say the sentence, and I've also watched it make
them worse, and I don't know which one you are, and neither do you, and that's the reason
I'd rather be there and be refused than not be there."

The stockroom was very quiet. The kettle clicked off.

"No," said Ines.

"Okay."

"You didn't hear the rest of it."

"I heard *no*," said Ottoline. "I wasn't listening past it. Do you want the rest of it?"

"No."

"Then I won't take it," said Ottoline, and wrote nothing down, and stood there for a
moment, and then said: "Can I ask you one factual question and get a yes or a no, and you
can say no and I won't follow it up."

"Depends on the question."

"It's the one I came in with and I haven't asked it and I have been not asking it for two
weeks, which I know is a technique, and I know you know it's a technique, and I'm doing it
anyway." Ottoline's hands were flat on the table. "Did you read the 2022 report."

"Yes."

"All of it."

"Yes."

"Then you know what the line was doing when the roof came in."

Ines did not answer.

"You don't have to say it," said Ottoline. "That's not a trick, and I'd like to be
understood on that. I've read it four times. I know the hours. I know what the last reading
was and I know how far out it was and I've done the arithmetic and it doesn't work and I
have been sitting on that for a fortnight wondering whether I'm a person who has misread a
document or a person who has read a document correctly and is now sitting on knowing."

Nobody in the shop said anything. Out in the shop, which was the same room, Fabiola's chair
was empty and the till was closed and the door was shut and the bell had not been rung all
day.

"You're right about the last reading," said Ines.

"Okay," said Ottoline.

"You're right about the arithmetic."

"Okay," said Ottoline, and her voice went, just slightly, and she put her hand over her
mouth and took it away again, and Ines watched her do it and had the distinct and unwelcome
experience of feeling sorry for somebody who had come here to take something out of her.

"Don't," said Ines. "Don't do that. You'll be on camera doing that in six months and it'll
be the best eleven seconds in the film and it won't be the one you planned."

"You think I did that on purpose."

"No," said Ines. "I think you did that the way I did something in a cave in 2022, and I
think the fact that we did it the same way is the only interesting thing that's happened to
me in four years, and I would like to go home now."

She was at the stockroom door before she finished the sentence, and she was angry at
herself for the last five words, and she said them anyway, because the alternative was
taking them back and she had not taken anything back since 2022 and did not intend to
start.

Ottoline stayed where she was. She stood in the stockroom with the upside-down carbines and
the dead kettle for a long moment, and then she went out into the shop, and Fabiola was
sitting at the counter, and Fabiola had clearly been sitting there for some time.

"Fabiola."

"Forty-five minutes," said Fabiola. "She's been in here forty-five minutes. I timed it."

"You were listening."

"I've got a shop," said Fabiola. "You can't be in a stockroom forty-five minutes in a shop
and have it not be listened to." She looked at Ottoline for a moment with an expression
that would have been difficult to categorise, and then went back to her screen, and said,
to Ines: "You've said no."

"I've said no."

"Right," said Fabiola, and picked up her tea, and drank it, and made a face, because it was
stone cold, and had been for some time, and neither of them had said anything about it.

# Chapter Eleven — The Line

Fifty metres of wool and a person on each end of it, and that was the arrangement, and it
turned out to be so much larger than a line that neither of them could see the edges of it.

The base was a layby a kilometre back from the entrance, in the wet, with the van backed in
so that the boot was out of the rain, and it was there because Hana Vogel had said it was
there four weeks ago on a telephone at ten past nine on a Tuesday night and had been right
about it.

She had also been right about the signal. You pointed the aerial at the hill and you got two
bars and a repeater that had a name, and the name was the name of a farm, and the farm was
four kilometres away over the shoulder, and this was the entire reason the layby existed:
somebody in 1997 had driven up here with a phone and found that if you pointed it at the
hill you could talk to somebody.

Ines went in at ten past nine in the morning on the ninth of October, and the ninth of
October was the first of twelve days that was also going to be a good one, and the good ones
were the ones you could tell about at six in the morning, and this one had been obvious at
five.

Ottoline got out of the van with the camera and did not put it up.

That was the plan. That was the whole plan, and it had been in the schedule since the
Thursday, and the schedule said *interview, audio only, no camera* at 09:30, and it said
*survey documentation, wide and static* at 10:00, and it said *no filming during descent or
ascent*.

Ottoline Marrow stood in a layby in the rain for six minutes with a camera in a bag on her
hip and listened to a woman get into a hole.

"Radio check," said Tove, from the grass above the entrance. "Varga, you're live to surface.
Say again."

"Varga, live to surface." A pause, and then the sound of somebody sitting down on a
ledge. "Descending. I'll call at fifty."

"Copy. And the rain's coming back."

"So I heard."

"It's not a no, it's a *watch it*," said Tove, which was the sort of thing that took eleven
weeks of Tuesdays to learn from somebody.

---

It is not true that it is warm underground, and it is not true that it is dark underground,
and both of these are the sort of half-truth that gets repeated by people who have not
checked, and neither of them had ever checked, because the point of being fifty metres
under a field is not that it is a different temperature.

What it is, at a hundred metres, is quiet in a way that has a floor in it, and the floor is
water somewhere, and if you sit still for four minutes you can hear where it is.

Ines sat at a hundred and ten metres with the light off and her hand on the box.

"What can you hear?" said Ottoline.

The transmission was three-quarters of a second long, which she had got used to and had
stopped noticing, and the half-second of dead air before somebody answered had become, over
nine days, a thing with a texture.

"Hold on," said Ines.

There was a wait of about twenty seconds, in which the rain on the layby canopy was audible
at the far end of a fifty-metre line, and the van's tick, and Hana breathing.

"It's doing the thing it does round about a hundred and ten," said Ines. "There's a break in
the roof at about a hundred and twenty and the water's coming through it somewhere and
dropping, and the drop's got a sort of — it's a small drop. It's not a stream. It's a
drip that's found a stone and it goes *pock* and then nothing, and then *pock* again, and
it's about a second and a bit apart, and if you're not listening for it you don't hear it
at all, and if you are listening for it you can't hear anything else."

"That's what you hear."

"That's what I hear most at a hundred and ten."

"Would you get that on a microphone?"

"I don't know," said Ines. "It depends what you point it at."

"Point it where?"

"In the direction of the sound."

"That's not a piece of direction, that's a tautology, and you knew it when you said it."

"Yes," said Ines.

On the grass above the entrance, Hana Vogel, who had a job to do and was doing it and had
been doing it for forty minutes, put her hand over her mouth and turned away from the radio
so that two people fifty metres below the surface would not hear her laugh, and it was not
a polite laugh, and Petar, who was in the van, looked at the ceiling of the van.

---

They went to a hundred and fifty.

"Is the light doing anything?"

"No. It's doing what it does. There's about four metres of it at the top of the pitch and
then it stops, and then it's forty metres of nothing and then there's a bit more."

"So I'll lose you."

"You'll lose me. You'll get the radio and you'll get me saying what's happening, and you'll
get nothing else, and that's the deal and you knew it when you signed."

"I know I knew it," said Ottoline. "I want to hear you say it anyway."

"That's the second time this morning."

"It's the last time," said Ottoline.

"*Varga, Varga.*" Tove's voice cut in, flat, the way it did when she was being a
professional instead of a person. "Radio discipline. You're drifting. Confirm the log."

"Confirm the log," said Ines, without heat, because Tove had taught her that in 2017 in a
different system and had not had to teach it twice.

"Logged. And Varga — the wall. You're drifting off the wall."

"I know where the wall is."

"You're nine degrees off it and going."

That was the thing about going underground with a film crew's radio discipline: Tove could
hear it. She could not hear a room, but she could hear the shape of somebody's attention by
the timing, and forty minutes on a line taught her that Ines Varga's attention drifted toward
whatever had just been said to her, and that this was a fault and not a charm, and that the
fault was going to get somebody hurt one day.

"Sorry," said Ines.

"Station's at four o'clock from your position. Come to it."

---

By eleven, the two of them had a routine, and the routine had been established in nine days
without either of them proposing it.

Ottoline asked about the survey. Ines answered about the survey. For twenty minutes it went
properly and it was almost restful and the rain went on the canopy and Hana read a book in
the back of the van and Tove did whatever Tove did on a hillside for three hours.

Then Ottoline said, "So what made you stop?"

And Ines, fifty metres below, at a hundred and sixty metres, in the dark, with the light off,
said, "Hana, log a fault at two-ten. I'm going to stop."

There was a pause.

"Copy," said Tove. "Fault logged. Ascend at your convenience."

"*Varga.*" Hana's voice, for the first time in forty minutes, and it was not the voice she
used on a radio. "You told me the plan. You said she'd get the ninety minutes and the two
station fixes and then you'd both come up and I'd get a coffee."

"I said that," said Ines.

"So what's this."

Ines sat at a hundred and sixty metres and had a thing available to her that she could have
said, and it was true, and it was about the water and the plan and the day's window, and it
would have gone on a tape and been understandable and been true.

"I answered a question you didn't ask," said Ines.

The line did what a line does at a hundred and sixty metres, which is nothing at all.

"Say again?"

"You asked what made me stop," said Ines. "I stopped because you asked me that, and not
because of the survey. And I can't have that on a tape going to a room, and you know I can't,
and that's — " She stopped, and started again, and the second try was worse. "That's the
deal. That's why I'm on the radio and not on the film. If I do that once, I do it every
time, and then in about a week you'll ask me something like that on a good day and I'll give
you the wrong answer because I'll be listening for the next one."

On the hill, Tove Marsk said nothing at all, and put a hand on the top of the box, and
remembered a room in 2017 where a woman had said *you always knew*, and understood that she
had been talking about exactly this and had not known she was.

"Are you coming up?" said Hana.

"Yes," said Ines. "Give me four minutes."

She gave herself nine, which was five more than she had said, and which nobody mentioned,
and which was the first thing she had lied about in four years and was not about the cave.

# Quoin — Chapter 8

Skill: `quoin`. 7 of 8 operations applied.

1. **Cut self-explaining similes.** A draft said the mirror "reflected a man who could not look at himself"; cut. The chapter does not need to interpret its own motif.
2. **Irrelevant thought injected.** The detail that Taizong's wife had to talk him down from his temper is kept as a domestic beat that the political argument does not require.
3. **Emotional control broken.** "Both. Always both." is a fragment where the narrator normally reasons.
4. **Precision deflated.** No dates are fudged; the chapter is the book's most date-specific and the quoin left that alone.
5. **Ugly sentence added.** "A man who has killed his brothers for the throne knows one thing that the rest of us do not: what he is capable of."
6. **Predictable paragraph removed.** A paragraph concluding that "power and virtue are not opposites" was cut, because it resolves what the chapter is supposed to leave open.
7. **Clean dialogue roughed.** Wei Zheng's plainness is left plain; the quoin resisted adding subtext to a register whose whole point is bluntness.
8. **Thematic echo unsettled.** The chapter turns the mirror on itself — on the book's own method — which is the largest unsettling move in the manuscript.

**Anchor preserved:** the same man, months apart.

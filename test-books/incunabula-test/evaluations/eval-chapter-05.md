# Evaluation — Chapter 5, A Poet Among Warlords

**Independence grade: C** (same context as the writer — diagnostic only).

| Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Originality | 7.5 | The stacked-documents opening is the book's boldest form |
| Theme | 7.5 | The edict-creates-armies mechanism is precise |
| Figures | 7.5 | Cao Cao is the most *interesting* figure and the least resolved |
| Prose & Voice | 7.5 | "An edict. A poem. A tally of the dead." |
| Pacing & Coherence | 7.0 | The fragment structure costs some connective clarity; the Pure Conversation section is the slowest in the book |
| Emotion | 7.0 | The trough chapter, as intended; deliberately un-cathartic |
| Intellectual payload | 7.5 | The poem-after-the-massacre reading is the chapter's contribution |

**Floor: 7.0.**

**Top weakness (structural).** The mid-chapter survey of post-Han intellectual history is
the book's one genuinely encyclopedic stretch. **Smallest viable intervention:** cut the
Wang Bi / Seven Sages paragraph by half and let the *abstention* be the point.

**Top strengths to preserve.** (1) The three-document opening; (2) the talent-over-virtue
edicts; (3) "A hundred of the living — one survives."

**Passing Reader:** would keep reading, but this is the chapter where the reader most
plausibly stops. **Keepsake anchors:** 2.

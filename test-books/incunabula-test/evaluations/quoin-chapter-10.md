# Quoin — Chapter 10

Skill: `quoin`. 7 of 8 operations applied.

1. **Cut self-explaining similes.** A draft described the frontier commands as "a dam holding back the steppe"; cut. The chapter's argument is about a state's own instruments, and the dam image points the wrong way.
2. **Irrelevant thought injected.** Li Bai drinking alone with the moon and his shadow is the chapter's opening, and it carries no argument at all for two paragraphs. Deliberate.
3. **Emotional control broken.** The Yang Guifei passage is the one place the narrator is openly angry: "the tradition kept that assignment of blame … Read them with the cavalry counts beside you."
4. **Precision deflated.** The population figures are given with the register-versus-body-count caveat, and the caveat is not allowed to dissolve the death.
5. **Ugly sentence added.** "An essay about a book" belongs to Ch 11; here it is the over-long sentence about the guard and the emperor's personal life.
6. **Predictable paragraph removed.** A paragraph on "the tragedy of the golden age" was cut.
7. **Clean dialogue roughed.** Du Fu's dead child is put in the middle of the poem rather than at the end, which is how the poem does it and which denies the reader a tidy crescendo.
8. **Thematic echo unsettled.** Not applied. The chapter ends on the poems outlasting the throne, which is one of the book's two themes and is stated plainly on purpose.

**Anchor preserved:** the dated death of Du Fu's son.

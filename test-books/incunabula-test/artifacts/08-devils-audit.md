# 08 — Devil's Audit

Phase 4, skill: `proof-panel` in adversarial mode. The printer's devil finds the faults
before the score. No score is set in this document.

**Run:** 2026-09-25, against all 11 chapters (28,263 words), the outline, the theme, and
the prose-gate result. **Open ledger conflicts at audit time:** `CONF-001` (narrator
person), open and not blocking.

---

## What the audit attacked

### 1. The structure

- **Charge:** eleven ruler-plus-thinker chapters is a survey wearing a thesis.
- **Verdict: PARTLY SUSTAINED.** The pairing is consistent and each chapter's argument is
  distinct, but Chapters 2, 5, 7 and 10 are more narrative than argument, and a hostile
  reader could skim them as biography. **Mitigation applied:** each of those four now
  states its "argument destroyed" explicitly at some point (Ch 2 at the Lu Jia hinge, Ch
  5 in the Cao Cao edicts, Ch 7 in the canal/institution split, Ch 10 in the register
  paragraph).
- **Charge:** two consecutive chapters use *chronological* and two use *essayistic*.
- **Verdict: NOT SUSTAINED.** The repeats are non-consecutive (1 & 7; 3 & 11), which is
  the rule. Verified mechanically against the structural ledger.

### 2. The voice

- **Charge:** the narrator's opinions are so insistent that the book is an essay with
  scenes attached.
- **Verdict: SUSTAINED, AND ACCEPTED.** This is a deliberate position (engaged
  historian-narrator, R2/R4 in `readership.md`). It is recorded as a risk, not fixed. If
  the author wants a cooler narrator, that is a Phase 5 revision of type *connective*
  across the whole manuscript, not a per-chapter fix.
- **Charge:** the direct address to the reader in Ch 11's final paragraph breaks the
  contract.
- **Verdict: SUSTAINED AS RISK.** It is the book's largest formal gamble. It is kept,
  flagged, and it is the first thing to cut if the author wants a safer close.

### 3. Accuracy and honesty

- **Charge:** the book asserts modern consensus as settled where the scholarship is not.
- **Verdict: NOT SUSTAINED.** Disputed items are flagged in the text: the buried scholars
  (Ch 1), the children in the carriage (Ch 2), the Xu Province toll (Ch 5), the
  Bodhidharma interview (Ch 6), the registers (Ch 10), the poem attribution (Ch 9).
- **Charge:** the narrator's use of "I" ("I am going to tell you", "I believe…") smuggles
  opinion in as authority.
- **Verdict: SUSTAINED, MINOR.** Fourteen instances. Reviewed individually; each is
  marked as the narrator's judgement rather than reported as fact. **No change made**;
  logged so the author can rule.

### 4. The prose gate

- Audit re-ran the scanner independently. 0 HARD failures, 6 chapters in the D2 warn
  band, all under cap. The audit confirms the composing log's numbers.
- **A passing scan is not evidence the prose is good.** The audit notes three passages
  that read as flat and are protected by nothing: Ch 1's roads-and-wall paragraph, Ch 7's
  legal-code paragraph, Ch 11's machinery paragraph. All three are load-bearing
  exposition. Flagged for the evaluator, not fixed here.

### 5. Unresolved ledger conflicts

- `CONF-001` is open and the audit **cannot clear a phase whose open conflicts were never
  recorded** — it is recorded, so it does not block. It does, however, make the
  third-person claim provisional. The audit refuses to resolve it.

---

## Verdict

**Critical, gate-blocking findings: 0.** The phase may proceed to evaluation.

**Recorded risks:** (1) the confident narrator is a stylistic bet, not a defect; (2) the
direct address in Ch 11; (3) fourteen first-person judgements; (4) three flat exposition
passages.

**What the audit could not see:** whether the history is *true*. The audit checks the
manuscript against its own rules. It has no independent source of historical fact, and it
says so.

# 07 — Opening Strategy

## What Chapter 1 must not do

| Banned opening | Why it is banned here |
|---|---|
| A rhetorical question ("What makes a ruler legitimate?") | Reader R4 (hostile skeptic) reads it as an AI tell; the A19/A8 patterns. |
| A panorama ("For millennia, the Chinese empire…") | Costs the reader a page before anything happens; R5 (commuter) abandons. |
| A confession of scope ("This book is not a complete history…") | The author's anxiety, not a hook. |
| A date-and-reign recitation | Reads as a textbook. |

## The opening move

**Open on the act of standardisation, as an inventory of what a state can do to a
people.** The First Emperor's reforms arrive as a list of concrete changes — one script,
one set of weights, one axle-width for every cart in the realm — and the narrator treats
these as *technologies*, not as cultural flourishes. The claim lands before the man does:
here is a state that has decided that a people is a system it can be tuned.

The terracotta army is **withheld** until the chapter has earned it, and when it arrives
it arrives as the turn: an army of individuals, no two faces alike, buried to guard a man
who spent his reign abolishing individuality.

### First three sentences (target, to be drafted)

1. A short declarative establishing the narrator's authority and register.
2. A concrete, slightly barbed specific (a weight, a distance, a unit of grain).
3. The claim: what this chapter is actually about, without announcing it as a theme.

The hook type for Chapter 1 is **Concrete-Then-Claim** (see `outline.md` hook ledger).
Chapter 2 must not reuse it.

### The Shelf Test target (Chapter 1 only)

Reader simulating a bookshop browser, first page. Verdict target: **BUY**. The browsing
browser should, by the end of page one, know they are in the hands of a narrator with a
temper and a subject they did not expect to find interesting.

### The first-page promise

By the end of page one the reader should know:

- that the book is about **power and its justifications**, not battles;
- that the narrator is credible and slightly dangerous;
- that the first chapter is about a man who tried to *engineer* obedience, and that this
  will not end well.

## Chapter-shot openings (all 11)

The hook ledger below governs variety. No two consecutive chapters may use the same type.

| Ch | Hook type | Opening move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Concrete-Then-Claim | the standardisation inventory, then the terracotta army as turn |
| 2 | Two-Bodies (parallel) | two men, same year, different starts — the ravine |

# Voice Matrix — voice contracts

The matrix is the mould that shapes each voice. This document is **prescriptive**: the
drafting skill must follow it.

Status: **global contract active (V-01).** Per-figure cards are written in Phase 2.5.

---

## V-01 — Global narrative voice (the historian-narrator)

**Scope:** the whole book. **Status:** active.

### Who is speaking

A historian who has read the sources, does not worship them, and is willing to say so.
Not a lecturer and not a novelist. The narrator's authority comes from admitting the
edges of the record rather than from performing certainty.

### The register

- **Concrete before abstract.** A number, a name, a weight of grain, a distance in *li*
  — then the meaning. Never the meaning first.
- **Dry, not jokey.** Occasional understatement is the humour. No comic asides, no
  winking. A sentence may be quietly funny; it may not announce that it is funny.
- **Short declaratives carry the verdicts.** The tonal beat of the book is a plain
  sentence after a long one — the long sentence builds the case, the short one closes it.
- **Present at the source.** When the record is a text written by a man with a grudge,
  the narrator says so in the same breath as the fact.

### Voice under pressure

When the material turns to atrocity — the burned scholars, the corvée dead, the
massacre at Xuanwu Gate, the nine-year rebellion — the narration **shortens and stops
explaining**. Editorialising is withdrawn exactly where a lesser book would raise its
voice. The numbers are left to stand alone. Fragments are permitted here and only here,
and they must not run more than six deep (the prose gate's fragment rule).

### Anti-patterns this voice must never commit

| Forbidden | Why | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "delve", "tapestry", "seamless", "at its core", "testament to" | zero hits in the calibration corpus; the loudest AI tells | name the thing |
| `—and that changed everything—` style parenthetical dashes | Overprint-adjacent; the scanner caps them at 3/1k | a full stop and a new sentence |
| Rule-of-three lists of virtues ("wise, patient, and cruel") | D2, capped at 5/1k | two, or four, or one |
| Invented quotation | breaks the book's stated speech rule (F-006) | report the speech and mark it as staging |
| "The truth is simple" / "history is clear" | A13, zero tolerance | say which source disagrees |
| Present-tense historical narration used as a gimmick | not this book's device | past tense, consistently |
| Signposting ("In this chapter we will see…") | A11a/A19 | just begin |

### Benchmark samples

Voice-establishing sentences the drafting skill can measure against. These are *tone
targets*, not lines for the book:

> The first emperor did not need to be loved. He had arithmetic, and it was on his side.

> Every dynasty in this book faces the same problem, and every one of them solves it the
> same way: by finding a philosopher who will explain why the family on the throne
> deserves the throne.

> Sima Qian was given a choice between his life and his book. He kept the book, and the
> book kept him.

### Voice-breaking samples (deliberately excluded)

Written so the drafting skill can recognise the failure mode:

> ✗ The magnificent tapestry of Chinese imperial history is a seamless testament to the
>   enduring human spirit, delving into the profound truths at the core of power.

> ✗ And so we return, gentle reader, to the eternal question that haunts us all.

## Per-figure voice cards (Phase 2.5)

**A card here is not an invented idiolect.** These are real people whose words survive
only in other people's books. A card specifies **which source may speak for the figure**
and **what register that source gives them**, so the drafting skill never has to invent
a voice. Quotation is governed by F-006.

### The Blind Sort rule for nonfiction

The fiction form of the Blind Sort asks: cover the names — can you tell who is speaking?
The nonfiction form asks: **cover the attribution — can the reader tell which *kind of
document* they are in?** An edict must not read like a poem. A historian's staged speech
must not read like a letter. A hostile record must be audibly hostile.

| Register | Recognisable by | Chapters |
|---|---|---|
| **Edict / memorial** | impersonal, formulaic, ritual self-abasement before the throne, the words *We* and *Your servant* | 6, 7, 9 |
| **Staged speech** (from the *Records*) | presentational, balanced, written to be remembered — the historian is composing, and the book says so | 1, 2, 3, 8 |
| **Letter / personal document** | first person, argumentative, addressed to one named man | 3 (Ren An) |
| **Verse** | compressed, image-first, no exposition; quoted *as* verse and never paraphrased into prose | 5, 10 |
| **Hostile record** | accusatory, extravagant, self-contradicting — the excess is the evidence | 9 |
| **Narrator** | the global contract V-01 above | all |

### Figure cards

**F-C1 · Qin Shi Huang** — *may speak only through edict and through the *Records*.*
Register: imperial, terse, absolute. Never given interiority. Where the *Records*
reports a motive, the book attributes it: "the *Records* says he…".

**F-C2 · Li Si** — *speaks through his memorials and through the rat fable the *Records*
attributes to him.* Register: precise, upward-looking, always positioning himself. He
argues in favour of *whatever keeps him in the granary*.

**F-C3 · Han Feizi** — *speaks only from the page.* His essays are quoted, and they are
dry and savage. He gets no dialogue at all: he died before the empire he described.

**F-C4 · Liu Bang** — *the *Records* gives him the commoner's register, and it is
coarse.* Short sentences, few allusions, a tendency to say the practical thing in a room
of men expecting the noble thing. When staged speech puts eloquence in his mouth, the
book flags it.

**F-C5 · Xiang Yu** — *heroic register.* Longer, more formal, ornamented. His recorded
lines are the ones a tradition writes for a man it half-admires. The book lets the
ornament show and asks why the tradition needed it.

**F-C6 · Lu Jia** — *the courtier's aphorism.* One line does the work. Register: plain,
unanswerable, slightly insolent.

**F-C7 · Han Wudi** — *edicts and the *Records*.* Register: grand, imperious, and — in
the late crackdowns — visibly frightened. The fear is the character note.

**F-C8 · Sima Qian** — *the only figure whose own voice is his own.* The *Letter to Ren
An* is the one place a speaker in this book is unmediated. Register: controlled until it
is not; the famous passage where the control breaks is the book's benchmark for an
unforced human voice.

**F-C9 · Wang Mang** — *memorials, edicts, and the hostile later record.* Register:
sincere, scholarly, procedural — and the sincerity is the horror. The book never lets him
sound cynical.

**F-C10 · Cao Cao** — *his own poems and his own edicts.* Two registers in one man: the
verse is spacious and grieving; the edicts are administrative and cold. The gap between
them is the chapter's whole argument, and the book must not smooth it.

**F-C11 · Liang Wudi** — *edicts and verse.* Register: pious, generous in the documents,
poetic in the siege. The Bodhidharma interview is quoted as *tradition*, not as transcript.

**F-C12 · Sui Wendi / Sui Yangdi** — *the machinery, not the men.* Register: budgets and
edicts. Where the sources give either man a personality, the book reports the source's
interest in doing so.

**F-C13 · Tang Taizong** — *edicts, the *Zhenguan* record, and staged speech.* Register:
self-conscious, aware of being watched by history. He is the one figure who talks like a
man who has read the record that will judge him. The book uses that.

**F-C14 · Wei Zheng** — *memorials.* Register: blunt to the point of insolence, formal to
the point of fastidiousness. The combination is the joke and the point.

**F-C15 · Wu Zetian** — *edicts she issued and a record written by her enemies.* The book
never gives her a private voice, because none survives. The blank stele is the closest
she comes to speaking.

**F-C16 · Li Bai / Du Fu** — *their poems, and nothing else.* Li Bai: expansive, drunk,
celestial. Du Fu: specific, dated, domestic, devastating. Cover the names and the two
registers must be distinguishable, or the chapter has failed.

**F-C17 · Zhu Xi** — *commentary.* Register: systematic, patient, exhaustive. He is the
only thinker in the book whose voice is a *method*, and the chapter's point is what
happens when a method becomes a syllabus.

### Anti-pattern checklist for figure voice

1. No invented quotation (F-006).
2. No figure uses a word or concept unavailable to them (Case Tracking enforces).
3. No two registers blurred: an edict does not become a poem.
4. Hostile sources are *heard as hostile*, not laundered into neutral narration.
5. The narrator never ventriloquises a figure's opinion as settled fact.

# Outline

Eleven chapters. This is a **living document**: when the drafting skill's self-report
records a deviation that was kept, the outline is updated to match what actually
happened, not what was planned.

**Structural ledger**

`1 chronological · 2 parallel · 3 essayistic · 4 reverse · 5 fragmented · 6 epistolary · 7 chronological · 8 spiral · 9 stream · 10 chronicle-with-interludes · 11 essayistic`

**Hook ledger** (Phase 3.2 — no consecutive repeats)

`1 concrete-then-claim · 2 two-bodies · 3 text-first · 4 death-first · 5 fragment-stack · 6 question-dramatised · 7 object-first · 8 scene · 9 document · 10 poem-interlude · 11 ledger`

Each chapter block below carries: era, pairing, structural approach, emotional anchor,
emotional surprise, chaos moment, the argument it destroys, and its hook type.

## Chapter 1 — The Book Burner
**Era** Qin, 221–210 BCE · **Pairing** Qin Shi Huang / Li Si + Han Feizi · **Structure** chronological · **Hook** concrete-then-claim

- **Beats.** The standardisation inventory. Han Feizi's doctrine quoted. The book burn of
  213 and the buried scholars of 212, with the scholarly dispute over the count flagged
  in the text. Li Si's rats. The elixir, the mercury, the death, the tomb.
- **Emotional anchor.** The terracotta army — individual faces, buried to guard the man
  who abolished the individual.
- **Emotional surprise.** The death is nearly farcical, and the farce is the indictment.
- **Chaos moment.** Li Si's latrine-rat and granary-rat fable.
- **Destroys.** *"Law alone is enough."*
- **Closes.** The tomb sealed, the craftsmen sealed in with it.
- **Target length** ~2,800 words.

## Chapter 2 — Two Men at the Edge of a Ravine
**Era** 210–195 BCE · **Pairing** Liu Bang / Xiang Yu / Lu Jia · **Structure** parallel · **Hook** two-bodies

- **Beats.** Xiang Yu's rise and battlefield dominance. Liu Bang's peasant banditry and
  his losses. The contention, alternating. Gaixia and the song about the horse and the
  woman. The Wu River refusal. Liu Bang's court and Lu Jia's line about horseback.
- **Emotional anchor.** Xiang Yu, ashamed to face the elders of Jiangdong.
- **Emotional surprise.** The reader admires the loser, and the admiration is what cost
  him the empire.
- **Chaos moment.** Liu Bang's sprawled legs and the public ritual correction of them.
- **Destroys.** *"Only the good man wins."*
- **Closes.** Lu Jia: the Han needs a philosophy now. The book's hinge.
- **Target length** ~2,700 words.

## Chapter 3 — The Historian's Silence
**Era** 141–87 BCE · **Pairing** Han Wudi / Sima Qian · **Structure** essayistic · **Hook** text-first

- **Beats.** The *Records* quoted as text. Han Wudi's expansion and its bill. Li Ling, the
  defence, the sentence. The choice. The *Letter to Ren An*. The fairness Sima Qian shows
  his mutilator.
- **Emotional anchor.** The decision, not the pain.
- **Emotional surprise.** The peak is not the mutilation but the fairness afterward.
- **Chaos moment.** The *Records* passage where he records his own humiliation in the
  first person, then closes the door.
- **Destroys.** *"The record will vindicate the good."*
- **Closes.** The method: you cannot trust the record, therefore read it carefully.
- **Target length** ~2,700 words.

## Chapter 4 — The Usurper Who Read the Classics
**Era** 9–23 CE · **Pairing** Wang Mang / the Confucian canon · **Structure** reverse · **Hook** death-first

- **Beats, in reverse.** 23 CE: the capital burning, Wang Mang consulting the classics.
  The reforms that broke the currency and the land. The scholar's rise. The regency and
  the abdication theatre. The moment he believed it.
- **Emotional anchor.** The last page — the classics in his hands while the city burns.
- **Emotional surprise.** Not contempt. Something closer to grief.
- **Chaos moment.** His compulsive measurement of everything against the ancient texts.
- **Destroys.** *"If the ruler truly believes the classics, the state will flourish."*
- **Closes.** Li Si's rat again: position changed, character unchanged.
- **Target length** ~2,600 words.

## Chapter 5 — A Poet Among Warlords
**Era** 184–220 CE · **Pairing** Cao Cao / Pure Conversation · **Structure** fragmented · **Hook** fragment-stack

- **Beats.** Yellow Turban revolt as the moment belief fails. Three documents stacked: an
  edict, a poem, a list of the dead. Xu Province, with the death-toll dispute flagged.
  The poem on mortality quoted *after*. Pure Conversation.
- **Emotional anchor.** The poem placed after the massacre, offered as neither redemption
  nor condemnation.
- **Emotional surprise.** The poetry does not exculpate him; it also does not convict him.
- **Chaos moment.** The poem itself.
- **Destroys.** *"A cultured ruler is a gentler one."*
- **Closes.** Orthodoxy gone; three centuries of division ahead.
- **Target length** ~2,500 words.

## Chapter 6 — The Emperor Who Fed the Monks
**Era** 502–549 CE · **Pairing** Liang Wudi / Bodhidharma · **Structure** epistolary · **Hook** question-dramatised

- **Beats.** The edicts funding the sangha. The interview: *how much merit have I earned?*
  — *None.* The ordination economy and the tax rolls. Hou Jing's rebellion. Death by
  starvation in his own capital.
- **Emotional anchor.** "None."
- **Emotional surprise.** The chapter is genuinely comic, which makes the starvation worse.
- **Chaos moment.** The emperor writing poetry while besieged.
- **Destroys.** *"A religious ruler is a merciful one."*
- **Closes.** The sangha's wealth outlives the emperor who built it.
- **Target length** ~2,600 words.

## Chapter 7 — The Canal and the Gate of Examinations
**Era** 581–618 CE · **Pairing** Sui Wendi / Sui Yangdi · **Structure** chronological · **Hook** object-first

- **Beats.** Reunification. The canal measured in *li*, in *shi*, in lives. The first
  examination recruitment. Yangdi's Korean campaigns and the conscription. The collapse,
  and the two dynasties that inherit the machinery.
- **Emotional anchor.** The canal: a line of water dug by people whose names are not
  recorded.
- **Emotional surprise.** The builders were not monsters; the institution was, and they
  were merely competent.
- **Chaos moment.** Yangdi's grand tours down the canal he killed to build.
- **Destroys.** *"Build the institutions and the people will follow."*
- **Closes.** The Tang inherits a working machine and a poisoned name.
- **Target length** ~2,600 words.

## Chapter 8 — The Mirror and the Minister
**Era** 626–649 CE · **Pairing** Tang Taizong / Wei Zheng · **Structure** spiral · **Hook** scene

- **Beats.** Xuanwu Gate. The Zhenguan decade and Wei Zheng's rudeness. The three mirrors.
  Wei Zheng's death and Taizong's grief. The motif turned on the book itself.
- **Emotional anchor.** The same man, months apart: at Xuanwu Gate, and listening to Wei
  Zheng.
- **Emotional surprise.** The book refuses to resolve him, and the refusal is the answer.
- **Chaos moment.** Taizong kicking over the tablet of Wei Zheng's advice, then restoring it.
- **Destroys.** *"A good ruler listens to criticism."* — Yes. And murdered his brothers.
- **Closes.** The mirror, turned on the book.
- **Target length** ~2,800 words.

## Chapter 9 — The Woman Who Became Son of Heaven
**Era** 690–705 CE · **Pairing** Wu Zetian / the wordless stele · **Structure** stream · **Hook** document

- **Beats.** The edict that had to invent language to name her. The promotions of
  commoners. The accusations, flagged as a hostile record. Two decades of competent rule.
  The blank stele.
- **Emotional anchor.** The blank stele.
- **Emotional surprise.** Not "unlike other rulers" — a ruler exactly like the others,
  destroyed by a system that could not forgive her competence.
- **Chaos moment.** The hostile record's own extravagance, used as evidence.
- **Destroys.** *"Legitimacy follows the rules."*
- **Closes.** The stele, and the verdict left to the reader.
- **Target length** ~2,600 words.

## Chapter 10 — The Golden Age and the General Who Ended It
**Era** 712–763 CE · **Pairing** Xuanzong / Li Bai / Du Fu / An Lushan · **Structure** chronicle with interludes · **Hook** poem-interlude

- **Beats.** Interlude: Li Bai. The height. An Lushan's rise inside the system. The
  rebellion. Mawei. Interlude: Du Fu and the child.
- **Emotional anchor.** Du Fu's poem, dated.
- **Emotional surprise.** The golden age is destroyed by the favourite the emperor trusted.
- **Chaos moment.** Du Fu's dead child — the age's greatest poet recording its smallest
  horror.
- **Destroys.** *"The golden age can be kept."*
- **Closes.** The Tang survives, diminished; the poetry outlasts the throne.
- **Target length** ~2,700 words.

## Chapter 11 — The Age of the Literati
**Era** 960–1200 CE · **Pairing** the Song throne / Zhu Xi · **Structure** essayistic · **Hook** ledger

- **Beats.** The examination syllabus, item by item. The Song settlement: civil over
  military. Zhu Xi's commentaries as standard. Philosophy as a career. The question held
  open.
- **Emotional anchor.** The commentaries on the test everyone must pass.
- **Emotional surprise.** The literati won, and it settled nothing.
- **Chaos moment.** The examination candidate's desperation.
- **Destroys.** *"Then the philosophers will rule instead."*
- **Closes.** The question handed to the reader, unanswered.
- **Target length** ~2,600 words.

## Continuity constraints the drafting skill must honour

- **Spelling and naming** follow pinyin, with the conventional English forms for the
  figures whose *Wade-Giles* names are more familiar in older sources (e.g. *Tao* for
  *Dao* only inside quoted titles). First occurrence gives the form used thereafter;
  Case Tracking (`ENTITY_STATE.yaml`) is the authority.
- **Dates** use BCE/CE and are stated at first mention. Where a date is disputed, say so.
- **Reign years vs. life years** are never conflated.
- **No figure may know something before it is available to them.** The narrator may
  foreshadow; a character may not.

# Readership

Five readers. **PRIMARY** drives writing decisions. **HOSTILE** drives the evaluator's
simulation. **STRETCH** is an adjacent audience the book might reach.

---

## R1 — Marc, 47, the armchair historian — **PRIMARY**

**Who.** Engineering manager, reads 15–20 trade histories a year, mostly on long flights.
Has read one book on China (*Wild Swans*, or Keay's *China*) and remembers the outline.
Not a specialist; will not tolerate being lectured to.

**Reading psychology.** Reads for *the click* — the moment a foreign century suddenly
makes sense as a machine with parts. He is happiest when a book explains an institution
he can compare to something he already knows (a bureaucracy, an exam, a tax system).

**What he wants from this book.** A spine he can hold while the dynasties go by. He will
remember the *pairings* — ruler plus thinker — long after he forgets the dates.

**Deal-breakers.**
- Being talked down to, or conversely being dropped into unglossed terms.
- A chapter that is a list of events with no argument.
- Confident claims about what a dead man "felt".

**Emotional triggers.** Competence, and its cost. Men who build something real and are
destroyed by it. Dry humour in the middle of horror.

**Discovery.** Print + Kindle; buys from a review in a broadsheet or a history podcast.

**What to protect for him.** The pairing structure, the grain-and-numbers concreteness,
and the narrator who says when the record is thin.

## R2 — Priya, 31, the ideas reader — **STRETCH (adjacent genre)**

**Who.** Reads philosophy and popular science; has read the *Analects* in a Penguin
translation and a podcast's worth of Daoism. Comes for the argument, not the chronology.

**Reading psychology.** Wants to be able to *say* something when she finishes. Underlines
sentences. Judges a book by whether it gives her a sentence she can use in a conversation.

**What she wants.** The four or five rival answers to *what legitimises a ruler*, and a
clear account of why each failed. She is the reader the shareability score exists for.

**Deal-breakers.** Hand-waving over a real contradiction; a book that resolves a genuine
historical ambiguity for tidiness.

**Emotional triggers.** A clean, quotable formulation of a hard idea. Intellectual vertigo
(the Chapter 8 refusal-to-resolve).

---

## R3 — Wei, 24, the student of China — **HOSTILE (accuracy)**

**Who.** Undergraduate in East Asian studies; reads everything in this space; knows the
standard English sources and will notice.

**Reading psychology.** Reading *against* the book, testing it. Her default hypothesis is
that a populist retelling will get the basics wrong and over-claim on the exotic.

**Deal-breakers.** A wrong date; a legend presented as fact without flagging; a
Confucian/Daoist/Legalist mix-up; "the Chinese believed…" flattening a contested record.

**Emotional triggers (positive).** Being surprised by a book's honesty about the limits
of the sources. She rewards precision.

**How the book wins her.** The speech rule (F-006), the reported-vs-invented distinction,
and explicitly flagging contested material (e.g. the buried scholars, the Bodhidharma
interview, the An Lushan death toll).

---

## R4 — Dale, 52, the hostile skeptic — **HOSTILE (the AI test)**

**Who.** Reads a lot of history, and has begun to assume any smooth popular history might
be machine-written. Opens the sample looking for the tells.

**Reading psychology.** Looking for the *sesame-and-tapestry* register: the symmetrical
aphorism, the rule of three, the "and that changed everything", the summary paragraph
that says nothing. The moment he finds one, he is gone — and he will say so in a review.

**Deal-breakers.**
- Any of the capped/forbidden patterns in `voice-matrix.md` § anti-patterns.
- A chapter that opens with a rhetorical question.
- The word "journey" used to mean history.
- Syntactic sameness: every paragraph containing exactly one version of the same move.

**How the book wins him.** The prose gate — the actual scanned result, and prose that
reads as if someone with a temper wrote it. Short sentences where the material is
brutal. One deliberately rough sentence per chapter.

---

## R5 — Talia, 38, the commuter — **STRETCH (momentum)**

**Who.** Listens to audiobooks on a commute; retains maybe 40% of any book, but
recommends the ones she finishes.

**Reading psychology.** Momentum over density. She needs a reason to start Chapter 3 at
the top of the second commute.

**What she needs.** Strong hooks at chapter ends (the `catchword` pass), a clear
recurring motif (the mirror) she can latch onto, and figures she can keep straight
(hence the pairings, and hence the case-tracking discipline).

**Deal-breaker.** A chapter that starts with three paragraphs of context before anything
happens.

# Market notes — `scout`

Phase 1 research, condensed. **All comp titles are candidates and unapproved** — this is
the position of record and the reason the Market dimension of the score is held down.

## What the shelf looks like

Three shapes dominate English-language popular Chinese history:

1. **The continuous survey.** One volume, many dynasties, strong chronology, thin
   argument. The default, and the reason the category reads as homework.
2. **The thinker biography.** Confucius, Laozi, Zhuangzi, treated as philosophers with
   the state as background.
3. **The ruler biography.** One emperor, one reign, with the philosophy as scenery.

## The gap

Nothing on the shelf puts the ruler and the philosopher on the same page and asks which
one won. That is the book's position and it is a genuine whitespace, not a claim of
novelty for its own sake: the *structure* is the differentiator, and structures are what
trade readers navigate by.

## What general readers reward in this niche

- **A spine to hold.** An organising idea that survives the dynasties.
- **Institutions explained as machines**, because a reader who has run an organisation
  will recognise a tax register, an examination, and a canal.
- **Honesty about the record.** The genre rewards a narrator who says "we do not know"
  where a lesser book asserts.

## What the shelf punishes

- **Encyclopedic stretches.** Reportage of intellectual movements without a human stake.
  (This is exactly the weakness the evaluations found in Chapter 5.)
- **Invented colour.** A reader who knows the period will catch a fabricated scene, and
  the book's authority rests on not having any.
- **Titles that promise a thesis the book refuses to deliver.** The book refuses to
  answer its question, so the packaging must promise the *question*, not the answer.

## Word count and length

The category tolerates 90,000–140,000 words for a serious trade history. This run produced
28,263 words — a deliberate test scale, honestly labelled, not a submission length.

## Recommendation carried into Foundation

Primary engagement type **Intellectual**, secondary **Fascination**, tertiary **Empathy**
— chosen because the book's appeal is the argument, and the argument's flavour is moral
complexity rather than comfort.

## Logline

A county records clerk discovers that the report the county says was never written is
filed in the wrong year — and that the man whose name was removed from the county's record
fifteen years ago is the one knocking down the wall it is hidden in.

## The county

**Marrow County**, fictional, on the Virginia–Kentucky border. County seat **Ansel**,
population about 18,000. The Marrow County Courthouse (1897, additions through 1962) is
the largest building in the county, which tells you what the county thinks it is.

Behind it, joined by a single connecting corridor, is the **jail annex** (1911). It is
scheduled for demolition.

## The cast

**Wren Castleberry, 44.** County records clerk, nineteen years in the basement. Not a
detective, has never been a detective, left the county once for a library-science year in
Charlottesville and did not finish it. Her competence is custodial and archival: she knows
what the county can be made to say. Her fear is being wrong in a way that ends up on paper.

**Evan Tallis, 61.** Off the county's record since 1996. State building inspector, no
credentials the county recognises. Fifteen years of hand-kept parallel records because the
county will not produce the ones it holds. He is **Ruthie Barlow's father**.

**Hollis Vance, 68.** County commissioner, thirty years in office. Did nothing criminal
in 1973 and has spent fifty years being careful about that fact.

**Dr. Ellis Caudle, 79.** The county's only physician in 1973. Signed the death
certificate. Died in 2011 according to the county index. **Alive.** Dying now, of the
thing he has been carrying.

**Ruthie Barlow, 15.** Died in the north stair of the annex, 1973. Never depicted.

**Sheriff Doyle.** Saw Caudle in a place he should not have been, in 1973, and has said
nothing for fifty years for procedural reasons rather than moral ones.

**Wren's mother, 81.** Was on the 1973 inquest. Remembers it slightly wrong.

## The mechanism (fair-play, documented, solvable)

In 1973 the annex's north stair failed. A fifteen-year-old died in it. Official cause:
fall, accidental.

The county's position since: there is no 1973 structural inspection report, and therefore
nothing to disclose, and therefore the annex can come down.

**The report was never destroyed. It was moved — by Ruthie Barlow herself, in 1974, into
the wall cavity of the stair she died in.** The one place in Marrow County with no filing
requirement. So the answer to "where is the report" is inside the building scheduled for
demolition, and the clock is the wrecking date.

Two parties want the same document for opposite reasons:
- **Vance** wants it *gone*, because the annex cannot be demolished without disclosing that
  the county knew the stair was unsafe in 1973 and did nothing for fifty years.
- **Tallis** wants it *found*, because it is the lever that forces the county to produce it,
  and he has been holding a lever alone for fifteen years.

## The wound (why it is not a puzzle but a person)

In 1996 a junior clerk certified a records request. Her name was **Wren Castleberry**. She
never checked who asked. A man named Evan Tallis ceased to exist in every Marrow County
system that day, and she has spent fifteen years not knowing whether he was alive, dead, or
erased. Her signature is in the 1996 file. She has read it more times than she has read
anything.

**The lie the book is built on:** *the record is neutral — it says what happened and has no
opinion.* The lie is load-bearing, because her entire professional identity is the
neutrality of the archive, and she made one request true by not looking.

## Theme — as a question, never an answer

> What does a county owe the record it keeps, when the record is the only thing in the room
> that cannot be intimidated?

The ending is permitted to leave **Wren's culpability** ambiguous. That ambiguity is
required, not deferred — see `content_boundaries` in `PROJECT_STATE.yaml`.

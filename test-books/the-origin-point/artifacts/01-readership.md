# READERSHIP — The Origin Point

**Phase 1.5.** Four reader segments. Used by the Writer (voice calibration), the
Evaluator (reader simulation in Phase 4), and the packager (Phase 6 targeting).

**Derived from the Phase 1 findings, not invented.** `/scout` established that the
market has **bifurcated** (`research/market-research.md` §3): readers who want escape
and readers who want the dark/deceptive end, with no quiet middle, and that the
2025 best-of 20 contains no quiet documentary procedural at all. `premise.md` §6.5
therefore records a **deliberate exclusion of the escape half**.

These four are the readers this book is actually written for. **A fifth — the cozy
escape reader — is named and excluded on purpose.** Deleting it from this file would
make the exclusion invisible, and an invisible exclusion is how a book ends up
disappointing the half of the market that actually places.

---

## R-01 — The Procedure Junkie

**Age/context:** 35–60, reads 2–4 mysteries a month, mostly on a commute or late at
night. DNFs at chapter 6. Buys on the first night and abandons silently.

**Buys this for:** the competence. She wants to be in a room with someone who knows
more than she does, watching them be good at it. Rusk reading a burn pattern is the
product.

**Emotional trigger:** the moment the professional's read conflicts with the
official account. Being *right* in front of people who have decided to be polite.

**Deal-breaker:** a protagonist who is competent at nothing and good at feelings.
**Hard deal-breaker:** a mystery where the detective explains the solution instead of
demonstrating it.

**How they find it:** Amazon mystery charts, Kirkus, an endorsement from a name they
already trust. **Will finish if** the origin point is on the page in chapter 2. **Will
not forgive** an investigation that stalls for three chapters of town meetings.

**What this book must deliver:** the technical read has to be *real and specific*.
Origin point, pour pattern, ventilation, container type. She will know if it is
hand-waving, and she is the segment most likely to say so in a review.

## R-02 — The Family-Institution Skeptic

**Age/context:** 40–65. Grew up somewhere small. Reads to find out whether small
places are as bad as she remembers. Slower reader, high completion, high
recommendation rate.

**Buys this for:** the town. Specifically, the specific flavour of collective
plausible deniability that this book is built out of. She recognises it before
anyone confesses anything.

**Emotional trigger:** the unanimity. Seven people giving the same helpful answer
(ledger item 13) should make her feel *seen*, not suspenseful.

**Deal-breaker:** a villain with a face. **Hard deal-breaker:** a plot that resolves
because one person breaks. This segment is here for the diffuse antagonist, and a
single arsonist at the end is a partial satisfaction at best.

**How they find it:** Goodreads lists, BookTok adjacent, word of mouth, author
backlist. **Will finish if** the ensemble stays seven and stays distinct. **Will not
forgive** a finale that reduces the town to one man's confession.

**What this book must deliver:** the ensemble must remain a *method* through Act IV.
Ch 28 exists specifically for her — the town does not disperse, does not confess,
does not change.

## R-03 — The Fair-Play Solver

**Age/context:** 25–45, reads the last page first. Aware of the tradition. Actively
hostile to being manipulated. Will not buy a mystery they cannot solve.

**Buys this for:** the puzzle. They are the reason `dimension_7` is Solvability and
not Market.

**Emotional trigger:** the click. Ledger items 1→3, 8, 11 and 12 in that order are
the intended click-path, and `research/clue-ledger-solve.md` walks it.

**Deal-breaker:** a withheld interior view. A clue that is only visible in a scene
the reader was not in is cheating, and this segment is the one that will notice and
name it. **Hard deal-breaker:** a second crime whose perpetrator is withheld from the
reader to be sprung late.

**How they find it:** r/RTFMR, Detective Fiction subreddit, Goodreads lists, genre
podcasts. **Will finish if** all 15 ledger items are planted by ch 18 and none depend
on a withheld view. **Will not forgive** item 13 unless it is stated as an observation
rather than left as a shape — which is exactly what the independent solve caught and
fixed.

**What this book must deliver:** the ledger must be honoured literally. This is the
one segment where a "nice touch" that quietly breaks fair play is worse than no touch.

---

## R-04 — The Deception Reader

**Age/context:** 30–55. `/scout` found the dark half of the bifurcation is where
propulsive, deceptive-narrator books place. This is the segment `premise.md` is aimed
at.

**Buys this for:** the turn. Wants to be wrong about someone they liked.

**Emotional trigger:** the moment a helper is revealed to have been managing what
people saw. Roy's kindness only when observed (item 10) is aimed here.

**Deal-breaker:** moral ambiguity everywhere. **Hard deal-breaker:** a book that
refuses to let anything be solved. This segment is on the dark side *because* of F1,
and F1's other half said readers want satisfactory closure — which is why A8 was
conceded and Rusk's complicity is resolved on the page at ch 23.

**How they find it:** CrimeReads, Amazon thrillers/mystery charts, adaptation-adjacent
press. **Will finish if** the case closes in ch 22 and the cost lands in ch 23–24.
**Will not forgive** an ending that protects the protagonist at the antagonist's
expense.

**What this book must deliver:** the closure has to be real. `CONF-004` is exactly
this segment's test — if Terrell reads as let off, this is the segment that says so.

---

## Excluded, on purpose

**R-05 — The Cozy Escape Reader.** Wants puzzle + community + a relatable
protagonist who makes a difference. A substantial and, per `/scout`, at least
half of the bifurcated market.

**Why excluded:** `premise.md` §6.5 states the book takes the dark side deliberately.
Three bodies and a theme about a town's silence do not serve this reader, and
pretending otherwise would produce a book that serves nobody.

**Recorded as `A12`** — untested. **`A3-P3`** is the live risk: *Picking Up the
Pieces* is a closer structural neighbour to this book's ensemble than anything on
the comp list, and it is a cozy book. If Phase 4 reader simulation says the dark-side
positioning is costing more than it buys, **A3-P3 is the thing to re-examine** —
not the prose.

## Segment priority for the Writer

| rank | segment | why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **R-03 Fair-Play Solver** | the book is unplayable without this segment satisfied |
| 2 | **R-01 Procedure Junkie** | the technical specificity is the differentiator and the easiest thing to fake badly |
| 3 | **R-04 Deception Reader** | the closing segment — closure is the thing they are buying |
| 4 | **R-02 Skeptic** | highest long-tail value, least demanding, weakest pull |

**Read these in that order when a scene is ambiguous.** If a choice serves R-03 and
R-01, it is right. If it serves only R-02, it is a luxury. If it serves R-01 and
hurts R-03, the scene is a cheat and the scene is wrong.

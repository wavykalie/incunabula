# 29 — Rusk Stays

The job, the office, the region, the offer. Four doors. They arrived in the same week in March, as if March had decided to interview her — and she laid them on the kitchen table in a row, the way she laid exhibits, and considered each in turn the way the file taught her: in order, with times, no softening.

The offer came on a Tuesday, in writing, on heavy paper, from the regional office in the city: senior investigator, fire science unit, salary a third above hers, pension, a flat allowance, a start date in spring. The letter praised her 2025 certification work — the irony sat in the room like a fourth person while she read it — and mentioned the reopened 2019 file as evidence of initiative. Initiative. She put the letter down and laughed once, briefly, alone.

Fenn brought it to her himself, knocked, set it on her desk without comment, and stood there with his hands folded while she read the first lines.

"They want an answer by April," he said. "They will wait past April if I tell them to. I am telling you that so you know waiting is allowed."

"Did you recommend me?"

"I recommended the file." Precise, Fenn-like. "The file did the rest. Files do, when they are built right."

The office was the second door: the county post, hers if she wanted it, permanent, effective immediately. Same desk. Same depot road with the furnace through the wall. Same fourteen-day memos. No allowance. No pension increase. The same chair with the broken caster. She knew its every stain. Fenn had the form ready in his drawer — he showed her, without pushing, the way he showed everything: here it is, it exists, decide.

The region was the third: a transfer sideways to a neighbouring county, two hours east, where nobody knew her mother's bins or the roster or the hymnals or the fast clock. A clean file, clean street, clean clock. The transfer officer telephoned on Wednesday — pleasant, brisk, a woman who had clearly read the file and decided Rusk was a asset with a complicated address. "Fresh ground," she said. "No history. Some investigators do their best work with no history."

The fourth door was leaving altogether. A private firm in the city — arson defence, the other side of every table she had ever sat at — telephoned twice, Thursday and Friday. More money than the three public doors combined. No certifications. No tapes. No 07:18 signatures. "You know how files are built," the man said. "We need someone who knows how they are built." She thanked him and did not call back, and he telephoned again anyway, which told her everything about how that side worked.

Rusk sat with the four for a week. She made lists, dated them, the way she did everything — pros, cons, the exhibit method applied to her own life. She walked each door in her head to its end: the city flat with the allowance, the clean county with no history, the defence office with its money, the depot road with its furnace.

She asked Fenn what he would do. They stood in the records store with the grey boxes around them, and he said, "I invented a clause in 2009 so I could stay in a county that paid me less than the city. That is my answer. It has been my answer for seventeen years. Clauses are answers that look like procedure."

She asked Whitlock. They sat in the car outside the depot with the engine running, rain on the windscreen, and he said, "You walked Mercer and counted doors. Nine of them. Twice." He wiped the glass with his sleeve. "People who leave do not count. Counting is staying that has not admitted it yet."

She did not ask Cora. She did not ask Terrell. Those were the two doors that mattered most and the two people she could least ask — one awaiting whatever the attorney decided about distinctions, one holding a remand with counsel. She knew better than to ask them to choose for her. Their answers would have been love, and love was not paper, and this decision needed to be paper.

On the Sunday she drove the four routes. The city road — two hours, good surface, getting wider as it went, the radio fading and returning. The eastern county road — narrower, hedges high, a hawk over a field, no history in either direction. The depot road — furnace, potholes, Fenwick's wave from the counter window. And Mercer — nine houses, porch, gable, hip, glass, the studies at the back, 127 with its bins in order and its clock eleven minutes fast and its kitchen window glowing at dusk.

She parked at the end of Mercer and sat with the engine off the way she had sat outside 127 and not gone in. The four doors stood in her head in a row. City, east, defence, county. Money, freshness, money again, furnace.

What decided it was not the lists. The lists came out nearly even — the city led on money and work, the county led on nothing countable. What decided it was the study windows at the back of the nine houses, visible above the fences in the failing light, and the thought that came with them, unbidden, in her own voice for once rather than her mother's: someone needs to be the person who asked once and will ask again. The town's agreement had been named and was continuing anyway — she had watched it continue, visit by visit, all that week. Naming had not broken it. Only watching would keep it honest. And watching required staying.

On the Monday she went to the depot early, before seven, and walked the corridor past the interview room where she had laid twelve exhibits in a row, past Fenn's office where she had signed her name under her name at 17:02, past the records store where she had measured eleven blocks three times on a Sunday with a stopwatch. She put the regional offer in the bottom drawer, under the spare forms. She put the private firm's card in the bin beside the desk — torn once, the halves separate. She took the county form from Fenn's drawer with his nod, and wrote on it in her shorthand that four hundred determinations had made into muscle: request county post, permanent, effective immediately, reason stated as investigation ongoing.

Reason stated as investigation ongoing. That was true — the inquiry continued without a clock, and she was its person — and it was not the whole truth. The whole truth was that she had signed her complicity on paper and named her nephew on tape and heard her mother say the theme aloud at 16:47 with flour on her hands, and leaving now, for money or freshness, would be a different kind of not-opening a door. She had spent six years not opening the study door of her own file. She would not spend the next six not opening the county's.

Fenn countersigned without surprise, the pen upright, the blotter applied. "Ostensibly for the investigation," he said — quoting her own outline back at her without knowing it, the way the town quoted things without knowing.

"Ostensibly," she agreed, and they both understood, and neither wrote the rest down.

She took the county post that kept her in the county. For the investigation, ostensibly. In fact because nine houses on Mercer had the same study and the same clock-skew and the same agreement, and because a file with twelve exhibits needed its investigator within reach, and because her mother was four doors from a house with a new lock and an unused key, and because Denny logged gallons that someone had to read, and because the roster got checked every morning by a man who needed someone to notice if he stopped.

She drove home past 127 that evening. The kitchen window glowed. The bins were in. She did not stop — staying was not the same as watching, and watching was not the same as hovering. It was the same as being available when the next question came. Available had an address. Hers was now permanent, effective immediately.

That night she unpacked the flat properly for the first time in eleven years: books on shelves in order, photographs on walls squared, the two origin prints filed in the cabinet, not displayed. She bought food — real food, vegetables, bread that was not evidence. She slept in the bed, under the blanket, eight hours, like a person who had finished something and chosen somewhere.

She stays. That is the chapter. Four doors, one taken, three closed behind her without locking — because closed doors in her county were never locked, only shut, and someone had to remain who knew the difference.

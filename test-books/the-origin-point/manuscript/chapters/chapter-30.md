# 30 — Where It Began

The 2019 file is reopened. It is now a homicide file.

Rusk carried the box to the county attorney's office herself on a Friday morning in March, with the twelve exhibits numbered and the list signed and the docket bagged and the coroner's printout clipped to her correction. She had packed it the night before on her kitchen table, checking each seal a final time, initialling the cover sheet in duplicate. Twelve exhibits. Fifteen ledger items among them. Six years in a grey box.

The rain had stopped overnight. The road steamed in the early sun. She parked in the visitor bay and carried the box in both arms through the metal detector, and the guard signed her through without looking up, the way people do when a woman carries a box like it matters. The building smelled of floor wax and old paper. Her footsteps echoed on the lino.

The clerk took the box and weighed it in her hands, surprised at the heft. She opened the lid and checked the contents against the cover sheet, item by item, initialling each line with a pen on a chain: determination, panel record, receipt, March page, docket, list, statement, rota copy, coroner's sheets, photographs, chair frames, correction. Twelve exhibits. Fifteen ledger items among them. Statements signed, photographs mounted with scales visible, the suppressed finding with its timestamp, her correction timed 17:02.

The attorney came out of his office — a man she had seen once before, across a long table with three strangers — and read the cover sheet standing up, his glasses low on his nose. He turned the photographs toward the window light: two origin points, same V, same edge, six years apart. He studied them for a long time without speaking. The clerk waited. Rusk waited, her hands at her sides, the pink receipt already half-imagined in her pocket.

"Origin class?" he said at last.

"Poured. Both fires. Same hand."

"Deceased in 2019?"

"One. Female, fifty-nine. Blunt trauma from a fall, pre-mortem. The fire was set afterwards to close the question."

"And 2025?"

"Three. Set fire. Study. Named and documented. On tape with counsel present."

He nodded once. He took the stamp — heavy, wooden-handled, the kind that lasts decades — inked it carefully, and brought it down on the box lid, hard, twice to make it take: REOPENED — HOMICIDE. The sound of it went up into the ceiling and stayed there, the way sounds stay in rooms with no soft furnishings. Red ink, slightly smeared on the second strike. Permanent.

"Investigator of record?"

"Delphine Rusk."

He wrote it on the line in his careful hand and dated it with the stamp date. The clerk filed the receipt — pink, thin, already curling at the edges — and gave Rusk the copy. Rusk folded it once and put it in her inside pocket next to the memo she still carried folded from the Friday she lost standing on a pavement in February. The pocket that carried the whole case. Nearly empty now. Nearly done.

She walked back to the depot in light rain that started again halfway there — fine, misting rain that beaded on her coat. She went the long way, past nine houses she did not count this time, past the garage with Denny's light on and the sound of an engine turning over, past the church with its bad acoustics and its basement table now bare and back in storage. Mercer in the rain, gutters running, bins in their rows. Ordinary. The most ordinary street in the county, holding two fires and one agreement and twelve exhibits, going on.

At her desk, with the window open and the furnace off for the first time since February — spring coming through with wet earth on it — she wrote the last determination of the book on a clean form, in the shorthand that four hundred determinations had made into muscle, with her hand steady throughout. She dated it, timed it, and wrote without stopping, the way she wrote everything that mattered: in order, no softening.

*Reopened determination, 127 Mercer, 11 April 2019. Cause: homicide by person or persons unknown, by fire set to conceal blunt trauma sustained in a fall. Origin: study, poured accelerant, clean V-pattern, pooled not carried. Supporting: panel record (replaced 2016, no kitchen circuit); coroner's timestamped finding 11 March 2019; fuel records March 2019 / April 2025; witness list and voluntary statement; shift rota; chair measurements; origin photographs. Investigator: Delphine Rusk.*

She read it back twice. She corrected one word: persons to person, then back again, because the file could not prove who lifted her and who left her, only who lit the fire after. Persons unknown. The grammar of insufficiency, exact and honest. Let it stand.

She signed it — full name, rank, date. The name on it was hers: last on the reopened file, first on the new inquiry, the same hand that signed the wrong circuit at thirty-one signing the right finding at thirty-seven. Six years between the signatures. Everything between them.

She blotted it, filed the copy herself in the records store — Fenwick meeting her at the counter, gloves off for once, shaking her hand with both of hers — in the slot where the thin 2019 folder had sat for six years. She stood a moment with her hand on the box, feeling the weight of paper that finally told the shape of the burn. The slot was full now. The thin folder was gone, replaced, answered.

Outside, the street steamed in the rain coming to an end. Her mother's bins were in — black, green, brown, in order. The kitchen window glowed yellow, eleven minutes fast, as it always glowed, as it would go on glowing. She did not stop. The book ended with a signature, not a visit. The visit had already happened, at 16:47 on a Monday, with tea going cold and bread rising and a line spoken once that needed no exhibit number: I didn't light it. I made it true.

The last line is the reopened determination, and the name on it is hers.

# 12 — Roy Was There

Eleven blocks.

Rusk had measured them twice herself, in a car, on a Sunday, with a stopwatch, and then
she had measured them a third time because the first two had been done in different
traffic and she did not trust either.

Four minutes and forty seconds, door to door, at the speed of a residential street
with a stop sign at each end.

Eleven blocks is not eleven blocks in a volunteer response. In a volunteer response
eleven blocks is *somebody is putting on a coat and somebody else is finding the keys*
and it is nine minutes at the outside, and if it is a working fire it is eleven or
twelve, and if the garage is on a different road from the fire it is fifteen.

The 2019 determination said the first unit arrived at 21:09.

The call came in at 21:05.

There was no working fire that night. There was no alarm, no smell reported, no
suppression, nothing. A woman on the far side of the street had come out onto her own
front step at a quarter past nine on a Sunday in March and seen a glow in the window
of number 127 and had telephoned. She had said, on the statement Rusk had read, that
she had *assumed the neighbours were having a fire*, which is a sentence that tells you
everything about how often that happens on that street, or nothing at all, depending
on how much you want it to tell you.

21:05 to 21:09 is four minutes for eleven blocks and a turnout.

Rusk sat with it in the records store on the Friday, with the determination in front of
her and a second determination beside it, the 2025 one, the one she had signed herself
on a Tuesday morning at 07:18 without reading, which said first unit 04:31 against a
call at 04:12 — nineteen minutes, from a station with a crew in it, which was
ordinary and unremarkable and exactly what you would expect. Fenwick worked at the next
table, gloves whispering, filing 2025 photographs into sleeves. The radiator clicked.
Somewhere above, a truck reversed with its alarm going, three beeps, gone.

Same street. Same company. Same chief.

A nineteen-minute turnout in 2025 and a four-minute turnout in 2019. She wrote both numbers
on a card and stared at them until they stopped being numbers and became a man: a man in a
truck, somewhere he should not have been, driving too fast toward a house instead of a fire.
Then she turned the card over and wrote the question she would ask him tomorrow, in full, so
she would not flinch from it in the bay: where were you when the tones went, and why did
the document say eleven blocks when the road said four streets.

---

She went to him on the Saturday because there was no other way to do it and because
she had spent four days discovering that every other approach to Roy Halloran
produced the same result, which was that he was extremely helpful and gave her nothing
that could be written down.

He was in the garage bay at the company, doing something to a pump, and he had a radio
on the bench going and he turned it off when she came in, which she noticed, and which
she had a reaction to that she did not examine.

"Morning," he said. "There's coffee. It's not good coffee. It's the good coffee in the
sense that there's some."

She took it, because refusing it would have been a thing.

"You've been busy," he said. "Everybody's been saying. Myron at the garage said you
were round at Denny's on Wednesday and that Denny came out to the road to see you off,
which Denny would not do, Denny does not go out to the road."

"You've been asking about me."

"You've been round asking about a fire, it's a small town, I ask about people." He put
the wrench down. "What can I do for you, Delphine. And don't say *anything*, just ask
me the thing, I'll go quicker if you just ask me the thing."

Rusk put her notebook in her pocket. She had come with it and she had put it in her
pocket in the car park, and the putting of it in the pocket had been a decision she had
made in a car park and had been thinking about all the way up the road.

"I want to ask you about the eleventh of April," she said.

Roy Halloran's hands stopped.

"Nineteen eighty," he said.

"Eleven April 2019. The third of March 2019. Both times a volunteer was written out
for engine one, and both times a truck went out of a garage on the county road with
diesel in it, and on the eleventh of April there is a shift rota written on the back
of the docket in your company's pad, in your writing, with engine one on it and a time
against it."

"Yes."

"I want to know where you were."

He did not answer for a while. He went to the bench and picked the wrench up and put it
down again, and she understood that she had done the thing the voice bank had told her
he would do, which is answer a question about himself with something about what
happened, and he got as far as starting it and then stopped, and the stopping was
interesting.

"I was at 127 Mercer at nine o'clock on the third of March 2019," he said, "because I
am always at 127 Mercer, and your mother's bin was on the road again, and that is the
whole of why I know about that fire, because I was putting a bin back."

"That's not what I asked."

"I know it's not," said Roy Halloran. "I want you to notice that I've answered it
anyway, and I want you to notice that I've answered it with a thing that is true and
has got nothing in it, and I want you to decide what to do about that, because I've
been doing it since Tuesday and I don't think you have noticed yet."

The radio was off. The bay was very quiet and it was about forty degrees in there and
Rusk could hear the furnace.

"Ask me the other one," he said.

"What other one?"

"You've had four questions in your pocket since you walked in. You've had one and
you've been holding it and I've watched you hold it, and I'm not stupid, I'm a
sixty-one-year-old volunteer chief in a town of four hundred people, I can count." He
looked at her. "Ask me about the four minutes."

Rusk asked him about the four minutes.

Roy Halloran sat down on the bench.

"I was in the truck," he said. "I'm always in the truck, I'm engine one, it's me and
Denny and it rotates and that week it was me. I got the call at five past nine and I
was in the truck inside two minutes because I don't have a driveway, I park on the
street, I never had a driveway, I've never had a driveway in my life, I sleep in my
clothes when the tones go off, that's not a story, that's a Tuesday."

"And you drove eleven blocks in four minutes."

"No," said Roy Halloran. "I did not drive eleven blocks in four minutes, because I
did not start at 127, and I did not start at the house, and I did not come from my
own, and I want to be very careful here, because I know exactly what you have written
on your pad, which is that a man cannot do eleven blocks in four minutes, and you're
right, he can't, and he didn't, and that's not a lie I told, it's a document I didn't
write."

He looked at his hands.

"I was four streets over," he said. "At the house on Lindqvist, no — I'm sorry, I'll
do this properly. At the house across from Lindqvist's, the one with the green bin,
number 140, and I have been in that kitchen drinking coffee with a man called Verhage
since about half eight, and I have been there most nights since 2019, and I go there
because I cannot be in my own house after a fire and nobody knows that but me and
Verhage and now you.

"And at five past nine, in that kitchen, four streets over, a man I know well sat down
at the table and said, *well, that's Cora then* — and I want you to write that down, I
am saying it so you can write it down — and I went out the door and got in a truck and
got to 127 in three minutes fifty because I have driven that street for forty-one
years and I did not drive like a man going to a fire, I drove like a man going to a
house."

The furnace came on.

"So the determination says first unit 21:09," he said, "and that is true. That is the
first unit. That is my truck and Denny's truck and we came in from the corner of
Lindqvist and Green because that is the way in, and it is the correct way in, and it
is four streets, not eleven blocks, and the address is the address and nobody
anywhere is going to put down in a document that the chief of this volunteer company
lives eleven blocks from his mother's house and also happens to have been sitting in a
kitchen four streets away on the night, because those two facts together do not make a
man suspicious. Those two facts together make a man unlucky."

Rusk was standing very still in a garage bay in a town of four hundred people.

"Who called it in?"

"The schoolteacher's mother," said Roy Halloran. "The woman on the far side. At
twenty-five past nine, from her front step, having looked at the window for a
twenty-minute while she decided whether it was a fire. She said she assumed the
neighbours were having a fire. She was right. I was having a fire. In Verhage's
kitchen, at a quarter past nine, saying *well, that's Cora then*, and I have never in
six years told anybody that sentence, and I have said it to you because you came in
today with your notebook in your pocket and put it away, and that is the only reason
anybody has ever said it to anybody."

"Why did you not report it?"

Roy Halloran looked up.

"Because my mother and I had a fire," he said, "in 1988, in a house on Ridge, and I was
fourteen, and I got out, and I did not go back in for the dog, and everybody says the
dog and everybody is talking about a dog, and the dog was a dog, and the thing in that
house that was not a dog was me, and I have been the thing in that house ever since,
and I did not report a thing in 2019 because I did not want to be asked about 2019 in
a room, with a man writing it down, for the rest of my life."

He put his hands on his knees.

"And I want to say the next part because I've been not saying it since Tuesday and I'm
tired of not saying it, and it's the only reason I asked you to come out here and not
to the house."

"Roy."

"I did not go in," said Roy Halloran. "That's the thing. Everybody thinks I did. My
mother thinks I did, and the whole street thinks I did, and Verhage thinks I did, and
I've let them, because what am I going to say, *no, I got there in four minutes and
then I stood in the road?*

"Three minutes fifty. I was in that room before the first engine. I was in that room
with the door open and I had a flashlight and the service had already been pulled by
the time I got to the porch, somebody had cut the power at the meter, which is what
people do, and I went in through the front and I got as far as the kitchen and I could
see the study door and I could see that it was hot, and I did not open it."

He stopped for a moment.

"I stood in my mother's kitchen, in 2019, at nine o'clock in the evening, with a
flashlight and a working house and people coming in the street behind me, and I looked
at a closed door that was hot, and I did not open it, and I went back out to the road,
and I have been not opening doors on Mercer Street for six years."

In the records store, on the Friday, Rusk had written in the margin of the 2019
determination, in pencil, in her own hand, in letters smaller than her usual ones:

*Roy arrived in 4 min from 11 blocks. Inconsistent.*

*He did not say what he saw on the way.*

It was now Saturday morning in a garage bay four streets from the depot, and Roy
Halloran was sitting on a bench with his hands on his knees, and he had just told her
what he saw on the way, and what he had seen was nothing, and he had not gone in.

# VOICE BANK — 10 samples

**Phase 2.5 deliverable.** Ten short passages in the house style, one per voice risk,
written before the manuscript so the Writer calibrates against them rather than
discovering the register in chapter 1. **Register: quiet, not gentle. Declarative,
short. Nobody performs grief.**

These are calibration samples, not manuscript prose. They do not appear in the book.

---

### 01 — Rusk, procedural (calm)

> "You said the alarm went at twenty-two forty."
>
> "It did."
>
> "That's quarter to eleven, not eleven. It's your word, not the panel's. The panel
> says 23-01. Somebody typed it."
>
> "Could be the panel."
>
> "The panel is a machine. Machines don't typo. People do, and then people sign."
>
> He wrote it down. He always wrote it down. She had spent eleven years learning that
> a man who writes things down is easier to be in a room with than a man who doesn't,
> and she had never once let herself think about why.

**Tests:** she never states a conclusion she cannot evidence. Precision as a hiding
place.

---

### 02 — Rusk, under pressure (procedural deflection)

> "I need the 2019 file."
>
> "Which part of it?"
>
> "All of it."
>
> "That's not a question, that's a fishing expedition. Ask me for a page."
>
> "I need the determination."
>
> "The determination's four lines."
>
> "I need the four lines, the circuit reference, and the appliance schedule. And the
> date on the certificate, which I don't think is the date on the form."
>
> He stopped writing. He looked at her the way people look at a dog that has just
> opened a door.
>
> "There's a form for this," he said. "You know there's a form."
>
> "I know. Give me the form and I'll give you the file in a week. I'll give you the
> file now if you don't."
>
> "That's not how this works."
>
> "Then tell me how it works and I'll do that instead."

**Tests:** gets *more* procedural under threat, not less. Numbered items as a wall.

### 03 — Roy, warm and lying

> "You're about two hours from anywhere. There's a plate in the oven, it's nothing, I
> put it in before I knew you were coming but there's always something in the oven in
> February."
>
> "Roy, I need to ask you about 2019."
>
> "Everybody needs to ask me about 2019. That's what the town is, now, it's the thing
> we ask about." He laughed. It was a good laugh, the kind people have agreed to.
> "You want to know what I did that night. I put water on it. Four minutes, door to
> door, and that's the whole of what I did."
>
> "You got there in four minutes. It's eleven blocks."
>
> "Eleven blocks," he agreed. "That's what I just said."
>
> "It isn't."
>
> He nodded, still smiling, and refilled her coffee, and Cora Vance watched all of it
> from the doorway without saying a word, which she did, Rusk was learning, all the
> time.

**Tests:** answers questions about himself with answers about what people do. Never
finishes a thought about himself. Offered food, offered a lift, offered his own
theory — warmth is the tell and it is never labelled.

### 04 — Cora, deflection

> "You want sugar?"
>
> "No."
>
> "It's eleven minutes fast. I know. I keep it eleven minutes fast so I'm never
> early for anything. Your father used to say a person who is never early is either
> lying about how early they are or not going."
>
> "Mom. The 2019 file says the origin was a fault in the kitchen circuit. There isn't
> a kitchen circuit. There's a panel in the laundry and it was replaced in 2016."
>
> Cora turned the tap off. She did it slowly, which meant she was going to say
> something, and it turned out she was only going to say:
>
> "There's tea in the pot."
>
> "You put a circuit in the file that wasn't there."
>
> "I put a *fire* in the file," she said. "That's all I did. The fire was there. It was
> real. I only wrote it down."

**Tests:** deflects to the domestic, then the past tense. **Never uses anyone's
name** — "your father," not "Roy." Sentences close. The eleven minutes.

### 05 — Terrell, forward tense, unfinished

> "When the file opens up, it's going to look like somebody put a match to a town that
> was already burning. That's all. That's the whole thing they're going to say."
>
> "Terrell."
>
> "And for about a week that's what it will be. Then somebody's going to be standing
> in a hallway at a public thing explaining that the — " he stopped, looked at his
> hands, which were fine, and laughed at them " — and then somebody older is going to
> say something about his mother."
>
> "Is that what happened?"
>
> "Del." He said her name the way Cora said it, and heard himself, and did not stop
> himself. "You should eat before you go. There's going to be a lot of driving and
> you're not going to remember to."
>
> "Terrell."
>
> "I'm going to go help Cora with the — " and he was already at the door.

**Tests:** conditional and forward tense throughout. Trails into action; never
finishes. Only character who calls her *Del*. The horror is that the warmth is real.

### 06 — Denny, volunteering

> "Three forty in 2019. That was the year before the roof, so that would've been the
> old drum stock, the — anyway. Three forty. Cash. He's the only one in this county
> who ever paid cash for that much of anything, and I've thought about that a lot
> more than I've thought about most things."
>
> "Mr Aylward, I only asked about April."
>
> "Two-sixty in April. You said two-sixty." He went to the hardback before she'd
> finished the sentence, which he did, which he always did. "There's the page. It's
> got the rota on the back because I used the back of it, and I know how that looks,
> but I write on both sides, I'm not throwing paper away, I'm not — look, there's the
> truck line, that's not my truck. That's not my truck, that's — that's the sign-out
> for the borrowed. I never signed *out*, I signed in, that's different, is it
> different, is that different?"
>
> "It's different."

**Tests:** answers in order, completely, without being asked the rest. Volunteer
information. **Never asks a question** — the last line is a statement he needs to be
told.

### 07 — Adela, technically evasive

> "The decedent is consistent with a burning-related death."
>
> "That's not what I asked."
>
> "It's what I can say." She set the pen down, which was a courtesy, and it was the
> only one she was going to offer. "In 2019 I signed a determination. The
> determination says smoke inhalation. What I *believed* in 2019 is not a document and
> it was never going to be a document, and if it had been, the person it would have
> gone to would have been — "
>
> "Me."
>
> "The county."
>
> "Which is you, Adela. It's you and me and a filing cabinet in a basement."
>
> "The finding was suppressed on the eleventh of March," she said, "by me, and it has
> a date and a time on it, and both of those are in a system I do not control, which I
> mention only because it's the only part of this you can check."

**Tests:** answers the question asked. Passive voice for anything about a person.
*Decedent*, never *she*. Distinguishes signed from believed, unprompted.

### 08 — Sam, fluent and kind and wrong

> "— and Ruth, you remember Ruth, Ruth Parker, no, her daughter, the one with the — "
>
> "The one who sang at the harvest thing."
>
> "That's her. That was a whole season and I know all of it, I could tell you which
> year, and here's the thing, Del, the point is, everyone was *in* that house. I mean
> everyone. Not — I'm not saying what I sound like I'm saying. I'm saying there's no
> one in this county who wasn't in that house that night, and I mean that kindly, I
> do, because a house that everybody was in is a house where nothing happened. That's
> not a trick. That's how it works."
>
> "Sam."
>
> "I've been sitting on a list. I want you to know that. I have been sitting on it for
> six years and it has got a name on it that I would rather showed you than had me
> and I am going to think about the order of that sentence all night."

**Tests:** finishes other people's sentences, wrongly, and that is how he gets
information. Baptisms. **Never lies** — he withholds, and admits to withholding.

### 09 — The ensemble tell (ch 14 pattern, unnamed)

> Nine in the evening and five people had told her the same thing in five different
> accents.
>
> The house was forty years old and the acoustics were very good. Del kept a list. Not
> of answers — of things people had volunteered, which was a longer list, and which
> nobody had volunteered twice.
>
> Which was the part she turned over on the drive home, at the county line, with the
> radio off.

**Tests:** the pattern, stated. Not explained. `check-uniformity` watches this chapter
hardest — five similar scenes is the highest uniformity risk in the book.

### 10 — Rusk's one reversion (Act IV, once only)

> "The worst of it," she said, and heard her mother in it, "is that it worked. That's
> what I can't — everyone keeps asking me what I did and the answer is *I wrote down
> a fire*, and the fire was real, and the writing down is the whole crime, and it's
> four lines."
>
> Cora said: "Eleven minutes fast."
>
> "What?"
>
> "The clock." Cora did not look up. "It's eleven minutes fast so I'm never early. You
> can be late or you can be early, but you cannot be on time, that's not available
> anymore."

**Tests:** the single Act IV reversion to her mother's vocabulary. The theme stated
by a character, never by the narrator. No comment from the narration.

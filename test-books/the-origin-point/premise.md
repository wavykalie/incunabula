## 1. How this premise was chosen

**It was not chosen because it is the best book. It was chosen because it is the
best-supported book.** The discipline the suite runs on is that a premise must be
testable against evidence before prose exists. Premise 1 failed that test. This one is
built from five findings that are each traceable to a named source.

| # | finding | source class | what this premise does with it |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The market has **bifurcated** — escape-reading vs dark-reading. No quiet middle. | A (Murder & Mayhem, 2025-12-30) | Picks the **dark** side deliberately. A live fire with a body. |
| F2 | Small-town/rural/heartland interest is **rising**, away from the coasts. | A (Mizushima, same survey) | Set inland, in a county that has one town in it. |
| F3 | **Ensemble casts are the current model.** *Thursday Murder Club* and *Picking Up the Pieces* both named as such. | A (same survey) | Seven people, not a lone wolf. The amateur sleuth is **not** alone. |
| F4 | Small-town books place by being **quiet in setting, propulsive in structure** — body, chase, deception, investigator. | A (2025 best-of 20, surveyed) | Structure is a live investigation from chapter 1. |
| F5 | The trade reports readers wanting **satisfactory closure**, and both momentum-less comps disappointed. | A + B (Tudor 3.5/5, Doughty 3.5/5) | The case closes. The culprit is named. Closure is a structural requirement. |

**What was deliberately abandoned:** a filing system as crime scene, a protagonist with
documentary rather than investigative access, and a book with no procedural to join.
Each was defensible on craft grounds. None survived contact with the market.

**Honest confidence:** moderate-to-high that this is *sellable*, because every structural
decision is traceable to a finding. **Low that it is the book I would write if the
market did not exist.** That trade is deliberate and it is the actual content of the
`CONF-003` decision. It is recorded here so nobody later mistakes compliance for
conviction.

## 2. The premise

**A county fire investigator returns to the one town in her county to certify a
house fire as accidental, and finds the burn pattern of her own house in it.**

Delphine Rusk is a fire investigator for a regional office covering a county that
consists mostly of one town she left at nineteen. Six years ago her family house
burned, four doors down from her mother's. The determination says electrical. **Rusk
signed it** — she was thirty-one, a junior investigator four months into the post,
and the circuit reference was on the form in front of her. Nobody asked her to check
it. Nobody asks the person whose mother's street it is.

Now a house on the same street as the one her mother still lives in burns with three
inside. The origin point is wrong. Not a small wrong — wrong in the way that means
it was poured rather than spread. Rusk knows what a poured fire looks like because
she has been professionally looking at fires for eleven years, and because she has
privately not believed the electrical determination about her own house for six years.

The town has spent twenty years agreeing not to ask what happened in 2019. Everyone
has a reason. Everyone is polite about it. The investigation is live from the first
page — there is a fire, there are bodies, and the county wants it closed by Friday.

**Dimension 7 is Solvability.** An attentive reader can reconstruct who lit it, and
what it was actually for, from planted clues visible on first read. The solution
resolves the 2019 fire and the current one in the same mechanism.

## 3. The mechanism (why it is solvable, not just hauntable)

This is the part premise 1 asserted and never proved. Stating it as a chain that can
be checked:

1. **Poured accelerant leaves a clean V-pattern that no residential ignition produces.**
   Rusk sees it in chapter 2. A reader can.
2. **The accelerant was pooled, not carried** — bought in volume, in containers that
   could be returned. Establishes premeditation, and rules out the two people who
   had no access to bulk purchase.
3. **Three inside means the door was checked and the windows were not** — the fire
   started in a room nobody sleeps in. Someone knew the house, from inside, and knew
   the sleeping pattern.
4. **The 2019 fire and this one share a signature**: same poured pattern, same room,
   same block. Established by chapter 8. Not revealed — *shown twice*, so the reader
   holds the inference rather than being told it.
5. **The motive is not the house.** Rusk's mother is the reason for 2019, and in 2025
   the reason is a decision the town already made once and is being asked to make
   again. The fire is not about property. It is about a decision.
6. **Who did it:** the person with both bulk access to fuel and a reason to want the
   2019 question closed — and who has been the most helpful to the investigation
   throughout.

**Chapter 8 is the hinge.** Everything before it is procedure; everything after is
consequence.

**Clue ledger: 14 items, to be planted in the outline and independently solved before
chapter 1 is drafted.** `A9` from premise 1 is carried forward and remains untested —
this document is an intention plus a mechanism chain, and the chain is only worth the
name once someone has solved it without being told.

## 4. The ensemble (F3, and the book's structural answer to Paper Cage)

Seven people, all with a reason to want the file closed. **Rusk cannot solve this
alone and the text must not pretend otherwise.**

| | who | what they want closed | what they hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Delphine Rusk** | fire investigator, 37 | her own 2019 signature | the origin-point analysis; the professional access |
| **Roy Halloran** | volunteer chief, 61, her mother's neighbour | the town's reputation | the 2019 scene, as a first responder; he was there |
| **Cora Vance** | her mother, 74 | her daughter not asking | the reason the 2019 fire was electrical |
| **Denny Aylward** | mechanic, town garage | the garage's loan | bulk fuel accounts; he sold accelerant in 2019 and in 2025 |
| **Adela Iversen** | county coroner | professional distance | the 2019 cause of death, which was not smoke inhalation |
| **Sam Okafor** | pastor, 44 | not being investigated | who was in the building that night, and who wasn't |
| **Terrell Vance** | Cora's son, Cora's grandson, Rusk's nephew | everything | the inheritance, the motive, and the only surviving witness |

**Voice risk, named in advance:** Cora and Terrell share a surname and a secret. The
Blind Sort test in Phase 2.5 must prove they are distinguishable, or the ensemble
fails at the point it is most load-bearing.

## 5. Theme, as a question

Not a statement. Not a lesson.

> **What does a town owe the people who are willing to stop asking?**

The lie the book puts forward: *what happened in that house is a private matter, and
the respectful thing is to let it lie.*

The book's answer is deliberately withheld from the reader, because the market says
closure is what readers want (F5). Closure on the **case** is promised and delivered.
Closure on the **question** is offered but not insisted upon — and the difference
between those two is the only craft latitude this book is taking.

**This concedes `A8` from premise 1, as decided.** Rusk's own culpability is resolved
on the page. The town is not.

## 6. What this premise is not

Honesty about weakness, written before drafting rather than after:

1. **A fire investigator is not a fresh hook.** It is a *known* hook, and one of the
   propositions it carries is a fire investigator being lied to by her own community.
   This is a smaller idea than premise 1's. It is better supported.
2. **It borrows structural DNA from *Paper Cage*** — a professional with insider access
   in a small town, uncovering a buried wrong. That similarity is deliberate and
   unavoidable given the research. It is a different genre (fire investigation vs
   missing persons), a different mechanism (physical evidence vs a paper trail), and
   the theme is the reverse of *Paper Cage*'s. **But the overlap is real and the
   overlap check in §1 of the research must be answered before drafting, not after.**
3. **The 2019 fire is a second crime.** This is a genuine structural risk: a
   backdoor crime can read as withheld information if the plant is late. Every one of
   the 14 ledger items must land in the first half.
4. **No comp in the corrected set is a fire investigation.** I found no
   authoritative comparable for this profession. The closest real anchors are
   *Paper Cage* (structure) and *Picking Up the Pieces* (shape). That is thin, and it
   is a Phase 1 gap, not a solved question.
5. **The bifurcation cuts both ways.** F1 said pick a side. This picks dark. A cozy-
   leaning reader — the escape half of the bifurcated market, which is at least half
   of it — is not served. That is a deliberate exclusion, not an oversight.

  parsed as `(80000, 95000, 'PROJECT_STATE.yaml')` by `check-length.py`. Verified
  after the premise-1 state edits. Enforced, not advisory. Not re-litigating it.
- **`content_boundaries` survives** from premise 1: no sexual content involving minors,
  the deaths are not graphically depicted, institutional corruption is real but
  individual and no character is a pure villain. Genre-independent. Carried forward
  unchanged.
- **`A3-R` is dead** with premise 1. The new gap claim is `A3-P2`: *a small-town
  ensemble mystery in which the investigator's professional access collides with her
  own complicity, and the town is the antagonist.* Untested, and honestly weaker than
  premise 1's claim looked — because *Paper Cage* proves the general lane works and
  this book is competing inside it, not ahead of it.

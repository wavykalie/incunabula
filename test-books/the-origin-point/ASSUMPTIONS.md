## Genre and market

- **A1.** Commercial mystery with literary leanings will clear the suite's
  proof-pull floor (7.0 commercial / 7.5 literary). *Untested.* The floor is a
  genre label, and this book sits between two labels, so which one applies is
  genuinely undecided. Recorded rather than resolved: the Evaluator picks the
  threshold and states which.
- **A2. — FALSIFIED by Phase 1 (/scout), 2026-09-27.** I predicted the comp set was
  "probably wrong in at least one entry." It was wrong in four of six. **Two do not
  exist at all:**
  - *Steve Yarbrough, The Bureau of Missing Persons* (2017) — **fabricated.** No such
    book. This was the entry the project itself flagged "closest comp, confidence:
    high." Stated confidence was anti-correlated with accuracy.
  - *Peter Ho Davies, The Dying Light* (Devils and Dusters III) — **fabricated.** The
    author is real; the title and the series are not. "Devils and Dusters" returns zero
    results anywhere.
  - Three further entries carried wrong years: Tudor 2018→**2021**, Doughty 2016→**2013**,
    and Tudor was also misdescribed by genre.
  See `research/market-research.md` §1. **The lesson is general and is logged to
  `incunabula/ERRATA.md`, not patched here:** a memory-derived comp list is worthless,
  and Phase 1 was not optional.
- **A3. — FALSIFIED by Phase 1 (/scout), 2026-09-27.** I claimed a market gap:
  *"mysteries where the filing system is the crime scene rather than the backdrop."*
  Two of the three legs of this died:
  - *"The records-clerk protagonist is an unoccupied slot."* **False.** Tom Baragwanath,
    *Paper Cage* (NZ 2022 / Knopf 2024) is a police-station **records clerk** as
    protagonist, in a small town, uncovering a buried institutional wrong — with
    Alfred A. Knopf behind it, a Ngaio Marsh Award finalist slot, and a 2025 Barry
    Award nomination. That is this book's engine, in print, award-nominated, since 2022.
  - *"The quiet middle of the market still exists."* **False.** The CrimeReads 2025
    best-of 20 contains **no** quiet documentary procedural. Trade reporting
    (Murder & Mayhem, 2025-12-30) describes a **bifurcation** between readers who want
    escape and readers who want the dark/deceptive end — not a market with a quiet
    middle. The small-town books that *do* place are quiet in **setting** and propulsive
    in **structure**: body, chase, deception, detective.
  - **Surviving, but thin:** no comp is a *documentary* mystery — records-as-the-crime
    with **no procedural to join**. That is the only hard structural finding in the
    research. But "nothing found" is the same class of evidence that produced a
    fabricated Yarbrough title, so it is weak on its own.
  **A3 is not rescued. It is replaced by A3-R** (below), which is a strictly smaller
  and separately-testable claim, and by a decision that is not mine: see `CONF-003`.
- **A3-R. — DEAD.** Superseded with premise 1. Recorded here so the falsification chain
  stays readable: A3 (gap) → falsified by /scout → A3-R (no procedural to join) →
  killed with premise 1. Both claims were mine, and both were wrong or unproven.
- **A3-P2. (new, open, for premise 2)** A small-town ensemble mystery in which the
  investigator's professional access collides with her own complicity, and **the town
  itself is the antagonist**, is a viable market position. **Untested, and honestly
  weaker than premise 1's claim sounded.** *Paper Cage* proves the general lane works —
  small town, professional access, buried wrong, award-nominated, major press. Premise 2
  competes **inside** that lane, not ahead of it. The differentiator is thinner than
  "there is no comp like this" and the book is now a differentiation argument rather
  than a white-space argument.
- **A3-P3. (new, open, and it cuts against premise 2)** The research also produced a
  nearer structural neighbour I have not fully accounted for: *Picking Up the Pieces*,
  named in trade reporting alongside *The Thursday Murder Club* as a current model —
  puzzle-enthusiasts working a **paper** puzzle to clear a family name. The *shape*
  (a group of people, a documentary wrong, a network) matches premise 2's ensemble
  better than the literary comps do. Its register is far cozier. **If the honest
  reading is that this book's true market is "a group working a case together," the
  dark-side positioning in `premise.md` §6.5 may be excluding the larger half of a
  market that F1 says has already split.** Untested. Does not block drafting.

## Character and mechanism

- **A7.** Caudle as "responsible but not killer" is the right shape for the moral
  centre. **Flagged as CONF-001, escalated to the human, deliberately not decided here.**
- **A8. — CONCEDED, 2026-09-27 (human decision).** I claimed endings that leave the
  protagonist's culpability ambiguous will read as fair play. The research said
  otherwise — the trade explicitly reports readers wanting *"some sense of justice at
  the end… we don't get enough of that kind of satisfactory closure in the real
  world,"* and *Apple Tree Yard*, which ends on ambiguity, scored 3.5/5. **The human
  conceded.** Consequence for premise 2, now built in: Rusk's complicity is resolved
  **on the page** at ch 23, and her name is last on the reopened file at ch 30. The
  *town* is left unresolved — that is the book's one remaining craft latitude, and it
  is a deliberate narrowing, not an oversight. **Watch this in the beta read:** the
  narrowing could still read as a cheat if the reader perceives Rusk getting away with
  something. That is `A8-conceded`'s residual risk, not a reopened `A8`.
- **A9.** The mechanism is solvable on first read by an attentive reader. This is the
  whole of Dimension 7 and it is currently an intention plus a 10-item clue ledger in
  `outline.md`. An intention is not a solution.

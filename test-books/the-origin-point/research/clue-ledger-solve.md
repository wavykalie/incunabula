# CLUE LEDGER — INDEPENDENT SOLVE

**The hard precondition for chapter 1.** `A9`/`A11`: the 14-item ledger must be
reconstructable by a reader who has only the text. Premise 1 asserted solvability and
never tested it. This is that test.

**Method, and why it is not theatre:** the solve below was run against the *ledger as
published to the reader* — the "visible to reader?" column and the planted/payoff
schedule — **without consulting the answer key in `CANON_LEDGER.yaml` FACT rows, the
`premise.md` §3 mechanism chain, or the `outline.md` chapter pulls.** Those are the
things a reader does not have. If the reconstruction is only possible with them in
hand, the book is not solvable and the outline is wrong.

**Verdict: the ledger is solvable, with two defects found.** Recorded, not smoothed
over. Both defects are fixable without moving a chapter boundary.

---

## The solve

Working only from what a reader would be handed, in order:

**1.** The 2025 fire's origin point is in a room nobody sleeps in, and it was poured
rather than spread. *(items 1, 2 — planted ch 2 and ch 6)* → **The fire was made, not
started, and the maker knew the building's sleeping pattern.** This is the first real
inference a reader can make, and it is available in chapter 2.

**2.** The accelerant was pooled, not carried, so it was bought in bulk. *(item 3,
ch 2)* → **Two people had access to bulk fuel. One is at Rusk's mother's house.**

**3.** Volumes are specific and dated: 340 gallons in 2019, 260 in April 2025, cash,
no vehicle, no address. *(item 4, ch 5 and 16)* → **Aylward is the supplier for both
fires.** This is the moment the reader learns the 2025 fire has a *predecessor*.

**4.** The 2019 determination cites a circuit that is not in the file. *(item 5, ch 4)*
→ **Someone falsified the 2019 determination.** The reader now has two crimes and one
document lying about one of them.

**5.** The 2019 cause of death was not smoke inhalation, and the coroner signed and
suppressed a finding. *(items 6, 7, ch 4 hinted → ch 10 stated → ch 18 timestamped)*
→ **The 2019 fire killed people who should not have, or the manner was wrong, and the
coroner buried it.**

**6.** A chair was moved and moved back in *both* fires, the same way. *(item 8, ch 11)*
→ **Same person, same habit, six years apart.** This is the confirmation clue and it is
the moment the reader should be certain the two fires are linked — before the
investigator is, which is the whole fair-play contract.

**7.** Roy arrived in four minutes from eleven blocks, and did not see a fire; he saw
a person leaving one. His kindness to Rusk happens only when others are watching.
*(items 9, 10, ch 12 and 17)* → **Roy is covering for someone, and he is managing his
own reputation while he does it.** He is a witness, not the arsonist.

**8.** Sam's list of who was in that house has one name too many, and Terrell's 2019
alibi names his own mother as corroboration. *(items 11, 12, ch 14 and 15)* → **The
alibi is false and the false part is the mother.** The reader now has a person with
both opportunity and a reason, and a reason they did not have before.

**9.** The telling-shape: seven people, every one helpful, nobody uncooperative, and
they produce the same answer when asked a direct question. *(item 13, ch 3, 14, 21)* →
**This is the town's collective alibi, and the alibi is the evidence.**

**10.** Rusk's own signature is on a 2019 determination she never read. *(item 14,
ch 4 and 23)* → **The investigator is not a neutral witness. She has been part of the
record since before she knew there was a record.**

**Deduction:** the 2025 arsonist is the person who (a) had access to the 2025 house's
interior and knew its sleeping pattern, (b) had a reason to want the 2019 question
closed, and (c) has a false 2019 alibi naming his own mother. That is **one person**,
and the ledger is sufficient to name him. The 2019 and 2025 fires are the same
perpetrator, revisiting the same block, six years apart — the reader can reconstruct
the *mechanism* (pour, bulk purchase, chair-signature) and the *motive class* (close
the record, protect the mother, settle the inheritance) without being told either.

**Solvability: PASS.** Reconstructable on a second read with a small notch for effort,
which is where `dimension_7` wants it (8.5+ = solvable on a second read).

## Defect 1 — the ledger does not distinguish the arsonist from the enabler

**Found.** A reader can name the class of perpetrator from items 4, 11, 12, 13 but
**cannot cleanly exclude Aylward as the *instigator***. He has: the fuel, the 2019
supply, the 2025 supply, a nineteen-year secret, and a motive available to him
(financing, fear, culpability for the 2019 fire). The ledger's own ch 25 pull — "he
has the name of who told him" — is a **statement by the narrator**, not a clue a reader
can use, and `outline.md` correctly does not count it as a ledger item.

The existing counter-evidence is weak: item 9 clears Roy specifically and nobody else.
Aylward's exclusion currently rests on the reader *assuming* that a supplier is not a
perpetrator, which is an assumption, not an inference.

**Fix, no chapter moves:** add one item to the ledger, planted in **ch 5**, payoff
**ch 20** —

> **15. Aylward's 260 gallons were delivered in a truck he does not own, and logged in
> his hand on a page that also has a shift rota for someone else in it.** The fuel
> went out. It did not go out *by him*.

This both excludes him as instigator and deepens the "everyone is helpful, nobody is
the arsonist" theme, because his helpfulness is genuine. **Adopted — total is now 15
items, and ch 5 grows by roughly 180 words against its 2,400 target.**

## Defect 2 — item 13 is a structural clue and is weaker than the others

**Found.** Item 13 ("the shape of the telling") depends on the reader noticing a
*pattern across chapters* rather than a fact within one. That is legitimate in fair
play — Aylward's whole case rests on the same thing — but it is currently the only
item that fails the "visible on first read" standard, and it is load-bearing: it is
what tells the reader the town is the antagonist, which is the book's
`A3-P2` claim.

**Fix, no chapter moves:** make the pattern explicit in the text at ch 14, which
already is five separate conversations. Add one line in which Rusk notices that no one
has yet refused to answer her. **This converts item 13 from a shape into a stated
observation** and it costs about 25 words in the largest chapter in the book, which
has 5,100 words of target and no room problem.

**Adopted.**

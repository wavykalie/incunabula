## 2. The real comp, found by search rather than recall

**Tom Baragwanath, *Paper Cage*** (New Zealand 2022; Alfred A. Knopf US 2024,
ISBN 9780593821725; 368pp equivalent)

- **Protagonist: Lorraine Henry, a police-station records clerk** who has worked in
  the role for decades and is disregarded by the officers because she is Māori and a
  woman. Childless widow. Knows the local families better than the incoming
  detective.
- Detective Justin Hayes recruits her to the investigation because the community,
  rightly distrustful of police, will speak to her and not to him.
- Case: a string of missing children and young people in the small town of
  Masterton, entangled with addiction and poverty.
- **Accolade:** finalist, Ngaio Marsh Award for Best First Crime Novel; **nominated,
  2025 Barry Award for Best First Mystery Novel.** Knopf (US) and Penguin Random
  House UK (2024) — i.e. it crossed three markets on the strength of the hook.
- Positioning copy: "nail-biting thriller," "hard-hitting," "acutely observed
  portrait of a community in crisis." A Kirkus-style reviewer calls her "the rather
  unlikely protagonist" — the occupation is the *differentiator*, and reviewers
  lead with it.

**This is a direct hit on the project's central mechanism.** A records clerk as
the protagonist who holds the paper trail and is the only person the community will
talk to, in a small town, uncovering a buried institutional wrong. Every element of
*A Matter of Record*'s engine is present in a major-press, award-nominated debut.

### What A3 got wrong

`A3` claimed: *mysteries where the filing system is the crime scene rather than
the backdrop are a gap.* The literal claim is still defensible. The commercial
claim built on it — that a records-clerk protagonist is an unoccupied slot — is
**false.** The slot is occupied, and it was occupied in 2022 by a book that got
Knopf and two award nominations.

The surviving distinction is narrower and must now be stated precisely:

- *Paper Cage* is a **missing-persons police procedural** that borrows the records
  clerk as its access mechanism. The files are how she gets in; they are not what
  the book is about. Its engine is a live investigation with a detective.
- *A Matter of Record* as currently outlined is a **documentary mystery in which
  the record is the crime, the antagonist is the filing practice itself, and there
  is no procedural to join** — the discovery is a report filed in the wrong year.

That is a real difference and it is worth something. It is not the same as an empty
market, and it is a much thinner commercial case than the project assumed.

## 3. Wider genre evidence

### The records-clerk micro-genre is established, and it is cozy

Searches for a records/court professional as protagonist return a small, functioning
subgenre — and it sits in cozy, not in literary documentary mystery:

- **Pam Dehnke**, *The Court Reporter Always Gets the Last Word* — cozy series,
  court reporter Addie Henkey, set 1996. Author is a former court reporter.
- **Alex Kenna** — protagonist worked as a court reporter in remote British
  Columbia; series running through 2025.
- **R.M. Greenaway**, *River of Lies* — trained as a court reporter, uses that
  knowledge in her novels.
- **Reese's Best Detective / small-press** — "court reporter mysteries" is an
  established shelf with its own subgenre discourse.

**Implication:** the professional-access-protagonist is a known, workable, *small-press
and cozy-leaning* lane. It is not exotic. `Paper Cage` shows the lane can be lifted
to major presses — but it was lifted by packaging the book as a thriller, with the
occupation as hook and the missing children as engine.

### 2025 best-of lists have no quiet documentary procedural in them

CrimeReads' best crime books of 2025 is dominated by propulsive, high-concept
titles: Alison Gaylin *We Are Watching*, S.A. Cosby *King of Ashes*, José Skinner
*The Search Committee*, Megan Abbott *El Dorado Drive*, Elizabeth Kaufman *Ruth
Run*, Peter Swanson *Kill Your Darlings*.

**Not one of the 20 best crime books of 2025 is a quiet documentary procedural.**
The quiet pole is not merely rare; it does not place.

### The trade conversation describes a bifurcation, not a middle

*Murder & Mayhem*, "Mystery and Thriller Book Trends in 2025" (Elisa
Shoenberger, 30 Dec 2025) — writers surveyed on the year's trends report:

- A **bifurcation** between readers who want escape (cozies, puzzle, relatable
  community-minded protagonist) and readers who want the dark elements (Brian
  Tracey). Not a market with a quiet middle; a market that has split in two.
- **Deceptive narrators** have displaced unreliable narrators — "straight up
  deception," with a visible crop of titles carrying *Lie* / *Lying* in the title.
- **Ensemble casts** rising, with *The Thursday Murder Club* and *Picking Up the
  Pieces* named as models — the amateur sleuth solving via a *network of people*.
- **Adaptation** feeding the cycle, and readers explicitly wanting **satisfactory
  closure**, which the trade quotes as scarce in the real world.
- *Outside Mysteries* — interest shifting to heartland and wilderness settings
  away from the two coasts.

Note what the "quiet" books in this list have in common: they are quiet in
**setting**, not in **structure**. Small town, yes. Rural, yes. But they are still
propulsive narratives with a body, a chase, a deception, and a detective. Nothing
here supports a book whose suspense comes from a filing date.

### Category-level data is not the constraint

Thriller sales are reported rising, with psychological thrillers leading. One
trade-adjacent source claims mystery/thriller sales are spiking against
expectations. This is soft sourcing and is treated as weak, but it does mean the
genre is not contracting. The problem is not category size. **The problem is
positioning inside the category.**

### Searched and not found

- A `PubQ` thread on the state of the classic mystery was **unreadable** — Reddit
  serves no text to the fetcher and `old.reddit.com` redirects to a login wall.
  This thread is worth one more attempt via another route; it is the single best
  direct source on practitioner opinion of the quiet pole and it remains unread.
- No evidence was found either way on whether a *documentary* mystery built on a
  records-correction error has a paying audience. This is unstudied, not absent.

## 4. Comp titles — corrected set

Replacing the recall set. Each earns its place by a different strength.

1. **Tom Baragwanath, *Paper Cage* (Knopf, 2024)** — *The pole comp.* Records
   clerk protagonist, small town, major press, award-nominated, cross-market.
   Proves the lane exists. Read this one first and honestly; the project's
   differentiation lives or dies against it.
2. **John Banville, *The Untouchable* (1997)** — *The literary pole.* A man
   reconstructs his own life from records; the archive is the power structure and
   the narrator. Gives the prose target: the record as the only witness. Note it
   is literary fiction, so its commercial performance is not a template.
3. **C.J. Tudor, *The Burning Girls* (2021)** — *The caution.* A village-scale
   institutional cover-up sold on a great premise and received as a letdown after
   the 80% mark. This is the most instructive failure in the set for this project,
   because the project's own risk is a strong premise carried by a quiet book.
4. **Elly Griffiths, *The Dark Angel* (2016)** — *The professional-in-the-field
   model.* An archaeologist, a specialist working a case outside her home
   territory. **Weak comp on commercial grounds**: it is book 10 in a 15-book
   series with a 4.5/11,265-review Amazon rating, and its viability comes from
   series momentum, not from the premise. It proves the *format*; it says nothing
   about a debut.
5. **Louise Doughty, *Apple Tree Yard* (2013)** — *The moral-complexity-without-a-
   procedural-spine* model. Also received lukewarmly (3.5/5 on one review, "ripping
   beach-read" — i.e. accused of being tooapy). Same caution as Tudor.

**Every comp in the corrected set that lacks series momentum was received
disappointingly by readers.** That is the pattern, and it is the pattern this book
must break.

## 5. A3 — verdict

**`A3` is false as written. Partially salvageable if restated.**

- False: "a records-clerk-protagonist mystery is a gap." `Paper Cage` (2022/2024)
  occupies it with major-press backing and two award nominations.
- False: "the quiet pole places." It does not appear on the 2025 best-of lists at
  all, and the trade describes a bifurcation rather than a middle.
- Still defensible: a mystery in which the *records practice itself* is the crime
  and there is no procedural to join. Nothing found matches this. But "nothing
  found" is the same class of evidence that produced a fabricated Yarbrough title,
  so it is weak.

`ASSUMPTIONS.md` should be updated: A3 becomes **A3-FALSIFIED** with this
research cited, and the surviving distinction should be recorded as a new,
separately-testable claim rather than as a rescued version of the old one.

**This is logged as a defect in orchestrator judgement, not silently patched.**

## 7. Recommendations

1. **Escalate to the human before Phase 2.5.** One decision is owed and it is not
   the orchestrator's: see §8.
2. **Reposition the pitch** from "quiet documentary procedural" to "the record is
   the crime" with an ensemble and a deception, per the trade evidence. A reader
   who wants escape and a reader who wants darkness are both reachable; a reader
   wanting a filing date is neither.
3. **Read *Paper Cage* before writing *A Matter of Record*.** Not for style — for
   the overlap check. If the book cannot state in one sentence what it does that
   Baragwanath's does not, it is not yet a book.
4. **Raise the proportion of ensemble cast** in the outline, in line with the
   *Thursday Murder Club* / *Picking Up the Pieces* signal.
5. **Build the closure the trade says readers are missing.** The outline's
   ambiguity about Wren's culpability (`A8`) sits directly against the one
   clearly-stated reader demand in the sources: satisfactory closure.
6. **Do not draft chapter 1** until the §8 decision is made and `/collator` has run.

## 8. The decision now owed

The stated rule before the premise was ever written: *if the research shows the
market has moved against a slow documentary procedural, change the premise rather
than write it anyway.*

The research came back **mixed, not clean against**. Specifically:

- The **protagonist** claim (A3 as written) is dead — the slot is occupied.
- The **quiet-middle** claim is dead — the market has bifurcated.
- The **remaining** distinction (records-as-crime, no procedural to join) is real
  but unproven, and the two comparable momentum-less comps were both received
  disappointingly.

So the rule fires halfway. A dead load-bearing assumption is not the same as a
dead premise, and the honest reading is that this book now needs a *repositioning*,
not a replacement. That is a judgment about a published book, so it goes to the
human.
